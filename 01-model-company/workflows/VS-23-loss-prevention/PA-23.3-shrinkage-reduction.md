## W1173. High-Risk SKU Protection Plan & Product Security Fixture Deployment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual shrinkage analysis identifies high-theft categories; or new high-value product category launch; or store remodel/refresh |
| **Frequency** | Annual plan review; quarterly deployment execution; ad-hoc for new categories |
| **Volume** | ~1,500–2,000 high-risk SKUs across chain (out of 35,000 active); ~200 stores requiring security fixture maintenance |
| **Owner** | LP Manager (Regional) |
| **Participants** | LP Analyst, Category Manager, Store Manager, LP Training Coordinator, Security Equipment Vendor |

### Background

BuildRight's product assortment includes categories with high theft appeal: power tools (PHP 3,000–50,000), electrical wiring and breakers (portable, resellable), plumbing fittings (brass, copper — high scrap value), adhesives and sealants (compact, expensive), and safety equipment. These high-risk SKUs represent ~4–5% of active items but account for ~25–35% of shrinkage value. Standard EAS tagging per W844 provides basic deterrence, but professional thieves defeat EAS tags, and internal theft by employees is harder to detect with physical security alone. This workflow establishes a tiered protection plan: Spider Wrap/Alpha Keepers for mid-range items, locked display cases for high-value tools, and secured behind-counter storage for premium products. Deployment must balance theft prevention against customer experience — customers want to touch and compare tools before purchasing, so over-securing merchandise reduces sales conversion.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **High-risk SKU identification**: Annual shrinkage analysis per W845 identifies top-theft SKUs by: (a) shrinkage value (quantity × cost), (b) shrinkage rate (shrinkage quantity ÷ sales quantity), (c) theft pattern (external shoplifting vs. internal employee theft); LP Analyst classifies SKUs into protection tiers: Tier A (locked case, behind counter): items > PHP 10,000 or top-50 shrinkage value; Tier B (Spider Wrap / Alpha Keeper): items PHP 2,000–10,000 with high shrinkage rate; Tier C (EAS tag + shelf positioning): items < PHP 2,000 with moderate shrinkage | LP Analyst | National LP Director | 2 days |
| 2 | **Protection plan per category**: For each Tier A/B category, LP Manager designs protection plan with Category Manager: (a) power tools: locked glass display case with sample tools on display; full inventory behind counter; customer requests specific model, staff retrieves from secure storage; (b) electrical fittings: Spider Wrap on multi-packs; individual fittings in Alpha Keepers; high-value breakers in locked peg hook area; (c) plumbing fittings (brass/copper): secured bin display with employee-access-only backing; (d) adhesives/sealants: EAS tag + shelf positioning near high-traffic aisle; (e) Category Manager approves plan ensuring sales conversion impact is acceptable | LP Manager (Regional) + Category Manager | National LP Director | 1 day/category |
| 3 | **Fixture procurement & deployment**: For new security fixtures: (a) LP Manager orders locked display cases, Spider Wraps, Alpha Keepers, secured peg hooks from security equipment vendor; (b) schedule installation during store remodel or low-traffic period; (c) Store Manager coordinates installation with vendor; (d) train staff on fixture access procedures (key management, Spider Wrap removal at POS); (e) system updates high-risk SKU protection tier assignment for POS deactivation procedures | LP Manager / Store Manager / Vendor | National LP Director | 1–2 weeks/store |
| 4 | **POS integration for security deactivation**: System ensures: (a) Tier A items: POS requires manager key swipe to authorize sale; (b) Tier B items: POS prompts cashier to remove Spider Wrap/Alpha Keeper and scan deactivation barcode; (c) all high-risk items: POS enforces quantity threshold alert — if customer purchases > 3 units of a Tier A item, alert LP Analyst for potential ORC per W840 | System / IT | LP Manager | Per IT sprint |
| 5 | **Quarterly compliance verification**: LP Analyst conducts quarterly store visit to verify: (a) security fixtures intact and functional, (b) high-risk SKUs correctly placed in designated protection fixtures, (c) Spider Wraps/Alpha Keepers applied to all Tier B items, (d) EAS tags present on all Tier C items, (e) locked case keys secured and access logged; compliance score feeds store LP scorecard | LP Analyst | LP Manager (Regional) | 60 min/store |
| 6 | **Annual plan refresh**: LP Manager reviews annual shrinkage data vs. protection plan: (a) did protected categories show shrinkage reduction? (b) are new categories emerging as high-theft? (c) should any categories be re-tiered? (d) fixture maintenance/replacement needs; update plan for next fiscal year | LP Manager (Regional) | National LP Director | 1 day |

### System Touchpoints
- Shrinkage analytics per W845 for high-risk SKU identification
- ERP item master with protection tier classification
- POS security deactivation prompts and quantity alerts
- Security fixture asset tracking per store
- LP store compliance scoring module
- Integration with W844 EAS management and W840 ORC detection

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Sales conversion impact**: locked display cases reduce impulse purchases; customers may leave if they cannot access product without staff assistance, especially during peak hours when staff availability is limited
- **Customer experience friction**: Spider Wraps and Alpha Keepers make it harder for customers to handle and compare products; trade-off between security and shopping experience
- **Key management risk**: locked case keys held by multiple staff members increase risk of internal theft; electronic key card access with audit trail preferred but more expensive; mitigated by the LP Manager (Regional) review and approval gate
- **Fixture maintenance**: Spider Wraps break, Alpha Keepers crack, locked case hinges jam; unmaintained security fixtures are worse than no fixtures (false sense of security)
- **Category boundary disputes**: LP wants maximum protection; Category Manager wants maximum accessibility; balanced approach requires executive arbitration


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "High-risk SKU identification" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System rule-based auto-approval of "approves plan ensuring sales conversion impact is acceptable" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-schedule of "schedule installation during store remodel or low-traffic period" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-312 (ensure controlled execution — Shrinkage Reduction (PA-23.3).)
- operational: LP Manager (Regional) review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **LP Analyst**: quarterly compliance visits — 200 stores × 60 min × 4 visits/year = 800 hours/year; ~0.5 FTE equivalent
- **LP Manager (Regional)**: plan design and deployment coordination ~10–15 days/year; absorbed by existing role
- **Store staff**: locked case access adds ~2–3 min per customer interaction for Tier A items

### Time Estimate
- Annual plan design: 2–3 days
- Per-category protection design: 1 day
- Per-store fixture deployment: 1–2 weeks
- Quarterly compliance visit: 60 min/store
- **Annual estimate: ~800 compliance hours (200 stores × 60 min × 4 visits/year per the staffing) + ~20 planning days**

## W1174. Loss Prevention Store Compliance Audit Program & Scoring

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — monthly per store; or triggered by shrinkage result spike, exception report anomaly (W837), or anonymous tip |
| **Frequency** | Monthly per store (200 stores = ~200 audits/month); within the monthly cadence each store receives ~1 comprehensive and ~2 spot-check audits per quarter |
| **Volume** | ~200 audits/month per the Frequency (comprehensive + spot-check variants); ~2,400 audits/year |
| **Owner** | LP Auditor |
| **Participants** | LP Auditor, Store Manager, Department Supervisor, LP Manager (Regional), National LP Director |

### Background

Systematic LP compliance auditing ensures that loss prevention policies and procedures are consistently applied across all 200 stores. Without regular auditing, LP compliance degrades — cash handling procedures loosen, receiving verification becomes cursory, EAS tagging rates drop, and high-risk SKU security fixtures fall into disrepair. The audit program provides a standardized scoring framework that enables: (a) store-to-store comparison, (b) trend tracking over time, (c) identification of systemic compliance gaps, and (d) targeted remediation for underperforming stores. Audit scores are incorporated into Store Manager performance evaluation and incentive compensation, creating accountability for LP results.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Audit schedule & preparation**: LP Auditor receives monthly audit schedule from LP Manager (Regional); each auditor covers ~40–50 stores; for comprehensive audits: pre-load audit checklist on tablet with store-specific data: current shrinkage rate, exception report trends per W837, previous audit findings and remediation status, high-risk SKU list per W1173 | LP Auditor | LP Manager (Regional) | 30 min/store prep |
| 2 | **Comprehensive audit execution** (quarterly per store): LP Auditor conducts in-store assessment covering 10 LP domains: (a) **Cash handling**: cash drawer procedures, safe access log, deposit preparation, dual-control compliance; (b) **Receiving**: DSD verification per W1168, goods receipt accuracy, dock door security; (c) **EAS compliance**: tag application rate per W844, deactivator functionality, gate alarm response; (d) **High-risk SKU protection**: fixture integrity per W1173, correct tiering, key management; (e) **Refund/return controls**: authorization compliance, ID verification, receipt validation per W841; (f) **CCTV & access control**: camera uptime per W838, recording integrity, restricted area access; (g) **Backroom security**: receiving dock access, stock room access, trash compactor area (concealment point); (h) **LP awareness**: staff knowledge of reporting procedures, whistleblower channel, LP policy; (i) **Vendor compliance**: vendor ID verification, delivery appointment adherence, dock security per W843; (j) **Documentation**: LP log maintenance, incident reporting accuracy, corrective action closure | LP Auditor | Store Manager | 3–4 hours |
| 3 | **Spot-check audit execution** (alternate quarters): Focused 60-min audit targeting 1–2 domains based on: (a) previous comprehensive audit deficiencies, (b) current shrinkage trend, (c) exception report alerts per W837; faster format allows more frequent store visits without disrupting operations | LP Auditor | Store Manager | 60 min |
| 4 | **Scoring & rating**: System scores each domain on 1–5 scale: 1 = Non-compliant (critical gap), 2 = Below standard, 3 = Partially compliant, 4 = Compliant, 5 = Best practice; overall store score calculated as weighted average: cash handling (20%), receiving (15%), EAS (15%), high-risk SKU (15%), refund controls (10%), CCTV (5%), backroom (5%), LP awareness (5%), vendor compliance (5%), documentation (5%); overall rating: A (≥ 4.5), B (3.5–4.4), C (2.5–3.4), D (< 2.5 — requires immediate action plan) | System / LP Auditor | LP Manager (Regional) | 15 min |
| 5 | **Findings debrief**: LP Auditor debriefs Store Manager on findings: (a) review each domain score with specific observations, (b) highlight critical findings requiring immediate remediation, (c) acknowledge areas of excellence, (d) agree on remediation actions with deadlines, (e) Store Manager signs acknowledgment | LP Auditor | Store Manager | 30 min |
| 6 | **Remediation tracking**: Store Manager executes corrective actions; system tracks remediation progress with deadlines; LP Auditor follows up on critical findings within 30 days; unresolved critical findings escalated to Regional Manager; repeated D ratings escalated to VP Store Operations with potential Store Manager performance action | Store Manager / LP Auditor | LP Manager (Regional) | 30 min/follow-up |
| 7 | **Monthly reporting & analytics**: LP Manager (Regional) compiles monthly audit analytics: (a) average store score by region, (b) most common deficiencies, (c) remediation closure rate, (d) score trend by store, (e) correlation between audit score and shrinkage rate; present to National LP Director for strategic decisions | LP Manager (Regional) | National LP Director | 4 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- LP audit checklist application on tablet with photo capture
- Domain scoring engine with weighted calculation
- Store LP compliance dashboard with trend tracking
- Remediation tracking module with deadline alerts
- Integration with W837 exception reporting, W844 EAS, W838 CCTV, W1173 high-risk SKU
- Store Manager performance evaluation linkage

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Audit fatigue**: monthly audits can feel punitive rather than supportive; auditor tone and approach must be coaching-oriented
- **Auditor consistency**: different auditors may score the same observation differently; calibration sessions needed quarterly
- **"Show time" effect**: stores may temporarily improve compliance when auditor is present but revert after; unannounced spot-checks per step 3 mitigate
- **Remediation resource constraints**: Store Managers may agree to corrective actions but lack staff time to execute; realistic remediation plans required
- **Data overload**: 200 stores × monthly audits = 2,400 audits/year; analytics must identify actionable patterns, not just generate reports


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "checklist on tablet with store-specific data" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "log, deposit preparation, dual-control compliance" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-report of "report alerts per W837" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-312 (ensure controlled execution — Shrinkage Reduction (PA-23.3).)
- operational: LP Auditor review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **LP Auditors**: 1 auditor per 40–50 stores = ~4–5 FTE national; each conducts ~40–50 comprehensive + ~80–100 spot-check audits/quarter ≈ 480–600 audits/year per auditor (consistent with the 2,400/year chain-wide cadence)
- **LP Manager (Regional)**: 4 hours/month on reporting; absorbed by existing role

### Time Estimate
- Per comprehensive audit: prep 30 min + execution 3–4 hours + scoring 15 min + debrief 30 min = **4.5–5.5 hours**
- Per spot-check: prep 15 min + execution 60 min + scoring 10 min + debrief 15 min = **1.5–2 hours**
- **Annual: ~2,400 audits × 3 hours average = ~7,200 auditor-hours/year**

## W1249. Consignment & Vendor-Managed Inventory Shrinkage Monitoring & Reconciliation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — monthly consignment reconciliation cycle; ad-hoc triggered by consignment sell-through variance, VMI replenishment discrepancy, or vendor audit finding |
| **Frequency** | Monthly reconciliation per vendor; quarterly deep-dive for all consignment/VMI partners |
| **Volume** | ~12 VMI vendors (300 SKUs); ~25 consignment vendors (appliances, tiles); ~37 monthly reconciliations |
| **Owner** | LP Analyst / Inventory Manager |
| **Participants** | Category Manager, Vendor Account Manager, Finance Controller, Store Manager |

### Background

BuildRight Depot operates both consignment inventory (major appliances, select tiles — owned by supplier until sold) and vendor-managed inventory (VMI — ~300 SKUs from 12 key vendors where the vendor manages replenishment). These arrangements create unique shrinkage risks: (a) consignment goods are not owned by BuildRight but are stored in-store, creating ambiguity about who bears the loss if goods are damaged or stolen; (b) VMI vendors rely on BuildRight's reported sell-through data to trigger replenishment — inaccurate data leads to incorrect vendor invoicing; (c) consignment goods do not appear on BuildRight's balance sheet inventory, creating a "shadow inventory" that may be excluded from standard shrinkage analysis; (d) VMI stock counts are performed by vendor representatives who may not follow BuildRight's counting procedures. Philippine retail consignment losses are estimated at 2–3× the standard shrinkage rate because consignment goods often receive less LP attention than owned inventory. This workflow ensures consignment and VMI shrinkage is monitored, reconciled, and allocated correctly.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monthly consignment physical count**: At month-end, Store Manager coordinates with vendor representative to conduct joint physical count of all consignment goods: (a) count every consignment SKU on sales floor and in backroom; (b) compare to vendor's consignment inventory record; (c) document variance by SKU (units over/short vs. vendor record); (d) identify damaged, defective, or display-worn consignment items requiring return to vendor | Store Manager / Vendor Representative | Category Manager | 2–3 hours per store per month (consignment section only) |
| 2 | **VMI sell-through reconciliation**: Monthly reconciliation of VMI vendor sell-through: (a) extract POS scan data for all VMI SKUs; (b) compare POS sell-through to vendor's reported replenishment deliveries; (c) calculate implied on-hand: opening stock + deliveries – POS sales = implied on-hand; (d) flag any SKU where implied on-hand differs from physical count by > 2%; (e) investigate variances as potential scanning fraud, unrecorded damage, or receiving discrepancies | LP Analyst / Inventory Manager | Category Manager | 4 hours/month |
| 3 | **Consignment shrinkage allocation**: For confirmed consignment losses: (a) review consignment agreement for loss responsibility clause — typically: (i) theft/damage during display: shared loss (50/50) or vendor bears if within vendor-controlled display fixture, (ii) theft due to inadequate store security: BuildRight bears loss, (iii) force majeure (typhoon, flood): vendor bears loss or insurance claim; (b) calculate loss value at vendor's consignment cost (not BuildRight SRP); (c) generate shrinkage allocation report with supporting documentation; (d) submit to vendor for acceptance or dispute | Category Manager | Finance Controller | 2 hours/month |
| 4 | **VMI replenishment discrepancy investigation**: When VMI replenishment quantity differs from expected: (a) compare vendor delivery docket to physical goods received; (b) verify VMI data feed accuracy — ensure POS sell-through data is being transmitted correctly to vendor system; (c) check for unreported in-store transfers or returns that were not captured in VMI data; (d) if vendor over-supplied (phantom replenishment), notify Procurement for vendor discussion | Inventory Manager | Category Manager | 2 hours per discrepancy |
| 5 | **Quarterly consignment/VMI shrinkage review**: LP Manager consolidates chain-wide consignment and VMI shrinkage results: (a) compare consignment shrinkage rate to owned-inventory shrinkage rate; (b) identify vendors with consistently high shrinkage rates (> 2%); (c) review VMI data accuracy scorecard — on-time delivery fill rate, data feed error rate, stock-out frequency; (d) recommend vendors for consignment/VMI program continuation, modification, or conversion to standard purchase; (e) present findings to VP Merchandising and CFO | LP Manager (Regional) | National LP Director | 4 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- **Inventory Module** — consignment stock visibility (non-valuated), VMI stock tracking, count reconciliation
- **POS System** — sell-through data for VMI reconciliation
- **Procurement Module** — consignment agreement terms, VMI contract parameters
- **LP Module** — shrinkage allocation tracking, vendor discrepancy case management
- **Vendor Portal** — VMI data sharing, consignment count results, shrinkage claim documentation
- **Finance Module** — consignment liability accounting, shrinkage allocation GL posting

### Pain Points / Risks
- Consignment inventory is often stored in premium display locations (appliance section, tile gallery) that are harder to secure with EAS
- Vendor representatives may resist conducting monthly joint counts, citing travel costs for provincial stores
- VMI data feed errors can accumulate over months before detection, creating large reconciliation gaps
- Philippine consumer appliance buyers may damage consignment display models (floor samples), creating losses not covered by standard consignment agreements
- Disputes over shrinkage allocation can delay vendor settlement and strain relationships


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "Monthly consignment physical count" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-scan of "scan data for all VMI SKUs" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "Consignment shrinkage allocation" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-225 (ensure shrinkage detection & case governance.)
- operational: LP Analyst / Inventory Manager review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Staffing Implication
- Store Managers: 2–3 hours/month additional time for consignment counts
- LP Analyst: 4 hours/month for VMI reconciliation
- Category Manager: 2 hours/month for shrinkage allocation
- Total: ~8–9 hours/month dedicated to consignment/VMI shrinkage monitoring

### Time Estimate
- Monthly reconciliation: ~8 hours per store cluster (10 stores) = ~160 hours/month chain-wide
- Quarterly review: 4 hours per region × 5 regions = 20 hours/quarter
- **Annual estimate: ~1,920 hours (monthly) + ~80 hours (quarterly) = ~2,000 person-hours/year**

## W1336. Return Fraud & Abuse Pattern Detection, Investigation & Prevention

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Return exception alert from POS system (W12); LP Analyst identifies return fraud pattern during weekly exception review (W837); or Store Manager reports suspicious return activity |
| **Frequency** | Weekly pattern analysis; daily exception monitoring; ad-hoc investigation per detected pattern |
| **Volume** | ~56,000 returns/month across 200 stores (~2% of transactions per W1627); estimated 5–8% return fraud/abuse rate = ~2,800–4,500 fraudulent returns/month |
| **Owner** | LP Analyst |
| **Participants** | LP Manager (Regional), Store Manager, POS System Administrator, Finance Analyst, VP Legal (for prosecution cases) |

### Background

BuildRight Depot processes ~56,000 product returns per month per W12/W1627, with an estimated 5–8% fraud/abuse rate representing PHP 60–100M in annual losses. Common return fraud schemes in hardware/home improvement retail include: (a) **Receipt fraud**: using forged or altered receipts to return stolen merchandise for cash, (b) **Wardrobing/renting**: purchasing tools or equipment for a one-time project use and returning used items, (c) **Switch fraud**: returning a cheaper or damaged item in the original packaging of a premium item (e.g., returning a used drill in a new drill box), (d) **Price arbitrage**: purchasing items on promotion and returning them after the promotion ends at the higher regular price, (e) **Employee-collusion returns**: employees processing returns for accomplices without verifying merchandise, (f) **No-receipt abuse**: repeatedly returning items without receipt to exploit the no-receipt return policy for store credit. The Philippine context adds challenges: high cash transaction volume (42% of POS), limited customer identification requirements for returns, and cultural reluctance to challenge customers. This workflow establishes systematic pattern detection, investigation, and prevention measures to reduce return fraud losses.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Automated return exception monitoring**: POS system generates daily return exception alerts: (a) high-value returns (> PHP 10,000) without receipt, (b) returns > 3 by same customer within 30 days, (c) returns of items with high shoplifting risk per W1173 (power tools, electrical fittings), (d) returns processed by same cashier > 5 per shift, (e) returns of items with activated serial numbers (power tools — indicates used item return), (f) returns at stores with return rate > 5% of transactions (~2.5x the chain average); alerts routed to LP Analyst dashboard | System | — | Automated |
| 2 | **Weekly return pattern analysis**: LP Analyst conducts weekly analysis of return data: (a) compile return exception report by store, cashier, customer (loyalty ID or tracked by ID), and SKU, (b) identify clusters: same customer returning at multiple stores, same cashier processing excessive exceptions, same SKU with disproportionate return rate, (c) cross-reference with shrinkage data per W845 — do high-return stores also have high shrinkage?, (d) cross-reference with CCTV review requests per W838 — correlate return timestamps with camera footage, (e) generate investigation list: top-10 suspicious patterns for the week | LP Analyst | LP Manager (Regional) | 4 hours/week |
| 3 | **Investigation & evidence gathering**: For each flagged pattern: (a) LP Analyst pulls transaction details: receipt images, POS logs, cashier ID, timestamp, items returned, tender refunded, (b) pulls CCTV footage from store per W838 for return timestamps — verify: did the customer bring the item? is the item condition consistent with return reason? (c) checks customer return history: how many returns in past 12 months? total value returned? which stores?, (d) checks cashier return history: does this cashier have disproportionate exception processing? cross-reference with employee access logs, (e) for switch fraud: compare returned item serial number/barcode to original sale record, (f) compile investigation file with evidence | LP Analyst / LP Manager (Regional) | National LP Director | 1–3 days per case |
| 4 | **Fraud confirmation & response**: When fraud is confirmed: (a) **Customer fraud**: (i) flag customer in system — future returns require manager approval and photo ID, (ii) for PHP 10,000+ fraud: LP Manager coordinates with VP Legal for criminal complaint per W123 if warranted, (iii) for organized return fraud rings: coordinate with ORC investigation per W840, (b) **Employee-collusion fraud**: (i) initiate disciplinary process per W123, (ii) LP Manager interviews cashier with HR representative present per W123.5, (iii) administrative hearing and disciplinary action per DOLE requirements, (c) **Process gap**: if investigation reveals a policy or system gap enabling fraud (e.g., no receipt verification for items > PHP 5,000), escalate to POS System Administrator and LP Manager for policy update | LP Manager (Regional) / VP Legal | National LP Director | Per W123 investigation timeline |

### Background

Industry data consistently shows that employee theft accounts for 40–45% of retail shrinkage — more than external shoplifting in most studies. ### System Touchpoints
- Shrinkage hotspot dashboard: POS sales vs. perpetual inventory variance by category/SKU/store, negative variance alert thresholds (W1528.1)
- CCTV review log: timestamped findings, incident screenshots, pattern documentation (W1528.2)
- Intervention tracking module: security device deployment by store/category, compliance photo upload, deployment status (W1528.3)
- Monthly shrinkage analytics: category-level shrinkage value, before/after comparison, store ranking, intervention ROI (W1528.4)
- POS sales impact analysis: sell-through rate comparison pre/post intervention to quantify security friction (W1528.4)
- Integration with W1497 (Sensor Tags), W1337 (ORC), W1338 (Employee Theft), W538 (POS Exception Monitoring)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Security vs. sales friction trade-off**: locking up power tools and premium paint reduces theft but also reduces impulse purchases and creates customer frustration (waiting 5–10 minutes for staff to unlock display); in provincial stores where staff-to-customer ratio is lower, the service degradation is more acute
- **Professional shoplifter adaptation**: organized retail crime groups adapt to security measures; once power tools are locked up, they shift to electrical wire (can still be pocketed from backstock areas) or plumbing fittings (small enough to bypass sensor gates); continuous monitoring and intervention adjustment is required
- **Perpetual inventory accuracy**: hotspot analysis depends on accurate perpetual inventory; if cycle counts (W6) are not current, the variance analysis compares stale data — resulting in false positives (flagging a store for shrinkage that is actually a counting timing issue) or false negatives (missing actual theft because the perpetual was already wrong)
- **LP staffing for 200 stores**: with ~20 stores flagged weekly for CCTV review, the LP team must cover significant ground; remote CCTV access helps, but physical intervention (locked display installation, staff coaching) requires store visits
