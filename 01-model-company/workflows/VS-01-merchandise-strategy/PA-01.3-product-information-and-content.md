## W50. Product Information Management (PIM)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New SKU creation (W1), seasonal item setup (W32), product content refresh cycle, vendor product update |
| **Frequency** | Continuous; ~1,500–2,500 new SKUs/year + seasonal rotations + content refreshes |
| **Volume** | 35,000 active SKUs requiring product content; ~55,000 total in item master |
| **Owner** | Marketing — Content Manager |
| **Participants** | Content Manager, Content Specialist, Merchandise Planner, Category Manager, Ecom Team, Photographer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trigger: Merchandise Planner creates new SKU in item master per W1.7 (or seasonal setup per W32); system flags SKU as "content pending" — item cannot be published to ecommerce until content is complete | System | — | Automated |
| 2 | Content Manager receives new SKU content request from system queue; assigns to Content Specialist by category | Content Manager | — | 5 min |
| 3 | Content Specialist gathers product information: (a) vendor product data sheet (specs, dimensions, weight, material, finish, color options), (b) Category Manager input (key selling points, target customer, recommended use), (c) competitive product content benchmarking | Content Specialist | Content Manager | 15–30 min/SKU |
| 4 | Content Specialist enriches product data in PIM/ERP: (a) completes all mandatory attributes per category schema (e.g., tiles: material, size mm, thickness, finish, PEI rating, pieces per sqm; lumber: species, dimensions, treatment, grade; paint: base type, coverage area, drying time, finish), (b) writes short description (50–100 chars for search results), (c) writes long description (200–500 chars for product page — features, benefits, application), (d) maps to category navigation path, (e) assigns search keywords and synonyms, (f) links related accessories and cross-sell items | Content Specialist | Content Manager | 20–30 min/SKU |
| 5 | Product photography: (a) for new SKUs with high online potential (A/B items): schedule product shoot with photographer (studio or on-location for large items); produce 3–5 images per SKU (front, detail, lifestyle/context, dimensions reference, packaging), (b) for C-items and low online potential: use vendor-provided images or placeholder category image with "image coming soon" flag; system marks these items for prioritized photography during quarterly content refresh | Content Specialist / Photographer | Content Manager | Shoot: 5–10 min/SKU |

| 7 | Content Manager reviews and approves completed content per SKU; checks attribute completeness, description quality, image quality, and keyword relevance | Content Manager | CMO | 5 min/SKU |
| 8 | System publishes approved content to ecommerce platform (catalog sync); updates in-store kiosk and POS product lookup if applicable; clears "content pending" flag | System | — | Automated |

| 10 | **Quarterly content refresh**: Content Manager reviews content quality metrics (incomplete attributes, missing images, low search ranking, high bounce rate on product pages); prioritizes 500–1,000 SKUs for content improvement per quarter | Content Manager | CMO | 8 hours/quarter |
| 11 | **Seasonal catalog refresh**: before each major promotional period (W13), Content Manager coordinates with Marketing for seasonal landing pages, featured product collections, and updated lifestyle imagery | Content Manager | CMO | Per W13 lead time |

### System Touchpoints
- PIM module or dedicated PIM system integrated with ERP item master (W50.4)
- Category-specific attribute schema with mandatory field enforcement (W50.4)
- Content workflow: Draft → In Review → Approved → Published → Refresh Needed (W50.7–8)
- Digital asset management: image storage, versioning, and linking to SKU records (W50.6)
- Ecommerce catalog sync with content completeness validation (W50.8)
- Content quality dashboard: attribute completeness % by category, missing images, search ranking, page views, bounce rate (W50.10)
- Integration with W1 (SKU creation trigger), W13 (promotional content), W32 (seasonal setup), W36 (vendor onboarding — vendor content import)

> **Seasonal PIM timeline coordination**: W32 seasonal buy planning requires SKU content to be ready for catalog/promo publication at least 6 weeks before season start. The PIM-to-seasonal timeline is: (a) W32 step 2 (Category Manager reviews seasonal forecast) triggers content request to Content Manager with target content-ready date = T-6 weeks before season; (b) W32 step 4 (Category Manager creates seasonal buy plan with SKU list) provides Content Manager with the final SKU list for content creation; (c) Content Manager prioritizes seasonal SKUs in the content queue — seasonal SKUs bypass the standard queue and are assigned to Content Specialists within 2 business days of receipt; (d) Content Specialists complete content (attributes, descriptions, images) within 2 weeks of assignment; (e) Content Manager reviews and approves within 3 business days; (f) system publishes approved content to ecommerce catalog at T-3 weeks before season start to allow Marketing to build seasonal landing pages and email campaigns; (g) if content is not ready by T-3 weeks, Content Manager escalates to CMO with risk assessment (incomplete content reduces ecommerce conversion for seasonal items); this timeline is tracked by Content Manager as part of the W50 quarterly content refresh calendar and reviewed in seasonal kick-off meetings with Category Managers and Marketing.

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Content bottleneck for seasonal SKUs**: W32 seasonal buy planning requires content ready T-6 weeks before season start, but vendor product data sheets often arrive late, compressing the enrichment window to days instead of weeks
- **Photography backlog for C-items**: ~25,000 C-class SKUs rely on vendor-provided images of inconsistent quality; low online conversion rates on these items directly impact ecommerce revenue targets
- **Attribute schema maintenance**: hardware/DIY categories have highly variable attribute requirements (tiles need PEI rating and pieces/sqm; lumber needs species, grade, and treatment) — keeping category schemas current as vendors introduce new product lines is an ongoing burden
- **Multi-channel content consistency**: price, description, and availability must stay synchronized across POS, ecommerce, in-store kiosk, and marketplace channels (Shopee/Lazada); sync failures cause customer complaints and potential DTI complaints
- **Vendor content quality variance**: local Philippine manufacturers often provide incomplete or inaccurate product specifications compared to international vendors, creating an uneven content baseline that requires disproportionate manual enrichment effort

## W97. Sample & Demo Inventory Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New product introduction requiring display samples; category reset (W1) requiring demo units; vendor-supplied display items; or existing samples needing replacement |
| **Frequency** | ~50–100 new samples/demos per month across all stores; seasonal resets ~2/year |
| **Volume** | ~3,000–5,000 sample/demo units across 200 stores at any time |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Category Manager, Store Manager, Stock Associate, Cost Accountant, Buyer, Visual Merchandiser |

### Background

In big-box home improvement retail, physical product samples and demo units are critical sales tools — tile and flooring swatches, paint color chips, tool demonstration units, appliance display models, fixture showrooms, and material sample boards. These items are inventory (they have cost) but are not for sale to customers. Without proper tracking, samples become unaccounted inventory, are accidentally sold (POS scan conflict), are not replaced when worn, or remain on the books after disposal. This workflow manages the lifecycle of non-sellable sample and demo inventory from procurement to disposal.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sample/demo request**: (a) Category Manager identifies need for sample/demo: new SKU introduction (W64), category reset (W1), vendor product launch, or replacement of worn/damaged existing sample; (b) creates sample/demo request specifying: SKU(s), quantity per store (may vary by store format/size), purpose (display, demo, color swatch, showroom model), expected useful life (3–12 months depending on item type) | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 15 min/request |
| 2 | **Procurement**: (a) **From existing inventory**: Buyer approves conversion of regular stock items to sample/demo status; system transfers items from saleable inventory to sample/demo inventory (not deducted from available-to-promise); (b) **From vendor**: Buyer orders samples from vendor — may be free-of-charge (vendor-funded display), at cost, or at special sample pricing; received at DC per W3 with "Sample" receipt type; (c) **Custom fabricated**: Visual Merchandiser designs custom display boards or sample kits; procured as non-stock items per W38 | Buyer / Visual Merchandiser | Category Manager | Per W2/W38 |
| 3 | **Inventory classification**: System classifies sample/demo items with: (a) inventory status = "Sample/Demo" (blocked from POS sale, blocked from replenishment calculation, excluded from ATP, excluded from cycle count of saleable inventory — but included in total inventory valuation for GL purposes); (b) assigned to specific store location; (c) cost center = store-level merchandising display cost; (d) useful life start date; (e) expected replacement date | System | Category Manager | Automated |
| 4 | **Distribution to stores**: (a) DC picks and ships sample/demo items to stores per standard W4 replenishment (but with "Sample" designation — not counted against store replenishment allocation); (b) Store Manager or Dept. Supervisor receives and places on display per planogram (W86); (c) Stock Associate confirms receipt and placement in system | DC Team / Store Manager | Store Manager | Per W4 |
| 5 | **Periodic condition assessment**: (a) Quarterly: Dept. Supervisor assesses sample/demo condition at each store: "Good" (continue display), "Fair" (schedule replacement), "Poor" (remove and dispose); (b) for tool demo units: check functional condition (power tools, appliance demos); (c) for tile/paint swatches: check for fading, staining, chipping; (d) system tracks condition by sample/demo item | Dept. Supervisor | Store Manager | 30 min/store/quarter |
| 6 | **Replacement cycle**: (a) At end of useful life or when condition reaches "Poor": system auto-generates replacement sample request to Category Manager; (b) Category Manager approves replacement and initiates procurement per step 2; (c) new sample received and displayed before old sample removed (continuous display) | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 10 min/replacement |
| 7 | **Disposal of old samples**: (a) System creates disposal request for sample/demo item reaching end of life; (b) disposition options: (i) return to vendor if vendor-funded with return provision, (ii) donate to trade school or community program, (iii) scrap/dispose (most common for worn samples); (c) Store Manager authorizes disposal; system removes from sample/demo inventory and posts GL: Dr. Sample/Display Expense / Cr. Sample Inventory (at original WAC); (d) for vendor-funded samples: if vendor requires return, ship to DC for consolidation per W88 | Store Manager / Stock Associate | Category Manager | 10 min/disposal |

### System Touchpoints
- Sample/demo inventory status: blocked from POS sale, ATP, and replenishment; included in GL inventory valuation (W97.3)
- Sample/demo request and procurement workflow (from existing stock, vendor order, or custom fabrication) (W97.1–2)
- Distribution tracking with receipt confirmation and planogram placement (W97.4)
- Periodic condition assessment with Good/Fair/Poor status tracking (W97.5)
- Auto-replacement request at end of useful life or poor condition (W97.6)
- Disposal workflow with GL posting (expense recognition at disposal, not procurement) (W97.7)
- Sample/demo inventory reporting: value by category, store, age; ROI analysis (W97.8)
- Integration with W1 (assortment — sample needs from category resets), W2/W38 (procurement — sample orders), W3 (receiving — sample receipt type), W4 (distribution — sample shipments), W42 (physical inventory — sample/demo items counted separately), W50 (PIM — sample images in ecommerce catalog), W63 (shelf labels — sample/display designation), W86 (planogram — sample placement), W91 (damaged goods — damaged sample disposal)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Sample shrinkage and unaccounted loss**: demo units (power tools, appliances) in open store environments are vulnerable to theft or misplacement; without periodic physical verification, sample inventory diverges from system records over time
- **Accidental sale of sample/demo items**: if the "Sample/Demo" status flag is not properly enforced at POS, a demo unit could be scanned and sold to a customer — causing inventory valuation errors and potential customer dissatisfaction with a non-new item
- **Worn sample impact on sales**: faded tile swatches, chipped paint chips, or non-functional tool demos actively reduce customer confidence and conversion in the category — the very sales lift the sample is meant to drive
- **Vendor-funded sample disputes**: when vendors provide free display items, there is often ambiguity about ownership and return obligations at end of life; disputes arise when BuildRight disposes of items the vendor expected returned
- **Budget opacity**: sample/demo expenses are buried in merchandising display costs; without dedicated category-level ROI tracking (W97.8c), Category Managers cannot demonstrate whether the ~3,000–5,000 sample units actually drive incremental sales

~15 min per new sample/demo request; quarterly condition assessment ~30 min/store across 200 stores (~100 total store-hours/quarter); Cost Accountant reporting ~1 hour/quarter; replacement procurement absorbed within existing W2 process

## W1313. Vendor-Supplied Merchandising Fixture, Display & Point-of-Purchase (POP) Material Lifecycle Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vendor offers merchandising fixture/display for new product launch; or seasonal display refresh; or existing vendor display requires replacement/repair |
| **Frequency** | ~50–80 new vendor displays deployed chain-wide per month; ~20–30 existing displays repaired/replaced per month |
| **Volume** | ~500–800 active vendor displays across 200 stores at any time; ~840–1,300 display lifecycle events per year (per the monthly Frequency) |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Category Manager, Visual Merchandising Coordinator, Store Manager, Department Supervisor, Vendor Account Manager, Facilities Coordinator |

### Background

Vendors frequently supply BuildRight with branded merchandising fixtures, displays, and point-of-purchase (POP) materials to enhance product visibility and drive sales. In the hardware/home improvement retail, vendor displays are especially impactful for: (a) **Power tools** — demonstration stations (DeWalt, Makita, Bosch) where customers can test tools, (b) **Paint brands** — color wall displays (Davies, Boysen, Nippon Paint) with fan decks and sample cards, (c) **Appliances** — branded display walls (Kolin, Condura, Samsung) with product specs and QR codes, (d) **Tile brands** — display galleries with sample boards organized by brand, (e) **Seasonal promotions** — end-cap displays, bin displays, and promotional signage for sale events. These displays are: (i) owned by the vendor (on loan to BuildRight) or gifted to BuildRight (becoming store property), (ii) governed by vendor agreements that specify: display duration, placement requirements (e.g., "must be at end-cap"), exclusivity (e.g., "no competing brands within 3 meters"), and condition maintenance, (iii) linked to co-op advertising fund commitments per W1303 (vendor display cost may be charged against BuildRight's co-op fund). This workflow manages the lifecycle of vendor-supplied displays from deployment through maintenance, refresh, and removal, ensuring compliance with vendor agreements and optimal sales floor presentation.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Display request & vendor agreement review**: (a) Vendor Account Manager submits display proposal: (i) display type (wall unit, end-cap, island, counter-top, demo station), (ii) dimensions and space requirements, (iii) products featured, (iv) proposed store placement (planogram location), (v) display duration, (vi) exclusivity requirements, (vii) cost allocation (vendor-funded, co-op funded, or BuildRight-funded), (viii) assembly and installation requirements; (b) Category Manager reviews: (i) does the display align with BuildRight's planogram and category strategy?, (ii) does the display conflict with other vendor displays?, (iii) is the space allocation reasonable (not displacing higher-margin products without justification)?, (iv) is the vendor's sales volume sufficient to justify the display space?; (c) Visual Merchandising Coordinator reviews for: (i) visual standards compliance (consistent with BuildRight store aesthetic), (ii) safety (stable, no tip-over risk, no sharp edges, no electrical hazards for lighted displays), (iii) ADA/PWD accessibility (not blocking aisles per W497); (d) Category Manager approves or requests modifications | Category Manager / Visual Merchandising Coordinator | VP Merchandising | 1–2 hours per display proposal |
| 2 | **Display deployment & installation**: (a) for chain-wide deployments (all 200 stores): (i) vendor ships display components to DC for distribution, (ii) DC consolidates with store replenishment shipments per W4, (iii) Department Supervisor receives and stages display components per planogram instruction sheet, (iv) for complex displays (power tool demo station, lighted appliance wall): vendor sends installation team or BuildRight Facilities Coordinator installs per vendor instructions, (v) for simple displays (counter-top, signage, bin display): Department Supervisor assembles and places per planogram; (b) system records display deployment: (i) display ID, vendor, store, location, products featured, installation date, display duration, removal date, (ii) display items tagged in planogram module for planogram compliance checking per W69; (c) photo documentation: Department Supervisor photographs installed display and uploads to ERP for vendor verification and Visual Merchandising audit | Department Supervisor / Facilities Coordinator | Category Manager | Per store: 30 min (simple) to 4 hours (complex) |
| 3 | **Display maintenance & condition monitoring**: (a) Department Supervisor monitors display condition during daily walkthrough (W5A): (i) physical condition: clean, no damage, all lights working (for lighted displays), all product facings stocked, signage current; (b) if display requires maintenance: (i) minor (replace light bulb, clean, restock product): Department Supervisor handles immediately, (ii) major (structural damage, electrical failure, missing components): Maintenance/Utility Staff creates work order per W47, vendor contacted for replacement parts; (c) monthly: Visual Merchandising Coordinator conducts display condition audit per store (rotating — each store audited at least once per quarter): (i) display present and in agreed location?, (ii) display in good condition?, (iii) products correctly stocked per display planogram?, (iv) competing brands not within exclusivity zone?, (v) photo audit uploaded to ERP | Department Supervisor / Visual Merchandising Coordinator | Category Manager | Weekly: 5 min/display; Monthly audit: 15 min/display |

## W1345. Product Barcode & Label Accuracy Verification, GS1 Compliance & Print Quality Audit

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New SKU onboarding (W1); vendor-supplied barcode quality complaint from POS scan failures; periodic GS1 compliance audit; or label printer maintenance cycle |
| **Frequency** | Per new SKU (~2,000–2,500/year); quarterly compliance audit across 200 stores; ad-hoc for scan failure investigation |
| **Volume** | ~2,000–2,500 new SKUs/year requiring barcode verification; ~35,000 active SKUs with shelf labels; 200 stores × ~175 shelf label changes/store/month |
| **Owner** | Content Manager |
| **Participants** | Content Specialist, Category Manager, Store Department Supervisor, IT POS System Administrator, Vendor (barcode source), GS1 Philippines |

### Background

BuildRight Depot's 35,000 active SKUs rely on accurate barcodes (EAN-13 or UPC-A per GS1 standards) for POS scanning, inventory management, and ecommerce fulfillment. Barcode and label errors cause significant operational problems: (a) POS scan failures force manual entry (increasing checkout time by 30–60 seconds per item and cashier error risk), (b) wrong barcodes linked to incorrect SKUs cause pricing errors, inventory discrepancies, and customer complaints, (c) vendor-supplied barcodes that don't meet GS1 print quality standards (bar width reduction, quiet zone violations, low contrast) have high scan failure rates, (d) shelf labels with incorrect prices create DTI (Department of Trade and Industry) compliance risk under the Consumer Act (RA 7394) — displaying a price different from the scanned price is a violation, (e) GS1 Philippines membership and proper barcode assignment are required for all BuildRight private-label items. This workflow ensures barcode accuracy, label quality, and GS1 compliance across the product lifecycle.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Barcode verification for new SKUs**: During new SKU onboarding per W1: (a) Content Specialist receives product sample with vendor-applied barcode from Category Manager, (b) scan barcode using POS scanner — verify: (i) barcode scans successfully on first attempt, (ii) scanned number matches item master GTIN/EAN in ERP, (iii) product description on scan matches physical item, (c) if barcode does not scan: (i) check barcode type (EAN-13, UPC-A, QR code — POS must support all), (ii) check print quality: bar width, contrast, quiet zone (minimum 5mm on each side), (iii) if vendor barcode is defective: request replacement barcode label from vendor or assign BuildRight internal barcode, (d) for BuildRight private-label items: Content Specialist assigns GS1 barcode from BuildRight's GS1 Philippines prefix; register in GS1 Philippines GEPIR (Global Electronic Party Information Register) | Content Specialist | Content Manager | 5 min/SKU |
| 2 | **Shelf label generation & accuracy**: (a) system auto-generates shelf labels from item master per W63 when: new SKU created, price change processed per W14, or planogram reset deployed per W86, (b) Content Manager configures label template: product description, price (current retail), unit of measure, barcode (shelf label barcode = item GTIN for scan-to-verify at POS), BuildRight logo, (c) for promotional labels per W13: include promotional price, original price, promotional period, (d) system validates label content against item master before print: description match, price match, barcode match — any discrepancy blocks label printing | System / Content Manager | Content Manager | Automated + 15 min/template update |

### Background

Product recalls in the hardware/DIY/home improvement sector carry heightened safety risk: defective electrical products can cause fire or electrocution, substandard structural materials (rebar, cement) can cause building collapse, faulty power tools can cause amputation or eye injuries, and lead-based paints pose chronic health risks. The Philippine Consumer Act (RA 7394) and the Bureau of Philippine Standards (BPS, formerly the Bureau of Product Standards) under DTI regulate product safety and have authority to order mandatory recalls. The DTI Consumer Protection Group can impose fines, confiscate inventory, and publicly name non-compliant retailers. BuildRight must respond rapidly to recall events — isolating affected inventory, notifying customers who purchased the product, processing returns/refunds, and reporting to DTI. This workflow covers the end-to-end recall process from trigger through closure, complementing W12 (returns processing) and W140 (safety incident reporting) with a recall-specific protocol.

## W1466. Seasonal Product Content Pre-Staging, Launch Readiness Verification & Cross-Channel Publishing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Seasonal buy plan finalized per W32; promotional calendar event trigger per W13; or Category Manager requests content readiness for upcoming season |
| **Frequency** | 6 major seasonal events per year (per model company seasonal calendar); each event requires ~1,000–2,000 SKUs to be content-ready |
| **Volume** | ~6,000–12,000 seasonal SKU content readiness checks per year across 6 events |
| **Owner** | Content Manager |
| **Participants** | Content Specialist, Category Manager, Ecommerce Manager, Marketing Campaign Manager, Visual Merchandising Coordinator |

### Background

BuildRight Depot's seasonal calendar drives major merchandise shifts: dry season (summer — garden, outdoor, aircon), rainy season (waterproofing, flood control), ber-months (holiday décor, home improvement), Christmas peak (lights, gifts, appliances). Each seasonal transition activates 1,000–2,000 SKUs that may have been dormant, are newly sourced, or require refreshed content (new images, updated descriptions, promotional pricing). If product content is not ready when the season starts, ecommerce pages show incomplete listings, marketing campaigns lack product imagery, and shelf labels are missing — directly impacting seasonal sales which represent 20–30% of annual revenue during peak periods. This workflow ensures that all seasonal SKU content is pre-staged, verified for completeness, and published simultaneously across all channels (POS, ecommerce, marketplace, in-store signage) before the season launch date.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Seasonal content intake**: Category Manager finalizes seasonal buy plan per W32 and provides Content Manager with: (a) seasonal SKU list (new items, returning seasonal items, carry-over items needing content refresh), (b) season launch date (e.g., "Summer 2026 — March 1"), (c) target content-ready date = T-6 weeks before launch; (d) Content Manager creates seasonal content project in project tracker with SKU assignments to Content Specialists | Category Manager / Content Manager | VP Merchandising | 2 hours/season |
| 2 | **Content gap analysis**: Content Manager runs content completeness check against seasonal SKU list: (a) for each SKU, system checks: product description (Filipino and English per W1346), images (minimum 3 per SKU), attribute completeness per category schema, SDS (for chemical items per W1465), barcode/label per W1345, pricing accuracy per W14; (b) system generates gap report: "Content Complete" (no action), "Content Partial" (missing elements listed), "Content Missing" (no content exists — new SKU); (c) Content Manager prioritizes: new SKUs first (full content creation per W50), then partial SKUs (gap filling), then complete SKUs (verification only) | System / Content Manager | Content Manager | 2 hours/season |
| 3 | **Content creation & enrichment**: Content Specialists execute per W50 with seasonal priority: (a) new seasonal SKUs: full content creation (attributes, descriptions bilingual, images, keywords) — 45 min/SKU; (b) returning seasonal SKUs: verify content is current, update if product specifications changed, refresh lifestyle imagery if previous season's imagery is dated — 15 min/SKU; (c) Content Manager monitors daily progress against T-6 week content-ready deadline | Content Specialist | Content Manager | Per W50 estimates |
| 4 | **Launch readiness verification** (T-2 weeks before season): Content Manager conducts formal launch readiness check: (a) 100% of seasonal SKUs have complete content (description, images, attributes, pricing) — system-enforced gate: any SKU with incomplete content is flagged and Content Specialist has 3 days to complete; (b) ecommerce category pages and seasonal landing pages built and reviewed by Ecommerce Manager; (c) marketplace listings (Shopee, Lazada) updated per W1464; (d) promotional pricing loaded in system per W13 and validated; (e) shelf label batch print files generated per W63 for distribution to stores; (f) seasonal signage and planogram updates ready per W86 | Content Manager / Ecommerce Manager / Marketing Campaign Manager | VP Marketing | 1 day/season |
| 5 | **Cross-channel synchronized publishing** (T-1 week): (a) system publishes seasonal content to ecommerce platform; (b) marketplace integration pushes updated listings to Shopee, Lazada per W180; (c) social media content (Facebook, TikTok) scheduled per W300 campaign plan; (d) shelf labels and planogram updates distributed to stores via store operations app; (e) POS product lookup and pricing updated; (f) Content Manager verifies cross-channel consistency: price, description, image, and availability match across all channels | System / Content Manager / Ecommerce Manager | VP Marketing | 4 hours/season |
| 6 | **Post-launch content monitoring**: During first 2 weeks of season: (a) Content Manager monitors ecommerce product page performance: high bounce rate indicates content quality issue; (b) Customer Service reports customer complaints about product information accuracy; (c) system monitors marketplace listing errors or sync failures per W1464; (d) urgent content fixes processed within 24 hours | Content Manager | VP Marketing | 2 hours/week during launch period |

### System Touchpoints
- Content completeness checker — automated validation of all required content fields per SKU (W1466.2)
- PIM project tracker — seasonal content project with SKU assignments and deadline tracking (W1466.1)
- Cross-channel publishing engine — simultaneous content push to ecommerce, marketplace, POS, and store signage (W1466.5)
- Ecommerce analytics — product page bounce rate, conversion rate, search ranking for seasonal items (W1466.6)
- Integration with W50 (PIM), W1345 (labels), W1346 (bilingual content), W32 (seasonal buy plan), W13 (promotions), W86 (planogram), W180/W1464 (marketplace), W300 (campaigns)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Content bottleneck at season boundary** — multiple seasons may overlap (e.g., rainy season wind-down coincides with ber-months preparation); Content Specialists must manage competing deadlines across concurrent seasonal projects
- **Late vendor product data** — seasonal vendor catalogs and product specifications often arrive after the T-6 week content deadline, compressing the enrichment window
- **Marketplace listing lag** — Shopee and Lazada content updates may take 24–48 hours to propagate, creating a gap where BuildRight's own site has updated content but marketplace shows stale information
- **Store compliance** — shelf labels and planogram updates distributed to 200 stores may not be applied uniformly by store staff; seasonal products may be on the sales floor without updated labels during the first week


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-create of "creates seasonal content project in project tracker with SKU assignments to…" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-validate of "check against seasonal SKU list" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-monitor of "monitors daily progress against T-6 week content-ready deadline" (replaces manual Step 3).

- **Content Manager**: ~2 days/season on planning, verification, and monitoring = ~12 days/year; absorbed
- **No incremental headcount**
