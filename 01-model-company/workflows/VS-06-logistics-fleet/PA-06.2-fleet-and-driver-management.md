## W1348. Fleet Vehicle Preventive Maintenance Scheduling, Work Order & Parts Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vehicle odometer or engine-hours threshold reached; or calendar-based service interval due |
| **Frequency** | Per-vehicle: every 10,000 km or 6 months (whichever comes first); ~20% owned fleet (40 trucks) + 80% third-party (160 trucks, third-party maintenance outside scope) |
| **Volume** | ~40 owned trucks × 2–3 services/year = ~80–120 preventive maintenance (PM) events/year; ~7–10 PM events/month |
| **Owner** | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor, Mechanic, Parts Coordinator, Logistics Planner, Finance (capex approval for major services) |

### Background

BuildRight's owned fleet (~40 trucks, a mix of 10-wheeler wing vans and 6-wheeler trucks) transports merchandise from 4 DCs to 200 stores. Vehicle breakdowns during delivery routes cause missed replenishment windows, spoiled perishable/seasonal shipments, and emergency 3PL spot-hire costs at premium rates. Preventive maintenance scheduling must balance vehicle availability against service needs, especially during peak seasons when all trucks are fully deployed.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System tracks vehicle odometer and engine-hours via telematics (W199); auto-generates PM alert when threshold is within 500 km or 2 weeks of due date | System | — | Automated |
| 2 | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor reviews upcoming PM schedule against delivery dispatch plan; selects service window that minimizes fleet impact (typically between dispatch waves or on low-demand days) | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | Logistics Manager | 30 min/week |
| 3 | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor creates digital PM work order: standard service checklist (oil change, brake inspection, tire rotation, fluid top-up, belt/hose inspection, electrical system check) | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | — | 15 min/work order |
| 4 | Parts Coordinator checks parts availability for scheduled PM items; orders parts if not in stock from approved automotive suppliers (lead time: 2–5 days for common parts) | Parts Coordinator | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | 30 min/work order |
| 5 | Mechanic performs PM per work order checklist; records findings, parts used, and labor hours in system; flags any issues requiring follow-up repair | Mechanic | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | 4–8 hours/truck |
| 6 | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor reviews completed work order; approves or schedules follow-up repair for flagged issues; updates vehicle maintenance history | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | — | 15 min/work order |
| 7 | Finance receives completed work order with parts and labor costs; processes vendor payment for externally sourced parts; records maintenance cost against vehicle asset | Finance (AP) | Controller | Per W7 |
| 8 | System updates vehicle availability status back to "active" in dispatch planning module | System | — | Automated |

### System Touchpoints
- Telematics integration (W199) for odometer/engine-hours tracking
- Automated PM alert generation with threshold configuration
- Digital work order creation, assignment, and completion tracking in **Oracle eAM** (odometer/engine-hours meters on the FA-linked vehicle asset drive the PM alerts above; fit-gap F5, fourth-pass true)
- Parts inventory management in the **eAM** maintenance subinventory for commonly stocked maintenance items (filters, belts, brake pads, fluids)
- Vehicle maintenance history log with cost accumulation per vehicle (**eAM** asset maintenance history, work-order costing to GL/AP)
- Fleet availability status feed to dispatch planning (W196)
- Finance integration for maintenance cost recording against vehicle fixed asset

### Pain Points / Risks
- PM scheduling conflicts with peak delivery periods: during Q4 ber-months and bi-monthly sale events, taking a truck offline for PM reduces delivery capacity when demand is highest; Supervisors may defer PM, increasing breakdown risk
- Parts availability for older trucks: BuildRight's fleet includes trucks of varying age; parts for older models may have longer lead times or be discontinued, forcing emergency sourcing at premium prices
- Inconsistent mechanic findings documentation: if mechanics do not thoroughly document wear patterns during PM inspections, emerging issues (e.g., brake lining approaching minimum thickness) are missed, leading to on-road failures


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "reviews upcoming PM schedule against delivery dispatch plan" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "creates digital PM work order" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-257 (ensure controlled execution — Fleet & Driver Management (PA-06.2).)
- operational: Fleet Maintenance Supervisor review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
- PM scheduling: 30 min/week for Fleet Maintenance Supervisor
- Work order creation: 15 min × 7–10 PM events/month = ~2 hours/month
- Parts coordination: 30 min × 7–10 PM events/month = ~4 hours/month
- Mechanic labor: 4–8 hours × 7–10 PM events/month = ~28–80 hours/month (≈0.2–0.5 FTE of mechanic capacity; the balance on ad-hoc repairs)
- Work order review: 15 min × 7–10 events/month = ~2 hours/month
- Total: ~35–90 hours/month depending on service complexity

### Staffing Implication
- 1 Fleet Maintenance Supervisor (dedicated role) manages PM scheduling, work orders, and parts coordination across all 4 DC locations; ~6–8 hours/week on PM activities
- 2 Mechanics (full-time, split across DC3 Laguna and DC1 Davao, the two largest operations) handle 7–10 PM events/month plus ad-hoc repairs; each mechanic can handle ~3–4 PM events/week
- 1 Parts Coordinator (shared with general DC supplies procurement) dedicates ~4 hours/month to PM parts management

## W1349. Fleet Tire Lifecycle Management, Tread Monitoring & Replacement Scheduling

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Tire tread depth reaches minimum threshold (3 mm for steer axles, 2 mm for drive/trailer axles per Philippine LTO regulations); or tire age exceeds 5 years; or tire damage detected during pre-trip inspection |
| **Frequency** | ~40 owned trucks × 10–18 tires per truck = ~400–720 tires under management; average replacement every 60,000–80,000 km or 2–3 years per tire; ~150–200 tire replacements/year |
| **Volume** | ~12–17 tire replacements/month across owned fleet |
| **Owner** | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Mechanic, Driver (pre-trip inspection), Parts Coordinator, Tire Supplier, Finance (AP) |

### Background

Tires represent a significant operating cost for BuildRight's owned fleet of 40 trucks navigating Philippine provincial roads, which include rough concrete, unpaved construction site access roads, and occasional flood-damaged surfaces. Tire failures during delivery cause route delays, potential cargo damage, and safety hazards. Systematic tread monitoring and proactive replacement scheduling prevents roadside failures and optimizes tire spend by replacing tires at the optimal point in their wear lifecycle — not too early (wasting remaining tread life) and not too late (risking blowout).

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Driver performs pre-trip inspection: visually checks tire condition, notes any visible damage (cuts, bulges, uneven wear); reports concerns via driver mobile app | Driver | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | 5 min/pre-trip |
| 2 | During scheduled PM (W1348), Mechanic measures tread depth on all tires using calibrated depth gauge; records readings per tire position (steer left, steer right, drive inner, drive outer, trailer positions) | Mechanic | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | 15 min/truck |
| 3 | System logs tread depth readings by tire ID; calculates remaining useful life based on wear rate trends; auto-generates replacement alert when tread approaches minimum threshold | System | — | Automated |
| 4 | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor reviews tire replacement forecast monthly: groups replacement orders by DC to consolidate supplier deliveries; considers retreading for drive/trailer positions where casing integrity is good | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | Logistics Manager | 2 hours/month |
| 5 | Parts Coordinator places tire order with approved tire supplier (Philippine distributors: Yokohama, Goodyear, Continental); arranges delivery to respective DC maintenance bay | Parts Coordinator | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | 30 min/order |
| 6 | Mechanic performs tire replacement or retreading; records old tire condition (tread depth, damage type, reason for removal) and new tire serial number/position | Mechanic | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | 1–2 hours/truck (2–4 tires) |
| 7 | Old tires evaluated for retreading potential, recycling, or disposal; serviceable casings sent to retreading partner; unserviceable tires disposed per DENR waste tire guidelines | Mechanic / Parts Coordinator | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | 15 min/set |
| 8 | Finance records tire replacement cost; capitalizes tires above PHP 5,000 per unit or expenses as maintenance consumable below threshold | Finance (AP) | Controller | Per W7 |

### System Touchpoints
- Tire register as serial-tracked **eAM** maintainable items with position assignment, purchase date, and mileage accumulation (fit-gap F5, fourth-pass true)
- Tread depth logging per tire ID with trend analysis and remaining-life calculation
- Automated replacement alert generation based on tread depth, age, or mileage thresholds
- Integration with the **eAM** PM work order system (W1348) for scheduled tread measurement
- Supplier purchase order integration for tire procurement
- Tire cost tracking per vehicle for total cost of ownership analysis

### Pain Points / Risks
- Premature tire wear from provincial road conditions: roads to construction sites and provincial stores accelerate tire degradation beyond standard wear models, making remaining-life predictions inaccurate
- Retreading quality varies among Philippine retreading shops; a poorly retreaded tire may fail within weeks, negating the ~40–50% cost savings vs. new tire and creating safety liability
- Tire theft or substitution: with ~400–720 tires in circulation across 4 DCs and multiple maintenance bays, tracking individual tire assets requires disciplined serial number management; discrepancies between system records and physical inventory indicate potential theft or unrecorded replacements


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "checks tire condition, notes any visible damage" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-record of "records readings per tire position" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "logs tread depth readings by tire ID" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-257 (ensure controlled execution — Fleet & Driver Management (PA-06.2).)
- operational: records and physical inventory indicate potential theft or unrecorded replacements

### Time Estimate
- Pre-trip tire inspection by driver: 5 min/truck/trip (absorbed in existing pre-trip routine)
- PM tread measurement: 15 min/truck × 40 trucks × 2–3 times/year = ~20–30 hours/year of mechanic time
- Monthly replacement forecast review: 2 hours/month × Fleet Maintenance Supervisor
- Tire replacement labor: 1–2 hours/truck × 12–17 replacements/month = ~12–34 hours/month
- Old tire disposition: 15 min/set × 12–17 sets/month = ~3–4 hours/month
- Total incremental: ~40–60 hours/month across all roles

### Staffing Implication
- Fleet Maintenance Supervisor: ~2 hours/month on tire forecast review; absorbed within existing fleet management duties
- Mechanics: tire replacement and measurement absorbed within ~28–80 hours/month PM capacity
- Parts Coordinator: ~30 min/month on tire procurement; minimal incremental effort

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |

| 7 | Fleet Safety Officer conducts investigation: reviews telematics data (speed, braking, location), driver history, route conditions; determines root cause (driver error, vehicle failure, third-party fault, road hazard); classifies preventability | Fleet Safety Officer | Fleet Manager | 3 days |
| 8 | For preventable accidents: Fleet Manager applies progressive discipline per driver safety program (W197); assigns remedial training; adjusts driver safety score | Fleet Manager | VP Supply Chain | Per W197 |
| 9 | Monthly: Fleet Safety Officer produces incident analytics: incident rate per 100,000 km, preventability rate, cost per incident, trend by driver/route/vehicle/time; feeds driver performance dashboard (W197) | Fleet Safety Officer | Fleet Manager | 2 hours/month |

- Fleet telematics (W199), incident reporting mobile app, insurance claims module (W610), driver management (W197), **eAM** repair work orders (W1348's system)

- System workflow notification of "contact, vehicle details, insurance" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-file of "files police report at nearest PNP station" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-257 (ensure controlled execution — Fleet & Driver Management (PA-06.2).)

## W799. Vehicle Acquisition, Registration, Insurance & Disposal Lifecycle Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Fleet expansion need (new store openings); vehicle end-of-life (mileage/age threshold); insurance renewal; annual registration |
| **Frequency** | Vehicle acquisition: 5-10 per year; registration: annual per vehicle; insurance: annual renewal; disposal: 3-5 per year |
| **Volume** | ~40 owned vehicles (20% of fleet); mixed fleet of owned and 3PL per model-company profile |
| **Owner** | Fleet Manager |
| **Participants** | VP Supply Chain, Finance, Procurement, Insurance Provider, LTO, External Dealer |

### Background

BuildRight operates approximately 40 owned trucks (20% of total fleet, per model-company profile) for DC-to-store delivery and inter-DC transfers. The remaining 80% of transport is handled by 3PL partners per W242. Each owned vehicle has a lifecycle from acquisition through registration, insurance, maintenance, and eventual disposal. Philippine vehicles require annual registration with the Land Transportation Office (LTO), emission testing per Clean Air Act, and comprehensive insurance including third-party liability. This workflow governs the full vehicle asset lifecycle.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## W1256. Returnable Transport Packaging (RTP) Pool Management, Tracking & Reconciliation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — weekly RTP inventory reconciliation; monthly pool health review; ad-hoc triggered by RTP shortage at DC or store accumulation alert |
| **Frequency** | Weekly reconciliation; monthly pool review; annual procurement cycle for RTP replenishment |
| **Volume** | ~2,000 RTP units (pallets, plastic totes, wire baskets) in circulation across 4 DCs and 200 stores; ~5,000 DC-to-store shipments per month using RTP |
| **Owner** | Logistics Planner / DC Operations Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Receiving Clerk, 3PL Transport Coordinator, Procurement Specialist, Finance Controller |

### Background

BuildRight Depot's DC-to-store distribution network moves ~5,000 replenishment shipments per month across 200 stores. Each shipment uses returnable transport packaging (RTP) — standardized plastic pallets, stackable totes for hardware/fasteners, wire baskets for lumber, and insulated containers for temperature-sensitive items (paint, chemicals). RTP represents a significant capital investment (~PHP 2,000–5,000 per unit) and is designed to be returned empty to the DC after each delivery cycle, reducing single-use cardboard and shrink-wrap costs by an estimated PHP 15–20M per year. However, RTP pool management is a persistent challenge in Philippine retail: (a) stores accumulate RTP and fail to return it promptly, creating DC shortages; (b) RTP is repurposed by store staff for in-store display or storage rather than returned; (c) 3PL transport partners may not load empty RTP on return trips, prioritizing loaded deliveries; (d) RTP damage rate is estimated at 5–8% per year due to rough handling, forklift damage, and UV degradation in outdoor lumber yards. This workflow manages the RTP pool lifecycle — tracking, reconciliation, return logistics, and replenishment procurement — to maintain adequate pool levels and minimize loss.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Weekly RTP inventory reconciliation**: DC Operations team reconciles RTP pool inventory: (a) count RTP units at each DC (by type: pallets, totes, wire baskets); (b) query store-level RTP on-hand from system (stores scan RTP on receiving and log returns); (c) count RTP on 3PL trucks (in-transit); (d) calculate pool variance: total acquired – (DC on-hand + store on-hand + in-transit) = unaccounted loss; (e) investigate variances > 2% of total pool | DC Shift Supervisor | DC Operations Manager | 2 hours/week |
| 2 | **Store RTP return enforcement**: When DC-to-store delivery is made, driver collects empty RTP from previous delivery: (a) driver scans returning RTP units with handheld — system matches to store's outstanding RTP balance; (b) store Receiving Clerk confirms RTP return count and condition; (c) system updates store RTP on-hand and DC RTP on-hand; (d) for stores with > 20 outstanding RTP units (accumulation threshold): system alerts Store Manager for mandatory return on next delivery; (e) stores with > 50 outstanding units for > 30 days: flagged to Regional Manager for action | Receiving Clerk / Driver | Store Manager | 10 min per delivery |
| 3 | **Damaged RTP disposition**: Stores and DCs inspect RTP condition at each return: (a) **Grade A** (serviceable): clean, no cracks, all features functional — return to pool; (b) **Grade B** (repairable): cracked but structurally sound — send to DC for repair (plastic welding, wire straightening); (c) **Grade C** (unserviceable): broken, warped, contaminated — disposition as scrap per W443; (d) system tracks damage rate by store, DC, and 3PL partner; (e) stores with > 10% damage rate receive handling retraining | Receiving Clerk / DC Quality Inspector | DC Operations Manager | 5 min per RTP return |
| 4 | **Monthly pool health review**: Logistics Planner reviews RTP pool health: (a) total pool size vs. target (minimum 2,500 units for current volume); (b) utilization rate — % of pool in active circulation vs. idle at stores; (c) loss rate trend — unaccounted losses as % of total pool per month; (d) damage rate trend; (e) average RTP turnaround time (DC → store → DC return); (f) calculate replacement procurement need: estimated losses + estimated damage = new RTP procurement quantity | Logistics Planner | DC Operations Manager | 3 hours/month |
| 5 | **Annual RTP procurement**: Procurement Specialist executes annual RTP replenishment purchase: (a) determine procurement quantity based on pool health review; (b) obtain competitive quotes from RTP suppliers (minimum 3 quotes for > PHP 500,000 purchase); (c) submit capex/expense request per approval matrix (W21); (d) manage delivery of new RTP units to DCs; (e) update pool master with new unit count and supplier warranty terms | Procurement Specialist | Finance Controller | 8 hours/year |

### Background

Under Republic Act 4136, all motor vehicles in the Philippines must be registered annually with the LTO. ## W1515. Delivery Vehicle Fuel Card Management, Consumption Tracking & Cost Control

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Ongoing fleet operations; monthly fuel cost review cycle; fuel price increase events |
| **Frequency** | Daily fuel card transactions (~80–120 fuelings/day across fleet); monthly reporting and reconciliation |
| **Volume** | ~40 owned + ~160 contracted 3PL delivery vehicles; ~PHP 3–5M/month fuel spend |
| **Owner** | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor, Drivers, Logistics Manager, Finance Controller, Fuel Card Provider |

### Background

Fuel is typically the second-largest variable cost in BuildRight's distribution operations after driver wages. With ~200 delivery vehicles (40 company-owned + 160 3PL-contracted) making daily deliveries across 4 DC territories, monthly fuel consumption reaches ~120,000–180,000 liters of diesel. Philippine diesel prices fluctuate PHP 5–15/liter within a quarter due to VAT adjustments, global crude movements, and DOE-mandated Biofuel blend changes. Without centralized fuel card management, drivers purchase fuel with cash/petty cash at random stations, leading to: no consumption tracking, fuel theft ("short-filling" where driver buys PHP 2,000 fuel but logs PHP 3,000), inability to negotiate volume discounts with fuel companies, and unreliable cost-per-kilometer data for route optimization.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Fuel card issuance and setup**: (a) Fleet Maintenance Supervisor assigns one fuel card per vehicle (owned fleet) or per driver (3PL fleet); (b) card configured with: (i) fuel type restriction (diesel only), (ii) daily transaction limit (PHP 3,000 for 6-wheeler, PHP 5,000 for 10-wheeler), (iii) allowed fuel station network (Petron, Shell, Caltex — negotiated discount agreement), (iv) odometer reading required at each transaction; (c) driver signs fuel card custody agreement acknowledging replacement fee for lost cards | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | Logistics Manager | 20 min/vehicle (initial setup) |
| 2 | **Daily fueling**: (a) driver fuels at approved station within network; (b) taps fuel card at pump; (c) enters current odometer reading at POS; (d) fuel card system validates: vehicle/driver match, daily limit not exceeded, station is in approved network; (e) transaction approved: fuel amount, liters, station ID, odometer, timestamp recorded | Driver | — | 10–15 min/fueling |
| 3 | **Exception monitoring** (daily): (a) system flags anomalies: (i) fueling amount exceeds expected consumption for distance driven (liters/km > vehicle model average + 15%), (ii) fueling at non-network station, (iii) multiple fuelings in one day, (iv) odometer reading decreased (tampering), (v) fueling outside DC delivery zone; (b) Fleet Maintenance Supervisor reviews daily exception report and investigates: contacts driver, reviews route manifest, checks GPS track vs. fueling location | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | Logistics Manager | 30–60 min/day |
| 4 | **Monthly reconciliation**: (a) system calculates per-vehicle: total liters, total cost, average km/L, cost/km, total distance (from odometer delta); (b) Fleet Maintenance Supervisor compares actual km/L vs. vehicle model benchmark (e.g., 10-wheeler: 3.5–4.5 km/L diesel; 6-wheeler: 5–6 km/L); (c) flags vehicles with > 20% deviation from benchmark for maintenance check (fuel system issue) or driver counseling (driving behavior) | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | Logistics Manager | 2–3 hours/month |
| 5 | **Monthly fuel cost reporting**: (a) Finance Controller receives consolidated fuel card statement from provider; (b) reconciles against GL fuel expense postings; (c) calculates: (i) total fleet fuel cost, (ii) cost per DC territory, (iii) cost per delivery (fuel portion), (iv) volume discount earned; (d) Finance Controller and Logistics Manager review monthly fuel cost trends and budget variance | Finance Controller / Logistics Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2–3 hours/month |
| 6 | **Quarterly fuel contract negotiation**: (a) Fleet Maintenance Supervisor compiles consumption data by station chain; (b) Logistics Manager negotiates volume discount agreement with top 2 fuel companies: (i) target 3–5% discount vs. pump price, (ii) priority lane access at high-traffic stations near DCs, (iii) quarterly rebate based on volume tiers | Logistics Manager | VP Supply Chain | 4–6 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Fuel card management module: card issuance, limits, restrictions, driver-vehicle assignment (W1515.1)
- Transaction processing: real-time validation, odometer capture, anomaly detection (W1515.2)
- Exception monitoring dashboard: anomaly rules engine, daily exception report, GPS-fueling location overlay (W1515.3)
- Fleet analytics: per-vehicle km/L tracking, cost/km, benchmark comparison (W1515.4)
- GL integration: consolidated statement import, fuel expense reconciliation (W1515.5)
- Integration with W196 (Route Planning) for distance validation, W1315 (Vehicle Loading) for weight-adjusted consumption benchmarks

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Fuel siphoning**: despite fuel card controls, drivers may siphon diesel from vehicle tanks after fueling; mitigated by daily km/L monitoring but hard to detect in real-time; GPS track + fueling location correlation reduces opportunity
- **3PL fleet compliance**: 3PL-contracted drivers may resist fuel card mandates (prefer cash purchases at smaller stations); contractual requirement for fuel card participation must be enforced during 3PL contract renewal
- **Rural station coverage**: approved fuel network (Petron, Shell, Caltex) has gaps in remote provincial areas where BuildRight stores operate; drivers may legitimately need to fuel at non-network stations, creating false exceptions
- **Price volatility**: PHP 5–15/liter quarterly swings make budget forecasting unreliable; a PHP 10/liter increase on 150,000 liters/month = PHP 1.5M/month unbudgeted cost


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "Fuel card issuance and setup" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-capture of "enters current odometer reading at POS" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews daily exception report and investigates" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-257 (ensure controlled execution — Fleet & Driver Management (PA-06.2).)
- operational: daily km/L monitoring with GPS-track and fueling-location correlation

### Time Estimate
- Initial setup: 20 min/vehicle × 200 vehicles = ~67 hours (one-time)
- Daily exception monitoring: 30–60 min/day = ~150–300 hours/year
- Monthly reconciliation: 2–3 hours/month = ~24–36 hours/year
- Monthly cost reporting: 2–3 hours/month = ~24–36 hours/year
- Quarterly negotiation: 4–6 hours/quarter = ~16–24 hours/year
- **Annual ongoing: ~215–400 hours (Fleet Maintenance Supervisor + Finance Controller)**

### Staffing Implication
- Fleet Maintenance Supervisor: ~30–60 min/day on exception monitoring + 2–3 hours/month reconciliation = ~200–350 hours/year; absorbed within existing role (currently monitoring fuel informally)
- Finance Controller: 2–3 hours/month on fuel cost reporting; absorbed
- Logistics Manager: 4–6 hours/quarter on contract negotiation; absorbed
- No incremental headcount

## W1536. Fleet Vehicle Registration Renewal, LTO Compliance & LTFRB Cargo Freight License Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vehicle registration renewal due (annual); or LTFRB freight license renewal (every 3–5 years); or new vehicle acquisition |
| **Frequency** | ~40–60 vehicle registrations/year (owned fleet of ~20% of total truck requirement); ~5–10 LTFRB renewals/year |
| **Volume** | Critical for BuildRight Logistics, Inc. owned fleet operations |
| **Owner** | Fleet Manager |
| **Participants** | Fleet Manager, Fleet Assistant, Logistics Planner, LTO/LTFRB liaison |

### Background

BuildRight Logistics, Inc. operates a fleet of owned vehicles (10-wheeler wing vans and 6-wheeler trucks) supplementing the 80% third-party truck fleet. Philippine Land Transportation Office (LTO) vehicle registration is mandatory and must be renewed annually based on the vehicle's plate number ending digit schedule. Failure to maintain current registration results in vehicle impoundment, fines, and supply chain disruption. For cargo freight operations, LTFRB (Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board) requires a Certificate of Public Convenience (CPC) or franchise for hire-and-reward operations.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Renewal calendar management**: System maintains vehicle registration master with plate number, renewal month (based on LTO plate ending schedule), LTFRB franchise expiry, and insurance expiry; generates automated alerts at 60 days and 30 days before each renewal due date | System / Fleet Assistant | Fleet Manager | 2 hours/month |
| 2 | **Pre-inspection preparation**: Fleet Assistant schedules vehicle for mechanical inspection — brakes, tires, lights, body condition, emission testing (required by LTO); coordinates with maintenance workshop (W188) for any needed repairs before LTO inspection | Fleet Assistant | Fleet Manager | 1–2 days/vehicle |
| 3 | **LTO transaction processing**: Fleet Assistant (or authorized liaison) processes registration renewal at LTO — submits required documents (OR/CR, insurance policy, emission test result, stencil of engine/chassis number), pays fees; for vehicles requiring physical inspection, brings vehicle to LTO inspection center | Fleet Assistant | — | 4–8 hours/vehicle |
| 4 | **LTFRB compliance**: For vehicles requiring LTFRB franchise renewal, Fleet Manager prepares application — proof of financial capacity, vehicle maintenance records, driver qualifications, insurance coverage; attends LTFRB hearing if required; CPC renewal typically takes 30–60 days | Fleet Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2–3 days/application |
| 5 | **System update & documentation**: Upon successful renewal, Fleet Assistant updates fleet master (W401) with new registration expiry, new OR/CR details, updated insurance policy; uploads scanned copies of all renewed documents to document management system (W255) | Fleet Assistant | Fleet Manager | 15 min/vehicle |
| 6 | **Compliance monitoring**: Fleet Manager runs monthly fleet compliance dashboard — vehicles with current vs. expired registration, upcoming renewals, vehicles out of service for non-compliance; any expired vehicle is blocked from dispatch assignment in routing system (W196) | Fleet Manager | VP Supply Chain | 1 hour/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Fleet master with registration, franchise, and insurance expiry tracking (W1536.1)
- Automated renewal alert calendar integrated with dispatch system (W1536.1)
- Vehicle maintenance integration with workshop management (W188) for pre-inspection repairs (W1536.2)
- Dispatch blocking for non-compliant vehicles (W1536.6)
- Document management integration for registration document storage (W1536.5)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **LTO processing delays**: Philippine LTO offices frequently experience system downtime and processing backlogs; renewal that should take 1 day can take 2–3 visits
- **Plate number ending schedule**: LTO assigns renewal months based on plate ending digit (1=January, 2=February, etc.); this creates predictable seasonal peaks in the renewal workload
- **Emission test failures**: Older vehicles may fail emission testing, requiring engine work before registration can proceed; unplanned maintenance delays renewal


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-generate of "generates automated alerts at 60 days and 30 days before each renewal due date" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-inspect of "inspection preparation" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-capture of "LTO transaction processing" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-257 (ensure controlled execution — Fleet & Driver Management (PA-06.2).)
- operational: downtime and processing backlogs

### Time Estimate
- Calendar management: 2 hours/month
- Per-vehicle renewal: 4–8 hours × 50 vehicles/year = 200–400 hours/year
- LTFRB compliance: 2–3 days × 8 applications/year = 16–24 days/year
- Compliance monitoring: 1 hour/month = 12 hours/year
- **Total: ~340–600 hours/year** (renewals 200–400 + LTFRB 128–192 + monitoring 12)

### Staffing Implication
- Fleet Assistant: handles bulk of LTO processing; ~250 hours/year on registration management (30% of role); dedicated position
- Fleet Manager: oversees LTFRB compliance and monthly monitoring; absorbed
- No incremental headcount

## W5554. In-Transit Cargo Hijacking, Armed Truck Robbery & Driver-Safety First Response Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Armed hijacking or robbery of a loaded BuildRight delivery truck in transit — own fleet or 3PL carrier under W1207 — where the driver or crew is held up and the tractor, trailer, or cargo is forcibly taken |
| **Frequency** | Rare criminal event: expected in the low single digits or fewer per year across the mixed ~190-truck operation (20% owned / 80% third-party), with readiness exercised through the W197/W653 driver-safety drill rotation rather than live events |
| **Volume** | 1 event × the load at risk (a full multi-stop trailer of power tools, appliances, or copper cable carries a PHP 1.5M–6M wholesale value) × the crew exposed (1 driver + 1 helper on own-fleet routes) × the recovery surface (PNP hot-pursuit window, cargo insurance claim, ORC intelligence feed) |
| **Owner** | Fleet Safety Officer |
| **Participants** | Driver & helper (victims first, per the W197 duty of care), Fleet Manager, Logistics Manager (re-dispatch of stranded store orders), National LP Director (W1337 law-enforcement coordination), Corporate Security / GSOC (VS-159), HR Business Partner and EAP Counselor (W2867 critical-incident support), Insurance Coordinator (W799 fleet policies; VS-26 claims), VP Supply Chain, PNP units with jurisdiction over the route |

### Background

Cargo theft is named inside PA-23.3 as an organized-retail-crime class — "cargo theft targeting BuildRight deliveries in transit or at DC loading docks" appears only in W1337's intelligence-gathering background — and police/military escorts are mentioned only for post-disaster relief loads in VS-180. 'Hijack'/'holdup'/'cargo robbery' appear in zero dedicated workflow headers: W653 owns the accident/cargo-damage event, W2814 owns the CIT-vendor robbery, E-21 owns armed violence on premises — nobody owns the roadside armed robbery of a loaded truck, where the first rule is the opposite of loss prevention (comply, surrender the load, protect the crew), the PNP hot-pursuit clock starts at the first alert, and the GPS/seal evidence chain decides both the police case and the cargo claim. This workflow is that single owner; the driver-violence dimension rides W717 and the trauma chain rides W2867, while 3PL-carrier events additionally route through the W1207 carrier-liability machinery.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Comply-first discipline at the event**: driver and helper follow the no-resistance, no-pursuit rule — hand over keys/load on demand; trigger the telematics panic alert (W199) at the first safe moment; move the vehicle only if ordered to, and drive away only when safe | Driver / Helper | Fleet Safety Officer | Immediate |
| 2 | **Immediate notifications & hot-pursuit window**: GSOC/Fleet Duty phone escalates to PNP with plate, tractor/trailer numbers, seal numbers, load description, and last GPS position from W199 within the first hour — the recovery probability collapses after the first hours | GSOC / Fleet Duty | Fleet Manager | 15–60 min |
| 3 | **Crew welfare & stand-down**: medical check per the W501-class first-aid protocol; crew stood down for the shift with same-day family notification; critical-incident stress support initiated per W2867; no same-day formal statement (memory-contamination rule) | HR Business Partner / EAP Counselor | Fleet Manager | Same day |
| 4 | **Evidence preservation**: GPS breadcrumb track exported and locked from the W199 platform before log rotation; last-known-position CCTV canvass list prepared for PNP; seal numbers, dispatch manifest (W196), and driver app timeline preserved as the claim/police pack | Fleet Safety Officer / LP Investigator | National LP Director | 1–2 days |
| 5 | **Load & order recovery**: affected shipments flagged; critical store replenishment re-dispatched per W196 routing; recovery watch on fencing channels for the high-resale SKU mix (power tools, cable) through the W1337 ORC network | Logistics Manager / LP Investigator | VP Supply Chain | 1–2 weeks |
| 6 | **Insurance & carrier liability**: own-fleet events file the motor-cargo claim per W799/W59-class machinery (police report is a claim prerequisite); 3PL-carrier events pursue carrier liability per W1207 and freight recovery per W90/W191 — never double-claim the same loss | Insurance Coordinator | CFO (claim sign-off) | 2–8 weeks |
| 7 | **Prevention hardening**: post-incident route/lane review (W196), high-value load splitting or scheduling rules, escort decisions for flagged lanes, driver debrief folded into the W197/W654 safety curriculum | Fleet Safety Officer / Fleet Manager | VP Supply Chain | 1–2 weeks |
| 8 | **Chain alert, ORC feed & review board**: modus advisory to fleet, DCs, and stores; full case file to the W1337 ORC intelligence network; post-incident review with LP and Corporate Security (VS-159) closing with documented lessons | National LP Director / Fleet Safety Officer | VP Supply Chain | 2–4 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- Telematics panic-alert event stream and GPS breadcrumb export with log-preservation hold (links to W199)
- Dispatch/load-manifest objects with seal numbers and driver-app timeline (links to W196)
- Incident case management with evidence attachments and restricted visibility (the W471-class pattern)
- ORC case file and law-enforcement coordination records (links to W1337)
- Motor-cargo insurance and freight-claim records (links to W799/W90)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Resistance-injury risk**: a driver fighting for an insured trailer converts a property crime into a fatality; mitigated by the comply-first/no-pursuit rule briefed at W654 onboarding and re-briefed quarterly, with a written no-discipline guarantee for loads surrendered under duress
- **Delayed-alert risk**: hours-old notification destroys the PNP hot-pursuit window and the freshest GPS evidence; mitigated by Step 2's first-hour escalation ladder with GSOC accountability
- **Evidence-loss risk**: the telematics track overwriting before export or the manifest being amended for re-dispatch destroys both the police case and the cargo claim; mitigated by Step 4's preservation-first holds
- **Recovery-blindness risk**: hijacked high-resale SKUs funnel into fencing channels nobody watches; mitigated by the Step 5/8 ORC-network recovery watch
- **Re-victimization risk**: the same lane, schedule, and load profile repeated the following week; mitigated by Step 7's mandatory route/load-profile review before the lane re-opens to high-value loads

### Automation Opportunity
- Telematics route-deviation and unscheduled-stop geofence alerting with automatic GSOC page and pre-assembled police pack (plate, seals, manifest, last position) — replaces the manual Step 2 notification scramble
- One-click hijack flag that freezes shipment documents, locks GPS history, and generates the insurer claim pack (replaces manual Steps 4/6 assembly)

### Controls
- CTL-257 (ensure controlled execution — Fleet & Driver Management (PA-06.2).)
- operational: comply-first rule evidenced in W654 training records; GPS and manifest evidence preserved before the vehicle or documents re-enter routine service; no driver disciplinary action for property surrendered under armed duress

### Time Estimate
First 24 hours: ≈ 16–24 staff-hours (GSOC, fleet, LP, HR). Full case life including claim and hardening: ≈ 20–40 additional staff-hours spread over 4–8 weeks. Readiness rides the existing W197/W653 drill cadence — no incremental headcount.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed by the Fleet Safety Officer, the GSOC watch, and the LP investigation bench; insurance handling rides the existing Insurance Coordinator remit. No incremental headcount.
