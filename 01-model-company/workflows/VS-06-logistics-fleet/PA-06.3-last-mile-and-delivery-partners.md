## W1163. 3PL & Delivery Partner Daily Operations Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Daily operations cycle; partner performance threshold breach |
| **Frequency** | Daily monitoring; monthly formal review |
| **Volume** | ~80% of outbound deliveries handled by 3PL partners (8–10 strategic partners; ~30–40 contracted carriers/forwarders in total per W1371) |
| **Owner** | Logistics Manager |
| **Participants** | Logistics Manager, 3PL Dispatch Coordinator, DC Dispatch, AP Clerk |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Daily Partner Availability Check**: Logistics Manager confirms 3PL partner vehicle availability for the day via partner portal or messaging; updates dispatch planning with confirmed truck count | Logistics Manager | VP Supply Chain | 30 min |
| 2 | **Dispatch Coordination**: DC Dispatch assigns deliveries to 3PL partners alongside owned fleet per W196; system generates partner-specific trip manifests; partner driver receives manifest via partner mobile app | DC Dispatch | Logistics Manager | 30 min |
| 3 | **Real-Time Tracking**: System ingests 3PL partner GPS/telematics data via API; DC Dispatch monitors partner vehicle locations alongside owned fleet on unified dashboard; delays flagged automatically | System / DC Dispatch | Logistics Manager | Continuous |
| 4 | **Delivery Confirmation**: Partner driver captures POD (signature + photo) via partner app; system updates order status in ERP; customer notification triggered per W708 | Partner Driver | DC Dispatch | 5 min/delivery |
| 5 | **Exception Handling**: For failed deliveries (customer unavailable, access denied, vehicle breakdown): DC Dispatch coordinates with partner for same-day reattempt or next-day reschedule; customer notified per W708 | DC Dispatch | Logistics Manager | 15 min/exception |
| 6 | **Daily Settlement Reconciliation**: System matches partner delivery records against ERP dispatch manifest; flags unmatched deliveries for investigation; feeds weekly partner scorecard per W242 | System / AP Clerk | Logistics Manager | 30 min |

### System Touchpoints
- 3PL partner API integration (vehicle availability, GPS tracking, POD capture)
- Unified dispatch dashboard (owned + 3PL fleet)
- ERP order management (delivery status, customer notification per W708)
- AP settlement (partner invoice reconciliation per W7)
- Partner scorecard module (W242, SCP-020)

### Pain Points / Risks
- Partner vehicle no-shows during peak season (Ber months) — must activate backup partners or own fleet overtime
- API integration reliability varies by partner technology maturity — some partners require manual manifest communication
- Partner driver quality inconsistency — POD photo quality, customer interaction, cargo handling standards
- Philippine traffic unpredictability makes SLA commitments difficult for partners in Metro Manila


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "Logistics Manager confirms 3PL partner vehicle availability for the day via…" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-generate of "generates partner-specific trip manifests" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-dispatch of "Dispatch monitors partner vehicle locations alongside owned fleet on unified…" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-258 (ensure controlled execution — Last-Mile & Delivery Partners (PA-06.3).)
- operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Staffing Implication
- **Logistics Manager**: ~1 hour/day on partner coordination; absorbed by existing role
- **3PL Dispatch Coordinator**: ~4 hours/day on manifest distribution and exception handling; 1 FTE per DC region

### Time Estimate
~2 hours/day for daily operations; monthly formal review ~4 hours.

## W1164. Inter-Island Logistics Coordination & Shipping Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | DC replenishment order requiring inter-island transfer; or vendor DSD to island store bypassing nearest DC |
| **Frequency** | ~30–40 inter-island shipments/month |
| **Volume** | ~5–10 ferry/ro-ro shipments/week across 3 island groups (Luzon, Visayas, Mindanao) |
| **Owner** | Inter-Island Logistics Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Supply Planner, DC Dispatch, Shipping Line Coordinator, Customs (if applicable), Receiving Store |

### Background

Operating across the Philippine archipelago requires managing inter-island logistics through roll-on/roll-off (ro-ro) ferries and containerized shipping. The 4-DC network (Davao, Cebu, Laguna, Clark) covers primary demand, but store-to-DC replenishment, emergency stock transfers, and seasonal surges require inter-island coordination. Key ferry routes include: Mindanao–Visayas (Davao–Cebu), Visayas–Luzon (Cebu–Manila/Batangas), and intra-Visayas connections.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Shipment Planning**: Supply Planner identifies inter-island transfer need from W22 or W214; creates transfer order in ERP with origin DC, destination store/DC, and required delivery date | Supply Planner | Logistics Manager | 30 min |
| 2 | **Route & Carrier Selection**: Inter-Island Logistics Coordinator selects optimal route (direct ferry vs. multi-hop) and shipping line based on: transit time, schedule frequency, cargo type compatibility, cost per CBM, and historical reliability per W727 | Logistics Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 30 min |
| 3 | **Booking & Documentation**: Coordinator books cargo space with shipping line; prepares shipping documents: bill of lading, cargo manifest, packing list; for hazmat shipments per W699, obtains Dangerous Goods Declaration | Logistics Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 1 hour |
| 4 | **Loading & Dispatch**: DC loads goods onto truck; truck driven to port; cargo loaded onto ferry/ship (ro-ro for truck-on-board or containerized); system updates transfer order status to "In Transit — At Sea" | DC Dispatch / Driver | Logistics Coordinator | 4–8 hours |
| 5 | **In-Transit Monitoring**: System tracks shipment via shipping line API or manual status updates; Coordinator monitors weather advisories (PAGASA) for potential delays; provides ETA updates to receiving location | Logistics Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 15 min/day per shipment |
| 6 | **Port Arrival & Delivery**: Truck offloads at destination port; drives to receiving store or DC; Receiving Clerk processes goods receipt per W109 or W3; system updates inventory and closes transfer order | Driver / Receiving Clerk | DC Manager / Store Manager | 4–8 hours |
| 7 | **Transit Damage & Loss Handling**: Receiving location documents any damage or shortage with photos; Coordinator files claim with shipping line per W500; tracks claim to resolution | Logistics Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 1–2 weeks |
| 8 | **Monthly Route Analytics**: Coordinator compiles inter-island logistics report: transit time by route, on-time delivery rate, cost per CBM by lane, damage/loss rate, carrier reliability ranking | Logistics Coordinator | VP Supply Chain | 4 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Transfer order management (W22)
- Shipping line booking API or portal integration
- In-transit inventory visibility per INV-014
- PAGASA weather advisory integration for route risk assessment
- Goods receipt processing (W109, W3)
- Damage claim management (W500)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Ferry schedule disruptions**: Philippine ferry services frequently delayed or cancelled during typhoon season (June–November), rough seas, and mechanical breakdowns; 24–48 hour delays common
- **Port congestion**: Manila port congestion can delay container pickup by days; Batangas port as alternative has limited roll-on/roll-off capacity
- **Cargo damage at sea**: Saltwater exposure, shifting cargo, and rough handling at ports cause higher damage rates than land transport; insurance claims per W610 are more frequent for inter-island shipments
- **Limited refrigerated transport**: Perishable or temperature-sensitive items (paint, chemicals per W492) require climate-controlled containers with limited availability on inter-island routes


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-create of "creates transfer order in ERP with origin DC, destination store/DC, and…" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "Logistics Coordinator selects optimal route" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "Booking & Documentation" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-258 (ensure controlled execution — Last-Mile & Delivery Partners (PA-06.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication
- **Inter-Island Logistics Coordinator**: ~30–40 shipments/month × ~2 hours/shipment for planning, booking, and monitoring = ~60–80 hours/month; 1 dedicated FTE
- **Logistics Manager**: monthly review and escalation ~4 hours/month; absorbed by existing role

### Time Estimate
Per shipment: planning 30 min, booking 1 hour, loading 4–8 hours, transit 12–48 hours, delivery 4–8 hours; monthly analytics: 4 hours.

- Order management system (W19, POS-023)
- Route optimization engine (W196)

- **Customer not-at-home rate**: ~15–20% failed first delivery attempts in Philippine ecommerce; reschedule costs erode delivery margin

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|

### System Touchpoints
- Carrier rate card master in ERP
- Freight audit engine (invoice matching per W244)
- AP processing (W7)
- Carrier scorecard (W44, SCP-020)
- Cost analytics (SCP-019)
- Contract management (W688)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Fuel price pass-through**: Most carrier contracts include fuel surcharge adjustments tied to Philippine petroleum prices; monthly rate volatility of 3–5% complicates budgeting
- **Carrier rate opacity**: Philippine trucking rates are relationship-based and lack published tariffs; benchmarking relies on competitive quotes which may not reflect actual market rates
- **Hidden accessorial charges**: Waiting time, detention, redelivery, and documentation fees often unlisted in rate cards; freight audit catches these but adds AP processing time
- **Port demurrage and storage**: Import container demurrage at Philippine ports per W249 adds unplanned freight cost; needs tighter coordination with W2B import timeline

- System auto-log of "Logistics Manager maintains carrier rate cards in ERP" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-dispatch of "dispatch manifest × contracted rate card" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-compile of "compiles monthly freight cost report" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-178 (ensure fleet compliance & freight-cost integrity.)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication
- **Logistics Manager**: ~6 hours/month on rate management and cost analysis; absorbed by existing role
- **AP Clerk**: ~4 hours/month on freight invoice audit; absorbed by existing role
- **Procurement**: ~8 hours/quarter on benchmarking and negotiation; absorbed by existing procurement function

### Time Estimate
Monthly: rate maintenance 2 hours, freight audit 4 hours, cost analysis 4 hours; quarterly: benchmarking 8 hours; annual: contract renewal per carrier.

## W1191. Construction Site Delivery Coordination, Access Assessment & Crane/Boom Truck Scheduling

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer places delivery order for heavy/bulky construction materials (cement, steel rebar, lumber, tiles, sand, gravel) to a construction site address |
| **Frequency** | ~3,000–4,000 construction site deliveries/month across all channels (B2B trade, B2C retail, ecommerce) |
| **Volume** | ~15% of total deliveries involve construction site destinations (vs. residential/commercial addresses) |
| **Owner** | Delivery Dispatch Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Delivery Dispatch Coordinator, 3PL Partner Driver, Store Manager, Customer (site contact), Category Manager (heavy materials) |

### Background

Delivering construction materials to active construction sites presents unique logistics challenges not found in standard residential delivery: unpaved access roads, narrow gates, absence of receiving docks, multi-story delivery requirements (materials needed on upper floors), crane/boom truck requirements for heavy loads, site safety protocols, and coordination with site foremen who may have limited availability. This workflow manages the specialized coordination needed to successfully deliver heavy and bulky construction materials to active job sites.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Site access assessment at order entry**: When customer provides a construction site delivery address: (a) system checks delivery address against "Site Access Profile" database (pre-assessed sites with known constraints); (b) if site is new or unprofiled, Delivery Dispatch Coordinator calls customer/site foreman to assess: (i) road accessibility (paved/unpaved, bridge weight limits), (ii) gate width (minimum 3 meters for 10-wheeler), (iii) unloading area availability, (iv) floor-level delivery requirement, (v) crane/boom truck requirement for multi-story or heavy items; (c) creates Site Access Profile in system for future reference | Delivery Dispatch Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 15 min/new site |
| 2 | **Vehicle assignment based on site profile**: System recommends vehicle type based on site access profile: (a) standard 10-wheeler wing van for paved-road sites with dock/ground-level unloading, (b) 6-wheeler truck for narrow-access sites, (c) boom truck/crane truck for multi-story delivery, (d) mini-dump truck for sand/gravel delivery; Logistics Manager confirms vehicle assignment and selects appropriate 3PL partner from approved heavy-haul partners | System / Delivery Dispatch Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 15 min/delivery |
| 3 | **Delivery scheduling with site contact**: Delivery Dispatch Coordinator schedules delivery date/time with site foreman; confirms: (a) site will be accessible on delivery date (no concrete pouring or excavation blocking access), (b) unloading crew available at site, (c) site safety requirements (PPE required, prohibited areas); sends SMS/email confirmation with delivery window and vehicle type | Delivery Dispatch Coordinator | — | 10 min/delivery |
| 4 | **Load securing for construction materials**: At dispatch, driver and warehouse crew follow special load-securing protocols for construction materials: (a) cement bags stacked on pallets, wrapped in plastic (moisture protection), (b) rebar bundles secured with steel strapping, (c) lumber loaded lengthwise with end flags for overhang, (d) tiles in original cartons, no more than 4 pallets high; weight distribution verified | DC Warehouse Crew / Driver | DC Supervisor | 30 min/load |
| 5 | **On-site delivery execution**: Driver arrives at construction site; (a) dons required PPE (hard hat, safety vest — mandatory on construction sites per DOLE), (b) coordinates with site foreman for unloading location, (c) if boom truck: operates boom crane to deliver materials to specified floor; (d) if ground-level: unloads with site crew assistance; (e) captures POD (signature + photo of delivered materials at site) | Driver / Site Foreman | Delivery Dispatch Coordinator | 30–90 min/delivery |
| 6 | **Shortage and damage resolution**: If delivery is short-loaded or materials are damaged during transit/unloading: (a) driver notes discrepancy on delivery receipt, (b) Delivery Dispatch Coordinator notifies Store Manager and customer within 2 hours, (c) replacement delivery scheduled within 48 hours for critical materials (cement, rebar — construction timeline dependent), (d) damage claim filed with 3PL partner if applicable per W1166 | Delivery Dispatch Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 15 min/exception |
| 7 | **Site Access Profile update**: After delivery, Delivery Dispatch Coordinator updates Site Access Profile with delivery notes: (a) actual access conditions encountered, (b) any issues or delays, (c) recommended vehicle type for future deliveries, (d) site contact updates; profile available for future orders to the same site | Delivery Dispatch Coordinator | — | 5 min/delivery |

### System Touchpoints
- Site Access Profile database with geo-tagged site records (W1191.1)
- Vehicle recommendation engine based on site constraints and load characteristics (W1191.2)
- Delivery scheduling with site contact confirmation (W1191.3)
- GPS tracking of heavy-haul vehicles with ETA notification to site foreman (W1191.5)
- POD capture with photo documentation at construction site (W1191.5)
- Exception management workflow linking to carrier claims (W1166) (W1191.6)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Site access changes between order and delivery**: active construction sites change daily; a road accessible during order placement may be blocked by excavation or material staging by delivery date; mitigated by re-confirming access 24 hours before delivery
- **Site safety liability**: delivery personnel entering active construction sites face injury risks from falling objects, heavy equipment, and open excavations; mitigated by mandatory PPE, site safety briefing from foreman, and company insurance coverage for delivery personnel
- **Boom truck/crane availability**: specialized vehicles (boom trucks) are limited in the 3PL fleet; peak demand periods (post-disaster, construction boom) may exceed capacity; mitigated by pre-contracting multiple heavy-haul partners and scheduling boom truck deliveries 3–5 days ahead
- **Multi-drop inefficiency**: construction site deliveries are typically single-drop (one site per trip) due to heavy loads and specialized unloading requirements; this increases per-delivery cost vs. multi-drop residential routes


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "checks delivery address against" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "Logistics Manager confirms vehicle assignment and selects appropriate 3PL…" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-send of "sends SMS/email confirmation with delivery window and vehicle type" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-258 (ensure controlled execution — Last-Mile & Delivery Partners (PA-06.3).)
- operational: re-confirming access 24 hours before delivery; operational: mandatory PPE, site safety briefing from foreman, and company insurance coverage for delivery personnel

### Staffing Implication
- **Delivery Dispatch Coordinator**: ~20–30 hours/week on construction site delivery coordination (60–70% of delivery dispatch capacity during peak construction months). Absorbed within existing role.
- **3PL Partner heavy-haul drivers**: engaged on per-trip basis per existing 3PL contracts.
- **No permanent incremental headcount**.

### Time Estimate
~85–145 min of dispatch-and-driver effort per site delivery (scheduling 10 min + vehicle assignment 10 min + load securing 30 min + on-site execution 30–90 min + access-profile update 5 min; first delivery to a new site adds a 15-min access assessment; shortage/damage exceptions 15 min each); at ~3,000–4,000 site deliveries/month ≈ 51,000–116,000 hours/year (85–145 min each) across drivers, warehouse crews, and dispatch coordinators.

## W1208. Customer Home Delivery White-Glove Service & Installation Add-On Dispatch

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer purchases large/bulky item (appliance, aircon, water heater, bathroom set) with delivery + optional installation add-on at POS or ecommerce |
| **Frequency** | ~2,000–3,000 white-glove deliveries per month across 200 stores |
| **Volume** | ~10–15 per store per month (2,000–3,000/month chain-wide per the Frequency); higher in Metro Manila and urban stores |
| **Owner** | Logistics Dispatcher |
| **Participants** | Logistics Dispatcher, Sales Associate, Installation Technician (per W600 accredited), Customer, Delivery Driver, 3PL Provider |

### Background

BuildRight Depot sells bulky home improvement items (appliances, aircons, water heaters, bathroom fixtures, kitchen cabinets) that require professional home delivery and often installation. The "white-glove" service includes scheduled delivery window, in-home placement, unpacking, debris removal, and optional installation by an accredited technician (per W600). This service is a competitive differentiator against pure-play ecommerce (Lazada/Shopee) that cannot provide professional installation for building materials and appliances. With ~30% of revenue from professional/trade customers and ~55% from walk-in retail, the white-glove service targets the retail DIY homeowner segment that needs help completing projects. This workflow complements W1165 (last-mile delivery dispatch) by adding the installation coordination layer and W138 (home installation services) by covering the delivery-to-installation handoff.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customer purchases bulky item at POS or ecommerce with delivery selected; Sales Associate or system captures: (a) delivery address, (b) preferred delivery date and time window (morning 8–12 / afternoon 1–5), (c) floor level and elevator access, (d) installation add-on selection (Yes/No), (e) site preparation status (plumbing rough-in complete? electrical outlet ready?), (f) contact number and alternate contact | Sales Associate / Customer | — | 5 min |
| 2 | System validates delivery address against serviceable coverage areas; estimates delivery fee based on distance zone and item weight/dimensions; if installation add-on selected: system checks technician availability for requested date using technician scheduling calendar; system confirms or proposes alternative date | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | Logistics Dispatcher reviews delivery queue for the day; groups white-glove deliveries by geographic zone to optimize routing; assigns deliveries to drivers and 3PL partners with appropriate vehicle type (6-wheeler for appliances, wing van for construction materials) | Logistics Dispatcher | Logistics Manager | 30 min/day |
| 4 | If installation add-on: Logistics Dispatcher assigns accredited Installation Technician from scheduling pool; system sends technician a job notification with: order details, item specifications, site access notes, customer contact, estimated arrival window | Logistics Dispatcher / System | Installation Manager | 15 min |
| 5 | System sends customer automated delivery confirmation via SMS/email: delivery date, time window, driver name and contact, technician name (if installation), preparation checklist (clear pathway, measure doorways, verify electrical/plumbing readiness) | System | — | Automated |
| 6 | Delivery driver and technician arrive at customer site; driver unloads and carries item to specified room (white-glove: in-home placement, not curbside); technician inspects site readiness: (a) measurements match product dimensions, (b) electrical/plumbing connections accessible, (c) wall/floor conditions suitable for installation | Driver / Technician | Logistics Dispatcher | 15–30 min |
| 7 | If site is NOT ready (missing electrical outlet, plumbing not roughed-in, access too narrow): technician documents issue with photos; communicates to customer what preparation is needed; Logistics Dispatcher reschedules delivery/installation; delivery fee may still apply per service terms | Technician / Logistics Dispatcher | Installation Manager | 10 min |

## W1440. Fuel Price Volatility Impact Assessment, Freight Cost Adjustment & Route Efficiency Optimization

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Weekly Philippine fuel price adjustment (every Tuesday); or monthly freight cost review; or fuel price change > PHP 3/liter in a single adjustment |
| **Frequency** | Weekly monitoring (fuel prices); monthly freight cost impact review; quarterly route optimization assessment |
| **Volume** | ~200 delivery routes across 4 DCs serving 200 stores; ~PHP 600–800M annual freight spend of which ~30–35% is fuel cost (~PHP 180–280M) |
| **Owner** | Logistics Manager |
| **Participants** | Logistics Manager, VP Supply Chain, Finance Controller, Route Planners, 3PL Partners |

### Background

Philippine fuel prices are among the most volatile in Southeast Asia due to: (a) 100% reliance on imported crude oil, (b) PHP/USD exchange rate fluctuations, (c) government fuel excise tax increases under the TRAIN law (additional PHP 6/liter on diesel and PHP 7–10/liter on gasoline since 2018), and (d) global oil price volatility. Diesel (the primary fuel for delivery trucks) has fluctuated between PHP 45–85/liter over the past 5 years. At ~PHP 180–280M annual fuel cost exposure, a PHP 10/liter increase represents ~PHP 40–60M annual cost increase. BuildRight's 3PL contracts may include fuel surcharge clauses, but the pass-through mechanics are often opaque and may not accurately reflect actual fuel cost changes. This workflow monitors fuel price volatility, assesses its impact on freight costs, adjusts delivery route efficiency to mitigate fuel cost increases, and ensures 3PL fuel surcharges are transparent and fair.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Weekly fuel price monitoring**: Logistics Manager tracks weekly diesel price adjustments (DOE Philippines publishes every Tuesday): (a) current diesel price per liter by region (Luzon, Visayas, Mindanao prices differ due to shipping costs), (b) week-over-week change, (c) year-to-date average vs. budgeted fuel price for freight cost planning, (d) if fuel price change > PHP 3/liter in a single week: trigger immediate impact assessment | Logistics Manager | VP Supply Chain | 30 min/week |
| 2 | **Monthly freight cost impact assessment**: Finance Controller and Logistics Manager assess monthly fuel cost impact: (a) calculate actual fuel cost component of freight spend (from 3PL invoices and own fleet fuel receipts), (b) compare to budgeted fuel cost (freight budget assumes PHP X/liter diesel), (c) calculate variance: actual fuel cost vs. budget × volume, (d) if variance > 5% of monthly freight budget: escalate to VP Supply Chain and CFO for freight budget adjustment or SRP impact review | Finance Controller / Logistics Manager | VP Supply Chain | 3 hours/month |
| 3 | **3PL fuel surcharge verification**: For 3PL partners with fuel surcharge clauses: (a) Logistics Manager verifies surcharge calculation against DOE published diesel prices, (b) validate surcharge formula matches contract terms (e.g., surcharge = (actual diesel price − base diesel price) × km × liters/km factor), (c) flag discrepancies where 3PL surcharge exceeds contractually agreed amount, (d) dispute incorrect surcharges through AP process per W7 | Logistics Manager | Finance Controller | 2 hours/month |
| 4 | **Route efficiency optimization (quarterly)**: In response to sustained fuel price increases: (a) Route Planners analyze delivery route efficiency: (i) average km per delivery, (ii) fuel consumption per delivery, (iii) load factor (kg delivered per liter of fuel), (iv) identify routes with below-average fuel efficiency, (b) optimization actions: (i) consolidate multi-stop routes to reduce total km, (ii) shift deliveries from low-efficiency to high-efficiency 3PL partners, (iii) evaluate DC-to-store direct routes vs. hub-and-spoke, (iv) consider intermodal options (RoRo ferry vs. truck-only for inter-island routes), (c) target: maintain or improve deliveries per liter of fuel despite fuel price increases | Route Planners / Logistics Manager | VP Supply Chain | 8 hours/quarter |
| 5 | **Annual fuel cost budget planning**: For annual freight budget preparation: (a) Finance Controller and Logistics Manager project next year's fuel cost based on: (i) DOE fuel price forecasts, (ii) planned store expansion (10–15 new stores = new routes), (iii) route efficiency improvement targets, (iv) 3PL contract rate and surcharge terms, (b) present freight budget to CFO with fuel price sensitivity analysis (best case, base case, worst case) | Finance Controller / Logistics Manager | CFO | 8 hours/year |

### System Touchpoints
- DOE fuel price API integration — automated weekly diesel price capture (W1440.1)
- Freight cost analytics — actual vs. budget fuel cost tracking (W1440.2)
- Route optimization engine — fuel efficiency simulation and route redesign (W1440.4)
- Integration with W196 (route planning), W1163 (3PL daily ops), W1439 (3PL QBR), W7 (AP payment)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Fuel price unpredictability**: Philippine diesel prices can swing PHP 5–10/liter within a month due to global events (OPEC decisions, geopolitical conflicts, refinery outages); budgeting based on a single fuel price assumption is inherently unreliable
- **3PL surcharge disputes**: 3PL partners may apply fuel surcharges that exceed their actual cost increase, using fuel price volatility as a profit center; BuildRight must audit surcharges rigorously
- **Route efficiency trade-offs**: consolidating routes to save fuel may reduce delivery frequency to stores (e.g., from 3x/week to 2x/week), impacting shelf availability; the trade-off between fuel cost and service level must be managed carefully


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "Logistics Manager tracks weekly diesel price adjustments" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "Monthly freight cost impact assessment" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "validate surcharge formula matches contract terms" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-258 (ensure controlled execution — Last-Mile & Delivery Partners (PA-06.3).)
- operational: Logistics Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
- Weekly monitoring: 30 min/week = 26 hours/year
- Monthly impact assessment: 3 hours/month = 36 hours/year
- Monthly surcharge verification: 2 hours/month = 24 hours/year
- Quarterly optimization: 8 hours/quarter = 32 hours/year
- Annual budget: 8 hours/year
- **Annual estimate: ~126 hours/year**

- Finance Controller: ~5 hours/month on freight cost analysis. Absorbed.

## W1488. Customer Bulk Sand, Gravel & Cement Delivery Scheduling, Weight Ticket Verification & Site Unloading Coordination

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer purchases bulk construction materials (sand, gravel, cement bags, fill soil, crushed stone) at store or via trade account; delivery required due to weight/volume |
| **Frequency** | ~15–30 bulk delivery requests per store per month; higher during dry season construction peak (Jan–May) |
| **Volume** | ~3,000–6,000 bulk deliveries/month chain-wide; average order: 2–5 cubic meters sand/gravel or 50–200 bags cement |
| **Owner** | Logistics Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Customer, Sales Associate, Logistics Coordinator, Delivery Driver, Third-Party Hauler (for oversized loads) |

### Background

BuildRight sells bulk construction materials — sand, gravel, crushed stone, fill soil, and bagged cement — that are impractical for customer pickup. A typical order of 3 cubic meters of sand weighs 4.5–5 metric tons, requiring a 6-wheeler or 10-wheeler truck. These deliveries are critical for construction projects and represent 15–20% of store revenue in the building materials category. Challenges include: (a) weight verification — customers dispute that they received the ordered volume; (b) site access — construction sites may have narrow roads, overhead wires, or soft ground that prevents truck access; (c) unloading method — some sites have a boom or crane; others require manual shoveling; (d) timing — construction projects run on tight schedules; late delivery causes idle labor cost for contractors. Philippine legal-metrology rules (DTI per RA 7394; NML/DOST-ITDI calibration standards per RA 9236) require calibrated weighing for commercial material sales; each delivery must be accompanied by a certified weight ticket.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Delivery order capture & site assessment**: At point of sale: (a) Sales Associate records delivery order: material type, volume/quantity, delivery address, site contact person and mobile number; (b) associate asks customer about site access: road width, overhead clearance, turning radius, ground firmness; (c) customer specifies preferred delivery date/time window; (d) if site is in a gated community or has restricted access hours: customer responsible for providing gate pass or access authorization; (e) order transmitted to Logistics Coordinator for scheduling | Sales Associate / Customer | — | 10–15 min |
| 2 | **Delivery scheduling & vehicle assignment**: (a) Logistics Coordinator reviews delivery queue for the requested date; (b) assigns appropriate vehicle based on volume: (i) < 2 cu.m.: 6-wheeler truck (max 6 cu.m.); (ii) 2–8 cu.m.: 10-wheeler truck (max 12 cu.m.); (iii) > 8 cu.m.: split into multiple trips or assign 18-wheeler dump truck via third-party hauler; (c) if all BuildRight trucks committed: third-party hauler engaged per PA-06.3 rate card; (d) customer receives SMS confirmation with delivery date, time window (±2 hours), truck plate number, and driver contact; (e) delivery fee calculated by distance zone: Zone A (0–5 km): PHP 500; Zone B (5–15 km): PHP 1,000; Zone C (15–30 km): PHP 1,800; Zone D (> 30 km): PHP 2,500+ | Logistics Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 10 min per delivery |
| 3 | **Weight ticket verification at loading**: At DC or supplier yard: (a) truck drives onto certified weighbridge → tare weight recorded; (b) material loaded (by volume scoop for sand/gravel, by pallet for cement); (c) truck re-weighed → gross weight recorded; (d) net weight = gross − tare; (e) weight ticket printed with: material type, net weight, date, weighbridge certification number, driver signature; (f) if net weight is < 95% of ordered volume-equivalent weight: additional material loaded; (g) weight ticket accompanies delivery as proof of quantity | Delivery Driver / Weighbridge Operator | Logistics Coordinator | 15–20 min per load |
| 4 | **Site delivery & unloading**: At customer site: (a) driver calls site contact 30 minutes before arrival; (b) driver verifies site access per assessment in Step 1; (c) unloading method depends on material and site: (i) sand/gravel/soil: truck dumps material at designated spot on site (hydraulic lift); (ii) bagged cement: driver + 1 helper stack bags at designated area (max 200 bags manually; larger quantities require customer-provided labor or forklift); (d) customer/site representative signs delivery receipt acknowledging quantity and condition; (e) driver photographs delivered material as proof of placement; (f) any discrepancy or damage noted on delivery receipt before signing | Delivery Driver | Logistics Coordinator | 30–60 min per delivery (includes travel time from last delivery) |
| 5 | **Delivery confirmation & weight ticket reconciliation**: (a) driver returns signed delivery receipt and weight ticket to Logistics Coordinator; (b) system matches delivery receipt to sales order; (c) weight ticket filed for DTI legal-metrology regime (per RA 7394/RA 9236) compliance; (d) if customer disputes quantity: weight ticket serves as primary evidence; (e) delivery status updated to "Completed" in system, triggering revenue recognition; (f) customer satisfaction SMS survey sent 24 hours post-delivery | Delivery Driver / Logistics Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 5 min per delivery |

### System Touchpoints
- Delivery order module integrated with POS transaction for bulk materials (W1488.1)
- Vehicle scheduling and assignment system with capacity calendar per truck (W1488.2)
- Weighbridge integration for tare/gross/net weight capture and weight ticket printing (W1488.3)
- GPS tracking for delivery truck location and estimated arrival time (W1488.4)
- Delivery receipt capture with digital signature and photo proof (W1488.4)
- Customer SMS notification system for delivery confirmation and satisfaction survey (W1488.2, .5)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Weight disputes**: customers frequently claim they received less than ordered volume; sand/gravel compaction during transport can reduce apparent volume by 5–10% even when weight is correct; weight ticket is legal proof but does not satisfy visual volume expectation
- **Site access failures**: ~5–8% of deliveries fail on first attempt because customer did not accurately describe site conditions; re-delivery costs PHP 500–1,800 per incident plus truck idle time
- **Seasonal demand spikes**: during dry season (Jan–May), bulk delivery demand can double; BuildRight's own fleet of ~40 trucks is insufficient; third-party hauler rates increase 20–40% during peak
- **Cement bag damage**: manual unloading of 50-kg cement bags has ~2–3% bag rupture rate; customer expects replacement but driver has no spare bags on truck


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "Logistics Coordinator for scheduling" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "reviews delivery queue for the requested date" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-generate of "Weight ticket verification at loading" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls

- operational: Logistics Coordinator review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

- No incremental headcount.
