## W1827. Construction-in-Progress (CIP) Account Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | First cost incurred on approved capex project |
| **Frequency** | Continuous for active projects |
| **Volume** | ~30–50 active CIP accounts at any time (one per active capex project, per the PA-40.1 ~30–50-project register) |
| **Owner** | Project Accountant |
| **Participants** | Project Accountant, Accounting Manager, FP&A |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CIP account structure in GL: (a) one CIP account per active capex project (e.g., CIP-BRD-001 Mindanao Store, CIP-IT-ERP-Upgrade), (b) sub-accounts by cost category: materials, labor, services, equipment, professional fees, capitalized interest, (c) entity-specific: each entity's CIP tracked separately, (d) group-level CIP visible for consolidation | System | Accounting Manager | Per project creation |
| 2 | Monthly CIP management: (a) all project costs accumulated in CIP (not depreciated — CIP assets are not yet in use), (b) monthly review per W1705 (CIP reconciliation), (c) CIP aging report: flag projects in CIP > 12 months (risk of stalled or abandoned projects), (d) CIP balance should increase as project progresses and decrease to zero when asset is turned over | Project Accountant | Accounting Manager | Monthly |
| 3 | CIP balance sheet presentation: (a) CIP classified as non-current asset (part of Property, Plant & Equipment) in balance sheet, (b) separate line item or combined with fixed assets (materiality-dependent), (c) CIP balance = total costs incurred but not yet capitalized as completed assets | Accounting Manager | CFO | At reporting date |
| 4 | Impairment of CIP: if project is stalled or abandoned, CIP must be assessed for impairment per PAS 36 (same as W1700 for completed assets); abandoned projects written off per W2750 | Project Accountant | Accounting Manager | If triggered |


### System Touchpoints
- CIP account structure — one GL CIP account per active project (step 1)
- Monthly CIP management — accumulation without depreciation, roll-forward (step 2)
- Balance-sheet presentation within PP&E (step 3)
- Impairment assessment for stalled/abandoned projects per PAS 36 (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Aging-CIP risk**: projects complete but costs linger in CIP, overstating PP&E and dodging depreciation; mitigated by monthly roll-forward with turnover follow-up (step 2)
- **Impairment-blindness risk**: stalled projects carried at cost despite no future benefit; mitigated by PAS 36 impairment assessment (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "account structure in GL" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "review per W1705" (replaces manual Step 2).

### Controls
- CTL-363 (ensure controlled execution — Construction-in-Progress & Asset Turnover (PA-40.3).)
- operational: Project Accountant review and approval gate; periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
CIP account structure maintained per project; monthly CIP management within the close ≈ 4–8 hours/month ≈ 48–96 hours/year across ~10–20 active accounts; balance-sheet presentation at reporting dates; impairment assessment on stall/abandonment triggers per W1700.

## W1828. Project Completion Certification & Asset Turnover

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Project reaches practical completion (asset ready for intended use) |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 store completions/year + ~20–35 other capex project completions (remodels, DC, IT, equipment) |
| **Volume** | ~30–50 turnovers/year |
| **Owner** | COO (construction) / CIO (IT) / Department Head (other) |
| **Participants** | Project Manager, COO, Store Manager (for new stores), Project Accountant, Fixed Asset Accountant |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Project Manager confirms practical completion: (a) all construction/installation work complete (punch list items may remain but are not critical to operation), (b) systems tested and operational (IT projects: UAT passed; stores: POS live), (c) safety inspection passed, (d) occupancy permit received (if required), (e) asset is **ready for its intended use** per PFRS — this is the trigger for depreciation start (not the grand opening date) | Project Manager | COO / CIO | 1 day |
| 2 | Completion certificate prepared and signed: (a) Project Manager certifies completion, (b) COO or CIO accepts, (c) Store Manager (for new stores) confirms operational readiness, (d) punch list items documented with agreed resolution timeline, (e) custody handover: the completed assets pass to the operating custodian of record — Store Manager for new stores, the owning department head otherwise — and registration assigns the custodian per W1690 so no asset turns over unowned | Project Manager / COO | COO | 1 day |
| 3 | Project Accountant initiates CIP-to-asset turnover per W1829: (a) reviews all CIP costs for completeness and accuracy, (b) ensures all invoices received and allocated, (c) accrues any known but uninvoiced costs, (d) transfers CIP balance to completed fixed assets | Project Accountant | Accounting Manager | 2–4 days |


### System Touchpoints
- Practical completion confirmation — work complete, asset ready for use (step 1)
- Completion certificate — PM certifies, COO/CIO accepts, Store Manager receives (step 2)
- Cost completeness review before turnover initiation (step 3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Premature-turnover risk**: asset declared complete while materially unfinished, starting depreciation early; mitigated by practical-completion criteria (step 1)
- **Cost-completeness risk**: late invoices arrive after CIP is cleared; mitigated by completeness review plus post-turnover charge controls (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-inspect of "Project Manager confirms practical completion" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-document of "documented with agreed resolution timeline" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews all CIP costs for completeness and accuracy" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-363 (ensure controlled execution — Construction-in-Progress & Asset Turnover (PA-40.3).)
- operational: COO (construction) / CIO (IT) / Department Head (other) review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
4–6 days elapsed per occurrence (1 day + 1 day + 2–4 days) — at ~10–15 store completions/year + ~20–35 other project completions ≈ 30–50 occurrences/year.

## W1829. CIP-to-Fixed Asset Conversion Processing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Project completion certified (W1828) |
| **Frequency** | ~30–50 conversions/year |
| **Volume** | 1 conversion per completed project (may result in multiple fixed assets) |
| **Owner** | Fixed Asset Accountant |
| **Participants** | Fixed Asset Accountant, Project Accountant, Accounting Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fixed Asset Accountant decomposes project cost into component assets per W1694: (a) **new store example** (total cost PHP 50M): (i) leasehold improvements: PHP 20M (depreciate over lease term 10–15 years), (ii) HVAC system: PHP 5M (10–15 years), (iii) electrical installation: PHP 4M (15–20 years), (iv) plumbing: PHP 3M (15–20 years), (v) racking/shelving: PHP 5M (10 years), (vi) POS system (3 terminals): PHP 1.5M (5 years), (vii) signage: PHP 2M (7–10 years), (viii) generator: PHP 1.5M (7 years), (ix) CCTV system: PHP 1M (5 years), (x) initial inventory: PHP 5M (not fixed asset — transferred to inventory), (xi) pre-opening expenses (training, marketing): PHP 2M (not fixed asset — expensed), (xii) contingency unused: returned to capex pool | Fixed Asset Accountant | Accounting Manager | 1–2 days/project |
| 2 | System processes CIP-to-asset conversion: (a) CIP account debited to zero, (b) new fixed asset records created with component structures, (c) each component assigned useful life and depreciation method per W1691, (d) depreciation begins from the **ready-for-use date** (completion certificate date, not grand opening date), (e) pre-opening and non-capitalizable costs reclassified from CIP to expense | System | Fixed Asset Accountant | 30 min/project |
| 3 | Journal entries posted: (a) debit: fixed asset accounts (gross) per component, (b) credit: CIP account (cleared to zero), (c) debit: pre-opening expense (for non-capitalized costs), (d) debit: inventory (for initial stock), (e) credit: CIP (for above reclassifications) | System | Accounting Manager | Automated |
| 4 | Fixed Asset Accountant verifies new asset records: (a) total asset value = CIP balance before conversion (no unexplained difference), (b) depreciation start date correct, (c) useful life per component is appropriate, (d) asset tags ordered per W1692 | Fixed Asset Accountant | Accounting Manager | 1 day |


### System Touchpoints
- Component decomposition per W1694 — building, fixtures, POS, HVAC with separate lives (step 1)
- CIP-to-asset conversion processing — CIP cleared, asset records created (step 2)
- Journal entries and depreciation-start setup (step 3)
- Verification — total assets = CIP balance before conversion (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Componentization error**: everything depreciated on a single life, misstating expense timing; mitigated by component decomposition per W1694 (step 1)
- **Conversion-imbalance risk**: assets created ≠ CIP cleared; mitigated by balance verification with unexplained-difference investigation (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "Accountant decomposes project cost into component assets per W1694" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-record of "records created with component structures" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-post of "Journal entries posted" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-363 (ensure controlled execution — Construction-in-Progress & Asset Turnover (PA-40.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
1 day elapsed per occurrence (1 day); cadence-carried steps as authored (1–2 days/project) — at ~30–50 conversions/year ≈ 30–50 occurrences/year.

## W2748. Post-Implementation Review & Actual vs. Projected ROI Analysis

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | 12 months after project completion |
| **Frequency** | ~30–50 post-implementation reviews/year (one per completed project, per W1828) |
| **Volume** | 1 review per completed project |
| **Owner** | FP&A |
| **Participants** | FP&A, Project Sponsor, Department Head, CFO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | FP&A compares actual project outcomes to original business case: (a) **cost**: actual total cost vs. approved budget (typically actual is 5–15% over budget for construction projects), (b) **timeline**: actual completion date vs. planned (typically 1–3 months delayed), (c) **revenue**: for new stores — actual Year 1 revenue vs. projected revenue per W1652, (d) **profitability**: store EBITDA margin vs. projected, (e) **payback**: actual payback trajectory vs. projected payback period, (f) **IRR/NPV**: recalculate with actual cash flows | FP&A | CFO | 1–2 days/project |
| 2 | For non-revenue projects (IT, equipment replacement): (a) cost savings achieved vs. projected, (b) operational improvements measured vs. baseline, (c) user satisfaction survey, (d) compliance achieved (if regulatory project) | FP&A | Department Head | 1 day/project |
| 3 | FP&A prepares post-implementation review report: (a) summary of actual vs. projected, (b) key learnings (what went well, what didn't), (c) calibration: how accurate were original projections? (d) recommendations for future similar projects, (e) presented to steering committee or CFO | FP&A | CFO | 1 day |
| 4 | Annual: FP&A compiles post-implementation results across all completed projects; identifies systematic biases in project estimation (e.g., consistently overestimating revenue, underestimating costs); adjusts estimation methodology for future business cases | FP&A | CFO | 1 day/year |


### System Touchpoints
- Actual vs. business-case comparison — cost, revenue, schedule at 12 months (step 1)
- Non-revenue project benefit measurement — savings and operational improvements (step 2)
- Post-implementation review report with learnings (step 3)
- Annual cross-project compilation identifying estimation biases (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **No-lookback risk**: projects approved, delivered, and never judged against promises; mitigated by the mandatory 12-month review (step 1)
- **Systematic-optimism risk**: the same estimation bias repeats every year; mitigated by annual bias compilation feeding W2752 calibration (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-calculate of "recalculate with actual cash flows" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-measure of "For non-revenue projects" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "FP&A prepares post-implementation review report" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-363 (ensure controlled execution — Construction-in-Progress & Asset Turnover (PA-40.3).)
- operational: FP&A review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
1 day elapsed per occurrence (1 day); cadence-carried steps as authored (1–2 days/project, 1 day/project, 1 day/year) — at ~30–50 post-implementation reviews/year (per the Frequency) ≈ 30–50 occurrences/year.

## W2749. Partial Asset Turnover (Phased Project Completion)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Part of a large project is ready for use before the entire project is complete |
| **Frequency** | ~5–10 partial turnovers/year (primarily multi-store builds, DC expansions) |
| **Volume** | Occasional — depends on project phasing |
| **Owner** | Fixed Asset Accountant |
| **Participants** | Fixed Asset Accountant, Project Manager, Accounting Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scenarios requiring partial turnover: (a) DC expansion: new wing operational while second wing still under construction, (b) multi-store build: first 3 stores of a 5-store batch open before remaining 2, (c) IT rollout: phase 1 modules live while phase 2 still in development | Project Manager | Department Head | Event-driven |
| 2 | Fixed Asset Accountant allocates CIP costs between completed and in-progress portions: (a) completed portion: identify specific costs attributable to the ready-for-use portion, (b) in-progress portion: remaining costs stay in CIP, (c) apportionment methodology: direct identification where possible; proportional allocation for shared costs (e.g., project management, permits) | Fixed Asset Accountant | Accounting Manager | 1–2 days |
| 3 | System processes partial turnover: (a) completed portion transferred from CIP to fixed assets, (b) depreciation begins on completed portion, (c) remaining CIP continues to accumulate costs until next phase completes, (d) subsequent turnovers processed same way until project fully complete | System | Fixed Asset Accountant | Per phase |


### System Touchpoints
- Partial turnover scenarios — DC wings, multi-store programs (step 1)
- CIP cost allocation between completed and in-progress portions (step 2)
- Partial conversion processing with depreciation starting on the completed portion (step 3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Arbitrary-split risk**: the completed/in-progress cost split is guesswork, misstating both depreciation and remaining CIP; mitigated by specific-cost identification with documented allocation (step 2)
- **Depreciation-start error**: wings in use but not depreciated; mitigated by depreciation commencing on partial turnover (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "Fixed Asset Accountant allocates CIP costs between completed and" (replaces manual Step 2).

### Controls
- CTL-210 (ensure capex authorization & commitment control.)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
~1–2 days per partial turnover (CIP cost allocation between completed and in-progress portions + system transfer to fixed assets) × ~5–10 partial turnovers/year ≈ 5–20 days/year.

## W2750. Abandoned Project Write-Off & Cost Recovery

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Decision to abandon or indefinitely suspend a capex project |
| **Frequency** | ~1–3 abandoned projects/year (low frequency but high impact) |
| **Volume** | Low |
| **Owner** | CFO |
| **Participants** | CFO, FP&A, Project Manager, CEO, Board |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Triggers for project abandonment: (a) site issues discovered during construction (soil, legal, environmental), (b) market conditions change (trade area no longer viable — competitor opens nearby, economic downturn), (c) regulatory block (permit denied, zoning change), (d) strategic pivot (company changes expansion plan), (e) contractor failure (contractor abandons project) | Project Manager / COO | CEO | Event-driven |
| 2 | CFO and FP&A assess financial impact: (a) total CIP costs incurred to date, (b) recovery potential: (i) materials salvageable and reusable (transfer to other projects), (ii) equipment already purchased (sell, return to vendor, or redeploy), (iii) contractor claims and counterclaims, (iv) insurance recovery (if applicable), (c) net write-off amount = CIP costs − recoveries | CFO / FP&A | CEO | 1–2 weeks |
| 3 | Write-off approval: (a) < PHP 5M: CFO approval, (b) PHP 5–50M: CEO approval, (c) > PHP 50M: Board approval, (d) write-off recognized as non-current asset disposal loss (not inventory write-off) | Approver per tier | Board (if material) | 1–5 days |
| 4 | Write-off processing: (a) CIP balance written off to P&L (debit: project abandonment loss, credit: CIP), (b) recoverable amounts recorded as receivable, (c) when recovery received: credit to project abandonment loss (partial offset), (d) Board notified at next meeting regardless of amount | Project Accountant / Fixed Asset Accountant | Accounting Manager | 1–2 days |
| 5 | Lessons learned documented per W2751; preventive measures implemented to reduce future abandonment risk | COO / CIO | CEO | 1 week |


### System Touchpoints
- Abandonment triggers — site issues, market shifts (step 1)
- Financial impact assessment — CIP incurred, salvage/recovery potential (step 2)
- Tiered write-off approval — CFO < PHP 5M; CEO PHP 5–50M; Board > PHP 50M (step 3)
- Write-off processing with recoveries recorded separately (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Zombie-project risk**: abandoned projects carried indefinitely in CIP; mitigated by write-off approval tiers forcing recognition (steps 3–4)
- **Recovery-overstatement risk**: salvage values assumed but never realized; mitigated by recoverables recorded separately from the write-off (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-issue of "issues discovered during construction" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-count of "CFO and FP&A assess financial impact" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-record of "recorded as receivable" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-363 (ensure controlled execution — Construction-in-Progress & Asset Turnover (PA-40.3).)
- operational: CFO review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
2.3–4 weeks elapsed per occurrence (1–2 weeks + 1–5 days + 1–2 days + 1 week); cadence-carried steps as authored (Event-driven) — at ~1–3 abandoned projects/year (low frequency but high impact) ≈ 1–3 occurrences/year.

## W2751. Project Closeout Documentation & Archiving

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Project completed and asset turned over (W1828–1829) or abandoned (W2750) |
| **Frequency** | ~30–50 closeouts/year |
| **Volume** | 1 closeout package per project |
| **Owner** | Project Manager |
| **Participants** | Project Manager, Project Accountant, COO, FP&A |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Project closeout checklist: (a) all costs received and allocated to project, (b) all POs closed (no open commitments), (c) all vendor invoices received and paid (including retention release if applicable), (d) CIP account cleared (zero balance after turnover), (e) fixed assets registered per W1690, (f) asset tags applied per W1692, (g) project completion certificate signed, (h) post-implementation review scheduled per W2748 | Project Manager / Project Accountant | COO / CIO | 1–2 weeks |
| 2 | Documentation archive: (a) original capex request and business case, (b) financial evaluation and approval documentation, (c) all vendor contracts and POs, (d) contractor progress billings and payment records, (e) change orders with approvals, (f) completion certificate and turnover documentation, (g) warranty certificates from contractors and equipment vendors, (h) as-built drawings (for construction projects), (i) post-implementation review, (j) budget vs. actual final report | Project Manager | COO / CIO | 1 week |
| 3 | Archive retention: (a) project documentation retained for asset useful life + 10 years (for tax and audit purposes), (b) stored in document management system (electronic) and physical archive (for signed originals), (c) indexed by project number for retrieval | Project Manager / Project Accountant | Accounting Manager | 1 day |
| 4 | System project status changed to "Closed"; no further costs can be charged to the project | System | Project Accountant | Automated |


### System Touchpoints
- Closeout checklist — costs complete, POs closed, vendor claims settled (step 1)
- Documentation archive — full project file (step 2)
- Retention for asset life + 10 years for tax/audit (step 3)
- System status "Closed" blocking further charges (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Open-tail risk**: project closed with open POs or unpaid claims surfacing later; mitigated by the closeout checklist (step 1)
- **Post-close-charge risk**: costs keep landing on a completed project; mitigated by the system block on closed projects (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "review scheduled per W2748" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-record of "records" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "documentation retained for asset useful life + 10 years" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-363 (ensure controlled execution — Construction-in-Progress & Asset Turnover (PA-40.3).)
- operational: Project Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
2.1–3.1 weeks elapsed per occurrence (1–2 weeks + 1 week + 1 day) — at ~30–50 closeouts/year ≈ 30–50 occurrences/year.

## W2752. Annual Capex Portfolio Review & Process Improvement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual strategic planning cycle (Q4) |
| **Frequency** | Annual |
| **Volume** | 1 comprehensive review |
| **Owner** | CFO |
| **Participants** | CFO, FP&A, VP Store Operations, COO, CIO, CEO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | FP&A prepares annual capex portfolio review: (a) total capex spent vs. budget (by category: new stores, renovations, DC, IT, equipment), (b) project delivery performance: % on time, % on budget, % achieving projected ROI, (c) cost estimation accuracy: average budget variance per project type, (d) timeline estimation accuracy: average schedule variance, (e) post-implementation ROI results: actual vs. projected across completed projects, (f) abandoned project cost and reasons, (g) capital productivity: revenue growth ÷ capex invested | FP&A | CFO | 1 week |
| 2 | CFO and C-Suite review capex portfolio performance: (a) which project types deliver best returns (new stores vs. renovations vs. IT)? (b) what systemic issues cause overruns? (c) what estimation biases need correction? (d) is capex spend aligned with strategic priorities? | CFO / C-Suite | CEO | 2 hours |
| 3 | Process improvements identified and implemented: (a) estimation methodology: adjust cost escalation factors based on actual construction inflation, (b) approval process: streamline for repeatable project types (new stores have standardized approval), (c) contractor management: update contractor qualification criteria based on performance, (d) system enhancements: automate CIP-to-asset conversion, improve cost tracking granularity per W1810 | CFO / COO / CIO | CEO | 1–2 weeks |
| 4 | Updated capex process guidelines distributed to all project stakeholders; training provided for new procedures | CFO / FP&A | — | 1 week |


### System Touchpoints
- Annual portfolio review — spend vs. budget by category, returns by project type (steps 1–2)
- Process improvements — estimation calibration, approval thresholds (step 3)
- Updated guidelines distributed with stakeholder training (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Repeat-overspend risk**: chronic overruns recur without methodology correction; mitigated by estimation calibration from actuals (step 3)
- **Guideline-decay risk**: improved procedures documented but never adopted; mitigated by distribution with training as a closing step (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "FP&A prepares annual capex portfolio review" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-issue of "issues cause overruns?" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "Process improvements identified and implemented" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-363 (ensure controlled execution — Construction-in-Progress & Asset Turnover (PA-40.3).)
- operational: CFO review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
2 hours per occurrence (per-step effort 2 hours); elapsed timeline 1 week + 1–2 weeks + 1 week — at Annual ≈ 1 occurrence/year ≈ 2 hours/year.
