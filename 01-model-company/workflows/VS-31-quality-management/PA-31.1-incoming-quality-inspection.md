## W1600. DC Goods Receipt Quality Sampling & Acceptance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Goods receipt at DC (against PO or transfer order per W18/W4) |
| **Frequency** | ~6,000 goods receipts/month across 4 DCs |
| **Volume** | ~1,500 GRs/DC/month; quality sampling on ~40% of GRs (high-value, new vendor, seasonal, flagged categories) |
| **Owner** | DC Quality Inspector |
| **Participants** | DC Quality Inspector, Receiving Clerk, DC Supervisor, Buyer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System auto-flags GR for quality inspection based on rules: (a) new vendor (first 3 shipments), (b) vendor with quality score < 80%, (c) high-value PO line > PHP 500K, (d) category flagged for inspection (electrical, paint/chemical, power tools), (e) random sample at configurable rate per category (default 10%) | System | DC Supervisor | Automated |
| 2 | Receiving Clerk completes standard GR (quantity, condition check); system places flagged items in "Quality Hold" status — inventory recorded but unavailable for allocation or shipment | Receiving Clerk | DC Supervisor | 5 min/pallet |
| 3 | DC Quality Inspector pulls AQL sampling plan per SKU category (per W1604 configuration): (a) selects random sample from received lot per AQL table (e.g., ISO 2859-1 General Inspection Level II), (b) performs visual inspection (packaging integrity, labeling accuracy, product appearance), (c) performs dimensional/functional checks per category checklist (electrical: plug fit test; tools: torque test; tiles: dimensional tolerance ±1mm), (d) checks required certifications/markings (PS mark, ICC sticker, FPA registration for chemicals) | DC Quality Inspector | DC Supervisor | 15–30 min/lot |
| 4 | **Pass**: Inspector records "Accept" in quality inspection module; system releases inventory from Quality Hold to available stock; GR proceeds to putaway per W18 | DC Quality Inspector | DC Supervisor | 2 min |
| 5 | **Fail — Minor**: Inspector records defect type and quantity; system accepts lot with defect notation; defect rate feeds vendor scorecard per W44; inventory released with "Accepted with Deviation" status | DC Quality Inspector | DC Supervisor | 5 min |
| 6 | **Fail — Major/Reject**: Inspector records "Reject" with defect description and photos; system keeps inventory in Quality Hold; notifies Buyer and Category Manager; defective items quarantined for RTV per W88/W1280 or vendor negotiation; acceptable portion released | DC Quality Inspector | DC Supervisor / Buyer | 15 min |
| 7 | Monthly: DC Supervisor generates quality inspection summary report — pass rate by vendor, by category, by DC; defect trend analysis; feeds into vendor quality review per W1608 (W31.2) | DC Supervisor | VP Supply Chain | 1 hour/month |

### System Touchpoints
- GR auto-flagging rules engine with configurable criteria per vendor, category, value threshold (W1600.1)
- AQL sampling plan lookup per SKU category (W1604)
- Quality Hold inventory status — blocked from allocation, ATP, and shipment (W1600.2)
- Oracle Quality inspection dispositions (fit-gap C12): Accept / Accept with Deviation / Reject with defect codes and photos (W1600.3–6)
- Vendor quality scorecard integration (W44, W1608)
- Monthly quality summary dashboard (W1600.7)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Sampling bias**: Random sampling may miss cluster defects (e.g., all defective items on one pallet); stratified sampling by pallet position recommended for high-risk categories
- **Inspection throughput**: At ~2,400 inspections/month across 4 DCs (~600/DC), peak receiving days may create inspection backlogs that delay putaway and store replenishment
- **Certification verification**: Manual check of PS/ICC/FPA marks is prone to oversight; OCR-assisted scanning of certification labels would improve accuracy
- **Quality Hold aging**: Items in Quality Hold beyond 48 hours without inspection delay store replenishment; auto-escalation to DC Supervisor recommended


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-inspect of "inspection based on rules" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-record of "recorded but unavailable for allocation or shipment" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "checks per category checklist" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-334 (ensure controlled execution — Incoming Quality Inspection & Control (PA-31.1).)
- operational: DC Quality Inspector review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
15–30 min per inspected lot; ~2,400 inspections/month × 20 min avg = ~800 hours/month across 4 DCs (~200 hours/DC/month)

### Staffing Implication
At ~200 inspection hours/DC/month, each DC needs ~1.25 FTE Quality Inspectors (assuming 160 productive hours/month). Recommendation: 1 QA Analyst per DC (4 total, per W819, plus a centralized QA Manager), with Receiving Clerks cross-trained for visual-only inspections on low-risk categories.

## W1601. Store-Level DSD Quality Verification

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Direct store delivery (DSD) goods arrival at store — ~500–600 DSD receipts/month (~30% of inbound by value) |
| **Frequency** | ~2–3 DSD deliveries per store per month (varies; stores with lumber/cement yards receive more) |
| **Volume** | ~500–600 DSD receipts/month across 200 stores (per profile §7.1 and the Trigger) |
| **Owner** | Receiving Clerk |
| **Participants** | Receiving Clerk, Department Supervisor, Vendor Driver |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Vendor driver arrives with delivery; Receiving Clerk accesses PO/ASN in system; checks delivery against expected PO: vendor, items, quantities | Receiving Clerk | Department Supervisor | 5 min/delivery |
| 2 | Quick quality verification: (a) visual inspection of packaging integrity (no crushed boxes, wet damage, broken seals), (b) count check against packing slip, (c) check cement for clumping/lumpiness (indicates moisture exposure), (d) check lumber for warping, cracks, or insect damage, (e) check tile boxes for breakage (shake test — rattling indicates broken tiles inside) | Receiving Clerk | Department Supervisor | 5 min/delivery |
| 3 | **Accept**: Clerk processes GR in system; inventory updated; driver signs delivery receipt | Receiving Clerk | — | 2 min |
| 4 | **Partial Reject**: Clerk records damaged/defective items with photo; processes GR for accepted quantity only; rejected items returned on driver's truck; system creates discrepancy record for AP matching per W7 | Receiving Clerk | Department Supervisor | 5 min |
| 5 | **Full Reject**: Clerk refuses delivery; records rejection reason with photos; notifies Buyer; system creates rejection record; vendor must reschedule delivery | Receiving Clerk | Store Manager | 5 min |
| 6 | Weekly: Store Manager reviews DSD rejection rate; stores with rejection rate > 5% flagged for Buyer investigation of vendor delivery quality | Store Manager | VP Merchandising | 15 min/week |

### System Touchpoints
- PO/ASN lookup at store receiving (W1601.1)
- Store-level GR with partial reject capability (W1601.4)
- Photo capture for rejected items (W1601.4–5)
- DSD rejection rate dashboard by store, vendor, category (W1601.6)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Driver pressure**: Vendor drivers may pressure Receiving Clerks to accept damaged goods to avoid return trip; Store Manager backup needed for disputes
- **Cement quality**: DSD cement (bags) is highly susceptible to moisture damage during transport; rainy season increases rejection rate significantly
- **Lumber grading**: Store-level staff may lack expertise to assess lumber grade/quality vs. what was ordered


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "checks delivery against expected PO" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-inspect of "inspection of packaging integrity" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-update of "updated" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-334 (ensure controlled execution — Incoming Quality Inspection & Control (PA-31.1).)
- operational: Receiving Clerk review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
10–15 min per DSD delivery; ~2–3 per store per month; ~0.5–1 hour/store/month

## W1602. Import Shipment Pre-Delivery Inspection (PDI)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Import container arrives at Philippine port; customs clearance pending or completed |
| **Frequency** | ~400–600 TEUs/month |
| **Volume** | ~100–150 TEUs/week requiring PDI |
| **Owner** | Supply Chain Manager — Imports |
| **Participants** | Import Coordinator, Customs Broker, DC Quality Inspector, Buyer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Import Coordinator receives container arrival notice from shipping line; schedules PDI at port/warehouse or at DC depending on customs status | Import Coordinator | Supply Chain Manager | 30 min/container |
| 2 | DC Quality Inspector performs PDI at container opening: (a) container condition check (seal integrity, water ingress, pest evidence, temperature log if applicable), (b) damage assessment of visible outer cartons, (c) count check of pallets/cartons against packing list, (d) random sampling of inner contents (open 3–5 cartons per container for detailed inspection), (e) photograph container condition and any damage | DC Quality Inspector | Supply Chain Manager | 1–2 hours/container |
| 3 | **Pass**: Inspector signs PDI report; container released to DC for standard GR per W18 | DC Quality Inspector | Supply Chain Manager | 15 min |
| 4 | **Fail — Damage**: Inspector documents damage with photos; notifies Import Coordinator; initiates cargo insurance claim per W26.3/W837; salvageable goods processed at discounted valuation; damaged goods dispositioned per W91 | DC Quality Inspector | Supply Chain Manager / Insurance Officer | 30 min |
| 5 | **Fail — Quality**: Inspector documents quality defects; notifies Buyer; Buyer contacts vendor for remediation (replacement shipment, credit note, price concession); defective goods quarantined pending vendor response | DC Quality Inspector / Buyer | Supply Chain Manager / Category Manager | 1 hour |
| 6 | Import Coordinator files carrier claim for transit damage if carrier liability established; tracks claim through to settlement | Import Coordinator | Supply Chain Manager | 30 min |
| 7 | Monthly: Supply Chain Manager reviews import PDI report — damage rate by origin port, carrier, and vendor; feeds into carrier selection and vendor quality evaluation | Supply Chain Manager | VP Supply Chain | 1 hour/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Container arrival notification and PDI scheduling (W1602.1)
- PDI report template with container condition checklist (W1602.2)
- Cargo insurance claim initiation linked to PDI report (W1602.4)
- Vendor/carrier quality scorecard update (W1602.5–7)
- Import PDI dashboard with damage rate analytics (W1602.7)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Port congestion**: Philippine ports (Manila, Cebu) experience chronic congestion; PDI scheduling is unpredictable and delays may expose goods to additional damage
- **Container seal tampering**: Hard to detect sophisticated seal tampering at port; discrepancy may only be discovered at DC unloading
- **Insurance claim complexity**: Cargo insurance claims require extensive documentation (survey report, commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading); incomplete documentation delays settlement


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-schedule of "schedules PDI at port/warehouse or at DC depending on customs" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "log if applicable" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-inspect of "Inspector signs PDI report" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-229 (ensure supplier quality & product-safety verification.)
- operational: Supply Chain Manager — Imports review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
1–2 hours per container PDI; ~500 containers/month = ~750–1,000 hours/month; requires dedicated inspection team at major ports

## W1603. Batch / Lot Quality Hold & Release

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quality inspection failure (W1600, W1601, W1602) or external notification (vendor recall notice, customer complaint pattern) |
| **Frequency** | ~20–30 quality holds/month across all DCs and stores |
| **Volume** | Variable — from single pallet to full container lot |
| **Owner** | DC Supervisor (DC holds); Store Manager (store holds) |
| **Participants** | DC Quality Inspector, Department Supervisor, Buyer, Category Manager, Compliance Officer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System or user places inventory batch/lot in "Quality Hold" status — inventory blocked from sale, allocation, transfer, and ATP calculation; physical items moved to quarantine zone | DC Quality Inspector / Department Supervisor | DC Supervisor / Store Manager | 10 min |
| 2 | Quality Hold record created: hold reason (inspection failure, customer complaint pattern, vendor notification, regulatory alert), affected items (SKU, lot/batch number, quantity, locations), hold date, initiating source | System / DC Quality Inspector | DC Supervisor | 5 min |
| 3 | Investigation: (a) DC Quality Inspector or Department Supervisor reviews quality data for the lot, (b) checks if affected lot is at multiple locations (DCs + stores), (c) if multi-location: system identifies all locations holding the affected lot/batch for potential cascade hold, (d) contacts vendor for technical assessment if root cause unclear | DC Quality Inspector / Buyer | DC Supervisor / Category Manager | 1–4 hours |
| 4 | **Release — Pass**: Investigation confirms quality acceptable; Inspector releases hold; inventory returns to available status; hold record closed with resolution notes | DC Quality Inspector | DC Supervisor | 5 min |
| 5 | **Release — Restricted**: Items pass secondary inspection but with restrictions (e.g., sell at specific discount with disclosure, restrict to non-critical applications); system applies restricted status with conditions | DC Quality Inspector / Category Manager | DC Supervisor | 10 min |
| 6 | **Reject**: Items confirmed defective/unsafe; route to disposition per W91 — RTV (W88/W1280), scrap (W92), or recall (W1610–W1614 per PA-31.3) | DC Quality Inspector / Buyer | DC Supervisor / Category Manager | 15 min |
| 7 | Quality Hold aging report: system alerts DC Supervisor for any hold > 48 hours without resolution; weekly aging review to prevent indefinite holds that consume warehouse space and working capital | System / DC Supervisor | VP Supply Chain | Automated + 15 min/week |


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "Quality Hold record created" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews quality data for the lot" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-inspect of "Inspector releases hold" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-334 (ensure controlled execution — Incoming Quality Inspection & Control (PA-31.1).)
- operational: DC Supervisor (DC holds); Store Manager (store holds) review and approval gate

### System Touchpoints
- Quality Hold inventory status with full blocking rules (W1603.1)
- Multi-location lot/batch traceability to identify all affected inventory (W1603.3)
- Hold record with lifecycle tracking (open → investigated → released/rejected) (W1603.2–6)
- Hold aging alert and escalation (W1603.7)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Cascade holds across 200 stores**: If a defective lot was already distributed to stores, placing holds at all 200 locations simultaneously is operationally disruptive; requires rapid communication and store-level execution
- **Hold aging**: Holds that sit unresolved consume warehouse space and lock working capital; 48-hour SLA is aggressive for complex vendor negotiations
- **Customer impact**: If hold is placed after items already sold, customer notification and potential recall is needed per W1610

### Time Estimate
1.75–4.75 hours per occurrence (per-step effort 10 min + 5 min + 1–4 hours + 5 min + 10 min + 15 min) — at ~20–30 quality holds/month across all DCs and stores ≈ 240–360 occurrences/year ≈ 420–1,710 hours/year.

## W1604. AQL Sampling Plan Configuration & Category Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New SKU category created, vendor quality issue identified, or annual quality policy review |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly review; ad-hoc updates as needed |
| **Volume** | ~13 product categories with distinct sampling plans |
| **Owner** | Quality Manager (proposed new role under VP Supply Chain) |
| **Participants** | Quality Manager, Category Managers, DC Supervisor, VP Supply Chain |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Quality Manager reviews AQL sampling parameters per category: (a) inspection level (I, II, III, or S-1 through S-4 per ISO 2859-1), (b) AQL limit (% defective acceptable: Critical 0, Major 1.0–2.5, Minor 2.5–6.5), (c) sampling size based on lot size and inspection level, (d) accept/reject numbers per sample size | Quality Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2 hours/quarter |
| 2 | Category-specific configurations: (a) Electrical — 100% certification check (PS/ICC mark), functional test on sample, (b) Paint/Chemical — batch/lot verification, shelf life check, FPA registration, (c) Power Tools — functional test sample, safety guard check, (d) Tiles — dimensional tolerance check, breakage rate, (e) Lumber — moisture content spot check, dimensional check, grading verification, (f) Hardware/Fasteners — dimension, material, and finish check on sample | Quality Manager | Category Managers | 4 hours/quarter |
| 3 | System updates AQL configuration; new parameters applied to next quality inspection event; historical parameters retained for audit trail | Quality Manager / System | VP Supply Chain | 1 hour |
| 4 | Annual: Quality Manager reviews sampling effectiveness — compare inspection pass rates to actual customer complaint and return rates per category; adjust sampling stringency if gap detected | Quality Manager | VP Supply Chain | 4 hours/year |


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "reviews AQL sampling parameters per category" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-validate of "check" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-inspect of "System updates AQL configuration" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-334 (ensure controlled execution — Incoming Quality Inspection & Control (PA-31.1).)
- operational: Quality Manager (proposed new role under VP Supply Chain) review and approval gate

### System Touchpoints
- AQL configuration module per SKU category with ISO 2859-1 sampling tables (W1604.1)
- Category-specific inspection checklists (W1604.2)
- Audit trail for configuration changes (W1604.3)
- Sampling effectiveness analytics (W1604.4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Over-inspection**: Aggressive AQL levels on low-risk categories slow DC throughput without proportional quality benefit
- **Under-inspection**: Relaxed AQL levels may miss systematic vendor quality deterioration until customer complaints surface
- **Standard maintenance**: ISO 2859-1 sampling tables are complex; incorrect configuration leads to wrong sample sizes

### Time Estimate
Quarterly configuration review 7 hours (AQL parameter review 2 hours + category-specific configuration 4 hours + system update 1 hour) ≈ 28 hours/year; annual sampling-effectiveness review 4 hours across the ~13 product categories.

## W1605. Paint & Chemical Product Quality Verification

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Paint/chemical goods receipt at DC or store; or periodic shelf-life audit |
| **Frequency** | ~200–300 paint/chemical GRs/month; monthly shelf-life audit |
| **Volume** | ~2,800 active SKUs in Paint & Finishes category |
| **Owner** | DC Quality Inspector (DC); Department Supervisor (store) |
| **Participants** | DC Quality Inspector, Department Supervisor, Buyer, Compliance Officer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | At GR: verify (a) FPA (Fertilizer and Pesticide Authority) registration for applicable products, (b) DENR Environmental Compliance Certificate (ECC) reference for restricted chemicals, (c) batch/lot number recorded in system, (d) manufacturing date and shelf life check (paint: 24–36 months from manufacture; adhesives: 12–18 months), (e) packaging integrity (no leaking, bulging, or dented containers) | DC Quality Inspector / Receiving Clerk | DC Supervisor | 10 min/lot |
| 2 | Spot test: (a) paint viscosity check (visual — no excessive thickening or separation), (b) colorant compatibility verification for tinting bases, (c) solvent/chemical flash point documentation check for hazmat storage compliance | DC Quality Inspector | DC Supervisor | 5 min/lot |
| 3 | Shelf-life audit (monthly): system generates list of paint/chemical inventory approaching expiry (within 6 months); Department Supervisor or Stock Associate checks physical condition of flagged items; items within 3 months of expiry flagged for markdown (W93); items past expiry quarantined for disposal per W82 (hazmat) | Department Supervisor / Stock Associate | Store Manager / DC Supervisor | 2 hours/location/month |
| 4 | Buyer notified of quality issues; vendor-specific trend tracked; repeated quality failures trigger vendor quality audit per W1608 | Buyer | Category Manager | As needed |


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "recorded in system" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-document of "documentation check for hazmat storage compliance" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "checks physical condition of flagged items" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-334 (ensure controlled execution — Incoming Quality Inspection & Control (PA-31.1).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### System Touchpoints
- Shelf-life tracking with batch/lot manufacturing date (W1605.3)
- Expiry alerting and markdown triggering (W1605.3)
- Hazmat disposal integration (W82)
- FPA/DENR certification tracking per vendor/SKU (W1605.1)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Philippine heat/humidity**: Accelerates paint and adhesive degradation; shelf life in actual Philippine warehouse conditions may be 20–30% shorter than manufacturer specification
- **Colorant contamination**: Contaminated colorant batches affect paint mixing quality (W14), leading to customer complaints and re-mixing costs
- **Hazmat disposal cost**: Expired paint/chemicals cannot be disposed through normal waste channels; DENR-compliant disposal is expensive (~PHP 50–100/kg)

### Time Estimate
At-GR checks 15 min/lot (FPA/registration verification 10 min + spot test 5 min) × ~200–300 paint/chemical GRs/month ≈ 600–900 hours/year; monthly shelf-life audit 2 hours/location/month across paint-carrying locations ≈ 4,900 hours/year at ~205 locations (predominantly store floor labor); buyer quality-issue notifications as needed.

## W1606. Electrical Product Safety Verification & Certification Check

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Electrical product goods receipt at DC |
| **Frequency** | ~150–200 electrical product GRs/month (~3,500 active SKUs) |
| **Volume** | Focus on wiring, breakers, switches, panels — safety-critical items |
| **Owner** | DC Quality Inspector |
| **Participants** | DC Quality Inspector, Buyer, Compliance Officer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | At GR: verify (a) PS (Philippine Standard) mark or ICC (Import Commodity Clearance) sticker on product and packaging, (b) DTI-BPS certification reference in vendor documentation, (c) product labeling compliance (voltage rating, wattage, country of origin, importer information), (d) for import electrical items: Bureau of Customs import clearance and BPS test report | DC Quality Inspector | DC Supervisor | 10 min/lot |
| 2 | Functional spot test on sample: (a) wire: conductor count, insulation thickness visual check, (b) breakers/switches: physical integrity, no cracks or damage, (c) continuity test on sample wires using multimeter | DC Quality Inspector | DC Supervisor | 10 min/lot |
| 3 | Non-compliant items: (a) items missing PS/ICC marks placed in Quality Hold per W1603, (b) Buyer notified to contact vendor for certification documentation or product replacement, (c) items without valid certification cannot be released for sale — BuildRight liability for selling uncertified electrical products per Philippine Consumer Act (RA 7394) and DTI regulations | DC Quality Inspector / Buyer | DC Supervisor / Compliance Officer | 15 min |


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-document of "documentation" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-validate of "Functional spot test on sample" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System workflow notification of "contact vendor for certification documentation or product replacement," (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-229 (ensure supplier quality & product-safety verification.)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### System Touchpoints
- PS/ICC certification tracking per vendor/SKU in product master (W1606.1)
- Quality Hold for non-compliant electrical items (W1603)
- Vendor certification expiry alerting (W1606.1)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Counterfeit certifications**: PS/ICC marks can be forged; verification against DTI-BPS database recommended
- **Import clearance delays**: Electrical items without BPS clearance may be held at customs, delaying GR
- **Liability exposure**: Selling uncertified electrical products creates significant liability for fire/electrocution incidents

### Time Estimate
At-GR checks 20 min/lot (PS/ICC mark verification 10 min + functional spot test 10 min) × ~150–200 electrical GRs/month ≈ 600–800 hours/year; non-compliant lots add 15 min each for quality-hold processing per W1603.

## W1607. Lumber & Building Materials Dimensional & Structural Quality Check

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Lumber/building materials goods receipt at DC or store (DSD) |
| **Frequency** | ~100–150 lumber/building materials GRs/month (high-value, bulky items) |
| **Volume** | ~4,900 active SKUs in Lumber & Building Materials category |
| **Owner** | DC Quality Inspector (DC); Receiving Clerk (store DSD) |
| **Participants** | DC Quality Inspector, Receiving Clerk, Department Supervisor, Buyer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | At GR (DC): verify (a) species/grade match PO specification (e.g., Tanguile, Apitong, Yakal — Philippine commercial timber species), (b) dimensional check on sample boards (length, width, thickness against tolerance: ±3mm for dimensional lumber), (c) moisture content spot check using pin-type moisture meter (target: < 20% for air-dried; < 15% for kiln-dried), (d) visual check for defects: cracks, knots exceeding grade limits, warping, insect damage, fungal growth | DC Quality Inspector | DC Supervisor | 15 min/lot |
| 2 | At store DSD: Receiving Clerk performs visual check for obvious defects (cracked boards, broken hollow blocks, damaged cement bags); dimensional spot check on sample | Receiving Clerk | Department Supervisor | 5 min/delivery |
| 3 | Cement quality: (a) check for lumps (indicates moisture exposure — hardened cement is unusable), (b) check manufacturing date (cement shelf life ~3 months from manufacture per Philippine standard), (c) check bag integrity (no tears, moisture staining) | Receiving Clerk | Department Supervisor | 3 min/delivery |
| 4 | Steel/rebar: (a) check diameter against specification using caliper on sample, (b) check for rust (surface rust acceptable; deep pitting is reject), (c) check length per piece, (d) check mill test certificate availability per lot | DC Quality Inspector | DC Supervisor | 10 min/lot |


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "check on sample boards" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-test of "test certificate availability per lot" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-334 (ensure controlled execution — Incoming Quality Inspection & Control (PA-31.1).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### System Touchpoints
- Dimensional tolerance parameters per SKU in item master (W1607.1)
- Moisture content recording against SKU threshold (W1607.1)
- Cement manufacturing date tracking for shelf-life management (W1607.3)
- Mill test certificate attachment to GR record (W1607.4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Moisture content variability**: Philippine tropical humidity makes consistent moisture targets difficult; lumber stored in outdoor yard areas is particularly susceptible
- **Grade substitution**: Vendors may substitute lower-grade lumber for specified grade; requires trained inspector to detect
- **Cement freshness**: 3-month shelf life in tropical conditions is aggressive; DC and store inventory rotation must be strictly managed (FEFO)
- **Rebar rust**: Surface rust is normal for outdoor storage but distinguishing acceptable surface rust from structural degradation requires experience

### Time Estimate
DC at-GR inspection 25 min/lot (lumber/building materials grade verification 15 min + steel/rebar checks 10 min) × ~100–150 GRs/month ≈ 500–750 hours/year; store DSD visual checks ~8 min/delivery (visual defect check 5 min + cement lump check 3 min) across ~500–600 monthly DSD receipts ≈ 800–960 hours/year of receiving-clerk time.
