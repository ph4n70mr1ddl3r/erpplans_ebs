### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |

| 2 | **L/C Opening (if applicable)**: Finance coordinates with Bank to open Letter of Credit; sends L/C to vendor bank | Finance (Treasury) | CFO | 2–3 days |

| 4 | **Shipping Documents**: Receive set of documents: Bill of Lading (B/L), Commercial Invoice, Packing List, Certificate of Origin (for FTA benefits like ATIGA/ACFTA) | Import Coordinator, Import Documentation Specialist | — | 1 day |

### System Touchpoints
- Import Shipment Tracker (Container level)
- Landed Cost Module (aggregating multiple invoices to a single PO line)
- Integration with BOC E2M (if via API) or manual entry of entry numbers
- Demurrage/Detention alert system based on "Last Free Day"

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Philippine port congestion**: Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) and South Harbor experience chronic congestion, especially during pre-Christmas peak (August–October); container dwell times can double, triggering demurrage charges (W249) and delaying DC replenishment
- **Customs clearance unpredictability**: BOC E2M system processing times vary widely; non-compliant documentation (incorrect HS code, missing Certificate of Origin) can add 3–7 days of clearance delay per shipment
- **Foreign exchange exposure**: Import POs are denominated in USD or CNY while revenue is in PHP; the 4–8 week shipment cycle exposes BuildRight to significant FX variance between PO creation and actual payment
- **Container damage and pilferage risk**: Long transit times and multiple handling points (origin port, transshipment, destination port, haulage) increase the risk of cargo damage; inadequate container seal verification at DC receiving can mask pilferage; mitigated by CTL-244 (controlled execution — Import & Customs Operations (PA-02.2))

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |

| 6 | **Dispute / Payment**: If charges valid, approve payment to avoid port hold; if invalid, initiate dispute via shipping line portal with log evidence. | Finance Specialist | Logistics Mgr | 2 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- Port Authority vessel tracking API integration
- Automated demurrage/detention free-time calculations and system alerting
- Hauler empty container gate-in/gate-out logs tracking
- Landed cost integration for port penalty allocations (W2B/W13)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Demurrage cost escalation**: Philippine port free time is typically only 5 days; customs clearance delays of even 1–2 days (common during BOC system outages or document queries) can trigger PHP 5,000–15,000/day demurrage charges per container, adding 2–5% to landed cost
- **Shipping line detention fee opacity**: Container detention fees (charged by shipping lines for late empty return) are calculated on different schedules than port demurrage; the dual-clock system is confusing and shipping line invoices are difficult to audit without precise gate-in/gate-out timestamps
- **Weekend and holiday compounding**: Philippine holidays (average 18 national holidays/year plus local LGU holidays) reduce effective working days for clearance and haulage; containers arriving mid-week before a long weekend are especially vulnerable to demurrage
- **Dispute resolution leverage**: BuildRight has limited negotiating power against major shipping lines (Maersk, CMA CGM, COSCO) when disputing demurrage charges; disputes are time-consuming and shipping lines may hold subsequent container releases pending payment

## W728. Port & Customs Clearance Daily Status Tracking & Escalation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Start of supply chain operations day; or customs/broker status update |
| **Frequency** | Daily review; real-time status updates from customs broker |
| **Volume** | ~400–600 TEUs/month import containers; ~15–25 containers in clearance pipeline at any time |
| **Owner** | Import Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Customs Broker, Logistics Manager, Finance (LC/duty payments), Warehouse Manager |

### Background

W144 covers international logistics and import operations at the strategic level. W464 covers in-house customs brokerage operations. W249 covers port demurrage management. This workflow addresses the daily operational tracking of import containers through the Philippine Bureau of Customs (BOC) clearance process — the critical path for ~40% of BuildRight's COGS. Philippine port clearance is notoriously complex (average 5-10 days from vessel arrival to gate-out) with multiple handoff points (discharge → customs examination → assessment → payment → release → haulage). Daily status tracking enables early identification of bottlenecks and proactive escalation to prevent demurrage and stockouts.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Morning import pipeline status review**: Import Coordinator reviews import pipeline dashboard: (a) vessels expected to arrive in next 7 days: vessel name, ETA, number of BuildRight containers, port of discharge (Manila South Harbor, Manila North Harbor, Cebu, Davao, Clark); (b) containers currently at port: days since vessel arrival, current clearance status (discharged, under customs examination, assessed, payment pending, released for haulage, gated out); (c) containers in transit to DC: estimated arrival at DC per W250 tracking; (d) containers exceeding standard clearance timeline (>7 days from discharge): flag as potential demurrage risk per W249 | Import Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 20-30 min/day |
| 2 | **Customs broker status update**: Import Coordinator obtains daily status from customs broker: (a) for each container in clearance pipeline: (i) current status and next step; (ii) expected timeline for next milestone; (iii) any issues: missing documentation, customs hold, examination required, assessment dispute, BOC system downtime; (b) for containers requiring customs examination: (i) examination type: green lane (no exam), yellow lane (document exam), red lane (physical exam); (ii) red lane: coordinate with broker for examination scheduling, prepare supporting documentation; (iii) estimate additional clearance time for examinations; (c) document all status updates in tracking system with timestamps | Import Coordinator / Customs Broker | Logistics Manager | 15-30 min/day |
| 3 | **Escalation management**: for containers stuck at specific stages: (a) stuck at discharge (>2 days): contact shipping line for container availability; (b) stuck awaiting customs examination (>3 days): escalate to broker for examination scheduling; contact BOC if broker unable to resolve; (c) stuck at assessment (>2 days): verify all documentation submitted; escalate duty assessment disputes per W239; (d) stuck at payment: coordinate with Finance for immediate duty payment via IPAY/Pass5 per W239 step 1; (e) stuck at release (>1 day after payment): escalate to broker for gate-out coordination; (f) for each escalation: document actions taken and timeline for resolution | Import Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 15-30 min/day |
| 4 | **Supply chain impact assessment**: Import Coordinator communicates clearance timeline impact: (a) for containers carrying critical stock (A-items, promotional items per W57): notify Supply Chain Planner of expected DC receipt date; (b) assess impact on store replenishment: will clearance delays cause stockouts at stores?; (c) for promotional items: alert Merchandising if promotional launch per W13 may be affected by delayed import receipt; (d) for seasonal items per W32: assess whether seasonal buy will arrive in time for seasonal window | Import Coordinator / Supply Chain Planner | VP Supply Chain | 10-15 min/day |
| 5 | **Demurrage and storage cost monitoring**: daily, Import Coordinator tracks cost exposure: (a) containers exceeding free time at port (typically 5-7 days free, then PHP 5,000-8,000/day demurrage): calculate daily cost exposure; (b) total monthly demurrage cost vs. budget; (c) identify root causes of demurrage incidents: documentation delays, customs holds, payment delays, carrier no-show for haulage; (d) demurrage cost data feeds into W680 supply chain cost analysis | Import Coordinator | Finance Manager | 10 min/day |

### System Touchpoints

- Import container tracking system with BOC status integration
- Customs broker communication portal with status update workflow
- Shipping line tracking integration for vessel ETA and container availability
- W250 supply chain control tower for in-transit visibility
- W249 demurrage management for cost tracking
- Finance integration for duty payment processing per W239
- Supply chain planning module for stock impact assessment
- W680 supply chain cost analysis for demurrage cost analytics

### Pain Points / Risks

- **BOC system downtime**: the Bureau of Customs electronic system (e2m) frequently experiences downtime, halting the entire clearance process with no workaround; BuildRight has no control over this
- **Red lane examination randomness**: BOC randomly selects containers for physical examination (red lane), adding 3-7 days to clearance time unpredictably; cannot be anticipated in supply planning
- **Port congestion**: Manila ports (South Harbor, North Harbor) regularly experience congestion, especially during peak import seasons (pre-Christmas, pre-back-to-school), extending vessel waiting time to 3-5 days before discharge
- **Customs broker capacity constraints**: during peak periods, customs brokers are overloaded with multiple clients, slowing document processing and examination coordination for BuildRight containers
- **Document accuracy dependency**: a single error in customs documentation (wrong HS code, missing certificate of origin, incorrect declared value) can trigger examination, assessment dispute, or rejection, adding 5-15 days to clearance


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "Morning import pipeline status review" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-document of "documentation, customs hold, examination required, assessment dispute, BOC…" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "verify all documentation submitted" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-244 (ensure controlled execution — Import & Customs Operations (PA-02.2).)
- operational: Import Coordinator review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication

Import Coordinator: ~1–2 hours/day on import pipeline tracking. Customs Broker: external service, cost absorbed in brokerage fees. Finance: ~15–30 min/day on duty payment processing. Absorbed within existing team. No incremental headcount.

### Time Estimate

**Total daily**: ~70–115 min (20-30 min pipeline review + 15-30 min broker update + 15-30 min escalation + 10-15 min impact assessment + 10 min demurrage tracking).

## W1233. Philippine Bureau of Customs Import Entry Processing & Duty Assessment Automation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Import container arrival at Philippine port (Manila, Cebu, Davao, or Subic/Clark) |
| **Frequency** | ~400–600 TEUs/month; ~15–30 containers/working day |
| **Volume** | ~1,800–3,600 import entries/year across all import vendors (multiple TEUs consolidated per entry per W464) |
| **Owner** | Customs Broker / Import Specialist |
| **Participants** | Customs Broker, Import Specialist, Category Manager, Finance – Tax, Freight Forwarder, Bureau of Customs |

### Background

BuildRight Depot imports ~40% of COGS (PHP 1.4B/month) from China, Taiwan, Indonesia, Malaysia, Japan, and Europe. Each import shipment requires a formal customs entry with the Philippine Bureau of Customs (BOC): tariff classification (AHTN/HS codes), duty rate determination, VAT assessment (12%), excise tax (if applicable), and customs value declaration (CIF — Cost, Insurance, Freight). With ~400–600 TEUs/month, manual entry processing is labor-intensive and error-prone. Common errors include: incorrect HS code classification leading to over/under-payment of duties, misdeclared customs value triggering BOC audit, missing documents (commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin) delaying release, and failure to claim preferential tariff rates under ASEAN FTAs (ATIGA, ACFTA). Errors result in surcharges of 250% of duties/taxes due for negligence (up to 500% for fraud) per CMTA (RA 10863) Sec. 2102, shipment delays (demurrage per W249), and potential smuggling charges. Automating the entry processing and duty assessment reduces errors, speeds clearance, and ensures accurate landed cost calculation for inventory valuation per WAC.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Shipment pre-arrival preparation**: Import Specialist receives advance shipping documents from vendor/freight forwarder: (a) commercial invoice, (b) packing list, (c) bill of lading (B/L) or airway bill, (d) certificate of origin (if FTA claim), (e) import permit or clearance (for regulated items per W467) | Import Specialist | Customs Broker | 1–2 hours/entry |
| 2 | **Automated HS code classification**: ERP system auto-matches item SKUs to HS codes from item master (maintained per W293 tax master): (a) system validates HS code against BOC latest tariff schedule; (b) system checks for preferential tariff eligibility (ASEAN, ACFTA); (c) system flags items with classification ambiguity for Customs Broker review; (d) system calculates estimated duty: duty amount = CIF value × duty rate (0–30% depending on HS chapter) | System / Import Specialist | Customs Broker | 15 min/entry |
| 3 | **Customs value declaration preparation**: (a) system calculates CIF value: invoice value + freight + insurance; (b) add dutiable ancillary costs (royalties, assists, packaging); (c) deduct non-dutiable items where applicable; (d) generate customs value declaration worksheet with supporting documentation | Import Specialist | Customs Broker | 30 min/entry |
| 4 | **BOC e2m (Electronic-to-Mobile) system filing**: Customs Broker files import entry via BOC e2m system: (a) select entry type (formal consumption, informal, warehousing per W284); (b) upload HS classifications, quantities, and values; (c) attach supporting documents electronically; (d) system auto-calculates duties, VAT, and other charges; (e) select payment mode (direct debit via AAB, or cash/manager's check) | Customs Broker | — | 30–60 min/entry |
| 5 | **Duty payment & assessment**: (a) BOC assesses entry; system calculates total duty liability; (b) Customs Broker authorizes payment via accredited bank (BDO/BPI); (c) BOC issues Single Administrative Document (SAD) with assessment; (d) for select shipments: BOC Green Lane (auto-release), Yellow Lane (document check), or Red Lane (physical inspection) | Customs Broker | — | 1–4 hours |
| 6 | **Cargo release & delivery order**: (a) once assessed and duty paid: BOC releases cargo; (b) Customs Broker obtains delivery order from shipping line; (c) arranges trucking from port to DC per W464; (d) system updates import PO status to "Customs Cleared" with duty and landed cost details | Customs Broker / Import Specialist | — | 2–4 hours |
| 7 | **Landed cost posting & WAC update**: (a) system calculates final landed cost per SKU: invoice cost + freight + insurance + duty + port charges + brokerage fees; (b) system posts landed cost to inventory receipt per W3; (c) WAC recalculated for received quantity; (d) duty and VAT posted to appropriate GL accounts per W90 | System / Finance – Tax | Finance Manager | Automated + 15 min review |
| 8 | **Monthly duty reconciliation**: Finance – Tax reconciles monthly: (a) total duties paid vs. BOC assessment; (b) VAT paid at customs vs. input VAT claimed on BIR 2550M; (c) FTA preferential duty savings vs. standard rates; (d) identify classification discrepancies for correction | Finance – Tax | Customs Broker | 4–6 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints

- W464 in-house customs brokerage for port operations coordination
- W284 customs bonded warehouse for duty-deferred imports
- W249 demurrage & detention for port storage cost management
- W3 goods receipt for landed cost posting and WAC calculation
- W90 tax filing for BIR input VAT reconciliation
- W293 tax master for HS code and tariff rate governance
- W2B import PO management for shipment tracking
- W239 customs duty reconciliation for monthly duty audit
- W144 import PO management for container tracking

### Pain Points / Risks

- **HS code misclassification risk**: incorrect HS code leads to either overpayment of duties (reducing margin) or underpayment (triggering BOC audit, penalties of 40–100%, and potential blacklisting); automated classification reduces but does not eliminate this risk for new or unusual items; mitigated by CTL-244 (controlled execution — Import & Customs Operations (PA-02.2))
- **BOC system downtime** — the BOC e2m system experiences periodic downtime; during peak clearance periods, delays cascade into demurrage charges per W249
- **Red lane inspection delays** — physical inspections (Red Lane) add 2–5 days to clearance; no advance warning; contingency plans must account for variable lead times in replenishment planning per W31
- **FTA utilization gaps** — failure to obtain valid certificate of origin from vendors results in paying standard duty rates instead of preferential (often 0%) ASEAN rates; BuildRight may leave significant savings unclaimed
- **Exchange rate volatility** — customs value is declared in PHP (converting from USD/CNY); exchange rate fluctuations between PO date and customs entry date create landed cost variance


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-document of "documents from vendor/freight forwarder" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "Automated HS code classification" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-calculate of "calculates CIF value" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-244 (ensure controlled execution — Import & Customs Operations (PA-02.2).)

### Staffing Implication

- 1 dedicated Customs Broker (licensed) + 2 Import Specialists: absorbed within existing Supply Chain team. Finance – Tax: ~6 hours/month on duty reconciliation. No incremental headcount.

### Time Estimate

- Per entry: 3–6 hours from document receipt to BOC filing. Monthly: ~150–300 entries (multiple TEUs per entry per W464) × 4 hours avg = ~600–1,200 hours/month (shared across Customs Broker, Import Specialists, and the brokerage partner). Duty reconciliation: ~6 hours/month.

## W1264. Import Container Consolidation, Multi-Vendor FCL Assembly & Space Optimization

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — weekly container consolidation cycle; triggered when multiple LCL (less-than-container-load) import POs from the same origin region can be consolidated into FCL (full container load) |
| **Frequency** | Weekly consolidation planning; ~400–600 TEUs/month across all import origins |
| **Volume** | ~20–30 container bookings per week; ~60% currently FCL, ~40% LCL — target: reduce LCL to 20% through consolidation |
| **Owner** | Import Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Buyer, Freight Forwarder, Customs Broker, DC Operations Manager, Finance Controller |

### Background

BuildRight Depot imports ~40% of COGS from international vendors in China, Taiwan, Indonesia, Malaysia, Japan, and Europe, representing ~400–600 TEUs (twenty-foot equivalent units) per month. Import procurement generates a ~40% share of the ~1,200 monthly merchandise POs (the ~400 international vendors of the §6.5 base), many of which are individually too small to fill a container (LCL — less than container load). LCL shipments cost 30–50% more per CBM than FCL (full container load) because: (a) LCL freight rate per CBM is higher, (b) LCL requires additional handling (consolidation/deconsolidation at CFS — container freight station), (c) LCL has longer transit time (7–14 days additional for consolidation), (d) LCL has higher damage risk from additional handling. This workflow systematically consolidates multiple vendor POs from the same origin region into FCL shipments to: (i) reduce per-unit import freight cost by 20–35%, (ii) reduce transit time by consolidating into direct vessel bookings, (iii) reduce damage claims from fewer handling touches, (iv) improve container utilization rate (target: > 85% CBM utilization per container).

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Weekly consolidation planning**: Import Coordinator reviews all open import POs: (a) group POs by origin port/region (South China, East China, Taiwan, Southeast Asia, Europe); (b) for each region, calculate total CBM and weight of all open POs; (c) identify consolidation opportunities: (i) if total CBM for a region ≥ 28 CBM (FCL 20ft) or ≥ 58 CBM (FCL 40ft), consolidate into FCL; (ii) if near FCL threshold, check with Buyer for additional POs that could be pulled forward to fill the container; (iii) if below threshold, evaluate holding POs for 1 week to accumulate more volume vs. shipping LCL to meet DC delivery schedule | Import Coordinator | Procurement Manager | 4 hours/week |
| 2 | **Multi-vendor FCL assembly**: For consolidated FCL shipments: (a) coordinate with freight forwarder to arrange vendor factory delivery to consolidation warehouse at origin; (b) communicate required delivery date to each vendor — all PO goods must arrive at consolidation warehouse by cut-off date; (c) freight forwarder receives goods from multiple vendors, inspects quantity and packaging, and loads into single container; (d) system generates consolidated packing list with per-vendor breakdown and per-PO breakdown; (e) verify container loading plan: heavy items at bottom, fragile items protected, maximum CBM utilization | Import Coordinator / Freight Forwarder | Procurement Manager | 3 hours per consolidation |
| 3 | **Rate negotiation & booking**: Import Coordinator negotiates FCL freight rate and books vessel space: (a) obtain FCL rates from 2–3 freight forwarders for the route; (b) compare FCL consolidated rate vs. sum of individual LCL rates — confirm consolidation savings; (c) book earliest available vessel with transit time meeting DC delivery schedule; (d) confirm container type: standard 20ft/40ft, high-cube (for voluminous but light items like insulation), or open-top (for oversized items); (e) issue booking confirmation to all vendors in the consolidation | Import Coordinator | Finance Controller | 2 hours per booking |
| 4 | **Consolidation cost allocation**: Finance team allocates consolidated freight cost to individual POs: (a) total freight cost = FCL rate + origin consolidation warehouse fee + documentation fee; (b) allocate to each PO proportionally by: (i) CBM (volumetric allocation), or (ii) weight (weight allocation), whichever produces the charge; (c) add allocated freight cost to each PO's landed cost calculation per W2B.12; (d) system updates PO landed cost estimate for accurate margin calculation | Finance Analyst | Finance Controller | 2 hours per consolidation |
| 5 | **Monthly consolidation performance review**: Import Coordinator reviews monthly consolidation metrics: (a) container utilization rate — average CBM per container vs. capacity; (b) FCL vs. LCL ratio — trend towards higher FCL percentage; (c) freight cost per CBM — compare consolidated vs. non-consolidated; (d) transit time comparison — consolidated FCL vs. LCL; (e) consolidation savings — total PHP saved per month from FCL consolidation vs. LCL alternative; (f) present results to Procurement Manager and Finance Controller | Import Coordinator | Procurement Manager | 4 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- **Procurement Module** — import PO management, vendor origin tracking, consolidation grouping
- **Freight Forwarder Portal** — consolidation warehouse management, container loading plan
- **Finance Module** — freight cost allocation, landed cost calculation, CBM-based allocation
- **BI Platform** — consolidation analytics, FCL/LCL ratio, freight cost benchmarking
- **WMS** — expected receipt planning for consolidated containers

### Pain Points / Risks
- Multi-vendor coordination requires all vendors to deliver to consolidation warehouse by the same cut-off date — delayed vendor holds up entire container
- Vendors may resist shipping to consolidation warehouse instead of direct to port — adds inland transport cost at origin
- Consolidated container customs declaration is more complex — must list all vendors and POs in single entry
- Container utilization depends on PO mix — mismatched item sizes (tiles + power tools) may leave wasted space
- If one vendor's goods fail QC at consolidation warehouse, container may ship incomplete or be delayed


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "reviews all open import POs" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-validate of "verify container loading plan" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-route of "Rate negotiation & booking" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-244 (ensure controlled execution — Import & Customs Operations (PA-02.2).)
- operational: Import Coordinator review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- Import Coordinator: 4 hours/week on consolidation planning
- Freight Forwarder: managed service (vendor cost)
- Finance Analyst: 2 hours per consolidation for cost allocation

### Time Estimate
- Weekly planning: 4 hours/week = 208 hours/year
- Per consolidation: 5 hours × ~15 consolidations/month = 75 hours/month
- Monthly review: 4 hours/month = 48 hours/year
- **Annual estimate: ~1,156 person-hours/year**
