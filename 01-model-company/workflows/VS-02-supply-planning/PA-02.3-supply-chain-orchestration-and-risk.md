## W183. Supply Chain Network Optimization Review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual strategic review; or significant change in logistics costs/fuel prices |
| **Frequency** | Annual full review; ad-hoc triggered by fuel price spikes > 15%, new store cluster openings, or DC lease renewal decisions |
| **Volume** | Covers all 200 stores, 4 DCs, and ~200 delivery routes across Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao |
| **Owner** | COO |
| **Time Estimate** | Data extraction: 1 day; Network modeling: 1 week; Cost analysis: 3 days; Recommendation and presentation: 2 hours; **Total**: ~2 weeks annual effort |
| **Participants** | VP Supply Chain, Logistics Manager, Finance Analyst, External Logistics Consultant |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Data Extraction**: Pull historical store sales, DC throughput, and transport costs by route | Finance Analyst | — | 1 day |
| 2 | **Modeling**: Use "Center of Gravity" and "Network Optimization" tools to simulate optimal DC locations vs. 200-store footprint | SC Director | — | 1 week |
| 3 | **Cost Analysis**: Compare current "4-DC" cost vs. potential "6-DC" or "Consolidated DC" scenarios; include lease exit costs (W117) | Logistics Mgr | CFO | 3 days |
| 4 | **Recommendation**: Present optimal network configuration (e.g., "Open DC6 in North Luzon") to Board | COO | CEO | 2 hours |
| 5 | **Implementation Plan**: If approved, trigger W116 (Site Selection) for the new DC | VP Supply Chain | — | — |

### System Touchpoints
- Big Data extraction from ERP (Sales + Inventory + Transport costs)
- Simulation/Integration with Logistics Optimization software
- Strategic decision support dashboards

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Data quality for modeling**: Store-level sales data, actual transport costs per route, and DC throughput data must be accurate and complete; Philippine logistics cost data (fuel, toll, driver wages) is volatile and modeling with stale data produces unreliable recommendations
- **DC lease commitment rigidity**: Existing DC leases (typically 3–5 year terms) with early exit penalties (W117) limit the financial viability of recommended network changes; an optimal 6-DC configuration may not be achievable until current leases expire
- **Inter-island logistics complexity**: Modeling optimal DC locations for an archipelagic network (Luzon, Visayas, Mindanao) requires incorporating RoRo ferry schedules, port fees, and inter-island shipping costs that change frequently and are difficult to forecast
- **Executive resistance to change**: Closing or relocating a DC disrupts existing vendor delivery patterns, employee commutes, and store replenishment cycles; the operational risk of network changes may cause leadership to defer action even when the business case is clear


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-route of "route" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-capture of "Center of Gravity" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System rule-based auto-approval of "approved, trigger W116" (replaces manual Step 5).

### Controls
- CTL-245 (ensure controlled execution — Supply Chain Orchestration & Risk (PA-02.3).)
- operational: COO review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
- Data extraction from ERP (step 1): 1 day (Finance Analyst pulls historical store sales, DC throughput, and transport costs by route for the full network)
- Network modeling and simulation (step 2): 1 week (VP Supply Chain and External Logistics Consultant run Center of Gravity and network optimization scenarios across 200 stores and 4 DCs; includes modeling 6-DC, 4-DC, and consolidated DC configurations)
- Cost analysis and comparison (step 3): 3 days (Logistics Manager compares current 4-DC cost baseline against alternative scenarios including lease exit costs per W117, transport cost projections, and service level impact)
- Board recommendation and presentation (step 4): 2 hours (COO presents findings and recommendation to Board)
- Implementation plan development (step 5): varies; if new DC approved, triggers W116 site selection process
- **Total annual review effort**: ~2 weeks concentrated effort (1 day data extraction + 1 week modeling + 3 days cost analysis + 2 hours presentation); primarily VP Supply Chain and External Logistics Consultant with Finance Analyst and Logistics Manager support


### Staffing Implication
Annual review requiring ~2 weeks of effort. VP Supply Chain leads (~1 week). Finance Analyst: ~1 day for data extraction. External Logistics Consultant: ~1 week for modeling (project-based fee). Logistics Manager: ~3 days for cost analysis. All absorbed by existing roles plus external consultant engagement. No incremental headcount.

## W250. Supply Chain Control Tower & Real-Time Shipment Visibility

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|

| **Frequency** | Ongoing per shipment |
| **Volume** | ~20–30 import shipments (multiple containers each; ~400–600 TEUs/month) and ~40–50 inter-island shipments/month |
| **Owner** | VP Supply Chain |
| **Participants** | Logistics Manager, Import Coordinator, Store Operations Rep, 3PL Partners |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Shipment Dispatch**: 3PL partner dispatches shipment and transmits Container ID / GPS tracker link via API/EDI to BuildRight. | 3PL Partner | — | Automated |
| 2 | **Milestones Tracking**: Control Tower system aggregates tracking data (vessel GPS, port gate-out/gate-in, inland truck GPS) to trace shipment in transit. | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | **Dynamic ETA Update**: System dynamically recalculates estimated time of arrival (ETA) based on weather, port speed, and transit history. | System | — | Automated |
| 4 | **Geofence Alerts**: System detects container entering port/DC zone; auto-alerts DC gate crew (W3) and Import Coordinator to prepare receiving. | System | — | Automated |
| 5 | **Exception Trigger**: If a shipment is delayed > 24 hours from original ETA, system triggers alert. Import Coordinator investigates bottleneck. | Import Coordinator | Logistics Mgr | 1 hour |
| 6 | **Re-routing / Mitigation**: If critical delay threatens store stockout, VP Supply Chain authorizes inventory reallocation (W105) or alternative local sourcing (W60). | VP Supply Chain | VP Supply Chain | 2 hours |
| 7 | **Carrier Review**: Monthly, Logistics Manager reviews carrier lead-time performance and delay metrics in S&OP dashboard (W133). | Logistics Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- Control Tower Dashboard with GIS integration
- Carrier EDI/API tracking integrations
- Dynamic ETA recalculation engine
- Automated SMS/Email delay exception notifications

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Carrier API reliability**: Not all 3PL partners and shipping lines provide reliable real-time API/EDI feeds; smaller inter-island carriers may only offer manual phone/SMS updates, creating visibility gaps for ~20–30% of shipments
- **Alert fatigue**: With 60–80 active shipments, minor delays generate frequent alerts; operations teams may start ignoring non-critical exceptions, potentially missing a genuine stockout-threatening delay
- **GPS coverage gaps in provincial routes**: Cellular/GPS coverage is unreliable in remote Philippine provincial areas (mountainous terrain in Cordillera, rural Mindanao), causing tracking blackouts during the last-mile portion of inter-island delivery
- **Reactive vs. proactive limitation**: Control Tower is primarily reactive (alerting after delay occurs); true proactive supply chain management would require predictive analytics based on port congestion patterns, weather forecasts, and carrier historical performance

| **Time Estimate** | Dispatch: 5 min; In-transit updates: Automated; Delivery & POD: 10 min |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Driver Loading & Scan**: Dispatch crew loads delivery vehicle; driver scans order barcodes using the last-mile mobile app, changing order status in ERP to "Out for Delivery". | Driver / Dispatch Crew | Last-Mile Supervisor | 5 min |
| 2 | **Customer Dispatch Alert**: System automatically triggers SMS/email notifications to customer with an updated ETA, contact details for the driver, and a link to the real-time tracking map. | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | **Real-Time GPS Tracking**: Driver's mobile app streams GPS location to the ERP Supply Chain Control Tower (W250); system dynamically recalculates ETA based on traffic conditions. | Driver / System | Last-Mile Supervisor | Automated |
| 4 | **Proximity Alert**: When the delivery vehicle enters a 2 km geofenced radius of the destination, the system sends an automated proximity SMS alert to the customer. | System | — | Automated |
| 5 | **Delivery & Site Inspection**: Driver arrives at address; unloads goods (cement, tiles, fixtures); customer inspects items to verify correct SKUs and quantity. | Driver / Customer | Last-Mile Supervisor | 5–15 min |
| 6 | **POD Capture**: Driver captures digital Proof of Delivery (POD) via mobile app: (a) customer's electronic signature, (b) photo of items staged at the site, (c) GPS coordinate stamp confirming vehicle was at delivery location. | Driver | Last-Mile Supervisor | 3 min |
| 7 | **Delivery Completion**: Mobile app uploads POD data; ERP changes order status to "Delivered", posts inventory reduction, generates official sales invoice to customer, and sends CSAT rating request (W65). | System | — | Automated |

### System Touchpoints
- Last-Mile Driver Mobile App (barcode scanning, signature & photo capture, offline sync capability)
- Real-time customer tracking web portal (GIS integrated route map & ETA update)
- ERP Control Tower tracking dashboard integration
- Integration with W19 (Home Delivery), W19B (Ship from Store), and W250 (Control Tower)

### Success Metrics / KPIs
- On-Time In-Full (OTIF) Delivery rate: > 95%
- Failed delivery rate: < 2.0%
- Delivery ETA notification accuracy: ±15 minutes
- Proof of Delivery (POD) capture compliance (photo + signature): 100%

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Connectivity Blackout (Offline Mode)**: In remote areas with no cellular signal, the mobile app caches signature and photo data locally, syncing with the ERP automatically once connection is restored.
- **Delivery Refusal / Damaged Goods**: If customer rejects delivery due to damage, driver marks "Refused - Damaged" in app, takes photo of damage, and returns goods to DC. ERP automatically flags case to CSR for expedited replacement order or refund processing (W19 step 12).
- **POD compliance gaps**: Drivers may forget to capture photos or signatures during high-volume delivery days, creating incomplete POD records that complicate dispute resolution and AR collection for large B2B deliveries.
- **GPS accuracy in dense urban areas**: Multi-story construction sites and dense Metro Manila neighborhoods produce inaccurate GPS stamps, undermining the geofence proximity alert and delivery location verification for disputed deliveries.


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-scan of "loads delivery vehicle" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-dispatch of "triggers SMS/email notifications to customer" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-calculate of "recalculates ETA based on traffic conditions." (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-245 (ensure controlled execution — Supply Chain Orchestration & Risk (PA-02.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
- Driver loading and barcode scan confirmation (step 1): 5 min per delivery stop; ~573 delivery orders/day across all DCs, batch-loaded per route with ~10–15 orders per vehicle = ~5 min/stop
- Customer dispatch alert and ETA notification (step 2): automated (instant)
- Real-time GPS tracking and ETA recalculation (step 3): automated (continuous; no manual effort)
- Proximity alert to customer via SMS (step 4): automated (instant)
- Delivery, site inspection, and customer verification (step 5): 5–15 min depending on item count and size (bulky items like cement bags, tiles, and lumber require longer unloading and staging time)
- Digital POD capture — signature, photo, and GPS stamp (step 6): 3 min per delivery
- Delivery completion, inventory posting, invoice generation, and CSAT request (step 7): automated (instant)
- **Per delivery (staff time)**: ~13–25 min total (5 min loading/scan + 5–15 min delivery/inspection + 3 min POD capture + automated steps)
- **Daily delivery volume**: ~573 deliveries/day across all DCs; ~17,200 deliveries/month requiring ~145–240 driver-hours/day
- **Exception handling**: failed deliveries (~2% rate = ~11/day) add 15–30 min each for return-to-DC processing per W19.12

### Staffing Implication
~17,200 deliveries/month. Last-Mile Operations Supervisor manages driver fleet and POD compliance (~4–6 hours/day). CSR handles delivery exception escalations (~1–2 hours/day). Driver POD capture: 3 min/delivery, absorbed into delivery routine. No incremental headcount beyond existing Last-Mile Supervisor and driver pool.

## W558. Supplier Risk Assessment & Supply Disruption Contingency Planning

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual supplier risk review cycle (Q1); ad-hoc disruption event (typhoon, port congestion, supplier bankruptcy, geopolitical trade restriction, raw material shortage) |
| **Frequency** | Annual full review of all ~1,000 vendors; continuous monitoring of ~50 critical/single-source vendors; ~4–6 significant disruption events per year |
| **Volume** | ~1,000 vendors assessed annually; ~20–30 critical single-source dependencies identified; ~8–12 contingency plan activations per year |
| **Owner** | VP Supply Chain / Procurement Director |
| **Participants** | Procurement Director, Category Manager, Supply Planning Manager, VP Merchandising (C), Finance Manager (C for cost impact), Customs Broker (C for import disruptions) |

### Background

W44 covers vendor performance scorecards (quality, delivery, cost). W491 covers supplier financial health and credit risk monitoring. W60 covers emergency procurement. W56 covers customer backorder management. W31 covers demand forecasting. However, no workflow covers holistic supplier risk assessment and supply disruption contingency planning — the systematic identification, assessment, and mitigation of supply chain risks including: single-source dependency, geographic concentration, financial instability, geopolitical trade restrictions, natural disaster exposure, and logistics bottleneck vulnerability. For a Philippine hardware retailer with ~40% of COGS from imports (China, Taiwan, Indonesia, Malaysia, Japan, Europe) and operations across an archipelago with 20+ typhoons per year, supply disruption risk is a constant operational reality. The company imports ~400–600 TEUs per month and relies on Manila North/South Harbor and regional ports that experience periodic congestion.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Annual supplier risk assessment cycle initiation (Q1): Procurement Director assigns risk assessment to each Category Manager for vendors in their category; provides assessment template with risk dimensions, scoring criteria, and deadline; Category Managers responsible for assessing all vendors with annual spend > PHP 500K and all single-source vendors regardless of spend | Procurement Director | VP Supply Chain | 1 day (kickoff) |
| 2 | Risk classification for each vendor across six dimensions: (a) **Financial risk**: pull W491 financial health data, payment behavior trends, credit reports (where available), years in business; (b) **Geographic risk**: country of origin political stability, distance from Philippines, port dependency (Manila vs. regional port), shipping route vulnerability; (c) **Single-source dependency** — count SKUs exclusive to this vendor with no alternative source identified, percentage of category spend concentrated with this vendor; (d) **Logistics risk**: shipping route exposure (South China Sea, Pacific typhoon belt), port congestion history (Manila North/South Harbor delays), inter-island dependency for domestic vendors; (e) **Regulatory risk**: import restriction exposure, tariff change probability, customs clearance delay history (BOC red lane frequency); (f) **Quality risk**: defect rate history per W44, product recall history, quality audit findings | Category Manager | Procurement Director | 15–20 min/vendor |
| 3 | Risk scoring: each dimension scored 1–5 (1 = Low risk, 2 = Minor, 3 = Moderate, 4 = High, 5 = Critical); weighted composite score calculated with configurable weights: financial risk (25%), geographic risk (15%), single-source dependency (25%), logistics risk (15%), regulatory risk (10%), quality risk (10%); system auto-calculates composite and flags dimension scores ≥ 4 for individual attention | Category Manager / System | Procurement Director | 5 min/vendor (system calculation) |
| 4 | Vendor classification by risk tier: (a) **Tier A — Critical** (composite score >4.0, OR single-source dependency score = 5, OR annual spend > PHP 50M); (b) **Tier B — Important** (composite score 3.0–4.0); (c) **Tier C — Standard** (composite score <3.0); system generates risk-tiered vendor list with drill-down by category and risk dimension; Procurement Director reviews and approves tier classifications | Category Manager / Procurement Director | VP Supply Chain | 4–6 hours (review session) |
| 5 | For Tier A Critical vendors: mandatory contingency plan per SKU category — Category Manager develops contingency plan in collaboration with Supply Planning Manager; plan must address: (a) qualified alternative source (minimum 2 qualified vendors per critical SKU per W36 vendor qualification); (b) safety stock policy override (increase safety stock per W312 for disruption-prone items — target: 45 days of supply for Tier A vendor SKUs vs. standard 21 days); (c) pre-positioned buffer inventory at alternate DC (e.g., if primary vendor supplies through Manila, pre-position buffer at Cebu DC); (d) emergency logistics route (alternative port, air freight for critical items — air freight cost premium documented and pre-approved per W60); (e) customer communication template for potential stockouts (pre-drafted per W571 store communication format) | Category Manager / Supply Planning Manager | Procurement Director | 4–8 hours/Tier A vendor |
| 6 | Contingency plan approval and activation readiness: Procurement Director reviews all Tier A contingency plans — verifies alternative vendor qualification status per W36, confirms safety stock levels are updated per W312, validates emergency logistics route feasibility with Logistics Manager; approved contingency plans loaded into supply chain control tower per W250 for activation when triggered | Procurement Director | VP Supply Chain | 2–4 hours (review session) |
| 7 | Supply disruption response protocol — **disruption detected**: (a) disruption signal received (supplier notification of force majeure, PAGASA typhoon warning Signal No. 3+, port closure advisory, BOC congestion alert, news of supplier bankruptcy, geopolitical trade restriction announcement); (b) Supply Planning Manager assesses impact within 4 hours: affected SKUs, current inventory on hand at DCs, days of supply by SKU, affected stores, customer order exposure (open SOs for affected SKUs); (c) **If >30 days of supply**: monitor only — increase monitoring frequency to daily, no immediate action | Supply Planning Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2–4 hours (initial assessment) |
| 8 | Supply disruption response — **escalation tiers**: (d) **If 14–30 days of supply**: activate safety stock buffer per contingency plan, expedite alternative vendor quotes per W36 (fast-track qualification for pre-identified alternatives), notify Category Manager for demand-side management; (e) **If <14 days of supply**: emergency procurement per W60, customer allocation per W105 (allocate remaining stock to highest-margin channels and B2B contractual obligations), substitute product recommendation per W279 (Merchandising identifies acceptable substitute SKUs); (f) VP Supply Chain authorizes emergency response expenditure > PHP 5M (air freight, spot-market premium purchases, expedited customs clearance fees) | Supply Planning Manager / Category Manager | VP Supply Chain | 4–8 hours/response |
| 9 | Disruption communication: Category Manager notifies stakeholders per disruption communication matrix — (a) Store Operations per W571 (store communication): affected SKUs, expected stockout dates, substitute product recommendations, customer messaging guidance; (b) Merchandising for assortment adjustment per W279: temporary product substitution, website/catalog update for out-of-stock items; (c) Customer Service for affected B2B orders: proactive outreach to top B2B accounts with open orders for affected SKUs, offer substitutes or revised delivery dates; (d) Finance Manager for cost impact assessment: emergency procurement premium, air freight cost, revenue at risk from stockouts | Category Manager | Procurement Director | 2–4 hours/disruption |
| 10 | Post-disruption review (within 2 weeks of resolution): (a) root cause analysis — what caused the disruption, was it predictable, could it have been prevented; (b) response effectiveness — how quickly was the disruption detected, how effective was the contingency plan, were alternative vendors able to supply, what was the stockout impact (lost sales, customer complaints); (c) contingency plan update — revise plan based on lessons learned; (d) vendor scorecard impact per W44 — document disruption in vendor performance record, adjust vendor risk score if warranted; (e) cost impact report — total disruption cost (premium freight, lost sales, substitute product margin impact) submitted to Finance Manager | Supply Planning Manager / Category Manager | VP Supply Chain | 4–8 hours/post-mortem |
| 11 | Quarterly disruption risk dashboard: Procurement Director presents to VP Supply Chain and VP Merchandising — (a) active disruptions and response status; (b) Tier A vendor risk score trends (improving/worsening); (c) contingency plan coverage (% of Tier A SKUs with qualified alternative source); (d) response time metrics (time from disruption detection to contingency activation); (e) disruption cost trend (quarterly comparison); (f) new risks identified during quarter (geopolitical, regulatory, market) | Procurement Director | VP Supply Chain | 4 hours/quarter (dashboard + presentation) |

### System Touchpoints

- Supplier risk assessment module with scoring and tiering (SCP-009)
- Purchase recommendation — alternative vendor suggestion (SCP-007)
- Vendor management — vendor data source (PUR-003)
- Import POs — import risk assessment (PUR-004)
- Vendor performance scorecard — quality/delivery data (W44)
- Supplier financial health — financial risk data (W491)
- Emergency procurement — disruption response (W60)
- Backorder management — customer impact (W56)
- Demand forecasting — demand data (W31)
- Replenishment parameters — safety stock override (W312)
- Multi-channel allocation — allocation during shortage (W105)
- Product substitution — substitute recommendation (W279)
- Store communication — disruption notification (W571)
- Supply chain control tower — real-time visibility (W250)

### Pain Points / Risks

- Philippine port congestion (especially Manila North/South Harbor) is chronic and unpredictable — container dwell times can spike from 5 days to 15+ days during peak periods (Ber months, pre-Christmas); mitigated by diversifying port of entry (Subic, Batangas, Cebu) per W144 and maintaining safety stock buffers per W312
- Typhoon paths are difficult to forecast beyond 3 days — PAGASA provides 3-day tropical cyclone warnings; supply chain decisions must be made quickly once a typhoon track is confirmed; mitigated by pre-positioned buffer inventory and pre-qualified alternative vendors
- Chinese New Year factory closures affect ~40% of import vendors for 2–3 weeks every January–February — this is a predictable annual disruption but requires significant pre-buying and inventory build; mitigated by W32 seasonal buy planning and W312 safety stock adjustment
- Alternative vendor qualification takes 3–6 months per W36 — during an active disruption, there is no time to qualify a new vendor; mitigated by pre-qualifying alternative vendors during the annual review (step 5) and maintaining a bench of warm standby vendors
- Political instability in source countries (Myanmar, Indonesia) can disrupt supply overnight — government-imposed export restrictions, currency controls, or civil unrest; mitigated by geographic diversification of source countries and maintaining inventory buffers for politically unstable source regions


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-assign of "assigns risk assessment to each Category Manager for vendors in their category" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "Logistics risk: shipping route exposure" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-calculate of "calculated with configurable weights" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-245 (ensure controlled execution — Supply Chain Orchestration & Risk (PA-02.3).)
- operational: diversifying port of entry (Subic, Batangas, Cebu) per W144 and maintaining safety stock buffers per W312; operational: pre-positioned buffer inventory and pre-qualified alternative vendors; operational: W32 seasonal buy planning and W312 safety stock adjustment

### Time Estimate

Annual full review: ~320 person-hours (1,000 vendors × ~20 min each). Per-disruption response: ~8–16 hours (initial assessment + escalation + communication). Quarterly dashboard: ~4 hours. Total annual effort: ~450–500 person-hours. Distributed across Category Managers (assessment), Supply Planning Manager (response), and Procurement Director (review).

### Staffing Implication

- Covered by existing Supply Planning Manager and Category Managers; no incremental headcount needed.
- Annual review adds ~320 person-hours in Q1 distributed across ~5 Category Managers (~64 hours each).
- Per-disruption response (~8–16 hours) absorbed by Supply Planning Manager and Category Managers as part of operational duties.
- Procurement Director quarterly review (~4 hours) absorbed within existing reporting cadence.
- **Capital expenditure**: Temperature monitoring sensors, climate-controlled storage expansion, and HVAC maintenance are Capex items (W21/W240).

## W1031. Customer Delivery Service Area Geo-Fencing & Coverage Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New store opening; periodic zone review (quarterly); customer address validation at checkout |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly review × 200 stores; real-time validation at every delivery order |
| **Volume** | 200 delivery zones; ~42,900 ecommerce orders/month + ~5,000–8,000 in-store delivery orders/month |
| **Owner** | Ecommerce Operations Manager |
| **Participants** | Supply Chain Planner, Store Manager, 3PL Partner Manager, IT GIS Analyst |

### Background

BuildRight Depot fulfills home delivery orders from 200 stores and 4 DCs, each serving a geographic delivery zone. The Philippine geography — 7,641 islands, dense urban areas with narrow streets, rural areas without standardized addresses, and variable infrastructure quality — makes delivery zone management uniquely challenging. Currently, delivery zone boundaries are loosely defined by zip code, which is insufficient for Philippine conditions where a single zip code can cover vastly different terrain (e.g., a Davao City zip code includes both urban commercial districts and remote mountain barangays). Without precise geo-fencing, BuildRight experiences: (a) customers outside the delivery zone placing orders that are later cancelled when the store/3PL cannot reach the address (8–12% cancellation rate for out-of-zone orders); (b) stores assigned deliveries that should be fulfilled by a different, closer store (increasing delivery cost and time); (c) inability to offer differentiated service levels (same-day delivery for Zone 1, 2–3 day delivery for Zone 3). This workflow defines delivery zones using geographic information system (GIS) polygons, assigns each zone to a fulfillment location (store or DC), sets delivery SLAs and pricing per zone, and manages zone updates as new stores open. This is distinct from W1004 (delivery scheduling self-service) which is the customer-facing interface; this workflow defines the underlying zone infrastructure.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Zone Boundary Definition & GIS Mapping**: IT GIS Analyst defines delivery zones for each store: (a) **Zone 1 (Core)**: 0–5 km radius from store; delivery SLA: same-day or next-day; delivery fee: PHP 99 or free above PHP 5,000; coverage: urban barangays within easy driving distance; polygon drawn on GIS map considering natural boundaries (rivers, highways, gated communities); (b) **Zone 2 (Standard)**: 5–15 km radius; delivery SLA: 1–2 business days; delivery fee: PHP 199 or free above PHP 8,000; (c) **Zone 3 (Extended)**: 15–30 km radius; delivery SLA: 2–3 business days; delivery fee: PHP 350+ based on distance; (d) **Zone 4 (Remote/DC-fulfilled)**: 30+ km from nearest store; delivery SLA: 3–5 business days; fulfillment from nearest DC; delivery fee: PHP 500+ based on distance and weight; (e) **Excluded areas**: areas unreachable by standard vehicles (island barangays without bridge access, mountain roads impassable during rainy season) — customer redirected to BOPIS (W11) or smart locker (W247); (f) zones defined as GIS polygons (not simple radius circles) to account for road network, natural barriers, and 3PL partner coverage areas | IT GIS Analyst / Supply Chain Planner | Ecommerce Operations Manager | 4–6 hours per store (initial setup); 2–3 hours per store (quarterly update) |
| 2 | **3PL Partner Coverage Mapping**: Ecommerce Operations Manager maps 3PL partner capabilities per zone: (a) Lalamove: strong coverage in Metro Manila, Cebu, and Davao urban areas; motorcycle and car for small-medium items; limited for bulky items (lumber, cement); (b) Transportify: covers wider geographic area including provincial routes; van and truck options for bulky items; (c) Own fleet (BuildRight Logistics): 10-wheeler wing vans for DC-to-store and large B2B deliveries; limited availability for B2C last-mile; (d) local provincial transporters: contracted per region for areas outside 3PL coverage; (e) system stores 3PL partner-zone mapping: which carrier serves which zone, at what cost, with what vehicle type, and SLA; (f) when multiple carriers serve the same zone, system selects based on: cost optimization, vehicle availability, SLA compliance history per W1013 | Ecommerce Operations Manager / 3PL Partner Manager | VP Supply Chain | 8–12 hours/quarter |

Would you like to pick up at [nearest store]? (BOPIS available)", (ii) customer redirected to BOPIS flow per W11; (e) if address geocoding fails (ambiguous Philippine address): (i) system prompts customer to add landmark and nearest major road, (ii) manual zone assignment by customer service agent within 2 hours for orders with failed geocoding | System / Customer Service Agent | Ecommerce Operations Manager | < 10 sec (automated); 5–10 min (manual override) |
| 4 | **Zone Conflict Resolution & Store Overlap Management**: When two stores' zones overlap: (a) system assigns order to store with: (i) shortest driving distance to delivery address (via Google Maps routing API), (ii) best in-stock position for ordered items (ATP check), (iii) lowest delivery cost (considering 3PL vs. own fleet); (b) tiebreaker: store with lower current fulfillment workload (balanced workload distribution); (c) DM can override zone assignment in exceptional cases (e.g., one store is temporarily out of a critical item); (d) monthly: system generates zone overlap analysis — identify orders where a different fulfillment store would have been more cost-effective; optimize zone boundaries accordingly | System / Supply Chain Planner | Ecommerce Operations Manager | 2–4 hours/month |

- Integration with W2B (import PO), W464 (customs clearance), W249 (demurrage/detention), W1169 (port drayage), W3 (DC receiving)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Multi-vendor consolidation complexity**: coordinating shipments from different vendors at the same origin requires aligned production schedules, compatible packaging, and clear cost allocation for shared container expenses; vendors may resist consolidation if it delays their individual shipments
- **Port congestion unpredictability**: Philippine ports can shift from normal (3–5 day dwell) to severely congested (14+ day dwell) within days due to vessel bunching, customs system downtime, or typhoon-related port closures
- **Container shortage during peak season**: global container shortages (recurring post-pandemic issue) may limit available space, forcing BuildRight to accept higher freight rates or delay shipments

## W1436. Philippine Construction Material Price Volatility Monitoring, Forward Buy Decision & Cost Hedge Planning

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly commodity price review; or significant price movement alert (> 5% change in key commodity index) |
| **Frequency** | Monthly formal review; weekly price monitoring during volatile periods; ad-hoc triggered by commodity price spikes |
| **Volume** | ~15–20 key commodities monitored (steel rebar, cement, plywood, G.I. sheets, copper wire, PVC resin, ceramic tiles, paint raw materials); ~PHP 42–45B annual COGS exposure |
| **Owner** | VP Merchandising |
| **Participants** | VP Merchandising, Category Managers (Lumber, Plumbing, Electrical, Tiles, Paint), Purchasing Manager, Finance Controller, VP Supply Chain |

### Background

Construction material prices in the Philippines are highly volatile due to: (a) global commodity price fluctuations (steel, copper, petroleum-based products), (b) PHP/USD exchange rate movements (imports are ~40% of COGS), (c) domestic supply constraints (cement industry consolidation, limited local steel production), (d) seasonal demand spikes (post-typhoon reconstruction, ber-months construction boom), and (e) government infrastructure spending cycles (Build! Build! Build! program). Price increases of 10–30% within a quarter are not uncommon for key materials (e.g., steel rebar, G.I. sheets, cement). BuildRight's ~28–32% gross margin can be eroded by 3–5 percentage points during commodity price spikes if purchasing decisions are reactive. This workflow establishes a proactive commodity price monitoring and forward buy decision process to protect margins while managing inventory carrying cost.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monthly commodity price review**: Category Managers compile price data for 15–20 key commodities: (a) current supplier quoted prices vs. previous month, (b) global commodity indices (LME steel, copper; Shanghai plywood index), (c) PHP/USD exchange rate trend, (d) DTI suggested retail price advisories for basic construction materials, (e) competitor pricing intelligence; present summary dashboard to VP Merchandising | Category Managers | VP Merchandising | 4 hours |
| 2 | **Price trend analysis and forecast**: VP Merchandising and Finance Controller analyze: (a) 6-month price trend for each commodity, (b) upcoming demand drivers (typhoon season, government projects, holiday construction), (c) supply-side risks (port congestion, supplier capacity constraints, import restrictions), (d) recommend forward buy decision for commodities expected to increase > 5% in next 90 days | VP Merchandising / Finance Controller | CFO | 2 hours |
| 3 | **Forward buy authorization**: For recommended forward buys: (a) Category Manager calculates forward buy quantity (3–6 months supply for A-items expected to spike), (b) Finance Controller evaluates working capital impact and storage capacity at DCs, (c) VP Merchandising approves forward buy up to PHP 10M; CFO approves PHP 10–50M; CEO approves > PHP 50M, (d) Purchasing Manager issues forward buy PO with negotiated price lock | Category Manager / Finance Controller | VP Merchandising / CFO | 2 hours |
| 4 | **Price protection pass-through analysis**: Finance Controller and VP Merchandising evaluate whether to: (a) absorb price increase (reduce margin but maintain competitive positioning), (b) pass through to SRP (protect margin but risk price-sensitive customer defection), (c) negotiate vendor cost sharing (vendor absorbs partial increase to maintain volume), (d) implement promotional pricing to offset customer perception of price increase | Finance Controller / VP Merchandising | CEO | 2 hours |
| 5 | **Quarterly hedge effectiveness review**: VP Merchandising and Finance Controller review: (a) actual commodity prices vs. forward buy prices locked, (b) total savings (or loss) from forward buy decisions, (c) inventory carrying cost of forward-bought stock, (d) working capital tied up in forward buys, (e) adjust forward buy strategy parameters for next quarter | VP Merchandising / Finance Controller | CFO | 3 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- Commodity price tracking dashboard — automated price index feeds from global and local sources (W1436.1)
- Forward buy PO module — price-locked purchase orders with future delivery dates (W1436.3)
- Margin impact simulator — scenario modeling for price increase pass-through vs. absorption (W1436.4)
- Integration with W2A (demand forecasting), W2B (import PO), W13 (pricing), W85 (product costing), W102 (margin analysis)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Forecasting inaccuracy**: commodity price movements are inherently unpredictable; a forward buy based on expected price increases may lock in a price that subsequently drops, resulting in above-market inventory cost
- **Working capital constraint**: forward buying ties up significant working capital (a PHP 350M forward buy of steel represents ~3 days of total COGS); during tight cash periods, forward buy opportunities may be missed
- **Storage capacity limitation**: DC capacity for bulk materials (cement, steel, tiles) is limited; forward buying 6 months of cement may exceed DC storage capacity, requiring leased warehouse space that erodes the cost savings
- **Competitor response**: if BuildRight raises SRP to pass through commodity costs but competitors absorb the increase, BuildRight loses price-sensitive customers (particularly trade professionals who comparison-shop)


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "Monthly commodity price review" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-import of "Price trend analysis and forecast" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-calculate of "calculates forward buy quantity" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-245 (ensure controlled execution — Supply Chain Orchestration & Risk (PA-02.3).)
- operational: VP Merchandising review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
- Monthly commodity review: 4 hours
- Monthly forward buy decisions: 2–4 hours
- Weekly price monitoring (peak): 1 hour/week
- Quarterly review: 3 hours
- **Annual estimate: ~120–160 hours/year**

### Staffing Implication
- VP Merchandising: ~4–6 hours/month. Absorbed within existing role.
- Category Managers: ~2 hours/month each for price data compilation. Absorbed.
- Finance Controller: ~2–3 hours/month for margin analysis. Absorbed.
- No incremental headcount.

## W1492. Typhoon Season Pre-Positioning of Emergency Construction Materials (Tarpaulins, Plywood, CGI Sheets) & Demand Allocation Across Store Network

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | PAGASA raises typhoon Signal No. 2 or higher over any region where BuildRight operates; also triggered by the annual pre-typhoon season review (May) |
| **Frequency** | Annual pre-positioning review (May); event-driven pre-positioning ~8–15 times per typhoon season (June–November); post-typhoon reallocation ~5–10 times/season |
| **Volume** | Emergency material stock: ~5,000–10,000 units tarpaulins, ~3,000–5,000 sheets plywood, ~2,000–4,000 bundles CGI sheets pre-positioned across 4 DCs; post-typhoon demand surge: 300–500% normal volume for affected stores |
| **Owner** | VP Supply Chain |
| **Participants** | Merchandise Planner, DC Manager, Logistics Manager, Store Manager, VP Merchandising |

### Background

The Philippines experiences an average of 20 typhoons per year, with 8–10 making landfall and 3–5 causing significant infrastructure damage. After a major typhoon, demand for emergency construction materials — tarpaulins for roof covering, plywood for boarding windows and temporary repairs, and corrugated galvanized iron (CGI) sheets for roof replacement — surges 300–500% in affected areas within 24–72 hours. Stores that are not adequately stocked lose sales to competitors and local hardware stores; worse, the company's reputation as "Your Home Building Partner" suffers when customers most need building supplies. Pre-positioning emergency stock at DCs and strategic stores before and during typhoon season, combined with rapid post-typhoon reallocation from unaffected stores, is essential for capturing surge demand while avoiding excess inventory after the season.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Annual pre-typhoon season emergency stock planning** (May): (a) VP Supply Chain convenes typhoon preparedness meeting with Merchandise Planning and Logistics; (b) review historical typhoon impact data: which regions affected, which stores experienced demand surge, stock-out duration; (c) determine emergency stock targets by material: (i) tarpaulins: based on average roof damage incidents × estimated units per incident; (ii) plywood: based on historical board-up and temporary repair demand; (iii) CGI sheets: based on historical roof replacement demand; (iv) nails, roofing nails, tie wire: complement materials; (d) allocate emergency buffer across 4 DCs proportional to historical typhoon exposure; (e) negotiate advance purchase commitments with key suppliers (plywood mills, CGI sheet manufacturers, tarpaulin importers) for seasonal supply guarantee; (f) emergency buffer budget approved by VP Merchandising | VP Supply Chain | VP Merchandising | 1–2 days |
| 2 | **Event-driven pre-positioning**: When PAGASA raises Signal No. 2+: (a) VP Supply Chain activates typhoon response protocol; (b) system identifies stores in projected typhoon path based on PAGASA track forecast; (c) for stores in high-impact zone: (i) emergency materials expedited from DC to store — maximum 24-hour delivery if store is accessible; (ii) standard replenishment orders for emergency SKUs converted to priority fulfillment; (iii) stores instructed to maximize shelf and yard display of emergency materials; (d) for nearby stores outside direct path: (i) pre-position additional stock as secondary supply points; (ii) these stores serve as replenishment sources for affected stores post-typhoon; (e) Logistics Manager coordinates truck availability for emergency deliveries, prioritizing over routine shipments | VP Supply Chain / Logistics Manager | COO | 4–8 hours per event |
| 3 | **Post-typhoon demand surge allocation**: Within 48 hours after typhoon passes: (a) affected stores report damage assessment and remaining emergency material stock; (b) Merchandise Planner estimates post-typhoon demand by store based on: (i) typhoon severity in store's area; (ii) historical post-typhoon sales patterns; (iii) news reports of damage extent; (c) remaining DC emergency stock allocated to stores proportionally; (d) if DC stock insufficient: (i) emergency procurement orders placed with suppliers at pre-negotiated surge pricing; (ii) stock transferred from unaffected stores with surplus (Visayas/Luzon stores may be unaffected while Mindanao stores are hit, or vice versa); (e) allocation priority: (i) stores with complete roof material stock-out (highest revenue impact); (ii) stores in areas with reported widespread housing damage; (iii) remaining stores based on demand forecast | Merchandise Planner / VP Supply Chain | VP Merchandising | 1–2 days post-event |
| 4 | **Post-season emergency stock disposition** (December): (a) remaining emergency buffer stock at DCs and stores counted; (b) if stock is sellable at normal prices: returned to regular inventory allocation; (c) if seasonal-specific items (e.g., heavy-duty typhoon tarps that don't sell in dry season): marked down 20–30% or held for next season with proper storage; (d) emergency stock performance review: (i) stock-out incidents during typhoon events; (ii) overstock cost (carrying cost of unsold emergency buffer); (iii) total revenue from emergency material sales during typhoon season; (iv) customer satisfaction data from post-typhoon period; (e) lessons learned feed into next year's pre-positioning plan | Merchandise Planner / VP Supply Chain | VP Merchandising | 1 day |

### System Touchpoints
- PAGASA weather alert integration with store location mapping (W1492.2)
- Emergency stock buffer management with pre-positioned inventory tracking at DC and store level (W1492.1–3)
- Automated store-in-path identification based on typhoon track forecast (W1492.2)
- Post-typhoon demand estimation model using historical data and severity scoring (W1492.3)
- Inter-store transfer optimization for emergency stock reallocation (W1492.3)
- Seasonal buffer disposition workflow with markdown recommendation (W1492.4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Forecast accuracy**: typhoon tracks can shift dramatically in 24 hours; pre-positioning stock at stores that end up unaffected wastes transport cost and disrupts normal operations
- **Supplier surge pricing**: suppliers know demand will spike and may increase prices 30–50% during typhoon events; pre-negotiated contracts with price caps are essential
- **Store accessibility**: typhoons often flood roads and knock out bridges; pre-positioned stock at a store that becomes inaccessible helps no one; distributing across multiple nearby stores mitigates this risk
- **Post-season excess**: an inactive typhoon season leaves BuildRight with PHP 5–15M in unsold emergency materials; while most items sell eventually, carrying cost and markdown risk are real
- **Competitor pre-emption**: local hardware chains and sari-sari stores also stock up before typhoons; speed of pre-positioning determines market share capture


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "Annual pre-typhoon season emergency stock planning" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-track of "track forecast" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-allocate of "allocated to stores proportionally" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-245 (ensure controlled execution — Supply Chain Orchestration & Risk (PA-02.3).)
- operational: VP Supply Chain review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- VP Supply Chain: ~1–2 days/year on seasonal planning + ~4–8 hours per typhoon event × ~10 events = ~50–80 hours/typhoon season. Primary responsibility.
- Merchandise Planner: ~1 day on seasonal planning + ~1–2 days per post-typhoon allocation × ~5 events = ~30–50 hours. Absorbed.
- Logistics Manager: ~4–8 hours per event for coordination. Absorbed.
- No incremental headcount.

### Time Estimate
- Annual planning: 1–2 days
- Per typhoon event: 4–8 hours (VP Supply Chain) + 1–2 days (Merchandise Planner)
- Post-season disposition: 1 day
- **Annual: ~100–150 hours total across involved roles**
