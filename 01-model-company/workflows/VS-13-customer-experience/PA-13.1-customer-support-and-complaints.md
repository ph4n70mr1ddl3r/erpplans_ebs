## W41. Customer Complaint Resolution

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer submits complaint in-store, by phone (call center), by email/chat, or on website/app |
| **Frequency** | ~2,000–3,000 complaints/month chain-wide; ~10–15 per store per month |
| **Volume** | Categories: product quality (30%), delivery issues (20%), staff behavior (15%), pricing/charging errors (15%), out-of-stock (10%), others (10%) |
| **Owner** | Customer Service Rep (Tier 1) / Customer Service Manager (Tier 2) |
| **Participants** | CSR, CS Manager, Department Supervisor, Store Manager, Buyer, Call Center Agent, Logistics |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customer submits complaint via any channel: in-store (CSR counter), phone (call center), email/chat, or website/app form | Customer | — | — |
| 2 | System creates complaint ticket: unique ticket number, channel, customer info (loyalty lookup or guest), category, description, priority (auto-assessed by category) | CSR / Call Center Agent | CS Manager | 5 min |
| 3 | **Tier 1 Resolution** (CSR or Call Center Agent): attempt immediate resolution — (a) product issue: offer exchange (W12), warranty claim (W33), or store credit; (b) pricing error: verify and issue refund; (c) delivery issue: check status, arrange redelivery | CSR / Call Center Agent | CS Manager | 5–15 min |
| 4 | If resolved at Tier 1: CSR closes ticket with resolution code and customer satisfaction rating (if provided) | CSR / Call Center Agent | — | 2 min |
| 5 | If unresolved at Tier 1: ticket escalated to Tier 2 (CS Manager or Store Manager); system reassigns ticket and starts SLA timer | System | CS Manager | Automated |
| 6 | **Tier 2 Resolution** (CS Manager / Store Manager): investigates root cause; coordinates with relevant department (Buyer for product issues, Logistics for delivery, HR for staff complaints) | CS Manager / Store Manager | VP Store Operations | 30–60 min |
| 7 | CS Manager proposes resolution to customer: refund, exchange, discount voucher, written apology, corrective action commitment | CS Manager | VP Store Operations | 15 min |
| 8 | If customer accepts: ticket closed with resolution; system triggers any financial actions (refund, voucher issuance) | CS Manager | — | 5 min |
| 9 | If customer rejects or complaint involves legal/regulatory risk: escalate to VP Store Operations or Legal; system logs escalation | CS Manager | VP Store Operations | 15 min |
| 10 | SLA enforcement: Tier 1 must respond within 4 hours; Tier 2 must resolve within 48 hours; if exceeded, system escalates to VP Store Operations | System | — | Automated |
| 11 | Monthly: CS Manager generates complaint analytics report: volume by category, by store, resolution rate, SLA compliance, top recurring issues | CS Manager | VP Store Operations | 2 hours/month |
| 12 | Monthly: VP Store Operations reviews complaint trends with Department Heads; assigns corrective actions for recurring root causes | VP Store Operations | COO | 1 hour/month |
| 13 | Quarterly: top complaint root causes feed into operational improvement initiatives (training, process changes, vendor quality requirements) | VP Store Operations | COO | Quarterly review |

### Ecommerce Order Issue Resolution

For ecommerce-specific order issues (W11 BOPIS, W19 Home Delivery) — the primary workload for the 30-person call center team (~2,100–4,300 issue tickets/month at 5–10% issue rate on 42,900 orders/month):

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Customer contacts call center (phone, chat, email) or submits issue via self-service portal/online form: order not received, wrong item received, item damaged in transit, wants to change delivery address, wants to cancel order, didn't receive BOPIS pickup notification | Customer / CSR | CS Manager | 3 min |
| E2 | System creates issue ticket linked to original order number; displays full order lifecycle (placement, payment, fulfillment status, carrier tracking) | CSR / System | CS Manager | 2 min |
| E3 | CSR resolves per issue type: (a) cancel-and-refund: system cancels unfulfilled order, initiates refund to original payment method (Dr. Revenue / Cr. Cash); (b) redelivery: system reschedules carrier pickup via 3PL integration (W19); (c) wrong/damaged item: initiates W12B return process and arranges replacement; (d) address change: updates delivery address if order not yet shipped; (e) BOPIS pickup issue: confirms correct store, resends notification, or extends hold period | CSR | CS Manager | 5–15 min |
| E4 | For partial refunds (damaged but usable item): CSR issues partial refund with CS Manager approval (Dr. Revenue / Cr. Cash); system logs partial refund with reason code and photo evidence if available | CSR / CS Manager | CS Manager | 10 min |
| E5 | System tracks issue SLA: target resolution within 24 hours for delivery issues, 48 hours for damaged/wrong items; escalated to CS Manager if SLA breached | System | CS Manager | Automated |
| E6 | Automated customer notifications at each status change: issue acknowledged, resolution proposed, refund processed, redelivery scheduled | System | — | Automated |

### System Touchpoints
- Multi-channel complaint capture (in-store, phone, email, chat, web) creating Service Requests in Teleservice on the TCA party model (W41.1–2); the cross-channel interaction record rides Customer Interaction History on the Common Application Calendar substrate (fit-gap D14, batch 27)
- Agent solutions knowledge base: Oracle Knowledge Management repositories rendered in the service workspace — solution capture with authoring review, categories, solution search (fit-gap D19, batch 27) (W41.4)
- Ticket categorization with auto-priority assignment; SLA timers and tiered escalation via Escalation Management (W41.2, W41.5, W41.10)
- Resolution code tracking and financial action triggers (refund, voucher) (W41.4, W41.8)
- Customer satisfaction capture at resolution (W41.4)
- Data Subject Access Request (DSAR) handling per RA 10173: system logs DSAR requests (access, correction, deletion, consent withdrawal) with 72-hour acknowledgment and 30-day resolution tracking; supports data anonymization for deactivated accounts after retention period; customer consent preferences viewable and editable via self-service portal (W41.2, W17.2)
- Complaint analytics dashboard: volume, category, store, resolution rate, SLA compliance (W41.11)
- Root cause analysis reporting (W41.12–13)
- Ecommerce order issue resolution: ticket creation linked to original order number with full order lifecycle visibility (placement, payment, fulfillment, carrier tracking); issue type routing (cancel-and-refund, redelivery, partial refund, address change, BOPIS pickup issue); financial adjustment posting (refund, partial refund) with GL entries; carrier rescheduling via 3PL integration (W19); automated customer notification at each status change; SLA tracking with escalation (24-hour delivery issues, 48-hour damaged/wrong items) (W41.E1–E6)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **SLA compliance across 200 stores**: Ensuring consistent 4-hour Tier 1 and 48-hour Tier 2 response times across 200 stores is operationally demanding; remote provincial stores may lack dedicated CS coverage during off-peak hours
- **Ecommerce issue volume scaling**: At 5–10% issue rate on 42,900 monthly ecommerce orders, the 30-person call center faces 2,100–4,300 issue tickets/month that compete with complaint handling for agent time; peak sale events (6x/year) can double this volume
- **Complaint-to-corrective-action gap**: Monthly analytics identify recurring root causes but converting findings into actual operational changes requires cross-functional commitment (Store Ops, Merchandising, Logistics) that may not follow through without executive accountability
- **Social media escalation velocity**: Viral social media complaints (Facebook, TikTok) can escalate from individual issue to brand crisis within hours; the current tiered SLA model is not designed for real-time social media response



### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-capture of "submits complaint via any channel" intake records (replaces manual Step 1 logging).
- System auto-create of "creates complaint ticket" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "check status, arrange redelivery" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-278 (ensure controlled execution — Customer Support & Complaints (PA-13.1).)
- operational: Customer Service Rep (Tier 1) / Customer Service Manager (Tier 2) review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
- Ticket creation and Tier 1 resolution: 5–15 min/complaint
- Tier 1 closure: 2 min
- Tier 2 investigation and resolution: 30–60 min/complaint
- Monthly complaint analytics report: 2 hours/month
- Monthly trend review with Department Heads: 1 hour/month
- Quarterly root cause review: 2 hours/quarter
- **Total**: CSRs absorb ~2–3 hours/store/month; Call Center handles ~50 complaints/agent/month at 10–15 min each

### Staffing Implication
- **CSRs (stores)**: 10–15 complaints/store/month × ~10 min each = ~2–3 hours/store/month. Absorbed within existing CSR role.
- **Call Center (30 people)**: Handles phone, email, chat, and web complaints. ~1,000–1,500 complaints/month via call center ÷ 30 agents = ~50 complaints/agent/month ÷ 20 working days = ~2.5/day. Each takes ~10–15 min. Manageable; call center also handles inbound inquiries, order tracking, and general support.
- **CS Manager (HQ or regional)**: 1 CS Manager oversees complaint analytics and Tier 2 escalations. May be a regional role (4 Regional CS Managers) or 1 centralized with store managers handling Tier 2 locally.

## W58. Corporate / Project Account Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Corporate account activated (W24) or new project added to existing corporate account |
| **Frequency** | ~200 active corporate accounts; ~50–100 active projects at any time |
| **Volume** | 10% of revenue (~PHP 6.2B/year); average project value PHP 1M–50M |
| **Owner** | Sales Rep (Trade & Corporate) |
| **Participants** | Sales Rep, AR Supervisor, Category Manager, Supply Planner, Store Manager, Finance Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sales Rep onboards new corporate account post-credit approval (W24): assigns dedicated Sales Rep, default store for pickup, delivery preferences, billing contact, and project delivery schedule | Sales Rep | AR Supervisor | 2 hours/account |
| 1a | **Customer project quotation**: when a trade or corporate customer (existing or prospect) requests a materials quotation — (a) Sales Rep receives customer's materials list (bill of materials, project specifications, or verbal requirements) via email, site visit, or walk-in; (b) Sales Rep creates Quotation in system — selects SKUs, enters quantities, and selects pricing basis: standard SRP, trade discount tier, or project-specific pricing (to be negotiated); (c) system calculates line totals, applies current pricing, and shows margin per line and total; (d) Sales Prop adds delivery cost estimate using carrier rate card per delivery zone (W5D/W19); (e) Sales Rep submits quotation for Category Manager approval if discounted below standard trade discount; (f) system generates quotation document (PDF) with BuildRight letterhead, validity period (typically 30 days), quotation number, customer details, line items, pricing, delivery terms, and payment terms; (g) Sales Rep sends quotation to customer via email or provides printed copy; (h) system tracks quotation lifecycle: Draft → Submitted → Accepted → Converted to Sales Order / Expired / Rejected; (i) if customer accepts: Sales Rep converts Quotation to Sales Order with one click — system carries all line items, quantities, pricing, and delivery details to the order; if customer negotiates changes, Sales Rep creates revised quotation (versioned) and re-submits; (j) expired quotations (beyond validity period) flagged for Sales Rep follow-up or archival; (k) monthly: Sales Rep reviews quotation conversion report — win rate by customer, category, and average quotation-to-order cycle time | Sales Rep / Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 30–60 min/quotation |
| 2 | For project-based accounts: Sales Rep creates project record in system — project name, location, estimated total material requirement, project timeline (start date, phases, completion date), project manager contact, billing milestones | Sales Rep | Finance Manager | 1 hour/project |
| 3 | Category Manager sets up project-specific pricing (if negotiated): discounted price list per SKU category, validity period aligned with project timeline, minimum commitment quantity (if any) | Category Manager / Pricing Analyst | VP Merchandising | 2–4 hours/project |
| 4 | Sales Rep creates material requirement plan per project phase: estimated SKU quantities by month; system generates forward demand signal for Supply Planner to pre-position stock at the serving DC/store | Sales Rep | Supply Planner | 2–4 hours/project |
| 5 | During project: Sales Rep takes orders from corporate customer via phone, email, or site visit; creates Sales Order in system linked to project; applies project-specific pricing; arranges pickup at designated store or scheduled delivery (W19 or own fleet) | Sales Rep | Store Manager | 15–30 min/order |
| 6 | System tracks cumulative project spending vs. approved credit limit; alerts Sales Rep and AR Supervisor at 80% and 95% of project budget or credit limit | System | — | Automated |
| 7 | Monthly: Sales Rep generates project billing summary for customer: all orders delivered, quantities, amounts, remaining project budget; customer reconciles and processes payment per terms (Net 60–90) | Sales Rep | AR Supervisor | 1 hour/project/month |
| 8 | Monthly: AR Supervisor reviews corporate account aging; project accounts with payments > 60 days overdue escalated to Finance Manager for collection action per W8.7–8 | AR Supervisor | Finance Manager | Part of W8 |
| 9 | Upon project completion: Sales Rep closes project record; system generates final project summary (total revenue, margin, items purchased, variance from initial estimate); AR Supervisor confirms all invoices settled | Sales Rep / AR Supervisor | Finance Manager | 1 hour/project |
| 10 | Quarterly: Sales Rep and Category Manager review corporate account portfolio: account profitability, project win rate, competitive positioning, upsell opportunities | Sales Rep + Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 4 hours/quarter |

> **Billing model note**: the workflow above uses monthly billing summaries as the standard billing method for corporate/project accounts, which is a deliberate simplification aligned with BuildRight's retail-oriented billing cycle. For large construction projects where customers require milestone-based or progress billing (e.g., foundation, framing, finishing phases), Sales Rep structures the project into billing milestones during W58 step 2 — each milestone has a target date and estimated value; system generates a milestone invoice when Sales Rep confirms milestone completion; retention amounts (standard 10% in Philippine construction practice) may be configured per project if contractually required — system tracks retained amount separately from invoiced amount and releases retention upon project completion sign-off; architect/engineer progress certification, if required, is captured as an approval step before milestone invoice generation; project change orders are processed as adjustments to the project record with Category Manager approval, updating the material requirement plan and billing milestones accordingly

### System Touchpoints
- Corporate account master with project sub-accounts, project-specific pricing, and delivery preferences (W58.1–3)
- Quotation management: quotation creation with SKU selection and pricing; margin visibility per line; Category Manager approval for below-standard pricing; quotation document generation (PDF) with validity period; quotation lifecycle tracking (Draft → Submitted → Accepted → Converted / Expired / Rejected); one-click conversion from Quotation to Sales Order; versioned revisions; quotation conversion analytics (win rate, cycle time, by customer and category) (W58.1a)
- Project record with timeline, material requirements, and billing milestones (W58.2)
- Project-specific pricing linked to POS (W5B.4c) and Sales Order entry (W58.3, 5)
- Cumulative project spending and budget tracking with automated alerts (W58.6)
- Project billing summary generation (W58.7)
- Project close-out with financial summary (W58.9)
- Corporate account portfolio analytics (W58.10)
- Integration with W5B.4c (corporate pricing at POS), W8 (AR and collections), W19 (delivery), W24 (credit application), W56 (backorder priority for corporate accounts)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Credit limit exposure concentration**: 200 corporate accounts representing 10% of revenue (~PHP 6.2B/year) with Net 60–90 terms creates significant AR concentration risk; a single large project default can materially impact cash flow
- **Project scope and material list volatility**: Construction projects frequently change scope mid-stream (design changes, owner additions, contractor substitutions); Sales Reps must continuously update material plans and pricing, and the system must handle change orders without losing audit trail
- **Collection difficulty on milestone disputes**: Corporate customers may withhold payment disputing milestone completion or material quality; AR escalation per W8 is slower than the project delivery cycle, creating tension between continued material supply and payment collection
- **Sales Rep capacity ceiling**: At 50–70 accounts and 15–25 active projects per rep, there is minimal capacity for new business development; growth in corporate account revenue requires proportional growth in Sales Rep headcount

## W61. Competitor Price Match Process

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests price match at POS based on competitor's current advertised price |
| **Frequency** | ~50–100 price match requests/day chain-wide; ~2–5 per store per week |
| **Volume** | Primarily on high-visibility items (power tools, paint, appliances, tiles) where customers actively compare prices |
| **Owner** | Cashier (execution); Store Manager (authorization) |
| **Participants** | Cashier, Store Manager, Pricing Analyst (review) |

### Price Match Policy Rules

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Eligible competitors** | Named competitors with physical stores or authorized online retailers within the same city/province; excludes marketplace sellers (Shopee, Lazada individual sellers), auction sites, and clearance/closeout sales |
| **Proof required** | Current printed advertisement, competitor website screenshot (with date visible), or competitor physical catalog; Cashier visually verifies proof |
| **Matching scope** | Match applies to identical item (same brand, model, size, color); does not apply to bundles, limited-quantity offers, or membership-only pricing |
| **Price match limit** | Price matched to competitor's price; will not go below BuildRight's cost (WAC) unless authorized by Category Manager |
| **Frequency limit** | Max 3 identical items per price match request per customer per day |
| **Exclusions** | Installation services, delivery fees, gift cards, special orders (W38), clearance items (W13.9a) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customer requests price match at checkout; presents competitor price proof (ad, screenshot, catalog) | Customer | — | — |
| 2 | Cashier verifies eligibility per policy rules: (a) competitor is on eligible list, (b) item is identical (brand, model, size), (c) proof is current (within 7 days), (d) quantity within limit | Cashier | — | 2 min |
| 3 | Cashier enters price override in POS with reason code "Competitor Price Match"; system prompts for competitor name and matched price; if override > 10% or > PHP 500: manager authorization required per W5B.4a | Cashier | Store Manager | 1 min |
| 4 | If matched price is below BuildRight's cost (WAC): system blocks override and alerts Store Manager; Store Manager contacts Category Manager for authorization; Category Manager may approve as strategic decision (customer retention, competitive response) with written justification in system | Store Manager / Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 5–10 min |
| 5 | System records price match transaction: original price, matched price, competitor name, cashier ID, authorizer ID, item, quantity, and reason code (W5B.4a audit trail) | System | — | Automated |
| 6 | Weekly: Pricing Analyst reviews price match report: frequency by SKU, competitor, and store; identifies items with high price match frequency (> 5 requests/week) for potential SRP adjustment (W40) or competitive response strategy | Pricing Analyst | Category Manager | 1 hour/week |

### System Touchpoints
- Price match reason code in POS price override workflow with competitor name capture (W61.3, W5B.4a)
- System enforces WAC floor price — blocks price match below cost without Category Manager override (W61.4)
- Price match analytics: frequency by SKU, competitor, store, and margin impact (W61.6)
- Eligible competitor list maintained by Pricing Analyst in system (configurable per region if needed) (W61 policy rules)
- Integration with W5B.4a (price override), W40 (potential SRP adjustment triggered by price match frequency)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Margin erosion on high-visibility items**: Price matching on power tools, paint, and appliances — categories where BuildRight competes most aggressively — can erode category margins if competitors intentionally use loss-leader pricing on these items
- **Competitor proof verification difficulty**: Cashiers cannot reliably verify competitor website screenshots (easily edited) or determine if a printed ad is current; the verification process relies heavily on cashier judgment and is vulnerable to both fraudulent customer claims and overly strict rejection
- **Below-cost override risk**: When Category Managers approve below-WAC price matches for strategic reasons (customer retention, competitive response), the system must track these as deliberate margin concessions; frequent below-cost approvals undermine the integrity of the WAC floor policy; mitigated by CTL-278 (controlled execution — Customer Support & Complaints (PA-13.1))
- **Data latency on price match analytics**: Weekly review by the Pricing Analyst may not catch a sudden competitor pricing change fast enough; if a competitor drops prices on a key SKU category, BuildRight could face hundreds of price match requests before the analytics surface the pattern

---


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "Customer requests price match at checkout" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-capture of "enters price override in POS with reason" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-match of "matched price is below BuildRight's cost" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-278 (ensure controlled execution — Customer Support & Complaints (PA-13.1).)
- operational: must track these as deliberate margin concessions

### Time Estimate
- Customer request and Cashier verification: 3 min/price match
- Manager authorization (if > 10% or > PHP 500): additional 5 min
- Category Manager authorization (if below WAC): additional 5–10 min
- Weekly Pricing Analyst review: 1 hour/week
- **Total POS impact**: ~50–100 price matches/day across 200 stores = minimal per-store time impact

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount. Price match adds ~2 min to occasional POS transactions. Weekly review by Pricing Analyst adds ~1 hour/week — absorbed within existing Pricing Analyst duties (team of 4 per §13.1).

## W84. Customer Account Reactivation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Former customer (dormant, closed, or written-off) requests to resume business; or Sales Rep identifies reactivation opportunity during prospecting |
| **Frequency** | ~20–40 reactivation requests/month; peaks during construction season (October–March) |
| **Volume** | ~5,200 active accounts; ~300–500 dormant/inactive accounts at any time; ~10–20 written-off accounts per year potentially recoverable |
| **Owner** | AR Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Sales Rep, AR Clerk, AR Supervisor, Finance Manager, Credit Committee (if large account) |

### Background

Customer accounts may become dormant (no transactions for 6+ months, per W8), closed (per W8 step 12), or written off (per W81). When these customers wish to resume business — often driven by new construction projects, change of ownership, or settlement of prior obligations — a formal reactivation process ensures appropriate risk assessment while avoiding unnecessary friction. This workflow covers all reactivation scenarios.

### Reactivation Scenarios

| Scenario | Prior Status | Risk Level | Reactivation Path |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dormant reactivation** | Inactive (credit limit reduced to PHP 0) | Low | Abbreviated credit review |
| **Voluntarily closed → reopen** | Closed by customer request | Low–Medium | Standard W24 credit application |
| **Written-off → repayment & reactivate** | Written off per W81 | High | Enhanced credit review + approval |
| **Written-off → new entity** | New company formed by same principal | High | Full W24 + legal review |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customer or Sales Rep initiates reactivation request: provides customer name or prior account number, reason for reactivation, and current business status | Customer / Sales Rep | AR Clerk | 10 min |
| 2 | AR Clerk retrieves prior account record from system; reviews account history: (a) prior credit limit and payment history, (b) reason for dormancy/closure/write-off, (c) outstanding balance (if any), (d) any pending disputes or unresolved issues | AR Clerk | AR Supervisor | 15 min |
| 3 | **Dormant reactivation** (no write-off, reactivation within 12 months): (a) AR Clerk contacts customer to confirm business is active and update contact details, (b) if prior credit assessment is < 12 months old, AR Clerk reactivates account with prior credit limit (AR Supervisor approval required), (c) if prior credit assessment is > 12 months old, process abbreviated W24 credit review — updated financial statements + trade references, (d) system changes account status from "Inactive" to "Active"; restores credit limit per approval | AR Clerk / AR Supervisor | AR Supervisor | 30 min–1 hour |

## W328. Customer Credit Limit Periodic Review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual credit review calendar (every 12 months from account opening or last review); or triggered early by material adverse event (payment default > 60 days, significant order volume increase, negative trade reference) |
| **Frequency** | Annual for all trade/corporate accounts (~5,400 accounts); ad-hoc for adverse events (~100–200 early reviews/year) |
| **Volume** | ~5,200 annual reviews ÷ 12 months = ~430 reviews/month; peaks at fiscal year-end when batch reviews are scheduled |
| **Owner** | Credit Analyst |
| **Participants** | Credit Analyst, Finance Manager, AR Clerk, Sales Rep, Customer (trade/corporate account holder) |

### Background

CRM-008 (W24) establishes initial credit limits at account opening. FIN-028 (W108) manages collection escalation for overdue accounts. However, credit risk is dynamic — a customer's financial health, payment behavior, and order volume change over time. Without periodic reassessment, credit limits set at onboarding may become misaligned with actual risk, leading to either excessive exposure (limit too high for deteriorating customer) or lost sales (limit too low for growing customer). This workflow closes that gap.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Batch review scheduling**: System generates annual credit review list each month — accounts due for review (12 months since last review or opening date); system ranks by exposure (highest outstanding balance + open orders first) | System | Credit Analyst | Automated |
| 2 | **Automated risk scoring**: System computes updated risk score per account using: (a) payment behavior (average days-to-pay, number of late payments in last 12 months, days sales outstanding trend), (b) credit utilization (outstanding balance ÷ current limit, trend), (c) order volume trend (current year vs. prior year), (d) any collection escalations in last 12 months (W108 history), (e) any credit memo/debit memo disputes (W70 history); system classifies accounts as Green (low risk), Yellow (moderate risk), Red (high risk) | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | **Green accounts — streamlined review**: Credit Analyst reviews Green-flagged accounts (typically 70–80% of batch); if risk score is stable and no adverse indicators, Analyst approves continuation of existing credit limit with notation; no customer contact required | Credit Analyst | Finance Manager | 5 min/account |
| 4 | **Yellow accounts — enhanced review**: Credit Analyst pulls updated financial information: (a) requests latest financial statements or bank certification from customer, (b) checks trade references, (c) reviews order and payment history in detail; Analyst prepares credit assessment memo with recommendation: maintain, increase, or decrease limit | Credit Analyst | Finance Manager | 30–60 min/account |
| 5 | **Red accounts — full review and action**: Credit Analyst conducts full credit reassessment: (a) pulls financial statements, trade references, and bank certifications, (b) evaluates if credit limit should be reduced, placed on hold, or revoked, (c) prepares recommendation with supporting evidence; Finance Manager reviews and decides | Credit Analyst | Finance Manager | 1–2 hours/account |
| 6 | **Limit adjustment**: If limit changes: (a) system updates credit limit in customer master (W253), (b) system generates customer notification letter stating new limit and effective date, (c) Sales Rep informed of limit change to manage customer relationship, (d) if limit reduced: system flags any open orders exceeding new limit for Sales Rep review | Credit Analyst / System | Finance Manager | 10 min |
| 7 | **Ad-hoc early review trigger**: If a material adverse event occurs between scheduled reviews (e.g., customer payment default > 60 days, customer reported in financial distress, significant order volume spike > 200% of average): system flags account for immediate credit review outside the annual cycle; Credit Analyst initiates steps 4 or 5 as appropriate | System / Credit Analyst | Finance Manager | As needed |
| 8 | **Batch completion and reporting**: At month-end, Credit Analyst produces credit review summary: number of accounts reviewed, limit changes (increases/decreases), accounts placed on hold, total exposure change; Finance Manager reviews and signs off; report feeds into W35 management reporting | Credit Analyst | Finance Manager | 2 hours/month |

- Ad-hoc review triggers integrated with W108 (collection escalation), W70 (credit/debit note aging), W5B.4c (POS trade pricing) (W328.7)
- Customer notification generation for limit changes (W328.6)
- Monthly credit review summary integrated into W35 management reporting (W328.8)
- Integration with W24 (credit application — initial limit), W108 (collection — payment behavior data source), W229 (B2B credit limit exception — temporary override), W253 (customer master — limit storage), W8 (AR — credit hold enforcement)

### Pain Points / Risks
- Customer relationship friction when credit limits are reduced — trade customers who have built their business around BuildRight credit terms may view a limit decrease as a loss of trust, especially if they are experiencing temporary cash flow difficulties rather than structural financial decline
- Financial statement collection from Philippine SME customers is difficult — many trade accounts are small hardware stores or contractors with informal or incomplete financial records, making quantitative credit assessment challenging
- Adverse event detection lag — the system relies on internal data (payment behavior, collection history) but lacks external credit bureau integration common in developed markets; a customer may be in financial distress with other suppliers before BuildRight detects the change
- Annual review batch size — ~430 reviews/month is manageable with 2–3 Credit Analysts for Green accounts (5 min each) but a cluster of Yellow/Red accounts in a single month can overwhelm capacity

- System auto-count of "Automated risk scoring" (replaces manual Step 2).

- operational: Credit Analyst review and approval gate

## W597. Customer Complaint Escalation Matrix & Resolution SLA Tracking

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer complaint received via any channel (in-store, call center, social media, email, website) and classified by severity |
| **Frequency** | ~2,000–3,000 complaints/month chain-wide; escalation varies by severity — Severity 1 (critical): ~50–80/month, Severity 2 (high): ~300–400/month, Severity 3 (medium): ~800–1,200/month, Severity 4 (low): ~800–1,200/month |
| **Volume** | Channels: in-store (~40%), call center (~25%), social media (Facebook, Instagram — ~15%), email/web form (~15%), chat (~5%); complaint types: product quality (30%), pricing errors (20%), service delays (20%), staff behavior (15%), safety incidents (5%), other (10%); DTI-reportable complaints: ~30–50/year |
| **Owner** | Customer Service Manager |
| **Participants** | CSR, CS Manager, Store Manager (R/A for in-store), Call Center Agent, Social Media Manager, VP Store Operations (A for Severity 1), Legal Counsel (A for DTI-escalated), HR Manager (I for staff behavior complaints), Department Head (I for product issues) |

### Background

W41 covers the general customer complaint resolution process with two-tier escalation and 48-hour resolution SLA. W469 covers DTI consumer adjudication board escalation. W258 covers omni-channel ticketing system management. W259 covers call center daily operations. W507 covers root cause analysis for recurring complaints. W65 covers customer satisfaction measurement. However, no workflow provides a comprehensive escalation matrix with severity-based SLAs, defined escalation paths, DTI compliance tracking, and SLA performance monitoring across all channels.

For a Philippine big-box retailer with 200 stores and ~2,000–3,000 monthly complaints, a structured escalation matrix is essential for three reasons: (1) **DTI compliance** — Republic Act 7394 (Consumer Act of the Philippines) requires businesses to respond to consumer complaints within specific timeframes and maintain records; failure to respond can result in DTI consumer adjudication board escalation per W469, with potential fines and reputational damage; (2) **operational efficiency** — without clear severity classification, every complaint gets the same treatment, resulting in over-investment in trivial issues and under-investment in critical ones; (3) **channel consistency** — complaints arriving via social media (where public visibility amplifies reputational risk) need different handling than in-store complaints (where resolution can be immediate).

### Severity Classification Matrix

| Severity | Definition | Examples | First Response SLA | Resolution SLA | Escalation Path |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Critical** | Safety risk, legal exposure, media/DTI escalation potential, social media viral risk | Product-related injury, building code violation in installation, DTI consumer adjudication notice, viral social media complaint (>1,000 shares/engagements), fraud accusation, racial/gender discrimination | 30 min | 4 hours | CSR → CS Manager → VP Store Operations → Legal → COO |
| **2 — High** | Financial impact >PHP 5,000, multiple affected customers, systemic issue | Batch product defect affecting multiple purchases, pricing error affecting multiple transactions, delivery failure for project-critical materials (contractor loses project day), staff behavior requiring HR investigation | 2 hours | 24 hours | CSR → CS Manager → Store Manager → VP Store Operations |
| **3 — Medium** | Standard product/service issue, single customer, financial impact <PHP 5,000 | Individual product defect (not batch), delivery delay (not project-critical), incorrect item delivered, billing discrepancy, loyalty points not credited | 4 hours | 48 hours | CSR → CS Manager |
| **4 — Low** | Minor issue, suggestion, non-urgent feedback | Product information request, store layout suggestion, general feedback, non-critical loyalty inquiry, minor packaging damage (product usable) | 8 hours | 72 hours | CSR (auto-resolve) |

- Trade Account Manager escalations: ~20% of calls require escalation × 10–30 min = ~3–8 hours/day across all TAMs

## W1521. Customer Verified Contractor & Installer Referral Directory Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests contractor/installer recommendation; quarterly contractor directory refresh cycle; contractor complaint or performance issue |
| **Frequency** | ~800–1,200 directory-governance actions/month across 200 stores (the directory layer; transaction-level matchmaking runs per W904 at ~1,500–2,000 requests/month); quarterly directory audit |
| **Volume** | ~300–500 verified contractors/installers in directory across all trade categories |
| **Owner** | Service Coordinator Lead |
| **Participants** | Service Coordinator Lead, Category Managers, Store Managers, accredited contractors/installers, Customer |

### Background

Many BuildRight customers are DIY-capable for simple projects but need professional help for complex installations (electrical, plumbing, structural, tile, aircon, solar). Unlike the installation services managed directly by BuildRight through subcontractors (W138, W1519, W1520), the contractor referral directory serves customers who want to hire their own contractor but value BuildRight's vetting. This is a trust-based service: BuildRight does not employ or guarantee the contractors, but verifies their credentials, tracks customer feedback, and removes underperformers. The directory also supports the estimation advisory services (W1517 structural, W1518 solar) where the Project Estimator refers customers to licensed engineers and electricians. This workflow manages the complete contractor directory lifecycle: recruitment, verification, customer matching, feedback collection, and periodic refresh.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Contractor recruitment and application**: (a) Service Coordinator Lead recruits contractors through: (i) existing BuildRight installation subcontractors who also take private jobs, (ii) walk-in contractor inquiries at store service counters, (iii) local contractor association referrals, (iv) trade professional customers (plumbers, electricians, tile setters) identified through trade account purchases; (b) contractor submits application with: (i) business name and DTI/SEC registration, (ii) trade-specific licenses/certifications (PRC license for engineers, TESDA NC-II for electricians/plumbers, TESDA RAC NC II for aircon technicians), (iii) portfolio of completed projects with photos, (iv) 2–3 customer references, (v) proof of general liability insurance | Service Coordinator Lead | — | 30–60 min/application review |
| 2 | **Verification and background check**: (a) Service Coordinator Lead verifies: (i) PRC/TESDA license validity and currency via online verification, (ii) business registration with DTI/SEC, (iii) calls 2–3 references and documents feedback, (iv) checks for any BuildRight customer complaints against the contractor (cross-reference W795 installation complaint data); (b) if verification passes: contractor added to directory with trade categories, service area (km radius from stores), and contact information; (c) if verification fails: contractor rejected with explanation of missing credentials | Service Coordinator Lead | Store Manager (local market) | 1–2 hours/contractor |
| 3 | **Customer referral** (in-store): (a) customer requests contractor recommendation at service counter; (b) Service Coordinator queries directory by: (i) trade category (electrician, plumber, tile setter, carpenter, painter, general contractor, structural engineer, architect), (ii) geographic proximity to customer's site, (iii) customer rating (4+ stars preferred), (iv) availability; (c) provides customer with 2–3 contractor options: name, trade, phone number, star rating, brief description of specialties; (d) important disclaimer: "BuildRight verifies credentials but does not employ or guarantee these contractors. Customer contracts directly with the contractor. BuildRight is not liable for contractor workmanship or pricing." | Service Coordinator | — | 5–10 min |
| 4 | **Customer feedback collection**: (a) after referred contractor completes a job, Service Coordinator contacts customer (SMS or in-store follow-up): (i) did the contractor perform satisfactorily?, (ii) 1–5 star rating, (iii) would you recommend this contractor to others?, (iv) any issues or complaints?; (b) system logs feedback against contractor profile; (c) if complaint: investigate per W795 if workmanship issue; if behavioral/ethical issue: flag contractor for review | Service Coordinator / System | Service Coordinator Lead | 5–10 min/referral |
| 5 | **Quarterly directory audit**: (a) Service Coordinator Lead reviews all contractors in directory: (i) average customer rating (remove contractors below 3.5 stars), (ii) license/certification expiry dates (remove or suspend contractors with expired credentials), (iii) activity level (inactive > 6 months: contact for renewal or remove), (iv) complaint volume (2+ unresolved complaints in quarter: suspend pending investigation); (b) updates directory: add new contractors, remove/suspend underperformers, update contact information and service areas; (c) publishes updated directory to store service counters and BuildRight website/app | Service Coordinator Lead | VP Store Operations | 4–6 hours/quarter |
| 6 | **Contractor dispute resolution**: (a) if customer files complaint against referred contractor: (i) Service Coordinator Lead documents complaint, (ii) contacts contractor for their response, (iii) mediates resolution if possible (rework, partial refund), (iv) if resolution fails: advises customer of their legal options, (v) flags contractor in directory — multiple unresolved complaints trigger suspension; (b) BuildRight does not accept financial liability for contractor work; mediation is a goodwill service | Service Coordinator Lead | VP Store Operations | 1–3 hours/dispute |

### System Touchpoints
- Contractor directory module: trade categories, service areas, license tracking, customer ratings, activity status (W1521.2)
- Customer referral interface: search by trade, location, rating, availability; disclaimer acknowledgment capture (W1521.3)
- Customer feedback collection: post-referral SMS survey with 1–5 star rating, complaint flag (W1521.4)
- Quarterly audit dashboard: contractor performance summary, license expiry alerts, complaint tracking (W1521.5)
- Integration with W795 (Installation Complaint), W600 (Contractor Accreditation — BuildRight's own subcontractors vs. external referral directory), W1517 (Structural — engineer referral), W1518 (Solar — installer referral)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Liability boundary**: despite disclaimer, customers may hold BuildRight responsible for referred contractor's workmanship; a high-profile contractor failure (e.g., electrical fire from referred electrician) creates reputational damage even if BuildRight has no legal liability
- **Directory currency**: contractors' credentials, insurance, and availability change frequently; a quarterly audit cycle may leave stale entries; customer frustration when a referred contractor's phone is disconnected or license has expired
- **Contractor quality drift**: a contractor verified 18 months ago may have deteriorated in quality (hired less-skilled workers, took on too many projects); customer feedback mechanism catches this but with lag — 2–3 negative reviews before action is taken
- **Coverage gaps**: rural stores may have few or no verified contractors in their area, limiting the service's value; urban stores may have too many, making selection overwhelming

- System auto-document of "Verification and background check" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-capture of "carpenter, painter, general contractor, structural engineer, architect" (replaces manual Step 3).

## W1550. Customer Voice-of-Customer (VOC) Monthly Analysis, Trend Dashboard & Strategic Insight Reporting

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly VOC analysis cycle (1st week of month for prior month data) |
| **Frequency** | Monthly |
| **Volume** | Aggregation across all customer feedback channels — POS CSAT (~300K–400K responses/month at the 10–15% response rate), ecommerce reviews (~2K/month), call center surveys (~5K/month), social media mentions (~10K/month), NPS quarterly surveys (~15K responses) |
| **Owner** | Customer Experience Manager |
| **Participants** | Customer Experience Manager, Data Analyst, VP Marketing, VP Store Operations, Category Managers |

### Background

BuildRight collects customer feedback across multiple touchpoints — in-store CSAT ratings, ecommerce product reviews, call center post-call surveys, social media sentiment, and quarterly NPS surveys. However, raw feedback data is meaningless without systematic analysis. This workflow transforms multi-channel feedback into actionable insights — identifying emerging issues, tracking sentiment trends, and informing strategic decisions across merchandising, store operations, and service delivery.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Multi-channel data aggregation**: Data Analyst extracts customer feedback data from all sources — POS CSAT scores by store/department/cashier, ecommerce product ratings and review text, call center CSAT and resolution scores, social media sentiment scores (positive/neutral/negative), NPS scores and verbatim comments; consolidates into unified VOC dataset | Data Analyst | Customer Experience Manager | 4 hours |
| 2 | **Text analytics & topic extraction**: System applies NLP text analytics to verbatim feedback — extract recurring topics/themes (e.g., "long queue", "product out of stock", "delivery late", "friendly staff", "good quality"); sentiment scoring per topic; identify emerging topics not seen in prior months | System / Data Analyst | — | 2 hours |
| 3 | **Trend analysis**: Data Analyst compares current month vs. prior month and vs. same month prior year — overall satisfaction score trend, NPS trend, topic frequency changes, store-level heatmap (bottom 20 stores by CSAT), category-level sentiment (which product categories generate most negative feedback) | Data Analyst | Customer Experience Manager | 3 hours |
| 4 | **Strategic insight synthesis**: Customer Experience Manager synthesizes analysis into monthly VOC Insight Report — top 5 positive themes, top 5 negative themes, top 3 emerging risks, recommended actions (with responsible team and deadline); examples: "Tile category complaint rate increased 40% due to breakage in transit → Packaging review with W1546 vendor audit"; "South Luzon stores underperforming on CSAT → W67 store performance review" | Customer Experience Manager | VP Marketing | 3 hours |

| 6 | **Action item distribution & tracking**: Customer Experience Manager distributes specific action items to responsible teams — merchandising (product quality issues), store operations (service issues), supply chain (availability issues), ecommerce (platform issues); tracks action item completion in closed-loop system (W87) | Customer Experience Manager | VP Marketing | 2 hours |
| 7 | **Quarterly deep dive**: Every quarter, Customer Experience Manager conducts deeper analysis — customer segment comparison (B2C vs. B2B vs. trade), loyalty tier analysis, competitor benchmark (where data available), correlation analysis (satisfaction vs. spend vs. churn); presents to executive team | Customer Experience Manager | CEO | 8 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
