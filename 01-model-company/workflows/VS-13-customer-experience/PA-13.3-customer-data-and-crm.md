## W65. Customer Satisfaction Measurement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Ongoing measurement program; continuous collection and periodic review |
| **Frequency** | Continuous survey collection; monthly reporting; quarterly deep analysis |
| **Volume** | Target: ~5,000–10,000 survey responses/month (from ~2.8M POS transactions ≈ 0.2–0.4% response rate) |
| **Owner** | Marketing — CS Manager |
| **Participants** | CS Manager, VP Store Operations, Store Managers, BI Analyst |

### Measurement Methods

| Method | Frequency | Sample | Channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **POS receipt survey** | Continuous | Every transaction (opt-in) | QR code printed on every receipt linking to 3-question mobile survey; incentivized with 50 loyalty points per completed survey |
| **Post-visit email** | Continuous | Loyalty members who made a purchase | Automated email 24 hours after purchase with 5-question NPS-style survey |
| **Ecommerce post-order survey** | Continuous | All completed ecommerce orders | Automated email after delivery confirmation with product and delivery satisfaction rating |
| **Mystery shopping** | Monthly | All 200 stores, 2–3 visits/store/month (per W566) | External mystery shopping agency evaluates store experience against standardized checklist (greeting, product knowledge, wait time, checkout, store cleanliness) |
| **Annual customer survey** | Annual | 10,000–20,000 loyalty members | Comprehensive brand perception and satisfaction survey via email/app notification |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CS Manager configures survey instruments in system: POS receipt QR code generation, post-visit email templates with survey link, ecommerce post-order trigger | CS Manager | CMO | 4 hours (setup) + 1 hour/quarter (maintenance) |
| 2 | System collects survey responses continuously: POS receipt scans, email click-throughs, ecommerce ratings; all responses linked to transaction and store (where applicable) | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | System calculates monthly CSAT score (1–5 scale) and NPS (0–10 scale → % Promoters − % Detractors) per store and chain-wide | System | — | Automated |
| 4 | Monthly: CS Manager generates CSAT/NPS dashboard: scores by store, by department, trend vs. prior month and vs. target (≥ 85% CSAT per profile §12.3); flags stores below threshold for Store Manager attention | CS Manager | VP Store Operations | 1 hour/month |
| 5 | Monthly: VP Store Operations reviews CSAT/NPS scores in management committee meeting (W35 step 14); bottom 10 stores flagged for improvement action plans | VP Store Operations | COO | 30 min/month |
| 6 | Quarterly: CS Manager presents comprehensive satisfaction analysis: trends, root causes of low scores, complaint correlation (W41), mystery shopping findings; recommends specific improvement actions | CS Manager | CMO | 2 hours/quarter |
| 7 | Mystery shopping results: CS Manager shares individual store reports with Store Managers; Store Manager develops corrective action for identified gaps; results factored into Store Manager performance evaluation | CS Manager / Store Manager | VP Store Operations | Per quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Survey platform integration: POS receipt QR code, email survey, ecommerce post-order survey; all feeding into centralized CSAT/NPS dashboard (W65.2)
- Survey response linked to transaction number, store, and customer account (where identifiable) for drill-down analysis (W65.2)
- CSAT/NPS calculation and trending per store, region, and chain-wide (W65.3–4)
- Mystery shopping results entry and reporting (W65.7)
- Integration with W5B (POS receipt printing — QR code), W17 (loyalty points incentive for survey completion), W35 (management reporting), W41 (complaint correlation — low CSAT stores often correlate with high complaint volume)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Low survey response rate**: With a target of 5,000–10,000 responses from ~2.8M monthly POS transactions (0.2–0.4% response rate), the sample may not be representative; dissatisfied customers may be over-represented in voluntary survey responses
- **Mystery shopping cost and coverage**: PHP 100–150K/month for the all-store program (~400–600 visits/month per W566) is the cost baseline; coverage pressure concentrates on keeping every store visited 2–3 times monthly
- **CSAT/NPS gaming by Store Managers**: When CSAT scores are tied to Store Manager performance evaluation, there is a risk of employees encouraging only satisfied customers to complete surveys or manipulating receipt QR code distribution
- **Actionability of survey data**: Generic satisfaction scores are easy to collect but difficult to translate into specific operational improvements; without granular, comment-level analysis and cross-referencing to complaint data (W41), CSAT reporting becomes a passive metric rather than a change driver

## W87. Customer Feedback-to-Action Loop

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly complaint analytics review (W41.11); or quarterly CSAT/NPS deep analysis (W65.6); or recurring pattern identified by BI Analyst |
| **Frequency** | Monthly review; quarterly action planning; annual strategic review |
| **Volume** | ~2,000–3,000 complaints/month + ~5,000–10,000 survey responses/month feeding into the loop |
| **Owner** | CS Manager (operational); VP Store Operations (store-level actions); CMO (strategic) |
| **Participants** | CS Manager, VP Store Operations, Regional Managers, Category Managers, Training Officer, BI Analyst, CMO |

### Background

BuildRight collects rich customer feedback through multiple channels: complaint tickets (W41), CSAT/NPS surveys (W65), loyalty program data (W17), mystery shopping results (W65), and social media monitoring. However, there is no formal workflow for systematically converting this feedback into operational improvements, product changes, or service enhancements. This workflow closes the loop — ensuring that customer insights drive continuous improvement rather than remaining as reports.

### Monthly Feedback Review (by day 15 of each month, after W41 and W65 reporting)

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CS Manager compiles monthly feedback synthesis from: (a) W41 complaint analytics — top 5 complaint categories, top 10 stores by complaint volume, recurring root causes, unresolved escalations, (b) W65 CSAT/NPS scores — stores below threshold (< 85% CSAT), trending decline, specific department-level issues from survey comments, (c) W17 loyalty data — points redemption patterns, tier movement, enrollment drop-offs, loyalty program feedback, (d) social media sentiment — review of mentions, reviews, and direct messages aggregated by social media monitoring tool, (e) W58 corporate account feedback — Sales Rep reports of customer dissatisfaction with pricing, delivery, or service | CS Manager | CMO | 4 hours/month |
| 2 | CS Manager categorizes feedback into actionable themes: (a) **Quick wins** — individual issues resolvable by a single department within 1 week (e.g., specific store cleanliness issue, individual staff behavior, specific SKU pricing error), (b) **Process improvements** — recurring issues requiring process or procedure changes across multiple locations (e.g., BOPIS pickup SLA not met consistently, DSD receiving causing customer wait times, returns policy confusion), (c) **Training needs** — patterns indicating skill or knowledge gaps (e.g., product knowledge complaints in specific departments, POS error rates by store), (d) **Product/service changes** — feedback suggesting product assortment gaps, new service needs, or pricing concerns, (e) **Strategic themes** — fundamental customer experience shifts (e.g., channel preference changes, competitive threats, brand perception issues) | CS Manager | CMO | 2 hours/month |
| 3 | CS Manager assigns quick wins to responsible department with specific action items and 1-week resolution SLA: (a) store-level issues → Regional Manager → Store Manager, (b) product issues → Category Manager, (c) pricing issues → Pricing Analyst, (d) delivery issues → DC Dispatch / Fleet Manager, (e) website/app issues → Ecom Team | CS Manager | Department Head | 1 hour/month |
| 4 | CS Manager follows up on prior month's quick win resolution status; reports completion rate to CMO; unresolved items escalated to department head | CS Manager | CMO | 30 min/month |

### Quarterly Action Planning (aligned with W35 quarterly management review)

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | CS Manager prepares quarterly customer experience improvement report: (a) complaint trend analysis — month-over-month volume, resolution rate, SLA compliance trend, (b) CSAT/NPS trend with store heat map, (c) top 5 recurring root causes with frequency and business impact (estimated revenue at risk from unresolved issues), (d) process improvement recommendations — specific process changes with expected impact, (e) training recommendations — specific programs with target audience, (f) product/service change recommendations — new products, discontinued products, service enhancements based on customer demand | CS Manager | CMO | 8 hours/quarter |
| 6 | CS Manager presents quarterly report to cross-functional customer experience review meeting — attended by VP Store Operations, VP Merchandising, Training Officer, BI Analyst, and CMO; meeting reviews each recommendation and assigns owners, deadlines, and success metrics | CS Manager / CMO | COO | 2 hours/quarter |
| 7 | **Process improvements**: VP Store Operations (for operational changes), VP Merchandising (for product/pricing changes), or CIO (for system changes) implements approved process improvements; changes documented in system with expected completion date; CS Manager tracks implementation status | Department Head | COO | Varies |
| 8 | **Training needs**: Training Officer incorporates approved training recommendations into W51 training calendar — develops targeted training materials for identified gaps (e.g., product knowledge for specific departments, returns handling refresher for CSRs, delivery communication for ecommerce team); training completion tracked and reported to CS Manager | Training Officer | HR Head | Per W51 |
| 9 | **Product/service changes**: Category Managers review product feedback recommendations during next W1 quarterly assortment review; CMO reviews service enhancement proposals for feasibility and budget; approved changes implemented through existing workflows (W1, W13, W40, W58) | Category Manager / CMO | VP Merchandising / COO | Per existing workflows |
| 10 | CS Manager tracks all quarterly action items to completion; reports status at next quarterly review; closed action items archived with outcome measurement (did the action reduce complaints or improve CSAT?) | CS Manager | CMO | 2 hours/quarter |

### Annual Strategic Review (aligned with W26 annual budget cycle)

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | CMO and CS Manager prepare annual customer experience strategic review: (a) year-over-year CSAT/NPS trend, (b) complaint volume and resolution efficiency trend, (c) customer retention rate (loyalty member active rate, trade account retention), (d) voice-of-customer themes for the year, (e) competitive customer experience benchmarking (where available), (f) investment recommendations — technology, staffing, training, or service enhancements to improve customer experience, (g) proposed customer experience KPIs for next year | CMO / CS Manager | CEO | 8 hours/year |
| 12 | CMO presents annual CX review to CEO and management committee; approved investments included in next year's budget (W26) | CMO | CEO | 1 hour/year |

### Feedback Loop Tracking Dashboard

| Metric | Source | Frequency | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quick win resolution rate | W87.4 | Monthly | ≥ 90% within 1-week SLA |
| Process improvement implementation rate | W87.7 | Quarterly | ≥ 80% of approved improvements implemented on schedule |
| Training completion rate for CX-targeted programs | W87.8 | Quarterly | 100% completion by target date |
| CSAT/NPS improvement vs. prior quarter | W65 | Quarterly | Positive trend in bottom 10 stores |
| Complaint reduction in top 5 root causes | W41 | Quarterly | ≥ 15% reduction per quarter |
| Revenue at risk from unresolved feedback | W87.5c | Quarterly | Decreasing trend |

### System Touchpoints
- Feedback synthesis dashboard: aggregates W41 complaint data, W65 CSAT/NPS scores, W17 loyalty insights, social media sentiment, and W58 corporate account feedback into a single view (W87.1)
- Action item tracking: assigns ownership, deadline, and success metrics per recommendation; tracks completion status (W87.3–4, 6–10)
- Quarterly improvement report generation with trend analysis and store heat map (W87.5)
- Annual CX strategic review archive with year-over-year comparison (W87.11)
- Integration with W1 (assortment changes from product feedback), W13 (promotional adjustments), W41 (complaint data source), W51 (training improvements), W65 (CSAT/NPS data source), W67 (store performance review — CX action items in store scorecard), W83 (marketing campaign feedback)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Feedback-to-action disconnect**: The most common failure mode is rich feedback collection with poor execution follow-through; department heads may deprioritize CX action items in favor of operational urgencies, and the CS Manager lacks authority to compel compliance
- **Cross-functional meeting attendance**: Getting VP Store Operations, VP Merchandising, Training Officer, BI Analyst, and CMO together quarterly for a CX-focused meeting competes with S&OP (W133), budget review (W26), and other leadership calendar commitments
- **Quick win SLA slippage**: 1-week resolution SLA for quick wins is aggressive when issues require store-level action across multiple locations; unresolved quick wins accumulate and erode confidence in the feedback loop
- **ROI measurement difficulty**: Demonstrating the financial impact of CX improvements (e.g., "did addressing complaint root cause X increase CSAT by Y points and revenue by Z?") requires sophisticated attribution analysis that may exceed BI Analyst capacity

## W156. Customer Data Platform (CDP) & Hyper-Personalization

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Real-time customer interaction (web visit, POS sale, app notification) |
| **Frequency** | Continuous |
| **Volume** | 600,000+ member profiles |
| **Owner** | Digital Marketing Manager |
| **Participants** | Data Analyst, CRM Team, IT, CMO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Data Ingestion**: Aggregate data from POS (W5), Ecommerce (W11), and Loyalty (W17) into a unified "Golden Record" | System | IT Mgr | Real-time |
| 2 | **Segmentation**: System auto-assigns segments based on behavior: "DIY Hobbyist," "Professional Contractor," "Lapsed Buyer" | Data Analyst | Digital Mktg Mgr | Weekly |
| 3 | **Triggered Journeys**: Auto-trigger email/SMS based on events: "Abandoned Cart," "Project Follow-up," or "Refill Reminder" (for paint/consumables) | CRM Team | Digital Mktg Mgr | Automated |
| 4 | **Personalized Offers**: Push unique promo codes to the Mobile App based on individual purchase history | Digital Mktg Mgr | CMO | 1 hour |

### System Touchpoints
- CDP data ingestion pipeline: real-time integration with POS (W5B), Ecommerce platform (W11), and Loyalty engine (W17) for unified "Golden Record" creation
- Customer segmentation engine: behavior-based auto-segmentation with configurable segment definitions (DIY Hobbyist, Professional Contractor, Lapsed Buyer, etc.)
- Marketing automation platform: triggered email/SMS journey builder with event-based triggers (Abandoned Cart, Project Follow-up, Refill Reminder, Purchase Anniversary)
- Mobile App push notification system: personalized offer delivery with unique promo code generation and redemption tracking
- Customer 360 profile view: unified dashboard accessible by CS Agents (W258), Sales Associates (W112), and Marketing for real-time customer interaction context
- Integration with W5B (POS transaction data), W11 (ecommerce behavioral data), W17 (loyalty points and tier data), W41 (complaint history), W65 (CSAT/NPS scores), W258 (support ticket history)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Data quality and matching accuracy**: Creating a reliable "Golden Record" from POS, ecommerce, and loyalty data requires robust identity resolution; Philippine consumers may use different mobile numbers, email addresses, or names across channels, leading to duplicate or fragmented profiles
- **Segmentation model staleness**: Auto-segmentation based on purchase behavior degrades over time if not refreshed; a "Professional Contractor" who hasn't purchased in 6 months should be reclassified as "Lapsed" but manual segment overrides are rarely maintained
- **Personalization creep concern**: Philippine consumers are becoming more aware of data privacy under RA 10173; overly aggressive personalization (e.g., "We noticed you bought paint last month, buy more!") can feel intrusive and trigger opt-outs or complaints
- **CRM team capacity**: With 600,000+ member profiles generating millions of data points, the CRM team must balance personalization sophistication with execution bandwidth; building and maintaining triggered journeys for every customer segment is a full-time analytical function


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "Golden Record" creation (Step 1, already system-executed).
- System auto-assign of "assigns segments based on behavior" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-remind of "Refill Reminder" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-280 (ensure controlled execution — Customer Data & CRM (PA-13.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
- Initial CDP setup and data integration: 4–6 weeks project
- Ongoing data quality monitoring: 2 hours/week (Data Analyst)
- Segment review and refinement: 1 hour/week (Data Analyst + Digital Marketing Manager)
- Triggered journey configuration: 2–4 hours/campaign
- Personalized offer creation: 1 hour/campaign
- **Total ongoing effort**: ~20–26 hours/week across the CRM/Digital Marketing team (per the Staffing Implication lines below)

### Staffing Implication
- **Digital Marketing Manager**: oversees CDP strategy, segmentation model, and personalized offer campaigns (~4–6 hours/week). Absorbed within existing Marketing role.
- **Data Analyst (1)**: dedicated to CDP data quality monitoring, segment review, and Golden Record maintenance (~8–10 hours/week). Absorbed within BI/Data team.
- **CRM Team (2–3)**: triggered journey configuration, personalized offer creation, and campaign execution (~8–10 hours/week combined). Absorbed within existing CRM/Digital Marketing team.
- **No incremental headcount** — CDP is a technology platform operated by existing Marketing and BI staff.

## W560. Customer Account Dormancy Identification & Deactivation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly dormancy scan schedule (1st business day of each month); quarterly deactivation batch |
| **Frequency** | Monthly scan; quarterly deactivation batch processing |
| **Volume** | ~2,000–3,000 newly dormant loyalty accounts per month (no transaction for 12 months); ~5,200 trade accounts and ~200 corporate accounts monitored quarterly; ~5,000–8,000 loyalty accounts deactivated per quarter |
| **Owner** | CRM Manager / AR Supervisor (for B2B accounts) |
| **Participants** | CRM Analyst, CRM Manager, AR Supervisor (R for B2B), Sales Manager (C for B2B relationship), Customer Service Lead (I for inbound inquiries) |

### Background

W84 covers customer account reactivation for dormant accounts. W24 covers credit application and approval. W156 covers the customer data platform. W328 covers credit limit periodic review. However, no workflow covers the front-end process of identifying dormant accounts, attempting re-engagement, and executing deactivation — the systematic process that feeds into W84's reactivation workflow. For a company with ~600,000 loyalty members, ~5,200 trade accounts, and ~200 corporate accounts, account dormancy is a natural lifecycle event. Dormant loyalty accounts create data quality issues (stale contact information) and inflate database costs. Dormant B2B accounts represent potential bad debt risk and tie up credit capacity that could be allocated to active customers.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monthly dormancy scan**: CRM system identifies accounts meeting dormancy criteria — (a) Loyalty: no transaction or login for 12 months; (b) Trade: no invoice or payment for 6 months; (c) Corporate: no order for 6 months and no active project per W162 | System | CRM Manager | Automated |
| 2 | **Dormancy classification**: (a) Early dormancy (12–18 months loyalty / 6–9 months B2B) — retain with re-engagement; (b) Extended dormancy (18–24 months loyalty / 9–12 months B2B) — prepare for deactivation; (c) Dead account (> 24 months loyalty / > 12 months B2B) — deactivate | CRM Analyst | CRM Manager | 2 hours |
| 3 | **Early dormancy re-engagement (loyalty)**: automated email/SMS campaign — "We miss you!" with personalized offer (bonus points, discount voucher) per CRM-007 | System / CRM Analyst | CRM Manager | Automated + 1 hour review |
| 4 | **Early dormancy re-engagement (B2B)**: Sales Manager personal outreach — call or visit to understand reason (switched to competitor, business closure, project completed) | Sales Manager | CRM Manager | 30 min/account |
| 5 | Data quality review for extended dormant accounts: verify contact information, update customer master per W253 if returned mail/bounced email | CRM Analyst | CRM Manager | 4 hours/quarter |
| 6 | **B2B extended dormancy**: AR Supervisor reviews outstanding balance — if zero balance, proceed to deactivation; if outstanding balance, escalate to W108 (credit collection) before deactivation | AR Supervisor | CRM Manager | 15 min/account |
| 7 | **Corporate extended dormancy**: verify no pending contract or project milestone per W162/W165 before deactivation | CRM Analyst / Sales Manager | CRM Manager | 15 min/account |
| 8 | **Quarterly deactivation batch**: CRM Manager approves deactivation list; system updates account status to "Inactive" (not deleted — retained per NFR-006 data retention) | CRM Manager | VP Marketing | 4–6 hours/quarter |
| 9 | **B2B deactivation**: credit limit set to zero per W253; Sales Manager notified; account remains in AR for historical reference | AR Supervisor / System | CRM Manager | Automated + 15 min review |
| 10 | **Loyalty deactivation**: points balance preserved but frozen; account marked inactive; customer can reactivate via W84 within 24 months with points restored | System | CRM Manager | Automated |
| 11 | Deactivation confirmation: system generates deactivation report with count by category and reason; CRM Manager reviews for accuracy | System / CRM Analyst | CRM Manager | 1 hour |
| 12 | Quarterly dormancy analytics: dormancy rate trend, re-engagement success rate, average dormancy period, root cause analysis (price, service, location, competitor) | CRM Analyst | CRM Manager | 3–4 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints

- CRM-019 (new requirement for account dormancy management with automated scanning and deactivation)
- CRM-002 (customer master B2C — data update for dormant accounts)
- CRM-003 (trade account management — B2B deactivation workflow)
- CRM-008 (credit application — credit limit set to zero on deactivation)
- W84 (account reactivation — reactivation path for deactivated accounts)
- W24 (credit application — credit status review for B2B deactivation)
- W156 (customer data platform — data quality monitoring)
- W328 (credit limit review — B2B review partner for dormant accounts)

## W756. Customer Post-Purchase Follow-Up & Satisfaction Verification

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer completes high-value purchase (> PHP 10,000) or installation/service order (W138/W544) |
| **Frequency** | Daily; triggered by qualifying transactions |
| **Volume** | ~4,500–5,000 qualifying transactions/day chain-wide (~5% of daily transactions by count, ~40% by value) |
| **Owner** | Customer Service Representative |
| **Participants** | CSR, Customer, Installation/Service Partner (if applicable) |

### Background
High-value purchases (power tools, appliances, building material packages, installations) represent 40% of revenue but only 5% of transactions. These customers have higher lifetime value and higher churn risk if dissatisfied. Proactive post-purchase follow-up catches issues early, generates positive reviews, and drives repeat business through the loyalty program (W17).

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Follow-up list generation — System generates daily follow-up list: (a) transactions > PHP 10,000 in past 48–72 hours, (b) completed installation/service orders (W138) in past 24–48 hours, (c) special orders (W545) picked up in past 48 hours | System | — | Automated |
| 2 | Channel selection — System selects follow-up channel based on customer preference: (a) SMS with satisfaction link (most common), (b) phone call for transactions > PHP 50,000 or installation completion, (c) email for project/account customers | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | Automated satisfaction survey — System sends 3-question survey: (a) "How would you rate your experience?" (1–5), (b) "Did the product/service meet your expectations?" (Yes/Partially/No), (c) "Would you recommend BuildRight to a friend?" (Yes/No) | System | — | Automated |
| 4 | Manual follow-up (high-value) — For transactions > PHP 50,000 or installation orders: CSR calls customer within 48 hours; follows structured script: product satisfaction, installation quality (if applicable), any issues or concerns, recommendation for complementary products | CSR | Department Supervisor | 5–10 min |
| 5 | Issue escalation — If customer reports dissatisfaction (survey rating ≤ 2 or negative phone call): auto-generates complaint ticket in W41/W258 for resolution; if product defect, triggers W33 warranty or W110 supplier CAPA | System / CSR | Customer Service Manager | 2 min |
| 6 | Positive follow-up action — If customer is satisfied: (a) prompt for product review on W510 ecommerce, (b) suggest loyalty program benefits if not yet enrolled (W551), (c) recommend complementary products based on purchase | System / CSR | — | Automated |
| 7 | Response analytics — System tracks: response rate, satisfaction score distribution, issue rate, escalation rate, review generation rate; feeds to W156 CDP for customer profiling | System | — | Automated |
| 8 | Monthly follow-up program review — Customer Service Manager reviews program effectiveness: response rate trends, satisfaction scores, issue resolution rate, repeat purchase rate for followed-up customers vs. control group | Customer Service Manager | Head of Customer Service | 2 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Transaction filtering engine — qualifying transaction identification by value and category
- Multi-channel follow-up engine — SMS, phone, email with preference-based routing
- Survey module — 3-question satisfaction survey with automated scoring
- W41/W258 complaint integration — auto-escalation for dissatisfied responses
- W510 product review integration — review prompt for satisfied customers
- W551 loyalty enrollment — enrollment prompt for non-members
- W156 CDP — follow-up response data enriches customer profile
- W610/W617 customer success integration — B2B follow-up coordination

### Pain Points / Risks
- Survey fatigue — customers receiving multiple follow-ups for different purchases may ignore or mark as spam; limit to 1 follow-up per customer per week
- Low response rates — typical retail post-purchase survey response rate is 10–20%; without adequate volume, data may not be representative
- Phone call intrusiveness — some customers perceive follow-up calls as intrusive; respect preference settings per NFR-010 data privacy
- Issue identification vs. resolution capacity — proactive follow-up may uncover more issues than the customer service team can handle, creating backlog


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-generate of "generates daily follow-up list" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-count of "Channel selection — System selects follow-up channel based on" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-send of "sends 3-question survey" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-280 (ensure controlled execution — Customer Data & CRM (PA-13.3).)
- operational: Customer Service Representative review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Automated follow-up: near-zero marginal cost. Manual follow-up: 5–10 min per high-value transaction × ~100/day chain-wide = ~500–1,000 min/day = ~8–17 hours/day distributed across CSRs. Monthly review: 2 hours.

### Staffing Implication
No incremental headcount for automated follow-up. Manual high-value follow-up: ~100 calls/day × 7 min avg = ~12 hours/day distributed across existing CSR team (absorbed by call center W259). Monthly review: Customer Service Manager.

## W782. Customer B2B Order-to-Cash Cycle Monitoring & Proactive Communication

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | B2B customer order placement; or weekly proactive monitoring cycle |
| **Frequency** | Continuous order monitoring; weekly proactive communication cycle |
| **Volume** | ~5,200 trade accounts + ~200 corporate accounts; ~3,500 AR invoices/month |
| **Owner** | Key Account Manager |
| **Participants** | Key Account Manager, Sales Coordinator, AR Accountant, Logistics Coordinator, Customer |

### Background
B2B customers (trade contractors, developers, government projects) represent 40% of BuildRight Depot's revenue and have significantly more complex order-to-cash cycles than walk-in retail: quotation (W542) → order → fulfillment → delivery → invoicing → payment collection. Delays or issues at any stage impact customer satisfaction, revenue recognition, and cash collection. While individual workflows exist for each step (W162-W166 for project sales, W108 for collections, W617 for customer success), this workflow provides end-to-end order-to-cash cycle monitoring — proactively identifying stalled orders, delivery issues, or payment delays before they become customer complaints.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Weekly order pipeline review**: Key Account Manager reviews all active B2B orders: (a) orders pending fulfillment ( > SLA target: 3 business days for DC stock, 10 days for procurement), (b) orders with partial delivery — outstanding items, (c) delivered but not invoiced, (d) invoiced but not yet due, (e) overdue invoices per W108 aging | KAM | — | 2 hours/week |
| 2 | **Stalled order intervention**: For orders stalled at any stage: (a) fulfillment delay — contact DC per W584 to expedite or identify stockout, (b) delivery delay — contact Logistics per W268 for carrier status, (c) invoicing delay — coordinate with AR per W8 to ensure timely invoice generation, (d) proactively communicate status to customer per W708 with revised timeline | KAM | Sales Manager | 30 min/stalled order |
| 3 | **Pre-delivery customer confirmation**: Before dispatch of large B2B orders (> PHP 100,000): (a) Sales Coordinator calls customer to confirm delivery readiness (site access, unloading crew, payment terms), (b) confirm delivery address and special instructions, (c) verify credit limit has available headroom per W572, (d) resolve any issues before truck dispatch | Sales Coordinator | KAM | 15 min/order |
| 4 | **Delivery confirmation and invoice trigger**: Post-delivery: (a) system generates delivery confirmation with POD per W592, (b) AR Accountant generates sales invoice per W8 with correct payment terms (Net 30/60/90 per W295), (c) system sends invoice to customer via W708 (email with PDF attachment), (d) KAM follows up within 24 hours to confirm invoice receipt | AR Accountant / System | KAM | Automated + 10 min follow-up |
| 5 | **Proactive payment follow-up**: Before invoice due date: (a) T-7 days: friendly reminder per W708 ("Your invoice #[X] for PHP [Y] is due on [Date]. Please arrange payment."), (b) T-3 days: phone call or SMS to confirm payment scheduling, (c) Due date: payment received check; if not — escalate per W108 collection process | KAM / AR Accountant | — | 5-10 min/account |
| 6 | **Monthly order-to-cash metrics**: KAM reports: (a) average order-to-delivery time (target: < 5 business days), (b) delivery-to-invoice time (target: < 2 business days), (c) average DSO per W108 (target: ≤ 45 days), (d) order-to-cash cycle time (target: < 50 days), (e) stalled order rate, (f) customer satisfaction with B2B service per W617 QBR | KAM | Head of Trade / Account Management | 2 hours/month |
| 7 | **Quarterly top account deep dive**: For top 50 B2B accounts: (a) order-to-cash cycle analysis per account, (b) recurring bottlenecks identification, (c) process improvement recommendations, (d) customer feedback integration per W617 QBR | KAM | Sales Manager | 4 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints

- Unified order management (W536) — end-to-end order visibility
- W162 quotation & bid management — order origin tracking
- W584 DC operations — fulfillment status
- W592 delivery tracking — delivery and POD status
- W8 accounts receivable — invoice and payment tracking
- W108 customer credit collection — payment aging and escalation
- W572 customer credit monitoring — credit limit verification
- W617 B2B customer success — QBR integration
- W708 customer communication — proactive notification at each stage
- W295 payment terms master — term enforcement

### Pain Points / Risks

- Visibility gaps — order status across fulfillment, delivery, and finance systems may not be unified; manual tracking creates information gaps
- Customer communication overload — excessive proactive contacts may annoy B2B customers; calibrate frequency based on account tier
- Credit limit bottlenecks — order-to-cash stalled because credit limit reached mid-cycle; real-time credit monitoring per W572 mitigates
- Manual invoicing delays — if invoice generation requires manual intervention (complex project billing per W165), delivery-to-invoice time increases
- B2B payment culture — Philippine construction industry typically has slow payment practices; DSO management requires persistent follow-up without straining relationships


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "Weekly order pipeline review" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "Logistics per W268 for carrier" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "verify credit limit has available headroom per W572" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-280 (ensure controlled execution — Customer Data & CRM (PA-13.3).)
- operational: Key Account Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate

Weekly: pipeline review (2 hours) + stalled intervention (varies) + proactive follow-up (~2 hours for 50 accounts) = ~6 hours/week. Monthly metrics: 2 hours. Quarterly deep dive: 4 hours.

### Staffing Implication

Absorbed within existing Key Account Manager and Sales Coordinator roles. Weekly time: ~6 hours per KAM. No incremental headcount.
