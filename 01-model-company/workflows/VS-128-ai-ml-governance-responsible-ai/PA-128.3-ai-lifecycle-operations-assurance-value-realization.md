### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | AAP runs the quarterly candidate-scoring cycle: the per-workflow Automation Opportunity inventory plus VS-133 process-mining surfaced candidates are scored hours × frequency × error rate × feasibility and ranked into the intake backlog | AAP Platform Lead / Evaluation-AI-QA | AAP Platform Lead | 4–6 hours/quarter |
| 2 | Owning product team proposes the agent with its Business Process Owner and a BPO case (task, volume, guardrail sketch, baseline human cost) | Owning team PM / BPO | Owning team Product Manager | 2–3 hours/candidate |
| 3 | SIB routes the sourcing per the sourcing-model §3 gate — platform-native in-suite automation = use EBS; custom agent on the SEP paved road = build (vendor agent products are not a sourcing exit; foundation-model APIs remain commodity procurement under tier-1 TPRM) — the same gate, one more domain | SIB / AAP Platform Lead | Head of EA | At monthly SIB |
| 4 | Agent is registered in the VS-128 registry (W3931) with a proposed risk tier; agent type, task scope, tool contracts, and owning team recorded — no AAP runtime identity is provisioned without this registry record | AI-Governance Liaison | AI Governance Lead | 1–2 hours/agent |
| 5 | Ethics/DPIA routing: any agent touching customers, employees, money, or personal data goes to the AI Ethics Review Board (W3952); an RA 10173 DPIA is opened where automated decisions affect data subjects (links to W3939) | AI Governance / DPO | AI Governance Lead | 1–2 hours/agent |
| 6 | Registered agent enters the evaluation pipeline (W5513); intake evidence — scorecards, SIB decision, registry record, DPIA disposition — archived for audit (links to W3949) | AAP Platform Lead | AAP Platform Lead | 0.5 hour/agent |

### System Touchpoints
- AAP intake/scoring pipeline fed by the per-workflow Automation Opportunity inventory and VS-133 process-mining event logs
- VS-128 agent/model registry (links to W3931) — the registration gate behind runtime-identity provisioning
- SIB Capability Sourcing Register (use-EBS/build decision record)
- DPIA toolkit & consent records (links to VS-91/W3939); ethics-review case log (links to W3952)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Shadow-agent risk**: teams deploy unregistered automation outside the VS-128 registry, bypassing tiering, ethics review, and the audit trail; mitigated by the registration gate (no runtime identity without a registry record — Step 4) and the W3949 controls-assurance sweep
- **Feasibility-overstatement risk**: intake scores overstate agent suitability (clean demo, messy production data), wasting build capacity; mitigated by the hours × frequency × error × feasibility scoring discipline and the owning team's BPO sign-off at Step 2
- **Sourcing-mismatch risk**: agents built where in-suite automation would have met the need; mitigated by the SIB use-EBS → build gate at Step 3

### Automation Opportunity
- Scoring pipeline auto-drafts candidate scorecards from the inventory and process-mining telemetry (replaces manual Step 1 collation).
- Registry record auto-drafted from the SIB decision and DPIA disposition (replaces manual Step 4 keying).

### Controls
- CTL-616 (ensure controlled execution — AI Lifecycle Operations, Assurance & Value Realization (PA-128.3).)
- operational: registration gate — no runtime identity without a VS-128 registry record
- operational: SIB use-EBS → build routing for every agent proposal

### Time Estimate
Scoring cycle ~4–6 hours/quarter plus SIB-pack admin ~2 hours/quarter = ~6–8 hours/quarter; × 4 quarters ≈ **~24–32 hours/year**. Registration & routing ~3–4 hours/agent × ~30–45 agents/year ≈ **~90–180 hours/year**. Total **~114–212 hours/year** (≈0.06–0.10 FTE), absorbed within the AAP platform team.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed within the sized AAP platform team (OM §5.3, 7 FTE) and existing VS-128 governance roles; no incremental headcount.
