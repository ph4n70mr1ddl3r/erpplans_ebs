## W5543. Price-File Integrity Event Response & Mass-Mispricing Rollback

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | A bad price-file push is detected — POS/ESL/shelf-label prices deviating from the approved price master beyond threshold (W3697 monitoring alarm, a store's shelf-label audit exception, a customer DTI complaint, or a margin-variance spike) — affecting > 50 SKUs or > 1 store |
| **Frequency** | Rare as a *mass* event; ~2–5 events/year chain-wide cross the threshold (single-SKU label errors are the W3697 daily-detection steady state, not this workflow) |
| **Volume** | 1 event × affected SKUs × affected stores × exposure duration (units sold in the mispriced window from POS data — the quantification base in Step 5); worst-case scenario: a master-to-store propagation error touching all 200 stores |
| **Owner** | Revenue Assurance Lead / Merchandising-Pricing |
| **Participants** | Merchandising Pricing Team (the W13 price-file execution canon owner), IT Platform (price-file pipeline ESB/integration), Store Operations (re-label execution via the W63 task channel), VP Legal / Compliance (RA 7581 Price Act / Consumer Act exposure), Customer Service (refund handling per W1622 tiers), Finance Controller (margin impact), DTI liaison (per W427 regulatory-desk pattern), Corporate Communications (if consumer-facing) |

### Background

The pricing surfaces are individually owned: W13 executes price-file changes, W3697 monitors override/accuracy integrity, W3698 watches promotion integrity, and VS-57 watches competitive/stale prices — but no workflow owned the *event*: confirming a mass deviation, deciding the customer-exposure posture (overcharge vs undercharge), executing a rollback through the W13 canon with a re-label sweep across stores, remediating overcharged customers, quantifying the margin leak, and handling a DTI complaint. The regulatory stakes are specific: RA 7581 (Price Act) SRP-mandated SKUs and the Consumer Act's price-tag regime expose the company to DTI enforcement even when the deviation was an innocent systems error, and an overcharge window is a refund obligation while an undercharge window is an unrecoverable margin leak (goodwill policy governs, not clawback). This workflow is the event canon; detection stays with W3697, rollback execution always rides W13, POS refund mechanics stay W1622/W1538.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Confirm & contain**: reproduce the deviation (staged vs master price comparison per SKU/store); localize the fault — master-data error, pipeline transform error, or store-sync failure; **freeze further price-file pushes** (change-control hold with IT) to stop propagating; scope the blast radius: SKUs × stores × window start (first affected push timestamp from the W13 file log) | Revenue Assurance Analyst / IT | Revenue Assurance Lead | 1–4 hrs |
| 2 | **Exposure & regulatory triage**: split the SKU set — overcharged vs undercharged vs SRP-mandated (RA 7581 list); VP Legal assesses DTI exposure (SRP violations are strict-liability notices even if accidental) and the conspicuous-error defense posture for undercharges; if a DTI complaint already arrived, the W658-pattern response desk opens with the corrected master and sync logs as exhibits | VP Legal / Revenue Assurance | VP Legal | 2–8 hrs |
| 3 | **Rollback & re-label sweep**: corrected file executes through the W13 canon (approve → distribute → verify POS/ESL sync at canary stores, then chain-wide); Store Operations issues the re-label task via the W63 channel with priority order: SRP-mandated SKUs, promo-endcap zones, high-velocity SKUs, then the tail; completion confirmed per store (label-count vs SKU-count match) | Merchandising Pricing / Store Operations | Revenue Assurance Lead | 4–24 hrs depending on scope |
| 4 | **Customer remediation**: overcharge window — refund the difference at the service desk on receipt/loyalty-lookup evidence (W1622 approval tiers; W1538 refund mechanics), proactive outreach for loyalty-identifiable purchasers, price-difference honor policy published if consumer-facing; undercharge window — no clawback beyond the conspicuous-error/legal posture Legal set in Step 2; goodwill points per the loyalty policy where brand damage occurred | Customer Service / Store Managers | Revenue Assurance Lead | 1–14 days |
| 5 | **Financial quantification**: compute exposure = Σ (price delta × units sold in window) per SKU/store from POS data; classify overcharge refunds (liability set aside per Controller) vs undercharge margin leak (revenue-leakage register per the VS-118.1 framework); feed the leakage-recovery analytics (W118.3) | Revenue Assurance Analyst / Finance Controller | Revenue Assurance Lead | 2–5 days |
| 6 | **Regulatory close-out**: respond to any DTI notice within deadline — corrective-evidence pack (master price history, push logs, rollback timestamps, remediation totals); compliance visit handled per the W658 inspection-response pattern; follow-up undertakings tracked to closure | VP Legal / Compliance | VP Legal | Per DTI deadline |

### Time Estimate
Per event: Steps 1–3 ≈ 40–120 staff-hours across Revenue Assurance, IT and store labor (a 200-store re-label sweep is the dominant term — ~20–60 store-hours per store at full scope); Steps 4–6 ≈ 40–160 staff-hours over the remediation fortnight; event-driven, no annual figure claimed. Steady-state sensor cost stays in W3697's daily detection.

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Regulatory risk**: SRP-mandated SKU violations draw DTI notices even for innocent errors; mitigated by the Step-2 triage placing SRP SKUs first in triage, rollback priority, and the Step-6 evidence-pack response
- **Propagation risk**: the fix itself re-propagates the error because the pipeline fault persists; mitigated by the change-control freeze (Step 1) and canary-store verification before chain-wide release (Step 3)
- **Remediation-parity risk**: overcharged customers without receipts or loyalty identity go unremediated; mitigated by the price-difference honor policy and proactive outreach to loyalty-identifiable purchasers (Step 4)
- **Quantification risk**: the window start is wrong (first affected push mis-dated), understating both refunds and leakage; mitigated by the W13 file-log timestamp reconstruction in Step 1
- **Recurrence risk**: root cause fixed but the pipeline lacks a gate; mitigated by the Step-7 pre-push diff gate and mass-deviation detector

### Automation Opportunity
- Mass-deviation detector: W3697 alert data auto-triggers this workflow's case with the blast-radius scope pre-computed (SKUs × stores × window) (replaces manual Step 1 scoping)
- Re-label campaign auto-tracks per-store completion with label-count reconciliation and escalates stragglers at 24 h (replaces manual Step 3 tracking)
- Refund-difference calculator pre-fills service-desk refunds from POS evidence in the exposure window (replaces manual Step 4 lookups)

- operational: every confirmed overcharge window produces a quantified refund liability and a remediation register entry reviewed by the Controller
