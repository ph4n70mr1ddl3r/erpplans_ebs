## W1787. Vendor Rebate Agreement Negotiation & Setup

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual vendor negotiation cycle; new vendor onboarding; contract renewal |
| **Frequency** | ~100–200 rebate agreements maintained; ~30–50 new/renewed per year |
| **Volume** | ~800–1,000 active vendors; ~15–25% have some form of rebate/incentive agreement (~100–200 agreements per this register) |
| **Owner** | VP Merchandising |
| **Participants** | VP Merchandising, Category Managers, Buyers, CFO, VP Supply Chain |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rebate agreement types negotiated with vendors: (a) **volume rebate**: % rebate on total purchases once annual volume threshold met (e.g., 2% rebate on all purchases after PHP 50M annual spend), (b) **growth rebate**: additional % rebate for year-over-year purchase growth (e.g., 1% additional if purchases grow > 10%), (c) **listing/introductory rebate**: flat payment or % rebate for agreeing to stock new products, (d) **quarterly performance rebate**: % rebate on quarterly purchases if targets met, (e) **category exclusivity rebate**: premium rebate for giving vendor dominant shelf space, (f) **new store opening rebate**: contribution from vendor toward new store setup costs | Category Managers / Buyers | VP Merchandising | Per negotiation |
| 2 | System setup of rebate agreement: (a) vendor number, agreement ID, effective dates, (b) rebate type and calculation method (% of purchases, fixed amount per unit, flat amount), (c) volume thresholds and tier structure, (d) eligible products (all purchases, specific categories, specific SKUs), (e) accrual basis: purchase-based or sales-based, (f) claim frequency (monthly, quarterly, annual), (g) required documentation for claim, (h) settlement method (credit note, cash payment, offset against AP) | Buyer / System | VP Merchandising | 30 min/agreement |
| 3 | CFO reviews rebate terms for margin impact: (a) project annual rebate income per agreement, (b) assess impact on vendor gross margin, (c) verify accrual methodology is consistent with PFRS, (d) approve agreements with total annual rebate value > PHP 5M | CFO | VP Merchandising | 1 day |
| 4 | Agreement stored in vendor contract management system; key terms communicated to AP team for accrual and claim processing | Buyer / AP | VP Merchandising | 1 day |


### System Touchpoints
- Rebate agreement types framework — volume, growth, new-store, and special rebates (step 1)
- Agreement setup in system — vendor, agreement ID, effective dates, calculation method, tiers (step 2)
- Margin-impact review by CFO — projected rebate income per agreement (step 3)
- Contract repository with terms communicated to AP for accrual (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Unattainable-threshold risk**: thresholds set above realistic volume make the rebate illusory while purchases are steered to the vendor; mitigated by CFO margin review of realistic earning (step 3)
- **Terms-in-system drift**: the executed agreement and the system's calculation parameters diverge; mitigated by AP verification of setup against the signed contract (steps 2, 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-document of "documentation for claim" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "methodology is consistent with PFRS" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-communication of "communicated to AP team for accrual and claim processing" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-209 (ensure vendor rebate accuracy & recognition.)
- operational: VP Merchandising review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Per new/renewed rebate agreement: system setup 30 min + CFO margin-impact review (batched ≈ 1 day per negotiation round) + contract filing ≈ 1–1.5 days of effort × ~30–50 agreements/year ≈ 30–75 days/year across ~100–200 maintained agreements.

## W1788. Rebate Accrual Estimation & Monthly Posting

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Month-end close (Day 1 of following month) |
| **Frequency** | Monthly |
| **Volume** | ~100–200 rebate agreements accrued |
| **Owner** | AP Manager |
| **Participants** | AP Manager, Accounting Manager, VP Merchandising |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System calculates estimated rebate accrual per active agreement: (a) volume rebate: YTD purchases × applicable rebate % (considering tier thresholds already reached), (b) growth rebate: estimated based on YTD actual purchases and projected full-year growth rate, (c) quarterly rebate: QTD purchases × applicable rate, (d) accrual = estimated full-year/quarterly rebate − previously accrued amount | System | AP Manager | Automated |
| 2 | AP Manager reviews accrual calculations: (a) verify purchase volumes match AP records (invoices paid to vendor), (b) check tier threshold calculations (is the vendor on track to hit threshold?), (c) compare accrual rate to historical claim rate (some vendors have 80–90% claim rate vs. 100% accrued; adjust accrual if consistently over/under-claimed), (d) flag agreements where vendor is significantly below volume threshold (reduce accrual accordingly) | AP Manager | Accounting Manager | 2–4 hours/month |
| 3 | Journal entry posted: (a) debit: accrued rebate receivable (asset), (b) credit: rebate income (contra-COGS — reduces cost of goods sold), (c) posted per vendor agreement, (d) entity-specific posting for 5-entity structure | AP Manager / System | Accounting Manager | Automated after review |
| 4 | Monthly: AP Manager prepares rebate accrual summary for Accounting Manager: total accrual by vendor, by category, by rebate type; YTD accrual vs. YTD claims received; projected annual rebate income | AP Manager | Accounting Manager | 1 day |

### System Touchpoints
- Trade Management monthly rebate accrual (fit-gap B10) — YTD purchases × applicable % with tier recognition (step 1)
- AP verification — purchase volumes vs. AP records and tier progress (step 2)
- Accrual journal entry — debit rebate receivable, credit contra-COGS (step 3)
- Monthly accrual summary by vendor/category/type (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Over-accrual risk**: Accruing 100% of projected rebates when vendors typically only pay 70–80% (documentation issues, disputes, vendor financial difficulty); results in reversal at year-end; mitigated by CTL-209 (vendor rebate accuracy & recognition)
- **Threshold estimation**: If vendor is at 90% of threshold in October, should we accrue assuming threshold will be met? Conservative approach: accrue only after threshold met
- **PFRS compliance**: Rebate income must be recognized using the same method consistently (net vs. gross); most retailers treat rebates as reduction of COGS

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-calculate of "calculates estimated rebate accrual per active agreement" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-record of "AP Manager reviews accrual calculations" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-post of "Journal entry posted" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-209 (ensure vendor rebate accuracy & recognition.)
- operational: AP Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
1 day elapsed per occurrence (1 day) — cadence: Monthly; annual effort is event-driven (annualize at realized volume).

## W1789. Volume-Based Rebate Tracking & Threshold Monitoring

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly purchase volume update |
| **Frequency** | Monthly review |
| **Volume** | ~200 vendors with volume thresholds |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Category Managers, Buyers, VP Merchandising |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System tracks YTD purchase volume per vendor against agreement thresholds: (a) display: vendor name, YTD purchases, threshold, gap to threshold, projected year-end purchases (based on run rate), (b) color coding: green (above threshold), yellow (80–100% of threshold), red (< 80% with time running out), (c) auto-notification to Category Manager when vendor crosses threshold | System | Category Manager | Automated dashboard |
| 2 | Category Manager reviews monthly: (a) vendors approaching threshold — can we push additional purchases to reach threshold (and earn rebate)? (b) vendors above threshold — is incremental volume pushing to next tier? (c) vendors below threshold — should we shift purchases to other vendors with better rebate terms? | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1–2 hours/month |
| 3 | For vendors approaching year-end below threshold: (a) Category Manager assesses if consolidation of orders to push vendor over threshold is economically justified (cost of additional purchases vs. rebate income), (b) if not justified: let threshold miss occur; adjust accrual down, (c) if justified: place additional orders before year-end | Category Manager / Buyer | VP Merchandising | As needed |


### System Touchpoints
- YTD volume tracking per vendor against thresholds with progress display (step 1)
- Category Manager monthly review — push/consolidate decisions near thresholds (steps 2–3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Threshold-miss risk**: purchases fall just short, forfeiting the entire rebate tier; mitigated by threshold monitoring with consolidation decisions near year-end (steps 1, 3)
- **Pull-forward distortion**: orders accelerated solely to cross thresholds inflate inventory; mitigated by carrying-cost assessment in the consolidation decision (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-track of "tracks YTD purchase volume per vendor against agreement thresholds" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "Category Manager reviews monthly" (replaces manual Step 2).

### Controls
- CTL-209 (ensure vendor rebate accuracy & recognition.)
- operational: Category Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Monthly volume-threshold review 1–2 hours ≈ 12–24 hours/year across ~200 threshold vendors (YTD tracking dashboard automated); year-end push-or-forfeit decisions as needed.

## W1790. Growth Incentive & New Store Opening Rebate Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New store opening; annual growth target review |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 new stores/year trigger new store rebates; annual growth assessment |
| **Volume** | ~50–100 vendors with growth/new store incentives |
| **Owner** | VP Merchandising |
| **Participants** | VP Merchandising, Category Managers, VP Store Operations |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | New store opening rebate: (a) key vendors provide per-store contribution toward opening costs (shelf fixtures, signage, initial stock discount), (b) typical structure: PHP 50K–500K per new store depending on vendor size and category, (c) triggered by proof of new store opening (photos, store certificate), (d) tracked per vendor per store opening | Category Manager / Buyer | VP Merchandising | Per new store |
| 2 | Growth incentive: (a) vendor offers additional rebate if BuildRight's purchases grow X% year-over-year (e.g., 1% additional rebate for > 10% growth), (b) system tracks YTD growth rate per vendor, (c) Category Manager assesses if growth target is achievable; adjusts procurement strategy if near target, (d) growth incentive accrual added to base rebate accrual per W1788 | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | Quarterly |
| 3 | Claim submission: Category Manager compiles evidence of new store openings (certificate, photos, grand opening documentation); submits to vendor for rebate payment | Category Manager | — | Per opening |


### System Touchpoints
- New-store rebate tracking — per-store vendor contributions to opening costs (step 1)
- Growth-incentive tracking — incremental rebate on YoY purchase growth (step 2)
- Claim evidence compilation — certificates, photos, grand-opening documentation (step 3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Unclaimed-contribution risk**: vendor opening contributions agreed verbally but never claimed; mitigated by evidence-based claim submission (step 3)
- **Growth-attribute error**: growth rebates claimed on volume that merely shifted from another vendor; mitigated by growth tracking net of cannibalization (step 2)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "New store opening rebate" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-track of "tracks YTD growth rate per vendor" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "documentation" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-358 (ensure controlled execution — Rebate Agreement & Accrual Management (PA-39.1).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
Per new-store rebate claim ~0.5–1 day (evidence compilation and submission) × ~10–15 openings/year ≈ 5–15 days/year; quarterly growth-incentive assessment within vendor reviews across ~50–100 incentive vendors.

## W1791. Special Pricing Agreement (SPA) Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | One-time special pricing request from vendor for specific customer/project |
| **Frequency** | ~200–300 SPAs/year |
| **Volume** | Primarily for B2B/project sales per VS-11 |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Category Manager, Buyer, Sales Team, AP Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sales Team identifies project opportunity requiring special pricing: (a) contractor tender for large project (e.g., 50-unit condominium), (b) government procurement bid, (c) competitive bid situation requiring below-SRP pricing | Sales Team | Category Manager | Per opportunity |
| 2 | Category Manager negotiates SPA with vendor: (a) vendor provides special pricing for the specific project (below standard wholesale price), (b) BuildRight passes savings to customer (with margin retention), (c) SPA documented: vendor, product, quantity, special price, valid period, project reference, (d) entered in system as special pricing arrangement | Category Manager / Buyer | VP Merchandising | 1–3 days |
| 3 | System applies SPA pricing when sales order matches SPA criteria (customer, product, quantity, date); margin tracked separately to show BuildRight's margin vs. vendor's contribution | System | — | Automated |
| 4 | Post-project reconciliation: AP Manager reconciles SPA purchases to SPA agreement; claims any volume or project completion rebates from vendor; settles SPA differential | AP Manager | Category Manager | 1–2 weeks post-project |


### System Touchpoints
- SPA request workflow — project opportunities requiring below-standard pricing (step 1)
- SPA negotiation and terms capture — customer, product, quantity, date window (step 2)
- System SPA pricing application on matching sales orders with separate margin tracking (step 3)
- Post-project reconciliation and rebate claims (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **SPA-leakage risk**: special pricing applied to non-qualifying orders; mitigated by system criteria matching (customer/product/quantity/date) (step 3)
- **Margin-invisibility risk**: SPA sales look like unexplained margin loss; mitigated by separate SPA margin tracking (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-capture of "entered in system as special pricing arrangement" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-track of "tracked separately to show BuildRight's margin vs." (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-reconcile of "reconciles SPA purchases to SPA agreement" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-358 (ensure controlled execution — Rebate Agreement & Accrual Management (PA-39.1).)
- operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
~4–8 hours of negotiation and processing effort per special pricing agreement (Category negotiation over a 1–3-day window; SPA application automated at order match; post-project reconciliation over 1–2 weeks elapsed) × ~200–300 SPAs/year ≈ 800–2,400 hours/year.

## W1792. Vendor Markdown Allowance & Damage Claim Recovery

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Markdown event (seasonal clearance, damaged goods, quality issue) |
| **Frequency** | ~100–200 markdown allowance claims/year |
| **Volume** | ~PHP 30–50M in annual markdown allowances recovered from vendors |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Category Manager, Store Manager, AP Manager, Vendor |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Markdown event triggered: (a) seasonal clearance (end-of-season items): vendor agreement typically provides markdown money (vendor funds a portion of the discount), (b) damaged goods (in-transit or in-store damage): vendor provides credit if damage is their responsibility, (c) quality issue: defective product identified per VS-31; vendor provides markdown or return credit, (d) slow-moving inventory: vendor may provide markdown support to clear aged stock | Store Manager / Category Manager | — | Event-driven |
| 2 | Category Manager prepares markdown allowance claim: (a) list of affected SKUs, quantities, original cost, proposed markdown, (b) vendor contribution requested (% of markdown cost), (c) justification (seasonal, damage, quality, slow-movement), (d) photos of damaged/defective items, (e) submit to vendor for approval | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1–2 days |
| 3 | Vendor approves/denies claim: (a) if approved: vendor issues credit note or markdown allowance payment, (b) if partially approved: negotiate remaining amount, (c) if denied: Category Manager decides whether to absorb markdown cost or escalate to VP Merchandising | Vendor / Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1–2 weeks |
| 4 | AP Manager records vendor markdown allowance: (a) credit note received from vendor applied to AP balance, (b) or cash payment received from vendor, (c) markdown allowance reduces COGS (contra-COGS treatment), (d) remaining markdown cost (BuildRight's share) recorded as markdown expense | AP Manager | Accounting Manager | Per claim |


### System Touchpoints
- Markdown allowance claim preparation — affected SKUs, quantities, costs, proposed markdown (step 2)
- Vendor approval/denial workflow with partial-approval handling (step 3)
- AP recording — credit note to AP balance or cash receipt (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Unclaimed-allowance risk**: agreed markdown money never claimed, absorbing vendor-caused cost; mitigated by claim preparation tied to every markdown event (steps 1–2)
- **Weak-evidence risk**: claims denied for missing documentation; mitigated by SKU-level claim evidence with cost support (step 2)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "Markdown event triggered" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-generate of "prepares markdown allowance claim" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-escalate of "escalate to VP Merchandising" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-209 (ensure vendor rebate accuracy & recognition.)
- operational: Category Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
~6–10 hours per markdown-allowance claim (claim preparation 1–2 days at partial occupancy; vendor approval 1–2 weeks elapsed; credit application per claim) × ~100–200 claims/year ≈ 600–2,000 hours/year on ~PHP 30–50M annual recovery.

## W1793. Rebate Claim Submission & Verification

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Rebate claim period end (monthly, quarterly, or annual per agreement) |
| **Frequency** | ~50–100 claims submitted/quarter |
| **Volume** | ~200–400 claims/year |
| **Owner** | AP Manager |
| **Participants** | AP Manager, Category Manager, Vendor |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System generates rebate claim worksheet per vendor per period: (a) eligible purchase volume (invoices paid during claim period), (b) applicable rebate rate per agreement tier, (c) calculated rebate amount, (d) supporting documentation: invoice list, proof of payment | System | AP Manager | Automated |
| 2 | AP Manager reviews and validates claim: (a) verify purchase volumes match AP records, (b) confirm rebate calculation per agreement terms (tier, rate, eligible products), (c) cross-check with Category Manager for any disputed purchases or returns, (d) attach supporting documentation | AP Manager | Category Manager | 1–2 days/claim batch |
| 3 | Claim submitted to vendor: (a) via vendor portal (preferred — direct system-to-system), (b) via email with supporting documentation, (c) vendor reviews and validates claim (typically 30–60 days), (d) vendor issues credit note or payment | AP Manager | — | 1 day submission |
| 4 | Claim follow-up: AP Manager tracks submitted claims; if vendor does not respond within 60 days: escalate to Category Manager for vendor relationship follow-up; unclaimed rebates reversed from accrual after 90 days | AP Manager | Category Manager | Monthly |
| 5 | Settlement received: (a) credit note: applied to vendor AP balance (reduces next payment), (b) cash payment: received in bank account, (c) system matches settlement to claim; accrual reversed; any variance between accrued and settled amount recorded as adjustment | AP Manager | Accounting Manager | Per settlement |


### System Touchpoints
- Rebate claim worksheet generation — eligible volume, rate, claim amount per vendor/period (step 1)
- AP validation — volumes vs. AP records, calculation per agreement (step 2)
- Claim submission via vendor portal or documented email (step 3)
- 60-day follow-up escalation to Category Manager (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Missed-claim risk**: eligible claims never submitted (direct margin leakage); mitigated by system-generated worksheets per period (step 1)
- **Silent-denial risk**: claims sit unanswered past vendor review windows; mitigated by 60-day escalation tracking (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-document of "System generates rebate claim worksheet per vendor per period" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-record of "AP Manager reviews and validates claim" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews and validates claim" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-358 (ensure controlled execution — Rebate Agreement & Accrual Management (PA-39.1).)
- operational: AP Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Claim cycles ~1–2 days per claim batch (worksheet validation) + 1 day submission × ~200–400 claims/year in monthly vendor batches ≈ 30–60 days/year; monthly claim follow-up and settlement application (worksheet generation automated).

## W1794. Rebate Accrual-to-Settlement Reconciliation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarter-end and year-end financial close |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly; comprehensive at year-end |
| **Volume** | ~100–200 active rebate agreements |
| **Owner** | AP Manager |
| **Participants** | AP Manager, Accounting Manager, External Auditor |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Quarterly: AP Manager reconciles rebate accruals to actual settlements: (a) total accrued per agreement YTD, (b) total claimed YTD, (c) total received (credit notes + cash) YTD, (d) accrual variance: accrued − received = still pending or overstated, (e) aging of claimed but not received: < 60 days (normal), 60–90 days (follow-up needed), > 90 days (risk of non-collection — consider reversal) | AP Manager | Accounting Manager | 2–3 days/quarter |
| 2 | Year-end: comprehensive reconciliation required for external audit: (a) all rebate agreements reviewed, (b) accrual vs. settlement variance explained per agreement, (c) overstated accruals reversed (reduce rebate income), (d) understated accruals increased (additional rebate income), (e) rebates claimed but not yet received at year-end: assessed for collectibility (vendor financial health, dispute status) | AP Manager / Accounting Manager | CFO | 1 week |
| 3 | External auditor reviews: (a) sample of rebate agreements: verify terms, accrual calculation, claim documentation, settlement evidence, (b) test rebate income recognition per PFRS (reliable measurement, probable economic benefit), (c) assess accrual estimation methodology and accuracy | External Auditor | CFO | During annual audit |
| 4 | Adjustments posted based on reconciliation; final rebate income recognized in financial statements | Accounting Manager | CFO | At year-end close |


### System Touchpoints
- Quarterly accrual-to-settlement reconciliation per agreement (step 1)
- Year-end comprehensive reconciliation for external audit (step 2)
- External auditor sample testing — terms, accrual, claims, settlement evidence (step 3)
- Adjustment posting and final income recognition (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Accrual-distortion risk**: accruals materially over/under state rebate income across quarters; mitigated by quarterly reconciliation with adjustment (steps 1, 4)
- **Audit-surprise risk**: unsupported accruals surface at year-end audit; mitigated by the comprehensive pre-audit reconciliation (steps 2–3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-reconcile of "reconciles rebate accruals to actual settlements" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "reviewed" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "methodology and accuracy" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-358 (ensure controlled execution — Rebate Agreement & Accrual Management (PA-39.1).)
- operational: AP Manager review and approval gate; periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
Quarterly accrual-to-settlement reconciliation 2–3 days/quarter ≈ 8–12 days/year; year-end comprehensive reconciliation for external audit 1 week ≈ 5 days; adjustments posted at year-end close.
