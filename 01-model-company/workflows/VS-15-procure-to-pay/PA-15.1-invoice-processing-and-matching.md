## W7. Accounts Payable — Vendor Invoice Processing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vendor invoice received (email, mail, or vendor portal) |
| **Frequency** | ~6,715 invoices/month |
| **Volume** | ~305 invoices/business day (6,715 ÷ 22; ~217/day on a calendar basis); peaks at month-end (+50%) |
| **Owner** | AP Supervisor |
| **Participants** | AP Clerk, Buyer (for discrepancies), Treasury (for payment), AP Supervisor |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | AP Clerk receives and scans/logs vendor invoice (via email, mail, or vendor portal submission) | AP Clerk | AP Supervisor | 5 min/invoice |
| 2 | System attempts auto-match: PO → Goods Receipt → Invoice (3-way match) | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | If matched (quantities and price within tolerance): auto-approve for payment | System | AP Supervisor | Automated |
| 4 | If mismatch (price, quantity, or missing GR): route to AP Clerk for review | AP Clerk | AP Supervisor | 10 min/invoice |
| 5 | If price discrepancy: AP Clerk contacts Buyer for resolution | AP Clerk | Buyer | 15 min/invoice |
| 6 | If quantity discrepancy (partial receipt): verify GR status; wait for remaining delivery or adjust | AP Clerk | AP Supervisor | 10 min/invoice |
| 6a | Exception SLA: all unmatched invoices must be resolved within 5 business days; system tracks aging of exceptions | System | AP Supervisor | Automated |
| 6b | If exception unresolved after 5 days: system escalates to AP Supervisor; AP Supervisor coordinates with Buyer and vendor for resolution | AP Supervisor | Controller | 15 min/invoice |
| 7 | Approved invoices queued for payment per vendor terms (Net 30, Net 60) | System | — | Automated |
| 7a | System identifies invoices eligible for early payment discount (e.g., 2/10 Net 30); displays discount amount, discount deadline, and annualized return on taking discount; Treasury Analyst includes discount opportunities in weekly cash flow planning (W30 step 8); AP Supervisor prioritizes discounted invoices in payment run when Treasury authorizes | System / AP Supervisor | CFO | 15 min/review |
| 8 | AP Supervisor reviews AP aging weekly; prioritize payments by due date and vendor relationship | AP Supervisor | CFO | 2 hours/week |
| 9 | Twice-weekly payment run: system generates payment file (checks, bank transfers) | AP Clerk | AP Supervisor | 1 hour/run |
| 9a | System calculates Expanded Withholding Tax (EWT) per vendor invoice based on configured Alphanumeric Tax Code (ATC) in vendor master (e.g., WI010 for purchases of goods at 1%, WI020 for services at 2%, WC010 for rentals at 5%); net payment to vendor = invoice amount − EWT; system posts EWT to Withholding Tax Payable (liability); AP Clerk validates EWT computation before payment run | System / AP Clerk | AP Supervisor | Automated + 15 min validation/run |
| 9b | Vendor credit memo processing: AP Clerk receives vendor credit note (mail, email, portal); selects source — RTV reference (W3.6a), rebate settlement (W27.9), price protection (W40.11), or manual entry; system attempts auto-match to original PO/invoice/RTV; AP Clerk posts credit memo: system reduces vendor balance and posts to GL (Dr. Accounts Payable / Cr. COGS or appropriate expense); if vendor has open invoices, system applies credit memo against oldest open invoice; if net vendor credit balance results, AP Clerk decides to request refund, apply to next invoice, or offset | AP Clerk | AP Supervisor | 10 min/credit memo |
| 10 | Treasury reviews and approves payment batch; transmits to bank | Treasury Analyst | CFO | 30 min/run |
| 11 | System posts payment; updates vendor balance | System | — | Automated |

**Match rate target**: ≥ 80% auto-matched (no manual intervention)

### System Touchpoints
- Invoice scanning / OCR / digital capture from email, mail, or vendor portal (W7.1)
- 3-way match engine (PO → GR → Invoice) (W7.2)
- Auto-approval with tolerance thresholds (W7.3)
- Exception routing and workflow (W7.4–6)
- Exception aging tracker with SLA enforcement and auto-escalation at day 5 (W7.6a–b)
- Early payment discount detection: system identifies invoices eligible for discount, calculates annualized ROI, and highlights during payment run; decision criteria — Treasury Analyst authorizes taking discount when (a) annualized ROI exceeds BuildRight's weighted average cost of capital (currently ~8–10%, configurable), (b) cash position (W30) supports early payment without creating liquidity risk, and (c) vendor is rated A or B on vendor scorecard (W44) — discounts for C/D-rated vendors are deferred to standard terms to preserve cash leverage; system auto-calculates annualized ROI per discount offer as: (discount % ÷ (365 ÷ days saved)) × 100 and displays alongside cash position impact in payment run review screen (W7.7a)
- AP aging report (W7.8)
- Payment file generation (bank formats) (W7.9–10)
- Expanded Withholding Tax (EWT): auto-computation per ATC in vendor master, net payment calculation (invoice − EWT), EWT Payable tracking, BIR 1601-EQ file generation (W7.9a)
- Vendor credit memo processing: credit note entry with source reference (RTV, rebate, price protection), auto-matching to original transactions, automatic application to open invoices, credit balance management (W7.9b)
- Non-PO / recurring expense invoice processing: 2-way match (invoice vs. contract or budget), store manager / department head approval routing, cost center allocation, utility and service bill capture (W7C.1–6)
- Shelf-life / expiry date management: capture manufacturing date and shelf-life at GR for date-sensitive items; system tracks expiry per batch; FEFO (First Expired First Out) pick direction in DC; alerting for near-expiry items; periodic expiry review feeds into slow-mover disposition (W3, W4, W6)
- GRNI aging management: AP Clerk runs weekly GRNI aging report showing all GRs without matching vendor invoices, grouped by aging bucket (0–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+ days); items > 30 days flagged for Buyer follow-up with vendor; items > 60 days escalated to AP Supervisor for direct vendor contact; items > 90 days reviewed by Controller for potential permanent accrual or write-off; system tracks GRNI aging with vendor-level drill-down; weekly GRNI aging dashboard visible to AP Supervisor and Controller (W7, W9A.2a)
- Duplicate vendor invoice detection: system automatically checks incoming vendor invoices against the AP sub-ledger for duplicate invoice number, duplicate vendor + amount + date combinations, and duplicate PO reference + invoice number; if a potential duplicate is detected, system blocks the invoice and alerts AP Clerk with the matching existing invoice reference; AP Clerk investigates (true duplicate vs. sequential invoice numbers from same vendor) and either rejects the duplicate or overrides with documentation; monthly: AP Supervisor reviews duplicate detection override log as part of AP controls review (cross-reference CTL-42 in Internal Controls Matrix) (W7)
- Evaluated Receipt Settlement (ERS): for configured VMI vendors (W20) and select blanket PO vendors (W2C), system auto-generates vendor invoice from PO + Goods Receipt data without requiring vendor invoice submission; AP Clerk validates auto-generated invoice against PO and GR; if within tolerance, auto-approved for payment; reduces GRNI accumulation and manual invoice processing for high-volume, trusted vendor relationships

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Duplicate invoice detection failures for recurring vendor invoices**: vendors that submit monthly recurring invoices (utilities, security services) often reuse reference numbers or submit with slight variations; the auto-duplicate check may miss these, leading to double payments that are only caught during vendor statement reconciliation (W7D) — sometimes months later
- **EWT computation errors for new 2307 forms**: changes in BIR Alphanumeric Tax Codes (ATCs) or incorrect ATC configuration in vendor master lead to under- or over-withholding; correcting EWT errors requires filing amended 1601-EQ returns and re-issuing vendor 2307 certificates, creating significant rework during tax season
- **GRNI aging escalation fatigue**: with ~217 invoices/day and goods often arriving before vendor invoices, GRNI balances grow steadily; AP Clerks may become desensitized to aging alerts, allowing items to reach 90+ days where Controller intervention is required for permanent accrual
- **Month-end invoice surge overwhelming AP capacity**: the +50% volume spike during close week (460–550 invoices/day) forces AP Clerks into overtime and requires redeployment of 2–3 other Finance staff, disrupting their own close responsibilities and increasing error rates under time pressure
- **Early payment discount leakage**: despite the system identifying eligible discounts (step 7a), Treasury may not consistently authorize early payments due to competing cash priorities; missed discounts on high-volume vendor invoices can represent PHP 5–10M in annual foregone savings


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-scan of "scans/logs vendor invoice" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-match of "System attempts auto-match" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System rule-based auto-approval of "approve for payment" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-09 (separate purchasing from receiving.); CTL-10 (separate payment initiation from approval.); CTL-14 (ensure AP invoices match PO and GR.); CTL-17 (ensure GRNI completeness.); CTL-44 (detect and prevent duplicate vendor payments.)
- operational: Controller intervention for GRNI items aged 90+ days with permanent accrual

### Time Estimate
- Invoice scanning/logging: ~25–31 hours/business-day (305–370 invoices × 5 min)
- Manual exception resolution (20% of invoices): ~25–30 hours/business-day (~70 invoices × 25 min avg)
- Payment run (twice weekly): ~2 hours/run (1 hour AP Clerk + 1 hour Treasury review)
- AP aging review: ~2 hours/week (AP Supervisor)
- GRNI aging follow-up: ~3 hours/week (AP Clerk + Buyer coordination)
- EWT validation per payment run: ~15 min/run
- Total AP processing: ~60–70 hours/business-day across 8–10 AP Clerks (~6–8 hours each/day)

### Staffing Implication
- **8–10 AP Clerks**: total ~395–440 invoices/business-day across merchandise (~305/day per W7: 6,715 invoices/month ÷ 22 business days) and non-PO (~91–136/day per W7C: 2,000–3,000/month ÷ 22); at 5 min logging each = ~33–37 hours for basic processing; with ~20% requiring manual resolution at 25 min each = ~20–30 additional hours; total ~53–67 hours/business-day; with 10 clerks that's ~5–6 hours each; reasonable with payment runs, GRNI follow-up, vendor statement reconciliation (W7D), and other duties; note that month-end peaks (+50% per W7) push daily volume to ~590–660 invoices/day during close week, requiring temporary overtime or redeployment of 2–3 other Finance staff (Cost Accountant, Treasury Analyst) to AP to maintain processing SLA; peak staffing contingency documented in W7
- **1 AP Supervisor**: Oversight, aging review, GRNI escalation management, escalations.
- **2 Treasury Analysts**: Payment approval, bank file transmission, LC management. Shared with AR and other treasury duties.

### W7C. Non-PO / Recurring Expense Invoice Processing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Utility bill, service invoice, rent, or other recurring expense received without a Purchase Order |
| **Frequency** | ~2,000–3,000 non-PO invoices/month across all locations (200 stores + 4 DCs + HQ); combined with ~6,715 merchandise/vendor invoices (W7), total AP invoice volume is ~8,500–9,500/month; the ~6,715 figure in model-company-profile §10.2 refers to merchandise/vendor invoices only |
| **Volume** | ~10–15 recurring vendor invoices per store per month (electricity, water, internet, rent to Property Mgmt Inc., security, cleaning, waste disposal) |
| **Owner** | AP Supervisor |
| **Participants** | AP Clerk, Store Manager / Department Head (approval), AP Supervisor |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | AP Clerk receives and logs non-PO invoice (utility bill, service invoice, lease payment); classifies as recurring or one-time | AP Clerk | AP Supervisor | 5 min/invoice |
| 2 | System attempts 2-way match: invoice amount vs. contract amount or budget allocation per cost center | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | If invoice within tolerance of contract/budget: route to cost center owner for approval (Store Manager for store expenses, Department Head for HQ expenses) | System | Cost center owner | 1 min/auto-approved |
| 4 | If invoice exceeds budget or no contract on file: route to cost center owner for review with explanation of variance | AP Clerk / Cost center owner | AP Supervisor | 5 min/invoice |
| 5 | Approved non-PO invoices queued for payment in standard payment run (W7.9–11); EWT computed per ATC for service invoices (W7.9a) | System | AP Supervisor | Automated |
| 6 | Monthly: AP Supervisor reviews non-PO expense summary by cost center; flags locations with unusual expense patterns for management review | AP Supervisor | Controller | 1 hour/month |

**Match rate target**: ≥ 70% auto-matched for recurring expenses (contract-based)

### System Touchpoints
- Non-PO invoice capture and logging: AP Clerk enters or scans utility bills, service invoices, and lease payments; system classifies as recurring or one-time based on vendor and expense type (W7C.1)
- 2-way match engine: invoice amount matched against contract amount or budget allocation per cost center; configurable tolerance thresholds (e.g., ±5% for utilities, ±2% for rent); auto-approve if within tolerance (W7C.2)
- Budget validation engine: system checks invoice amount against remaining cost center budget; flags over-budget invoices for cost center owner review with variance explanation (W7C.4)
- Cost center routing: approved invoices automatically routed to cost center owner (Store Manager for store expenses, Department Head for HQ expenses) based on GL account and location mapping; auto-approval for contract-matched invoices within tolerance (W7C.3)
- EWT computation for service invoices: system calculates Expanded Withholding Tax per ATC code (e.g., WC080 for professional services at 10%, WC010 for rentals at 5%) based on vendor master and expense type; net payment = invoice amount − EWT (W7C.5)
- Payment queue integration: approved non-PO invoices fed into standard payment run alongside PO-based invoices (W7.9–11); EWT, early payment discounts, and payment terms applied uniformly (W7C.5)
- Monthly non-PO expense summary: system generates cost center expense summary by expense category (utilities, rent, security, cleaning, waste disposal) with period-over-period comparison; flags locations with unusual expense patterns (> 20% variance from prior month) for AP Supervisor management review (W7C.6)
- Contract master data: recurring vendor contracts stored with billing schedule, tolerance bands, cost center allocation, and EWT ATC; enables auto-matching for contract-based invoices and identifies invoices without matching contract (W7C.2, W7C.4)
- Integration with W7 (AP invoice processing), W14 (intercompany rent to Property Mgmt Inc.), W89 (bank reconciliation for non-PO payments)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Contract coverage gaps**: ~30% of non-PO invoices have no matching contract in the system; every uncovered invoice requires manual review by the cost center owner, slowing throughput during month-end peaks when ~3,000 non-PO invoices land simultaneously across 200 stores + 4 DCs + HQ
- **Store-level variance investigation burden**: unusual expense patterns at individual stores (e.g., electricity spike at a Visayas store due to aging HVAC) require Facilities Coordinator follow-up that spans Finance, Store Ops, and Property Mgmt Inc., often taking weeks to resolve
- **Rent-to-related-party scrutiny**: all 200 store rent payments flow to Property Mgmt Inc. (IC); BIR transfer pricing audit risk is high if lease rates are not documented at arm's-length market benchmarks per W14 annual review
- **Recurring service invoice delays**: security, cleaning, and waste disposal vendors for 200+ locations often submit invoices late or with incomplete details, causing month-end accrual estimation errors that require subsequent period adjustments

### Time Estimate
- Non-PO invoice logging: ~8–11 hours/business-day (~91–136 invoices × 5 min)
- Manual review for uncovered invoices (~30%): ~7–10 hours/business-day (~40 invoices × 12 min avg)
- Cost center owner approval routing: ~1 min/auto-approved, ~5 min/manual review
- Monthly expense summary review (AP Supervisor): ~1 hour/month
- Non-PO processing during month-end peak: +50% volume surge requires ~4–6 additional hours/day for 3–5 days
- Total non-PO AP effort: ~20–25 hours/business-day (shared with W7 AP Clerks)

### Staffing Implication
- **Shared AP Clerks (from W7 pool)**: non-PO invoice processing adds ~20–25 hours/business-day to the existing AP team; with 8–10 clerks handling both W7 and W7C, this is ~2–3 additional hours per clerk per day; absorbed within current headcount but contributes to month-end overtime
- **1 AP Supervisor**: non-PO expense summary review adds ~1 hour/month; contract coverage gap remediation (coordinating with Facilities/Store Ops) adds ~2–3 hours/month; absorbed within existing role
- **No incremental headcount**: non-PO volume is predictable and spread across locations; peaks are seasonal (utility spikes in summer) and manageable

### System Touchpoints
- Vendor statement import: AP Clerk uploads vendor statement (PDF, Excel, or EDI); system parses line items for auto-matching (W7D.1–2)
- Auto-reconciliation engine: matches vendor statement lines to AP sub-ledger by invoice number, date, and amount with configurable tolerance (W7D.2)
- Reconciliation summary dashboard: matched, unmatched (vendor-side), unmatched (AP-side), and variance items with drill-down (W7D.3)
- Reconciliation documentation: auditor-ready log showing reconciliation result per vendor per period with aging of reconciling items (W7D.5)
- Vendor reconciliation analytics: match rate, average reconciliation time, recurring discrepancy vendors, aging of unresolved items (W7D.6)
- Integration with W7 (AP invoice processing), W7C (non-PO invoices), W44 (vendor performance — chronic reconciliation issues affect vendor scorecard)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Vendor statement format inconsistency**: top 50 vendors each submit statements in different formats (PDF, Excel, EDI, printed) with varying levels of detail; the auto-reconciliation engine struggles with non-standard formats, forcing AP Clerks to manually parse ~30% of vendor statements
- **Unreconciled items aging beyond 90 days**: EWT deductions not reflected on vendor statements are the most common reconciling item; Philippine vendors frequently omit EWT amounts from their statements, creating persistent unmatched AP lines that require manual explanation every month
- **Resource contention with month-end close**: vendor statement reconciliation for top 50 vendors is due monthly, coinciding with the W9A close cycle; AP Clerks already stretched by invoice processing peaks must split attention between close support and vendor reconciliation, degrading quality on both
- **Chronic discrepancy vendors eroding trust**: 5–10 vendors consistently show discrepancies (partial shipments, pricing errors, late credit memos); each monthly reconciliation repeats the same investigation, consuming disproportionate AP Clerk time without resolution until Buyer escalates per W44

### Time Estimate
- Monthly reconciliation (top 50 vendors): ~25–38 hours/month (50 vendors × 30–45 min avg including investigation)
- Quarterly reconciliation (~750 remaining vendors): ~187–375 hours/quarter (~15–30 min/vendor), spread across 13 weeks = ~15–29 hours/week
- AP Supervisor monthly review: ~1 hour/month (top 50 summary)
- AP Supervisor quarterly controls review: ~30 min/quarter
- Total vendor reconciliation effort: ~30–45 hours/month (monthly) + ~60–115 hours/month (quarterly cycle average)

### Staffing Implication
- **AP Clerks (shared with W7)**: vendor reconciliation adds ~90–160 hours/month averaged across the team; with 8–10 clerks, this is ~9–20 hours each/month (~1–2.5 hours/week); absorbed but contributes to workload pressure during close week when monthly reconciliations are due
- **1 AP Supervisor**: vendor reconciliation oversight adds ~1.5 hours/month; absorbed within existing role
- **No incremental headcount**: reconciliation volume is predictable and can be staggered across the month for non-top-50 vendors

## W100. Vendor Statement Reconciliation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly reconciliation calendar (10th business day of following month) |
| **Frequency** | Monthly for top 100 vendors (by spend); quarterly for remaining active vendors |
| **Volume** | Top 100 vendors = ~80% of AP spend; remaining ~700–900 vendors reconciled quarterly |
| **Owner** | AP Supervisor |
| **Participants** | AP Clerk, AP Supervisor, Buyer (discrepancy resolution), Controller (escalation) |

### Background

Referenced as "W7D" in W3 (RTV credit note aging report feeding into vendor statement reconciliation), this workflow fills a critical AP control gap. Without systematic vendor statement reconciliation, the company risks overpayment (duplicate invoices not caught by auto-matching), underpayment (vendor disputes damaging relationships), and inaccurate liability reporting (AP sub-ledger diverges from actual amounts owed). This is a standard month-end close control for any company with 800–1,000 active vendors and ~8,500–9,500 monthly invoices.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | AP Clerk requests or receives vendor statement of account (SOA) — via email, vendor portal, or mail; top vendors provide monthly SOA automatically; for remaining vendors, AP Clerk sends batch request at month-end | AP Clerk | AP Supervisor | 30 min/batch (top 100) |
| 2 | System prepares BuildRight's AP sub-ledger summary per vendor for the reconciliation period: total invoices received, payments made, credit memos applied, debit memos issued, and resulting AP balance | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | AP Clerk compares vendor SOA to BuildRight's AP sub-ledger per vendor: (a) match invoices — vendor SOA invoices vs. BuildRight recorded invoices, (b) match payments — vendor SOA payments received vs. BuildRight payments issued, (c) match credit/debit memos — vendor SOA adjustments vs. BuildRight credit memos (W7.9b) and debit notes (W70), (d) compare ending balances | AP Clerk | AP Supervisor | 15 min/vendor |
| 4 | **If balances match**: AP Clerk marks vendor as reconciled for the period; no further action | AP Clerk | AP Supervisor | 2 min/vendor |
| 5 | **If balances do not match**: AP Clerk documents reconciling items by type: (a) **Timing differences** — payments in transit (BuildRight issued but vendor not yet received), invoices received after SOA cutoff, GRs posted but vendor invoices not yet received (GRNI per W7), (b) **Missing invoices** — vendor shows invoice not in BuildRight's system (possible loss, email misrouted, or vendor error), (c) **Missing payments** — BuildRight shows payment not on vendor SOA (check not cleared, bank transfer not yet credited to vendor), (d) **Unrecorded credit memos** — vendor issued credit not yet in BuildRight's system (RTV credit note pending per W3, rebate settlement pending per W27), (e) **Disputed items** — vendor shows charge that BuildRight disputes (quality rejection, pricing discrepancy, unauthorized charge) | AP Clerk | AP Supervisor | 15–30 min/vendor with variances |
| 6 | AP Clerk resolves reconciling items: (a) timing differences — document and carry forward to next period, (b) missing invoices — request from vendor; if legitimate, process per W7, (c) missing payments — provide payment proof (bank reference, check copy) to vendor, (d) unrecorded credits — verify against RTV log (W88), rebate settlement log (W27), price protection log (W40); if valid, request vendor credit memo or record in system, (e) disputed items — route to Buyer for resolution with vendor per W44 dispute process | AP Clerk / Buyer | AP Supervisor | 15–60 min/item |
| 7 | AP Clerk prepares reconciliation summary per vendor: BuildRight balance, vendor SOA balance, reconciling items (with aging), net agreed balance; system stores reconciliation record with full documentation | AP Clerk | AP Supervisor | 5 min/vendor |
| 8 | AP Supervisor reviews reconciliation summary for top 20 vendors (by spend or by reconciliation complexity); approves or requests additional investigation | AP Supervisor | Controller | 1 hour/month |
| 9 | Unresolved items > 60 days auto-escalate to Controller for review; Controller decides: write-off, pursue with vendor, or adjust AP balance | Controller | CFO | 30 min/month |
| 10 | Quarterly: AP Supervisor generates vendor reconciliation KPI report: (a) reconciliation completion rate (% of top 100 vendors reconciled within SLA), (b) average reconciling items per vendor, (c) aging of unresolved items, (d) top vendors by reconciliation complexity (chronic discrepancies signal vendor billing quality issues — feed into W44 vendor scorecard), (e) duplicate payment recovery amount (if any duplicates caught during reconciliation) | AP Supervisor | Controller | 1 hour/quarter |

**Reconciliation SLA**: Top 100 vendors reconciled within 15 business days of month-end; remaining vendors reconciled within 30 business days of quarter-end.

### System Touchpoints
- Vendor AP sub-ledger summary generation per vendor per period (W100.2)
- Reconciliation workspace: side-by-side comparison of vendor SOA vs. BuildRight AP records with line-level matching (W100.3)
- Reconciling item classification and documentation: timing, missing invoice, missing payment, unrecorded credit, disputed (W100.5)
- Integration with W7 (invoice and payment records), W27 (rebate credits), W70 (debit note aging), W88 (RTV credit notes), W44 (vendor scorecard — reconciliation complexity as quality metric)
- Aging tracker for unresolved reconciling items with auto-escalation at 60 days (W100.9)
- Reconciliation KPI dashboard (W100.10)
- Audit trail: all reconciliation actions logged with AP Clerk ID, timestamp, and documentation

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Vendor statement availability delays**: not all vendors submit monthly statements on time; top 100 vendors may be compliant, but the remaining ~800 vendors are contacted quarterly and often do not maintain systematic SOA generation, making reconciliation reliant on BuildRight's AP sub-ledger alone

## W1504. Supplier Invoice Price Discrepancy Investigation, Debit Note Issuance & Resolution Tracking

| Field | Detail |

| **Owner** | AP Associate |
| **Participants** | AP Associate, Merchandise Planner, Buyer, Vendor Account Manager, AP Manager |

### Background

In a retail chain purchasing from 800+ vendors, price discrepancies between the agreed PO price and the invoiced price are common. Causes include: (a) vendor unilaterally applying a price increase that wasn't communicated or agreed; (b) promotional pricing confusion — vendor invoices at regular price when PO was placed at promotional price; (c) unit-of-measure errors — vendor invoices per piece when PO was per case (or vice versa); (d) tiered pricing errors — vendor applies wrong volume tier; (e) currency conversion discrepancies for import POs; (f) honest data entry errors at vendor's billing department. Left uninvestigated, these discrepancies cost BuildRight an estimated PHP 3–8M/year in overpayments. The three-way match (PO → Goods Receipt → Invoice) catches these discrepancies, but each one requires investigation to determine root cause and resolution.

- **PO price staleness**: if Buyers don't update PO prices when vendors communicate price increases, every subsequent invoice for that SKU will flag a discrepancy until the PO is corrected — creating a self-inflicted workload

## W5510. Supplier Service-Fee Billing & Account Deduction for Store-Rendered Services (Barcode Labels & Promotional Collaterals)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | (a) Chargeable service event auto-logged at source — barcode/price-label print run, vendor-initiated barcode change, promotional-collateral production, source-tag re-application; (b) monthly supplier service-fee statement cycle ahead of the W556 payment calendar |
| **Frequency** | Continuous event logging; monthly statement + debit-memo cycle |
| **Volume** | ~1,500–3,000 chargeable events/month chain-wide (~55–60% new-item barcode/price labels beyond free allowance and vendor barcode changes; ~25–30% promotional collateral production; ~10–15% source-tag re-application for missed vendor tags), concentrated in ~150–300 of the ~800–1,000 active vendors (≈10 events per affected vendor-month); ~5–10% of statements attract a dispute (~10–25/month) |
| **Owner** | AP Accountant |
| **Participants** | AP Clerk, Category Manager, Store Operations Manager (first-line dispute), Auto-ID Lead (event sources), Marketing Production Coordinator (collateral), Master Data Analyst (event evidence), Finance Manager, Legal (fee-schedule enforceability), Supplier |

### Background

BuildRight renders in-store services that consume its own labor, label stock, and print capacity on suppliers' behalf: printing barcode and price labels for supplier items (new-item onboarding and re-labels per VS-151), producing promotional collaterals (posters, wobblers, shelf talkers, endcap headers), and re-applying EAS tags the supplier promised at source but shipped without (W4498). Two recovery paths already exist — co-op marketing funds (VS-39, pre-agreed campaign reimbursement per W513/W1799) and compliance chargebacks (W245, penalties for non-performance) — but neither fits *per-event, store-rendered services billed to the supplier's account*. This workflow transposes the concessionaire service-fee model (W5507) onto merchandise suppliers: events auto-log with evidence at the point of service, aggregate into a monthly statement with a vendor-portal dispute window, and settle as AP debit memos (W770) deducted from the supplier's account at the next payment run (W556) — with a co-op coverage guard so a campaign already funded through VS-39 is never billed twice.

### System Touchpoints
- Vendor-master service-fee schedule (versioned, effective-dated, opt-in flag) on the trade-agreement/CLM record (W688)
- Chargeable-event log auto-fed from print management (W4490/W181), Auto-ID change control (W4488), campaign work orders (W1796/W1798), and source-tag receipt exceptions (W4498) — provisional-until-completed status with automatic reversal
- Co-op coverage flag from campaign funding records (W513) as the double-recovery guard against W1799 settlement
- Monthly statement generation with free-allowance balances and evidence links; vendor-portal (W705) statement view with 5-business-day dispute window and expedite-privilege suspension flag
- AP debit-memo creation per W770 (deduction type "service fee") with one-to-one credit-note matching guard; auto-application at the W556 payment run
- Service-revenue GL accounts separate from penalty income (W245) and co-op fund income (W1799); output VAT per BIR; e-invoicing integration (W473); AR fallback per W8/W108
- Quarterly recovery analytics: fee income vs. consumable/labor/production cost by event type

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Unenforceable-fee risk**: charging a vendor with no signed schedule (or at mid-term increased rates) is uncollectible and damages the trading relationship; mitigated by the opt-in flag gating event charging and schedule changes only at renewal (steps 1, 7)
- **Double-recovery risk**: the same collateral billed under both the service-fee schedule and the co-op fund claim (W1799), or offset twice when the vendor also issues a credit note; mitigated by the campaign coverage flag at event logging (step 2) and the one-to-one debit-memo/credit-note matching guard (step 5)
- **Phantom-event risk**: an event logged but not executed (rejected artwork, unreached label) still on the statement destroys portal credibility; mitigated by provisional-until-completed status with automatic reversal (step 2)
- **Netting-vs-BIR-evidence risk**: deducting service fees from AP means no cash payment, so a vendor needing to issue BIR 2307 on the service fee cannot do so on a netted deduction, and output VAT netted against input VAT on the vendor's invoices can confuse VAT reporting; mitigated by gross-up presentation (fee + VAT as separate statement lines) and the cash-settlement route where a 2307 is required (steps 5–6)
- **Un-nettable vendor risk**: consignment-only or seasonal suppliers with no open AP balance cannot be deducted, leaving fees unrecovered; mitigated by the AR invoice fallback per W8/W108 (step 6)
- **Overcharging perception**: fee schedules perceived as nickel-and-diming can push vendors to deprioritize BuildRight orders (the W245 retaliation risk); mitigated by free allowances, evidence-linked portal transparency, and Category Manager sign-off at renewal (steps 1, 4, 7)

### Automation Opportunity
- Auto-logging of chargeable events at source with evidence links and provisional-until-completed status (supports Step 2)
- Co-op-coverage auto-check before an event becomes chargeable (supports Step 2)
- Auto-aggregation into the monthly statement with free-allowance balances (supports Step 3)
- Portal statement auto-release 5 days pre-posting with in-portal dispute case creation (supports Step 4)
- Auto debit-memo creation per W770 and auto-application at the W556 payment run (supports Steps 5–6)
- Recovery analytics auto-computed from the event log and W4489/W181 cost data (supports Step 7)

### Controls
- CTL-284 (ensure controlled execution — Invoice Processing & Matching (PA-15.1).)
- operational: signed fee-schedule (vendor opt-in flag) required before any event becomes chargeable; provisional-event reversal before statement posting; dispute window precedes debit-memo posting; one-to-one debit-memo/credit-note matching
