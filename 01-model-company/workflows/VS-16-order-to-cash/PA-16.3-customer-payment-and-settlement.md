## W81. Bad Debt Provisioning, Write-Off & Recovery

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly AR aging review; or specific account identified for write-off after collection escalation (W8.8a) |
| **Frequency** | Monthly provision review; write-offs as needed (typically quarterly batch) |
| **Volume** | ~5,400 trade and corporate AR accounts; target bad debt rate < 0.5% of AR balance; estimated 10–20 write-off events/year |
| **Owner** | AR Supervisor (collections and write-off initiation); Controller (provisioning) |
| **Participants** | AR Supervisor, AR Clerk, Finance Manager, Controller, CFO, Legal, Sales Rep |

### Background

This workflow covers the complete bad debt lifecycle from initial provisioning through write-off and potential recovery. It formalizes and expands the bad debt steps embedded in W8.8a/8b into a standalone process with dedicated BIR documentation, provisioning methodology, and recovery tracking. The AR collection escalation process (W8 steps 6–8a) feeds into this workflow when accounts reach the write-off stage.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monthly AR aging review**: AR Clerk generates AR aging report by bucket (current, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90, 91–120, > 120 days); identifies accounts > 90 days overdue for bad debt assessment | AR Clerk | AR Supervisor | 30 min/month |
| 2 | **Individual account assessment**: for each account > 90 days overdue, AR Clerk and Sales Rep assess collectibility — (a) customer financial status (still operating, declared bankruptcy, unreachable), (b) collection history (payment promises made and broken, partial payments), (c) dispute status (customer disputes amount vs. simple non-payment), (d) security or collateral held (if any) | AR Clerk / Sales Rep | AR Supervisor | 15 min/account |
| 3 | **Bad debt provision computation**: monthly, Controller computes bad debt provision using: (a) **specific provision**: for individually assessed accounts > 90 days where collection is uncertain — provision at 50–100% of outstanding amount based on assessment, (b) **general provision**: portfolio-level provision based on historical loss rate applied to remaining AR portfolio (e.g., 0.5–1% of total AR not specifically provided); system calculates provision suggestion based on configured aging percentages per bucket | Controller | CFO | 1 hour/month |
| 4 | Controller reviews and approves monthly bad debt provision adjustment; system posts Dr. Bad Debt Expense / Cr. Allowance for Doubtful Accounts (balance sheet contra-asset) | Controller | CFO | 30 min/month |
| 5 | **Write-off trigger**: when all collection efforts exhausted per W8.8a escalation tiers — (a) demand letter sent and no response after deadline, OR (b) external collection agency confirms account uncollectible, OR (c) customer confirmed insolvent/bankrupt (court filing or public record), OR (d) cost of collection exceeds amount recoverable — AR Supervisor initiates formal write-off process | AR Supervisor | Controller | 15 min/account |
| 6 | **Write-off documentation package**: AR Supervisor prepares BIR-compliant write-off documentation: (a) **demand letter**: copy of formal demand letter sent via registered mail with return card, (b) **collection report**: summary of all collection actions taken with dates (phone calls, emails, demand letters, collection agency referral, legal filing if applicable), (c) **account history**: full AR transaction history showing original invoices, payments received, and outstanding balance, (d) **evidence of insolvency** (if applicable): court filing, SEC dissolution notice, or other public record, (e) **write-off recommendation**: amount, reason, and supporting evidence summary | AR Supervisor | Controller | 1–2 hours/account |
| 7 | **Write-off approval**: tiered by amount — (a) ≤ PHP 50,000: Finance Manager + Controller, (b) PHP 50,001–200,000: CFO, (c) PHP 200,001–1,000,000: CEO + CFO, (d) > PHP 1,000,000: Board resolution | Approver(s) | CEO / Board | 15–30 min/write-off |
| 8 | **System posting**: system posts write-off — Dr. Allowance for Doubtful Accounts / Cr. Accounts Receivable (if provision exists) or Dr. Bad Debt Expense / Cr. Accounts Receivable (direct write-off if no prior provision); AR balance reduced; account status changed to "Written Off"; customer blocked from new credit sales; full transaction history retained for 10-year BIR retention | System / AR Clerk | Controller | 5 min/write-off |
| 9 | **Post write-off tracking**: system maintains written-off accounts register — original amount, write-off date, reason, approving authority; written-off accounts excluded from standard AR aging but tracked separately for potential recovery | System | — | Automated |
| 10 | **Recovery — partial or full**: if customer subsequently pays on a previously written-off account — (a) AR Clerk receives payment (cash, bank transfer, or settlement agreement), (b) system posts recovery: Dr. Cash / Cr. Bad Debt Recovery (income — P&L), (c) recovery tracked separately from regular AR collections in GL and reporting, (d) if partial recovery: remaining balance remains in written-off register; if full recovery: account closed | AR Clerk / AR Supervisor | Controller | 10 min/recovery |
| 11 | **Recovery via settlement agreement**: if customer offers reduced settlement (e.g., 60% of outstanding), AR Supervisor negotiates; Finance Manager approves settlement terms; system posts: Dr. Cash (settled amount) / Cr. Bad Debt Recovery (settled amount); remaining written-off balance confirmed as final loss | AR Supervisor / Finance Manager | Controller | 30 min/settlement |
| 12 | **Quarterly**: Controller presents bad debt summary to CFO — (a) provision movement (opening balance + new provisions − write-offs + recoveries), (b) write-off volume and root cause analysis (customer bankruptcy, fraud, collection failure), (c) recovery rate on previously written-off accounts, (d) AR aging trend, (e) adequacy of general provision rate, (f) recommendation for provision rate adjustment if loss experience changes | Controller | CFO | 30 min/quarter |
| 13 | **Annual**: CFO reviews bad debt provisioning methodology with external auditors as part of year-end audit (W9B); auditors assess adequacy of provision against actual loss history; methodology adjustments documented and approved | CFO | Board | 2 hours/year |

### BIR Documentation Requirements

For a bad debt write-off to be deductible for Philippine income tax purposes (per BIR Revenue Regulations), the following must be maintained:
- Proof that the debt was previously reported as income (sales invoices, official receipts)
- Demand letter sent via registered mail with return card
- Documentation of collection efforts (call logs, email correspondence, collection agency reports)
- Court filing (if legal action was pursued)
- Evidence of insolvency or bankruptcy (if applicable)
- Board resolution (for amounts requiring Board approval)
- Write-off approval with sign-off by authorized officer
- All documentation retained for 10 years per BIR retention requirements

### System Touchpoints
- AR aging report with configurable bucket thresholds (W81.1)
- Individual account collectibility assessment with reason codes and supporting notes (W81.2)
- Automated bad debt provision calculator: specific provision per account + general provision based on aging matrix (W81.3)
- Provision posting with GL integration: Dr. Bad Debt Expense / Cr. Allowance for Doubtful Accounts (W81.4)
- Write-off documentation package generation with BIR-required attachments (W81.6)
- Tiered write-off approval workflow (W81.7)
- Write-off posting: provision drawdown or direct expense with AR reduction; account status change to "Written Off" with credit block (W81.8)
- Written-off accounts register with separate tracking from active AR (W81.9)
- Bad debt recovery posting: Dr. Cash / Cr. Bad Debt Recovery (separate P&L line from regular revenue) (W81.10–11)
- Quarterly bad debt summary dashboard: provision movement, write-off analysis, recovery rate, aging trend (W81.12)
- Integration with W8 (AR collections — W8.8a escalation feeds into W81 write-off trigger), W9A (month-end — provision posting included in close), W9B (year-end — bad debt methodology review with auditors), W24 (credit application — written-off customers blocked from new credit), W35 (management reporting — bad debt KPIs), W44 (vendor scorecard — recovery process mirrors vendor collection logic)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Provision methodology subjectivity and auditor challenge**: the general provision rate (0.5–1% of AR portfolio) and specific provision assessment (50–100% based on collectibility) involve significant judgment; external auditors (W95) may challenge provision adequacy, especially if actual write-offs exceed provisioned amounts in prior years
- **BIR write-off documentation burden**: Philippine tax law requires extensive documentation to support bad debt write-off as a deductible expense (demand letters, collection logs, court filings); missing any element results in the write-off being disallowed during BIR examination (W77), triggering additional tax liability plus penalties
- **Recovery of written-off accounts creating tax complexity**: when a previously written-off account is recovered (step 10), the recovery is recognized as Bad Debt Recovery income; if the original write-off was claimed as a tax deduction, the recovery must be reported as taxable income in the year of recovery, creating a tax planning timing issue
- **Collection agency contingency fees eroding recovery**: with agency fees at 10–20% of collected amounts, net recovery on referred accounts is significantly reduced; for smaller accounts (< PHP 100,000), agency fees may consume most of the recovery, calling into question the economic viability of agency referral
- **Customer relationship damage from legal action**: filing collection cases against trade accounts (step 5e) permanently damages the commercial relationship; even if the debt is recovered, the customer will not return, and negative word-of-mouth in the Philippine business community can deter other potential trade customers

- System auto-review of "Monthly AR aging review" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-count of "Individual account assessment" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-calculate of "calculates provision suggestion based on configured aging percentages per bucket" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-288 (ensure controlled execution — Customer Payment & Settlement (PA-16.3).)
- operational: AR Supervisor (collections and write-off initiation); Controller (provisioning) review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
- Monthly AR aging review for bad debt assessment: ~30 min/month (AR Clerk)
- Individual account collectibility assessment: ~15 min/account × ~20–30 accounts = ~5–8 hours/month
- Bad debt provision computation and posting: ~1.5 hours/month (Controller)
- Write-off documentation preparation: ~1–2 hours/account (AR Supervisor)
- Write-off approval and posting: ~5 min/account (system)
- Quarterly bad debt summary presentation: ~30 min/quarter (Controller)
- Annual methodology review with auditors: ~2 hours/year (CFO)
- Total bad debt management: ~8–12 hours/month (routine) + ~10–20 hours/year (write-off events)

### Staffing Implication
- **AR Supervisor**: adds ~2 hours/month for write-off initiation and documentation. Absorbed.
- **Controller**: adds ~1 hour/month for provision review + 30 min/quarter for summary presentation. Absorbed within existing close duties.
- **AR Clerk**: adds ~30 min/month for aging review escalation. Absorbed.
- No incremental headcount.

## W94. Customer Deposit & Advance Payment Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer places special order (W38); corporate/project account requires advance payment for large order; layaway deposit (W75); or customer requests credit balance refund |
| **Frequency** | ~800–1,200 customer deposits/month (special orders, project accounts, layaway) |
| **Volume** | Avg PHP 5,000–50,000 per deposit; project deposits can reach PHP 500,000+ |
| **Owner** | AR Accountant |
| **Participants** | AR Accountant, CSR / Sales Associate, Store Manager, Treasury Analyst, Customer |

### Background

Customer deposits (advance payments) are a common retail scenario for big-box home improvement: special orders for non-stock items, large project orders requiring down payments, layaway deposits, and corporate accounts with credit balances. Without a structured workflow, deposits can be mishandled — unapplied to invoices, forgotten refunds, or misallocated across entities. This workflow manages the full deposit lifecycle from receipt to application, refund, or forfeiture. It supports FIN-005 (AR for B2B) and POS-020 (layaway/installment sales).

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Deposit collection**: (a) **At POS** (store): Sales Associate or CSR collects deposit from customer for special order (W38), layaway (W75), or large in-store project order; system creates deposit receipt with deposit reference, customer account (loyalty, trade, or corporate), amount, and linked sales order or special order reference; payment captured via standard POS tender (cash, card, e-wallet); system posts to GL: Dr. Cash / Cr. Customer Deposits (liability); (b) **Online** (ecommerce): Customer prepays for BOPIS (W11) or home delivery (W19); system treats as advance payment captured by Digital Commerce Inc. and remitted to Depot Inc. per W14 IC model; (c) **Bank transfer** (corporate/project accounts): Corporate customer wires advance payment for large project order (W58); AR Accountant receives bank credit advice; posts deposit to customer account with project order reference | Sales Associate / CSR / AR Accountant | Store Manager / Controller | 5 min/POS deposit; 15 min/bank transfer |
| 2 | **Deposit tracking**: System maintains deposit register per customer showing: deposit reference, date, amount, linked order, status (open/partially applied/fully applied/forfeited/refunded), aging; deposits are customer-level liability (not order-level revenue) until applied to invoice at fulfillment | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | **Deposit application — at fulfillment**: (a) When linked sales order is fulfilled and invoice is generated (POS sale, special order delivery, project billing per W58), system auto-applies deposit to invoice: system reduces customer deposit liability (Dr. Customer Deposits) and reduces AR (Cr. Accounts Receivable) or cash balance; (b) if deposit exceeds invoice amount: system applies deposit to invoice and maintains residual credit balance on customer account for next invoice or refund; (c) if deposit is less than invoice amount: system applies full deposit and customer pays remaining balance at POS or via standard AR terms | System / AR Accountant | Controller | Automated + 5 min/review |
| 4 | **Deposit refund**: (a) Customer requests cancellation of special order before fulfillment: CSR processes deposit refund per cancellation policy (full refund, less cancellation fee per W38, or forfeiture per W75 layaway rules); (b) system creates refund transaction: Dr. Customer Deposits / Cr. Cash (for POS refunds) or Cr. AP (for bank transfer refunds); (c) refund processed within 3–5 business days per customer-facing SLA; (d) for card/e-wallet deposits: refund to original payment method; for cash deposits: cash refund at store; for bank transfer deposits: bank transfer back to customer | CSR / AR Accountant | Store Manager | 10 min/refund |
| 5 | **Unclaimed deposit aging**: System monitors unapplied/unrefunded deposits with aging: (a) 0–90 days: normal; (b) 90–180 days: system sends customer notification (deposit reminder); (c) 180–365 days: CSR contacts customer for disposition (apply, refund, or forfeit); (d) > 365 days: per Philippine law and BuildRight policy, unclaimed deposits may be recognized as revenue with Controller approval; system generates unclaimed deposit report for Controller review | System / CSR | Controller | 15 min/week |
| 6 | **Layaway deposit forfeiture** (W75 integration): (a) If customer cancels layaway or defaults on installment payments per W75 cancellation rules, system forfeits deposit per layaway agreement terms; (b) forfeiture amount posted as Dr. Customer Deposits / Cr. Other Income (cancellation fee) or Cr. Revenue (if goods retained); (c) inventory reservation released per W75 | System / CSR | Store Manager | Per W75 |
| 7 | **Project account advance billing** (W58 integration): For corporate/project accounts with milestone billing: (a) project contract may specify advance payment (e.g., 30% down, 40% at delivery, 30% at completion); (b) each advance collected per step 1; (c) at each billing milestone (W58), system applies collected deposits to milestone invoice; (d) retention amounts (typically 10% held for 90–180 days post-completion) tracked separately in deposit register with scheduled release date | AR Accountant | Controller | Per W58 |
| 8 | **Monthly reconciliation**: AR Accountant reconciles customer deposit liability account: (a) total deposit liability per GL must agree with deposit register total per system; (b) aging analysis of unapplied deposits; (c) investigate and resolve discrepancies; (d) report to Controller as part of W9A month-end close | AR Accountant | Controller | 2 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Deposit receipt creation at POS or AR with linked order reference (W94.1)
- Customer deposit register: per-customer, per-order deposit tracking with status and aging (W94.2)
- Auto-application of deposit to invoice at fulfillment (W94.3)
- Deposit refund processing with cancellation policy enforcement (W94.4)
- Unclaimed deposit aging report with escalation triggers (W94.5)
- Integration with W11 (BOPIS — prepayment), W12 (returns — refund from deposit), W19 (home delivery — prepayment), W38 (special orders — deposit collection and cancellation fee), W58 (project accounts — milestone advance billing), W75 (layaway — deposit and installment payments, forfeiture), W89 (bank reconciliation — deposit refunds in bank statement)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Unclaimed deposits aging into revenue recognition complexity**: Philippine law and BuildRight policy recognize unclaimed deposits as revenue after 365 days; however, the BIR may question the timing and taxability of unclaimed deposit recognition during examination, especially if the customer subsequently returns demanding a refund after revenue has been recognized
- **Special order deposit refund disputes**: when customers cancel special orders (W38), the cancellation fee policy may be unclear or inconsistently applied across stores; disputes over refund amounts create customer complaints and potential negative social media exposure in the Philippine retail market
- **Layaway deposit forfeiture compliance**: forfeiting layaway deposits (W75) must comply with the layaway agreement terms signed by the customer; if the agreement is missing or terms are ambiguous, the forfeiture is contestable and may result in full refund requirements plus customer relations damage
- **Deposit application errors for project accounts**: corporate/project accounts with milestone billing (W58) have complex deposit application requirements; applying deposits to the wrong milestone invoice or failing to apply deposits before invoicing creates AR discrepancies that are difficult to unravel


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "account" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-track of "tracking" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-generate of "Deposit application — at fulfillment" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-288 (ensure controlled execution — Customer Payment & Settlement (PA-16.3).)
- operational: AR Accountant review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
- POS deposit collection: ~5 min/deposit × ~800–1,200/month = ~67–100 hours/month (spread across 200 stores)
- Bank transfer deposit posting: ~15 min/transfer × ~50–100/month = ~12–25 hours/month (AR Accountant)
- Deposit application at fulfillment: automated + ~5 min review per application
- Deposit refund processing: ~10 min/refund × ~100–200/month = ~17–33 hours/month (CSRs + AR Accountant)
- Unclaimed deposit aging review: ~15 min/week (AR Accountant)
- Monthly deposit reconciliation: ~2 hours/month (AR Accountant)
- Total deposit management: ~100–160 hours/month (primarily at store level, ~20–30 hours Finance)

### Staffing Implication
- **AR Accountant**: adds ~2–3 hours/week for deposit management and reconciliation. Absorbed within existing Finance team.
- **CSRs**: deposit collection and refund adds ~5 min per transaction; with ~800–1,200 deposits/month across 200 stores = ~4–6 per store/month. Absorbed.
- **No incremental headcount.**

## W891. Customer Bad Debt Write-Off Proposal & Approval

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Account > 180 days overdue with exhausted collection efforts; or customer bankruptcy/closure confirmed |
| **Frequency** | ~10–20 bad debt write-off events/year (per W81) |
| **Volume** | Average write-off: PHP 100,000–1,000,000; annual bad debt target: < 0.5% of B2B revenue |
| **Owner** | Credit Manager / Finance Controller |
| **Participants** | Collection Agent, Credit Manager, Finance Controller, CFO, Legal Counsel, External Auditor |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identify write-off candidate — Collection Agent or Credit Manager identifies account meeting write-off criteria: (a) > 180 days overdue, (b) 3+ collection calls with no response or broken promises, (c) no active orders in 90+ days, (d) customer confirmed closed/bankrupt, or (e) legal collection deemed cost-prohibitive | Collection Agent | Credit Manager | 15 min |
| 2 | Compile write-off proposal — document: (a) account history (credit since date, total sales, payment pattern), (b) collection history (calls made, promises broken, last contact), (c) overdue invoice detail, (d) reason for write-off (bankruptcy, unresponsive, dispute, other), (e) legal opinion (if applicable), (f) recovery potential assessment (any assets or guarantors), (g) proposed write-off amount | Credit Manager | Finance Controller | 1–2 hours |
| 3 | Evaluate legal collection option — for amounts > PHP 500,000: Legal Counsel assesses viability of legal action — cost of litigation vs. recovery probability; recommend legal action or write-off | Legal Counsel | CFO | 1 hour |
| 4 | Obtain write-off approval — authorization: (a) ≤ PHP 50,000: Finance Manager + Controller, (b) PHP 50,001–200,000: CFO, (c) PHP 200,001–1,000,000: CEO + CFO, (d) > PHP 1,000,000: Board resolution (aligned to W81's write-off authority ladder); each approver reviews proposal and supporting documentation | Finance Controller | CFO / CEO | 30 min per approval level |
| 5 | Execute write-off — (a) post bad debt expense in GL, (b) reverse AR balance, (c) update customer status to "Written Off", (d) apply credit block to prevent future orders, (e) update allowance for doubtful accounts, (f) file BIR documentation for bad debt deduction (requires BIR-permitted evidence of collection efforts) | Finance Controller | CFO | 1 hour |
| 6 | External collection agency referral (optional) — for write-offs > PHP 200,000 with identifiable customer: refer to external collection agency on contingency basis (typically 15–25% of recovered amount); track recovery | Credit Manager | Finance Controller | 1 hour |
| 7 | Annual bad debt review — compile annual write-off summary: (a) total write-offs vs. target (< 0.5% of B2B revenue), (b) root cause analysis, (c) industry and geographic concentration, (d) credit scoring model accuracy, (e) recommendations for portfolio improvement | Credit Manager | CFO | 4 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- **ERP AR Module** — write-off processing, customer status update, GL posting
- **Collection Management System** — collection history documentation
- **Finance Module** — bad debt expense, allowance for doubtful accounts, BIR documentation
- **Legal Matter Management** — litigation assessment, external collection agency tracking
- **BI Dashboard** — bad debt trends, portfolio risk analysis, scoring model accuracy

### Pain Points / Risks
- BIR bad debt deduction requires extensive documentation — 10+ pieces of evidence per write-off
- Write-off may be premature — customer could recover and pay (construction project delays are common)
- External collection agency recovery rate is low (~10–20%)
- Bad debt write-off impacts P&L — management may resist timely write-offs to protect earnings
- Concentrated risk — single large contractor default can exceed annual bad debt budget
- Written-off customers may return with new applications — need clear policy on re-admission


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "account meeting write-off criteria" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-document of "Compile write-off proposal — document" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-collect of "collection option — for amounts > PHP 500,000" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-288 (ensure controlled execution — Customer Payment & Settlement (PA-16.3).)
- operational: Credit Manager / Finance Controller review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- Credit Manager: write-off proposal preparation (core responsibility)
- Legal Counsel: 1 hour per write-off > PHP 500,000
- Finance Controller: GL processing and BIR documentation
- External collection agency: contingency-based engagement

### Time Estimate
- Per write-off: 4–8 person-hours over 2–4 weeks
- **Annual estimate: 20 write-offs × 6 hours = 120 person-hours/year**

## W1326. Customer B2B Project Payment Plan Negotiation, Arrears Management & Restructuring

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Corporate/project customer (from ~200 B2B accounts) requests payment plan for outstanding invoice(s); or AR aging shows customer 30+ days overdue; or customer proactively contacts BuildRight regarding cash flow difficulties |
| **Frequency** | ~15–25 payment plan negotiations/month (primarily during post-project billing phase for corporate accounts) |
| **Volume** | Affects ~200 corporate/institutional accounts with Net 60–90 day terms; ~10–15% may require payment restructuring at any time |
| **Owner** | Credit Manager |
| **Participants** | Credit Manager, Credit Analyst, AR Collector, Sales Manager (B2B), CFO, Customer (Finance/Procurement contact) |

### Background

BuildRight's ~200 corporate/institutional accounts generate ~10% of revenue (~PHP 6.2B/year). These accounts carry Net 60–90 day credit terms for large construction and government projects. Project-based B2B sales (W58) often result in large outstanding invoices (PHP 500K–5M per project) that face payment delays due to: (a) customer's own project billing cycle (general contractors wait for developer payment before paying suppliers), (b) government bureaucratic payment processing (government accounts per W1180 may take 90–180 days), (c) project disputes (customer claims material quality issues or quantity discrepancies), or (d) customer cash flow problems. W81 covers bad debt write-off and W1200 covers monthly statement review. This workflow covers the intermediate step: negotiating a payment plan to recover the debt while preserving the customer relationship — a critical balance for accounts representing significant annual revenue.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Arrears identification & escalation**: System identifies overdue invoices per AR aging report: (a) 30 days overdue: system sends automated past-due reminder per W892; (b) 45 days overdue: AR Collector contacts customer for payment commitment; (c) 60 days overdue (past credit terms): Credit Analyst reviews account; if customer indicates inability to pay in full, escalates to Credit Manager for payment plan negotiation; (d) system flags account as "Payment Plan Candidate" with total outstanding, days overdue, customer payment history, and account lifetime value | System / AR Collector / Credit Analyst | Credit Manager | 1–2 hours/account |
| 2 | **Customer financial assessment**: Before negotiation, Credit Analyst assesses customer's financial situation: (a) payment history: has customer paid on time previously? is this a one-time delay or recurring pattern? (b) outstanding exposure: total AR balance including current invoices not yet due; (c) customer credit score: current score per FIN-044 monitoring; (d) external signals: has customer's business been affected by economic downturn, natural disaster, or project cancellation? (e) account value: annual revenue from customer, margin contribution, and strategic importance (government accounts, major developers); (f) Credit Analyst prepares recommendation: (i) standard payment plan (installments over 60–90 days), (ii) extended terms (restructure to longer payment period), (iii) accelerated collection (legal demand if high-risk), or (iv) write-off per W81 if uncollectable | Credit Analyst | Credit Manager | 2–3 hours/account |
| 3 | **Payment plan negotiation**: Credit Manager contacts customer's Finance/Procurement contact: (a) discuss reason for non-payment: cash flow timing, project dispute, billing error, or financial distress; (b) if project dispute: coordinate with Sales Manager and Category Manager to resolve dispute (material quality per W110, quantity discrepancy per W109) before payment plan discussion; (c) negotiate payment plan terms: (i) **Standard installment plan**: total outstanding split into 3–6 equal monthly payments; no interest if paid per schedule; (ii) **Partial payment with extended terms**: customer pays 30–50% immediately; remaining balance restructured over 90–180 days with 1% monthly late payment charge; (iii) **Project milestone payment**: payment aligned with customer's own project billing milestones (common for government and developer accounts); customer provides copy of their project billing schedule as commitment; (d) Credit Manager documents negotiated terms in system; customer confirms in writing (email or signed payment plan agreement) | Credit Manager / Customer | CFO (plans > PHP 2M) | 2–4 hours |
| 4 | **Approval & formalization**: (a) Credit Manager submits payment plan for approval: (i) plans ≤ PHP 1M: Credit Manager approves; (ii) plans PHP 1M–5M: CFO approves; (iii) plans > PHP 5M: CEO approves; (b) CFO reviews: (i) total exposure vs. customer lifetime value, (ii) risk of non-payment under the plan, (iii) impact on BuildRight's cash flow, (iv) precedent risk (will other customers request similar terms?); (c) if approved: system creates formal Payment Plan record linked to original invoice(s); payment schedule auto-generated with due dates and amounts; (d) if not approved: Credit Manager re-negotiates with customer or escalates to accelerated collection per W1381; (e) system updates customer account with "Active Payment Plan" status; new orders may require COD or deposit per Credit Manager's recommendation | Credit Manager / CFO | CEO (plans > PHP 5M) | 1–2 hours |

| 2 | Auto-matching engine attempts to match each payment to open AR invoices: (a) matches on customer name + exact amount → auto-apply; (b) matches on reference number containing invoice number → auto-apply; (c) matches on customer name + amount within tolerance (±5%) → flag for manual review; (d) no match found → route to unapplied cash queue | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | For auto-matched payments (estimated 60–70% match rate): system posts AR application — Dr. Cash / Cr. Accounts Receivable; invoice status updated to "Paid"; customer AR balance reduced; system sends payment confirmation to customer via email/SMS | System | — | Automated |
| 4 | For unmatched payments (estimated 30–40%): AR Clerk reviews unapplied cash queue daily — (a) check customer bank statement details for invoice references or project names; (b) cross-reference with customer's recent email or phone communication about payment; (c) contact customer to confirm which invoices the payment covers; (d) manually apply payment to correct invoices | AR Clerk | AR Supervisor | 5–10 min/payment |
| 5 | For partial payments: AR Clerk applies payment to invoices per customer instruction; if no instruction: apply to oldest invoices first (FIFO); remaining balance stays open in AR aging | AR Clerk | AR Supervisor | 5 min/payment |
| 6 | Weekly: AR Supervisor reviews unapplied cash aging — any payment unapplied for > 5 business days escalated to AR Supervisor for resolution; unapplied cash > 30 days flagged for Finance Manager review | AR Supervisor | Finance Manager | 30 min/week |
| 7 | Monthly: Treasury Analyst reconciles electronic payment bank statements to GL cash postings; identifies and resolves discrepancies | Treasury Analyst | Controller | Part of W89 |

### System Touchpoints
- Bank API integration: PESONet batch file import and InstaPay real-time webhook notifications (W1382.1)
- Auto-matching engine: configurable matching rules (customer name, amount, reference, tolerance) with confidence scoring (W1382.2)
- AR application posting: Dr. Cash / Cr. AR with invoice-level detail (W1382.3)
- Unapplied cash queue: daily worklist for AR Clerks with aging and escalation (W1382.4, W1382.6)
- Customer payment confirmation: automated email/SMS with invoice details and payment receipt (W1382.3)
- Integration with W89 (bank reconciliation), W889 (AR aging — electronic payments reduce AR balances), W1380 (PDC — alternative payment channel)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Low auto-match rate for InstaPay**: InstaPay's PHP 50,000 per-transaction limit means large B2B payments are split into multiple transfers; each transfer generates a separate bank record, and the customer may not reference invoices in each partial transfer
- **Customer name mismatch**: bank records show the remitting account name, which may differ from BuildRight's customer master (e.g., "Juan Dela Cruz Construction" vs. "JDC Builders" vs. "J. Dela Cruz General Construction"); fuzzy matching helps but generates false positives
- **PESONet processing lag**: PESONet batches are processed at cut-off times (typically 3:00 PM and end-of-day); a payment initiated on Friday afternoon may not settle until Monday, creating a weekend gap where the customer believes they have paid but AR still shows the invoice as open
- **Reference number limitations**: not all banking channels allow free-text reference fields; customers using mobile banking apps may only see "BuildRight Depot" as payee without the ability to add invoice numbers


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-suggest of candidate invoice matches (amount ±5% tolerance, reference-number heuristics) into the manual review queue (supports steps 2 and 4).
- System auto-route of unapplied cash >5 business days to the AR Supervisor escalation queue (replaces manual Step 6 triage).
- System auto-report of daily application-rate and aging metrics to the Finance Controller (replaces manual reporting atop steps 1–6).

### Controls
- CTL-288 (ensure controlled execution — Customer Payment & Settlement (PA-16.3).)
- operational: AR Clerk review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
~5–10 min/unmatched payment × ~40–80 unmatched payments/day = ~3.3–13.3 hours/day for AR Clerk. As auto-match rate improves (from 60% to 80%), manual effort drops to ~2–4 hours/day.

### Staffing Implication
At current volumes, 1 dedicated AR Clerk for electronic payment reconciliation and application. As auto-match rate improves, effort absorbed across existing AR team. No incremental headcount beyond current AR staffing.

## W1524. Philippine Government & Institutional Customer Tax-Exempt Sales Processing, BIR-Compliant Documentation & OR Requirements

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Government agency, LGU, state university, or tax-exempt institution presents purchase order with tax exemption certificate |
| **Frequency** | ~200–400 tax-exempt transactions/month across 200 stores (~1–2 per store per month) |
| **Volume** | Average transaction value: PHP 10,000–500,000 (government procurement of construction materials for public infrastructure, school buildings, barangay halls) |
| **Owner** | AR Supervisor |
| **Participants** | AR Supervisor, Cashier/Sales Rep, Store Manager, Customer (government purchasing officer), Tax Compliance Manager |

### Background

Philippine government agencies, local government units (LGUs), government-owned and controlled corporations (GOCCs), state universities, and certain tax-exempt institutions (e.g., Philippine Red Cross, religious organizations with BIR tax exemption ruling) are exempt from VAT on purchases under specific conditions defined by the Tax Code (NIRC) and BIR Revenue Regulations. Processing tax-exempt sales at BuildRight Depot requires: (a) verifying the customer's tax exemption eligibility, (b) issuing a BIR-compliant Official Receipt (OR) or Sales Invoice (SI) with VAT correctly shown as zero or exempt (not the same as VAT-inclusive), (c) maintaining documentation trail for BIR audit: tax exemption certificate, purchase order, delivery receipt, and OR/SI copy. Incorrect processing — charging VAT to an exempt government customer (causing reimbursement rejection) or wrongly granting exemption to a non-qualifying customer (causing BIR assessment against BuildRight) — both create financial and compliance issues.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tax exemption verification**: (a) customer presents government purchase order (PO) or purchase request with: (i) agency/LGU letterhead, (ii) authorized signatory, (iii) fund allocation reference (allotment class), (iv) tax exemption certificate or BIR ruling; (b) Cashier/Sales Rep verifies: (i) tax exemption document is current (not expired — most have annual renewal), (ii) purchasing officer has authorization letter from agency head, (iii) agency is on BIR's list of VAT-exempt entities; (c) if verification passes: proceed with tax-exempt sale; if uncertain: escalate to AR Supervisor; if clearly ineligible: inform customer that standard VAT applies | Cashier / Sales Rep | AR Supervisor | 10–15 min |
| 2 | **Transaction processing**: (a) Cashier creates sales order/transaction in POS with: (i) customer account linked to government entity master (separate from retail customer), (ii) VAT treatment set to "Exempt" (not zero-rated — these have different BIR treatments), (iii) PO number and fund allocation reference recorded; (b) system calculates: (i) item prices net of VAT (standard prices are VAT-inclusive per BuildRight's pricing policy — system backs out 12% VAT), (ii) total amount exempt; (c) Store Manager authorizes tax-exempt transaction (requires manager override in POS to prevent unauthorized VAT removal) | Cashier / System | Store Manager | 10–15 min |
| 3 | **BIR-compliant documentation**: (a) system generates: (i) Sales Invoice (SI) with: "VAT EXEMPT" printed prominently, customer TIN, agency name and address, PO reference, item details with unit prices (net of VAT), total amount, "VAT Exempt pursuant to Section 109 of the NIRC" notation; (ii) Official Receipt (OR) with matching details; (b) Cashier attaches: (i) copy of tax exemption certificate, (ii) copy of purchase order, (iii) copy of authorized purchasing officer ID; (c) all documents stapled/filed in store's government transaction folder for BIR audit readiness | Cashier / System | AR Supervisor | 10–15 min |
| 4 | **Delivery and acceptance**: (a) if order includes delivery: Delivery Receipt (DR) generated with matching SI number; (b) government customer's receiving officer signs DR with date and official stamp; (c) signed DR returned to store and filed with SI/OR documentation package | Delivery Staff / Customer | Store Manager | Delivery per schedule |
| 5 | **Payment collection**: (a) government customer payment typically via: (i) government check (MDS check from DBM or local treasury check), (ii) bank transfer to BuildRight's bank account; (b) AR Supervisor verifies: (i) check details match SI amount exactly (government cannot pay more or less than invoiced amount), (ii) check is from the correct government fund (some agencies have multiple accounts); (c) AR Supervisor applies payment to customer account with government check reference number; (d) if payment via MDS check: deposit to BuildRight's bank — government checks clear within 1–3 banking days | AR Supervisor | — | 15–20 min |
| 6 | **Monthly reconciliation**: (a) AR Supervisor compiles all government tax-exempt sales for the month: (i) total exempt sales amount, (ii) list of government entities, (iii) corresponding SI/OR numbers; (b) Tax Compliance Manager reviews for BIR VAT return preparation: exempt sales must be correctly reported in the monthly/quarterly VAT return (BIR Form 2550Q/2550M) in the "exempt sales" column; (c) documentation package archived for 10-year BIR audit retention per A6.5 | AR Supervisor / Tax Compliance Manager | Finance Controller | 2–3 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Government customer master: entity name, TIN, tax exemption certificate number and expiry, purchasing authority contacts (W1524.1)
- POS VAT exemption workflow: manager override required, VAT treatment set to "Exempt" with automatic price recalculation from VAT-inclusive to net-of-VAT (W1524.2)
- BIR-compliant SI/OR generation: "VAT EXEMPT" designation, NIRC Section 109 notation, government entity TIN (W1524.3)
- Government payment module: MDS check processing, government bank transfer reconciliation (W1524.5)
- Monthly exempt sales report: aggregation for BIR VAT return preparation, 10-year document archive (W1524.6)
- Integration with W527 (POS Tax Exemption Processing — base workflow), W540 (BIR Invoice Reprint), W1516 (BIR EIS Compliance)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **VAT calculation confusion**: BuildRight's standard prices are VAT-inclusive (per Philippine retail convention); when a government customer's PO shows VAT-exclusive prices, Cashier must understand whether to (a) simply back out 12% from the VAT-inclusive retail price, or (b) apply a different base price — errors lead to government audit findings and reimbursement shortfalls
- **Tax exemption certificate validity**: expired tax exemption certificates are a common issue; government agencies sometimes present last year's certificate; processing with an expired certificate exposes BuildRight to BIR assessment for the uncollected VAT (12% of the transaction value)
- **Government check processing delays**: MDS (Modified Disbursement System) checks from national agencies and local treasury checks from LGUs may take 30–60 days to be issued after delivery; during this period, the receivable ages on BuildRight's books — impacting cash flow
- **Documentation completeness for BIR audit**: if any document in the package is missing (tax exemption certificate copy, PO copy, signed DR), BIR may disallow the VAT exemption and assess BuildRight for the VAT amount plus penalties; store-level filing discipline is critical


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-document of "document is current" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-record of "recorded" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-generate of "printed prominently, customer TIN, agency name and address, PO reference, item…" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-288 (ensure controlled execution — Customer Payment & Settlement (PA-16.3).)
- operational: AR Supervisor review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
- Per transaction: 30–45 min (verification 10–15 min + processing 10–15 min + documentation 10–15 min)
- Payment collection: 15–20 min per transaction (government checks take longer to process)
- Monthly reconciliation: 2–3 hours
- **Chain-wide: ~300 transactions/month × 50 min average = ~250 hours/month (distributed across 200 stores + AR team)**

### Staffing Implication
- AR Supervisor: ~2–3 hours/month on government transaction oversight and reconciliation; absorbed
- Cashier/Sales Rep: ~30–45 min per government transaction × 1–2/month = ~1–1.5 hours/store/month; absorbed
- Store Manager: ~5–10 min/transaction for manager override authorization; absorbed
- Tax Compliance Manager: ~2–3 hours/month on exempt sales review for VAT return; absorbed
- No incremental headcount
