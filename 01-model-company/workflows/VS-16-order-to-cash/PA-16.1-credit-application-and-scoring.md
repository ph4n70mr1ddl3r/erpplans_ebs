## W24. Trade & Corporate Credit Application

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer applies for trade or corporate credit account |
| **Frequency** | ~100–150 new credit applications/month |
| **Volume** | ~5,200 active trade accounts; ~200 corporate accounts |
| **Owner** | AR Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Sales Rep, AR Clerk, AR Supervisor, Finance Manager, Credit Committee |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customer submits credit application (in-store, via Sales Rep, or online form) | Customer / Sales Rep | AR Clerk | 15 min |
| 2 | AR Clerk verifies application completeness: business registration, financial statements, trade references | AR Clerk | AR Supervisor | 30 min |
| 3 | AR Clerk runs credit check (bank references, trade references, external credit bureau if available) | AR Clerk | AR Supervisor | 30 min |
| 4 | AR Clerk prepares credit assessment with recommended limit and terms | AR Clerk | AR Supervisor | 15 min |
| 5 | Approval per tier (see matrix below) | Approver | Approver | 10–30 min |
| 6 | System creates customer account with approved credit limit, payment terms, and entity assignment | AR Clerk | AR Supervisor | 15 min |
| 7 | Customer notified of approval; account activated | AR Clerk | — | 10 min |
| 8 | Annual credit review: AR Clerk reviews all accounts for limit adjustment (increase, decrease, or suspension) | AR Clerk | AR Supervisor | 5 min/account |

### Credit Approval Matrix

| Credit Limit | Approver |
|---|---|
| ≤ PHP 200,000 | AR Supervisor |
| PHP 200,001 – 1,000,000 | Finance Manager |
| > PHP 1,000,000 | Credit Committee (CFO + Finance Manager + AR Supervisor) |

### System Touchpoints
- Credit application form (online or in-store) (W24.1)
- Customer master creation with credit limit, payment terms, entity assignment, and VAT treatment classification (W24.6)
- VAT treatment per customer account: AR Clerk classifies account as Standard (12% VAT), VAT-Exempt (government agencies with BIR ruling, PEZA-registered entities), or Zero-Rated (export sales) during account setup based on supporting documents (BIR ruling, PEZA certificate, government purchase order, diplomatic note); system stores VAT treatment in customer master; applied automatically at POS (W5B.4c) and AR invoicing (W8) (W24.6)
- Credit limit enforcement at POS/sales order (W24.6 → W8.3)
- Annual credit review scheduling and workflow (W24.8)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Insufficient credit bureau infrastructure in the Philippines**: unlike mature markets, Philippine credit bureaus (CIC, TransUnion) have limited coverage for SME trade accounts; credit assessment relies heavily on trade references and bank certification letters, which can be fabricated or from related parties
- **Credit limit override pressure from Sales Reps**: during promotional periods or large project orders, Sales Reps pressure AR Supervisors to approve higher credit limits beyond what the assessment supports; ad-hoc limit increases without proper assessment increase bad debt exposure (W81)
- **Annual credit review backlog**: with ~5,200 active accounts, reviewing each annually at 5 min/account = ~433 hours/year; AR Clerks often deprioritize credit reviews in favor of urgent collections work, leaving some accounts with outdated credit limits that no longer reflect the customer's financial condition
- **Corporate account concentration risk**: ~200 corporate accounts may represent a disproportionate share of AR exposure; a single large corporate account default (e.g., a major construction company) could exceed PHP 10M, dwarfing the combined exposure of hundreds of smaller trade accounts; mitigated by CTL-12 (separate credit approval from sales execution.); CTL-43 (prevent Sales Rep self-approval of credit limits on own accounts)


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-submit of "submits credit application" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-validate of "AR Clerk runs credit check" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-generate of "prepares credit assessment with recommended limit and terms" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-12 (separate credit approval from sales execution.); CTL-43 (prevent Sales Rep self-approval of credit limits on own accounts.)
- operational: AR Supervisor review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
- Credit application processing: ~90 min/application (verification + credit check + assessment)
- Monthly volume: 100–150 applications × 90 min = ~150–225 hours/month
- Approval per tier: ~10–30 min/application
- Account creation and activation: ~25 min/application
- Annual credit review: ~5 min/account × ~5,200 accounts (system-generated tickler with 20–30 min manual depth only on flagged accounts per W1300) = ~433 system hours + ~500–800 manual hours/year
- Total credit processing: ~200–275 hours/month across 3–4 AR Clerks

### Staffing Implication
- **AR Clerks** (3–4): 100–150 applications/month × ~90 min each = 150–225 hours/month. Spread across 3–4 clerks = ~50 hours/clerk/month. Absorbed within existing AR team workload, primarily in the first half of the month when collection activity is lighter.

## W108. Customer Credit Collection & Escalation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Trade or corporate account customer exceeds invoice payment terms (Net 30 or Net 60–90); or monthly scheduled collection review cycle |
| **Frequency** | Daily collection activities; weekly escalation review; monthly portfolio review |
| **Volume** | ~5,400 active AR accounts (5,200 trade + 200 corporate); ~3,500 AR invoices/month; ~30–40% of accounts typically carrying an overdue balance at any time |
| **Owner** | AR Collector (daily); AR Supervisor (escalation); Finance Manager (legal escalation) |
| **Participants** | AR Collector, AR Supervisor, Finance Manager, Sales Rep, Sales Manager, Controller, Legal, Collection Agency |

### Background

W8 covers AR processing — invoice generation, credit limit enforcement, and credit memo handling. W81 covers bad debt provisioning, write-off, and recovery. However, the active collection workflow between an invoice becoming overdue and reaching bad debt write-off is not detailed. At ~5,400 B2B accounts carrying ~40% of company revenue (~PHP 24.9B/year: 30% trade + 10% corporate per §9.1), effective collections directly impact cash flow and working capital. Philippine collections practice involves specific legal requirements (demand letters, BIR documentation) and cultural considerations (relationship-based collection approach). This workflow bridges W8 (AR processing) and W81 (bad debt write-off).

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Daily overdue identification**: System generates daily AR aging report with overdue accounts grouped by aging bucket: Current, 1–30 days overdue, 31–60 days, 61–90 days, 91–180 days, 181+ days; each account shows: customer name, account number, total overdue amount, oldest invoice date, assigned Sales Rep, last payment date, and last contact date | System | — | Automated |
| 2 | **1–30 days overdue (soft collection)**: (a) System sends automated payment reminder via email/SMS on day 1 past due and day 15 past due with outstanding invoice details and payment instructions; (b) AR Collector reviews accounts > 15 days overdue; calls customer contact to confirm receipt of invoice and understand payment timeline; (c) AR Collector logs contact attempt, customer response, and committed payment date in system; (d) if customer disputes invoice: AR Collector coordinates with Sales Rep and Billing to resolve dispute within 5 business days | AR Collector / System | AR Supervisor | 10–15 min/account |
| 3 | **31–60 days overdue (active collection)**: (a) AR Collector makes weekly follow-up call/email per account; (b) AR Collector and assigned Sales Rep make joint call to customer (Sales Rep relationship leverage); (c) if customer acknowledges debt but requests extension: AR Collector processes payment term extension request with AR Supervisor approval (maximum 30-day extension, one-time per invoice); (d) if customer is unresponsive after 3 contact attempts: system flags account as "Unresponsive" and escalates to AR Supervisor | AR Collector / Sales Rep | AR Supervisor | 20 min/account |
| 4 | **61–90 days overdue (escalation)**: (a) AR Supervisor sends formal demand letter (first demand) via registered mail with return card — includes outstanding amount, invoice references, payment demand within 15 days, and notice of potential credit hold; (b) system places account on **credit hold** — new orders blocked until overdue balance resolved; credit hold override per CTL-05 requires Finance Manager approval; (c) AR Supervisor contacts customer's management (not just accounts payable contact) to negotiate payment plan; (d) if customer agrees to payment plan: AR Supervisor records plan in system with installment dates and amounts; system monitors compliance and auto-escalates if any installment missed | AR Supervisor | Finance Manager | 30 min/account |
| 5 | **91–180 days overdue (legal escalation)**: (a) Finance Manager sends second demand letter via registered mail with notice of potential legal action; (b) Finance Manager and Sales Manager jointly evaluate account: (i) is the customer still active (recent orders, store visits)?, (ii) is there a legitimate dispute?, (iii) is the business still operating?, (iv) what is the total exposure including unbilled orders?; (c) for active customers with temporary cash flow issues: Finance Manager may approve structured payment plan (maximum 6 months) with personal guarantee or post-dated checks (received and warehoused per W1380); (d) for unresponsive or unwilling customers: Finance Manager escalates to Legal for legal demand letter (third and final demand per Philippine legal practice); (e) Legal reviews account documentation (invoices, delivery receipts signed by customer, demand letters sent, contact logs) and issues final demand letter; (f) system reclassifies receivable to "Litigation" status | Finance Manager / Legal | Controller | 1 hour/account |
| 6 | **181+ days overdue (external collection / write-off)**: (a) Controller reviews all accounts in 181+ day bucket monthly; (b) for accounts with total exposure > PHP 200,000: Controller recommends engagement of external collection agency per W62 service contract; (c) for accounts where Legal confirms no viable recovery path: Controller initiates bad debt write-off per W81 with supporting documentation (demand letters, contact logs, Legal opinion, agency report); (d) for accounts where customer has partially paid and committed to remaining balance: AR Supervisor monitors payment plan compliance; (e) system maintains full collection history per account for future credit decisions | Controller / AR Supervisor | CFO | 30 min/account review |
| 7 | **Collection agency coordination**: (a) Finance Manager engages licensed collection agency per W62 non-PO contract; (b) AR Supervisor provides agency with complete account documentation package; (c) agency operates on contingency fee basis (typically 10–20% of recovered amount); (d) AR Supervisor monitors agency performance monthly; (e) recovered amounts posted by AR Collector against customer's outstanding balance; (f) agency fees invoiced and processed per W7 | AR Supervisor / Finance Manager | Controller | Ongoing |
| 8 | **Weekly collection review**: AR Supervisor conducts weekly collection team meeting: (a) review accounts entering new aging buckets, (b) track contact completion rates (target: 100% of accounts contacted within SLA), (c) review payment plan compliance, (d) assign escalated accounts to collectors, (e) identify accounts requiring Sales Rep intervention | AR Supervisor | Finance Manager | 1 hour/week |
| 9 | **Monthly collection performance reporting**: AR Supervisor prepares monthly collection performance report for Controller and CFO: (a) total AR aging by bucket and trend, (b) days sales outstanding (DSO) — target: < 45 days, (c) collection effectiveness index (CEI) — target: ≥ 85%, (d) bad debt write-off rate as % of revenue — target: < 0.3%, (e) accounts on credit hold — count and value, (f) payment plan portfolio — total value, compliance rate, (g) top 20 overdue accounts by value with status and action plan, (h) Sales Rep territory collection performance comparison | AR Supervisor | Controller | 2 hours/month |
| 10 | **Quarterly credit review trigger**: Accounts that reached 61+ days overdue during the quarter are flagged for credit limit review in the next quarterly credit review cycle per W24; AR Supervisor provides collection history and recommendation (maintain limit, reduce limit, convert to COD-only, or close account) to Credit Committee | AR Supervisor | Finance Manager | Part of W24 quarterly cycle |

### System Touchpoints
- Automated daily AR aging report with customer contact details, Sales Rep assignment, and collection status (W108.1)
- Automated payment reminders via email/SMS at configurable intervals (W108.2)
- Collection activity logging: contact attempts, customer responses, committed payment dates, dispute notes (W108.2–3)
- Payment term extension request workflow with AR Supervisor approval (W108.3c)
- Credit hold automation: system places hold at 61 days overdue; manual override per CTL-05 (W108.4b)
- Payment plan creation and compliance monitoring with auto-escalation on missed installments (W108.4d)
- Demand letter generation with registered mail tracking (W108.4a, W108.5a)
- Collection agency engagement and performance tracking (W108.7)
- Monthly collection performance dashboard: DSO, CEI, aging trend, write-off rate (W108.9)
- Integration with W7 (AP — collection agency fee processing), W8 (AR — invoice aging, credit memos, credit holds), W24 (credit application — credit limit review triggered by collection history), W58 (corporate accounts — project billing collection), W81 (bad debt write-off — accounts reaching write-off threshold), W84 (customer account reactivation — written-off accounts)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Collection fatigue and staff burnout**: making daily collection calls to ~1,500–2,000 overdue accounts is repetitive and emotionally draining; AR Collectors may become desensitized to aging escalation, processing calls mechanically without effective negotiation, leading to declining collection effectiveness over time
- **Sales Rep resistance to collection collaboration**: Sales Reps are compensated on sales volume, not collections; when asked to participate in joint collection calls (step 3), they may resist straining customer relationships; without management enforcement, the Sales Rep collaboration tier of the collection process is inconsistently executed
- **Demand letter compliance for BIR documentation**: formal demand letters sent via registered mail with return card (steps 4–5) are required for BIR-compliant bad debt write-off (W81); if registered mail documentation is not properly filed and retained, the write-off may be disallowed during BIR examination
- **Credit hold override pressure during peak season**: blocking sales to overdue accounts during peak retail season (October–December) generates intense internal pressure from Sales Reps and Store Managers; Finance Manager override approvals may become rubber-stamped under pressure, undermining the credit hold as a collection tool
- **External collection agency performance variability**: licensed collection agencies in the Philippines vary widely in effectiveness; some focus on easy accounts while ignoring difficult ones, and contingency fee structures may incentivize quick settlements at significant discounts rather than full recovery


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "accounts grouped by aging bucket" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "logs contact attempt, customer response, and committed payment date in system" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-escalate of "escalates to AR Supervisor" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-286 (ensure controlled execution — Credit Application & Scoring (PA-16.1).)
- operational: registered-mail demand-letter documentation (Steps 4–5) required for BIR-compliant bad debt write-off (W81)

### Time Estimate
- Daily overdue identification: automated
- 1–30 day overdue soft collection (calls/emails): ~10–15 min/account × ~800 accounts = ~133–200 hours/month
- 31–60 day overdue active collection: ~20 min/account × ~400 accounts = ~133 hours/month
- 61–90 day overdue escalation (demand letters, credit holds): ~30 min/account × ~150 accounts = ~75 hours/month
- 91–180 day overdue legal escalation: ~1 hour/account × ~50 accounts = ~50 hours/month
- 181+ day write-off/agency coordination: ~30 min/account review × ~20 accounts = ~10 hours/month
- Weekly collection team meeting: ~1 hour/week
- Monthly collection performance reporting: ~2 hours/month
- Quarterly credit review inputs: absorbed in W24 cycle
- Total collection effort: ~400–570 hours/month across 2–3 AR Collectors (~150–190 hours each/month = ~7–9 hours/day)

### Staffing Implication
- **2–3 AR Collectors** (within the existing ~46-person Finance team per profile §3.3): ~5,400 active accounts with ~30–40% overdue at any time = ~1,500–2,000 accounts requiring active collection management. At ~15 min average per account per month = ~375–500 hours/month. With 2–3 collectors that's ~150–250 hours each/month ≈ ~7–11 hours/day. This is a full-time role for 2–3 staff. Current AR team can absorb with dedicated collectors.
- **AR Supervisor**: adds ~1 hour/week for team meeting + ~2 hours/month for performance reporting + escalation handling. Absorbed within existing AR Supervisor role.
- **No incremental headcount.**

## W174. Store-Level Cash-in-Transit (CIT) & Armored Car Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Daily store cash accumulation exceeds threshold; scheduled armoured car pickup |
| **Frequency** | Daily per store (365 days/year) |
| **Volume** | ~PHP 350K/day average cash per store (42% of ~PHP 5B monthly retail sales ÷ 200 stores ÷ 30 days) |
| **Owner** | Treasury Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Lead Cashier, Armoured Car Vendor, Treasury Analyst |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cash Consolidation**: Lead Cashier performs EOD cash count; reconciles with POS Z-reports (W5F) | Lead Cashier | Store Manager | 30 min |
| 2 | **Secure Bagging**: Reconciled cash placed in tamper-evident CIT bags with serialized seals; system logs seal numbers | Store Manager | Store Manager | 10 min |
| 3 | **Pickup Verification**: Armoured car arrives; Store Manager verifies guard IDs and vehicle number against authorized list | Store Manager | Store Manager | 5 min |
| 4 | **Handover**: Guard scans CIT bags; Store Manager and Guard sign electronic/physical manifest; copy stored in ERP | Store Manager / Guard | Store Manager | 5 min |
| 5 | **System Posting**: Store Manager marks cash as "In-Transit to Bank"; ERP reduces Store Cash account, increases CIT account | Store Manager | — | Automated |
| 6 | **Bank Acknowledgment**: Bank/CIT vendor processes cash; sends daily credit report | CIT Vendor | — | Next Day |
| 7 | **Reconciliation**: Treasury Analyst matches Bank Credit to Store CIT record; investigates variances > PHP 100 | Treasury Analyst | Treasury Mgr | 2 hours/day |

### System Touchpoints
- POS EOD / Z-report integration
- Serialized CIT bag/seal tracking
- Guard/Vehicle authorization whitelist
- Bank credit file auto-matching (W30)
- Shortage/Overage exception reporting

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Cash shrinkage at high-volume stores**: top-performing Metro Manila stores may handle PHP 5M+ daily cash; even a 0.1% shortage rate equals PHP 5,000/day per store; without real-time variance alerting, cumulative losses across 200 stores can reach PHP 30M+/year before detection
- **CIT vendor reliability in provincial areas**: armored car service coverage is inconsistent outside Metro Manila, Cebu, and Davao; provincial stores may rely on next-day bank branch deposits, increasing overnight cash holding risk and exposure to robbery during holidays and peak seasons
- **Guard authentication fraud risk**: fake or compromised armored car guards are a known risk in the Philippines; the authorized guard/vehicle whitelist (step 3) must be updated in real-time when CIT vendor rotates personnel, which smaller vendors do frequently and with poor notification discipline; mitigated by periodic reconciliation against source documents
- **Reconciliation bottleneck**: Treasury Analyst spends ~2 hours/day matching 200 store deposits; if CIT vendor delays the bank credit report (step 6), reconciliation backlog cascades into W30 daily cash position inaccuracy, affecting cash sweep timing and inter-entity transfer decisions


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "count" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "logs seal numbers" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System rule-based authorization of "authorized list" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-286 (ensure controlled execution — Credit Application & Scoring (PA-16.1).)
- operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
- Per-store daily cash consolidation and bagging: ~40 min/store/day (Lead Cashier + Store Manager)
- Pickup verification and handover: ~10 min/store/day
- System posting: automated
- Daily reconciliation (Treasury Analyst): ~2 hours/day for 200 stores
- Variance investigation: ~30 min/variance × ~10–20 variances/day = ~5–10 hours/day
- Total CIT effort: ~2 hours/day Treasury + ~50 min/day per store (200 stores × Store Manager/Lead Cashier)

### Staffing Implication
- **Treasury Analysts (2)**: CIT reconciliation adds ~2–4 hours/day to the Treasury team's daily workload; with 200 stores generating daily deposit reconciliation items, this is a significant portion of the Treasury Analyst's day; during peak reconciliation backlogs, a second analyst may be needed to maintain W30 cash position accuracy
- **Store Managers (200)**: daily CIT cash consolidation and handover adds ~40 min to each Store Manager's closing routine; absorbed within W5F store closing procedures but contributes to overtime risk at high-volume stores
- **No incremental headcount**: the Treasury team absorbs CIT reconciliation as a core daily responsibility; however, if store count grows beyond ~250, a dedicated Cash Operations Analyst may be needed

## W572. Customer Credit Monitoring & Automated Alert Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Continuous monitoring via automated credit scoring engine; monthly credit portfolio review meeting |
| **Frequency** | Continuous automated monitoring (24/7); monthly portfolio review; quarterly credit policy review |
| **Volume** | ~5,200 trade accounts and ~200 corporate accounts monitored continuously; ~50–100 automated alerts per month requiring review |
| **Owner** | Credit Manager / AR Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Credit Analyst, AR Supervisor, Credit Manager, Finance Manager (I), Sales Manager (C for B2B relationship) |

### Background

W24 covers the initial trade/corporate credit application and approval process. W328 covers annual credit limit periodic review. W108 covers customer credit collection and escalation for overdue accounts. W8 covers accounts receivable processing. W229 covers B2B credit limit exception and escalation. However, no workflow covers ongoing continuous credit monitoring with automated alerts — the real-time surveillance of customer credit health indicators including: payment pattern changes, credit utilization spikes, overdue aging trends, external credit deterioration signals, and industry/market risk factors. For a company with ~5,400 B2B accounts generating ~40% of revenue (PHP ~24.9B annually, per W108 and §9.1), proactive credit monitoring is essential to minimize bad debt exposure while supporting sales growth.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Credit scoring engine continuously monitors credit portfolio using configured rules: (a) payment aging trends — rolling 6-month average days-to-pay vs. prior 6-month period; (b) credit utilization % — current outstanding balance / approved credit limit; (c) average days-to-pay trend — acceleration or deceleration; (d) NSF/bounced check history per W1381 — any instance in last 12 months; (e) order-to-invoice ratio — significant drops may indicate customer financial distress (reducing purchases); (f) external signals — credit bureau updates (TransUnion, CIBI) if enrolled | System | — | Continuous (24/7) |
| 2 | Alert generation — configurable thresholds trigger tiered alerts: (a) **Amber alert** (watch list): credit utilization >75%, average days-to-pay increased by >10 days vs. rolling average, first bounced check, order volume drop >30% vs. rolling average; (b) **Red alert** (action required): credit utilization >90%, any invoice >60 days overdue, second bounced check within 12 months, external credit bureau deterioration, customer under litigation or insolvency filing; alerts queued in Credit Analyst dashboard with priority ranking | System | — | Automated (real-time) |
| 3 | Credit Analyst reviews daily alert dashboard: (a) new alerts generated in last 24 hours; (b) aging of unreviewed alerts (SLA: Amber reviewed within 3 business days, Red reviewed within 1 business day); (c) accounts with multiple concurrent alerts (indicates compounding risk); (d) accounts transitioning from Amber to Red (deterioration trajectory) | Credit Analyst | Credit Manager | 1–2 hours/day |
| 4 | For Amber alerts: Credit Analyst conducts preliminary review — (a) pull full payment history for last 12 months; (b) assess if seasonal pattern (construction industry seasonal slowdown June–September during rainy season) or genuine deterioration; (c) check recent payment notes per W108 collection records; (d) review Sales Manager relationship notes; (e) if seasonal or explainable anomaly → no action, add monitoring note, revisit if trend continues for 2 consecutive months; (f) if unexplained deterioration → upgrade to enhanced monitoring with weekly check-in | Credit Analyst | Credit Manager | 20–30 min/account |
| 5 | For Red alerts: Credit Analyst conducts immediate comprehensive review — (a) full account history: all invoices, payments, credits, disputes for last 24 months; (b) collection notes per W108 — last contact date, promised payment dates, dispute status; (c) bounced check details per W1381 — amounts, dates, resolution status; (d) order book: open orders and unshipped SOs that would increase exposure; (e) B2B relationship context: discuss with Sales Manager — is customer experiencing project delay, market downturn, or one-time issue? | Credit Analyst / Sales Manager | Credit Manager | 45–60 min/account |
| 6 | Credit Analyst recommends action based on review: (a) **Temporary credit hold** — block new sales orders pending payment of overdue amount (system blocks SO creation per W24 authorization matrix); (b) **Credit limit reduction** — formal notification to customer, reduce limit to current outstanding + 20% buffer; (c) **Payment term tightening** — change from Net 60 to Net 30, or Net 30 to COD; (d) **Cash-on-delivery conversion** — remove credit terms entirely, all future transactions COD; (e) **No action** — false alarm, seasonal pattern, or customer has provided satisfactory explanation with committed payment plan | Credit Analyst | Credit Manager | 15 min/recommendation |
| 7 | Credit Manager approves recommended action: (a) credit hold and credit limit changes require Credit Manager approval per W24 authorization matrix; (b) payment term changes require Credit Manager approval with Finance Manager notification; (c) COD conversion requires Credit Manager approval with Sales Manager acknowledgment; (d) Credit Manager reviews recommendation, supporting analysis, and Sales Manager input before decision | Credit Manager | Finance Manager (for term changes) | 10–15 min/account |
| 8 | System implements approved credit action: (a) update customer master per W253 — credit status, credit limit, payment terms; (b) if credit hold applied: system blocks new sales orders for customer, notifies Sales Manager and assigned Sales Representative, generates hold notification for customer service; (c) generate formal customer notification letter (credit limit change, term change, or COD notification) — reviewed by AR Supervisor for tone and accuracy before sending; (d) all changes logged in credit action audit trail | System / AR Supervisor | Credit Manager | 10 min/account |
| 9 | Customer outreach: (a) Sales Manager contacts customer for relationship management — discusses business situation, conveys credit action as temporary measure, explores payment plan or partial payment arrangement; (b) AR Supervisor contacts customer for payment discussion — presents overdue balance details, negotiates payment commitment date, documents agreement in collection notes per W108; (c) if customer provides payment commitment: Credit Analyst monitors follow-through, escalates missed commitment to Credit Manager within 2 business days | Sales Manager / AR Supervisor | Credit Manager | 30–60 min/account |
| 10 | Monthly credit portfolio review meeting: Credit Manager, Finance Manager, and Sales Manager review — (a) top 20 exposures by outstanding balance with risk rating; (b) portfolio aging distribution (current, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+ days) vs. prior month; (c) provision adequacy per W81 — compare actual loss experience to provision rates; (d) alert effectiveness metrics — alerts generated vs. actions taken vs. false positives; (e) policy compliance — accounts exceeding credit limit without approval, credit holds overridden; (f) bad debt forecast for next quarter | Credit Manager / Finance Manager / Sales Manager | CFO | 2–3 hours/month |
| 11 | Quarterly credit policy review: Credit Manager assesses — (a) alert threshold effectiveness: review Amber-to-Red transition rate, false positive rate, missed deterioration (accounts that defaulted without triggering alert); (b) recommend threshold adjustments to optimize detection vs. false positive balance; (c) update scoring model weights based on latest 12 months of default data; (d) review industry/market risk factors — construction sector outlook, government infrastructure spending, interest rate environment | Credit Manager | Finance Manager | 4 hours/quarter |
| 12 | Annual credit scoring model calibration: Credit Analyst recalibrates scoring model using actual default data — (a) analyze defaulted accounts (written off per W81) vs. performing accounts to identify predictive factors; (b) update weighting of risk factors (utilization, aging, bounced checks, external scores); (c) back-test revised model against historical data; (d) present calibration results to Finance Manager for approval; (e) deploy updated model parameters to production | Credit Analyst / Credit Manager | Finance Manager | 1 week/year |

### System Touchpoints

- Credit monitoring and automated alert module with configurable thresholds (FIN-044)
- AR for B2B — account data source for credit health indicators (FIN-005, W8)
- Bad debt provisioning — monitoring feed for provision adequacy review (FIN-022, W81)
- Credit application — initial assessment baseline (W24)
- AR processing — transaction data source (W8)
- Credit collection — escalation partner for overdue accounts (W108)
- Credit limit exception — exception handling partner (W229)
- Credit limit periodic review — annual review partner (W328)
- Bounced check recovery — NSF signal source (W1381)
- Customer master data — credit action update target (W253)

### Pain Points / Risks

- Philippine construction industry is highly fragmented with many small contractors who have limited financial transparency — credit assessments rely heavily on trade references and payment history rather than formal financial statements; mitigated by conservative initial credit limits per W24 and proactive monitoring
- External credit bureau data (TransUnion, CIBI) may be incomplete for provincial B2B customers — many small contractors and developers outside Metro Manila do not have robust credit bureau profiles; mitigated by relying primarily on internal payment behavior data
- Construction project payment delays cascade from developer to contractor to supplier — customer's late payment may reflect upstream project delay rather than customer financial distress; mitigated by Sales Manager relationship context (step 9) before taking credit action

## W1300. Trade Account Credit Limit Annual Review, Adjustment & Exposure Monitoring

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual credit review cycle (each account reviewed on the anniversary of credit approval); or triggered by payment pattern deterioration (30+ days past due); or customer request for credit limit increase |
| **Frequency** | ~5,200 annual reviews (one per active trade account per year); ~500–800 ad-hoc reviews triggered by payment deterioration |
| **Volume** | ~5,200 active trade AR accounts representing 30% of revenue (~PHP 18.7B/year) |
| **Owner** | Credit & Collections Manager |
| **Participants** | Credit & Collections Manager, Credit Analyst, Sales Manager (Trade), CFO, Customer |

### Background

BuildRight Depot extends trade credit to ~5,200 accounts (contractors, builders, interior designers, architects) with combined outstanding AR of ~PHP 1.5–2.0B at any time. Credit terms range from Net 30 (standard trade) to Net 60–90 (corporate projects). Each account has a credit limit set during initial credit scoring per W100. However, trade customer financial health changes over time: (a) a contractor may take on too many projects simultaneously, overleveraging and missing payments to suppliers including BuildRight; (b) a previously stable corporate account may face cash flow problems from delayed government project payments (common in the Philippines — government infrastructure project retainers are released on 60–90 day cycles); (c) construction industry downturns (post-pandemic, typhoon damage to contractor cash flow) affect entire segments. Without systematic annual review, BuildRight may continue extending credit to deteriorating accounts, increasing bad debt exposure. This workflow manages the systematic review, adjustment, and monitoring of trade account credit limits.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Annual review scheduling & data compilation**: (a) system generates monthly batch of accounts due for annual review (by credit approval anniversary month); (b) Credit Analyst compiles review package per account: (i) payment history over past 12 months (on-time %, average days past due, highest aging bucket reached), (ii) purchase volume trend (increasing, stable, declining), (iii) current outstanding balance vs. credit limit utilization, (iv) number of credit limit increase requests in past 12 months, (v) any returned checks or payment disputes, (vi) external credit bureau data (if available — Philippine credit bureaus like CIC, TransUnion Philippines) | Credit Analyst / System | Credit & Collections Manager | 15–20 min per account |
| 2 | **Credit scoring recalculation**: System recalculates credit score using updated data: (a) payment score (40% weight): on-time payment rate, average days past due, returned check count; (b) volume score (20% weight): purchase volume trend and consistency; (c) utilization score (20% weight): credit limit utilization rate — chronically maxed-out accounts score lower; (d) tenure score (10% weight): years as BuildRight trade customer; (e) external score (10% weight): credit bureau score if available; (f) resulting score maps to credit tier: (i) Platinum (score ≥ 85): credit limit up to PHP 5M, terms Net 60, (ii) Gold (score 70–84): credit limit up to PHP 2M, terms Net 30, (iii) Silver (score 55–69): credit limit up to PHP 500K, terms Net 30, (iv) Bronze (score 40–54): credit limit up to PHP 200K, terms Net 30 with 10% holdback, (v) Probation (score < 40): credit limit reduced to PHP 50K or suspended, COD terms | System / Credit Analyst | Credit & Collections Manager | Automated scoring + 5 min review |
| 3 | **Credit limit decision**: Credit Analyst reviews score and recommends: (a) maintain current limit and terms, (b) increase limit and/or improve terms (for improving accounts), (c) decrease limit and/or tighten terms (for deteriorating accounts), (d) suspend credit and convert to COD (for severely deteriorating accounts); for limit increases > PHP 1M: Credit & Collections Manager approval required; for limit increases > PHP 3M: CFO approval required; for credit suspension: Credit & Collections Manager approval with Sales Manager notification | Credit Analyst | Credit & Collections Manager (or CFO for large increases) | 5–10 min per account |
| 4 | **Customer communication**: (a) for credit limit increases: system sends notification to customer with new limit and terms — positive engagement opportunity; (b) for credit limit decreases: Credit Analyst calls customer to explain change, discusses remediation path (e.g., "bring all payments current within 60 days and your score will improve"); (c) for credit suspension: Sales Manager and Credit Analyst meet with customer to discuss: is the account salvageable? can customer provide additional guarantees (personal guarantee of company owner, post-dated checks)?; (d) all decisions documented in ERP credit management module | Credit Analyst / Sales Manager | Credit & Collections Manager | 10–20 min per account (for decreases/suspensions) |
| 5 | **Portfolio-level exposure monitoring**: Monthly, Credit & Collections Manager reviews portfolio-level exposure: (a) total AR outstanding vs. portfolio credit limit (total approved limits), (b) aging distribution: current, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+ days, (c) concentration risk: top 20 accounts as % of total AR, (d) industry segment exposure (construction, government projects, interior design, etc.), (e) bad debt provision adequacy: actual write-off rate vs. provision rate, (f) report to CFO with recommendations for portfolio-level action (tighten new credit approvals, increase bad debt provision, engage collection agency for 90+ accounts) | Credit & Collections Manager | CFO | 4–6 hours/month |
