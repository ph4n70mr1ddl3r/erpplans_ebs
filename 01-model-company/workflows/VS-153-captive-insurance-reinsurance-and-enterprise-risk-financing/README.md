## Overview

Captive Insurance, Reinsurance & Enterprise Risk Financing workflows for BuildRight Depot Corp. —
owning the enterprise **risk-financing and self-insurance discipline** by which BuildRight
structures a captive insurance vehicle to retain, finance, and reinsure its own insurable risk at
PHP 62.3B-revenue, 5-entity, 205-site scale, reducing total cost of risk and stabilizing coverage
that the commercial market under-serves in the Philippines.

Today this discipline is **unowned as a program**. "Captive insurance", "reinsurance", and
"self-insur" appear in only 1–4 PA files as incidental references (a Treasury funding option in
VS-18, a risk note in VS-21.2), and in **zero** PA files as dedicated workflow headers / **zero**
value-stream directory names. No value stream owns the end-to-end **captive risk-financing
discipline**: feasibility & business case (total cost of risk vs commercial premium), captive
formation & domicile (onshore/offshore, capitalization, licensing), underwriting scope (which
risks — property/catastrophe, liability, fleet, cyber, DSAR/privacy — the captive writes), risk
transfer & the reinsurance treaty/program, fronting/carrier arrangements, claims administration
through the captive, captive finance/accounting (statutory, PFRS), regulatory compliance
(domiciliary regulator, IC/NAIC-equivalent, RBC), and portfolio analytics (loss-ratio, combined
ratio, capital adequacy).

This is distinct from **VS-26 (Business Continuity & Insurance)** which *buys and claims on
commercial insurance policies* — this value stream *operates the captive that underwrites and
reinsures* a portion of that risk. It is distinct from **VS-21.2 (Risk/ERM)** which *identifies
and measures* risk — this value stream *finances* it. It is distinct from **VS-18 (Treasury)** which
*manages cash/facilities* — this value stream manages the *captive's capital and investment* assets.
It is distinct from **VS-130 (M&A)** — captive formation is a *corporate-transaction-like* act
(formation/acquisition of a regulated insurance entity) handled here, while VS-130 handles
commercial M&A.

BuildRight's exposure is structural: at PHP 62.3B revenue, 205 sites in a typhoon/earthquake-prone
archipelago, 600 POS, ~42.9K monthly ecommerce orders, ~6,911 employees, and ~5,200 trade accounts,
the total cost of risk (property catastrophe, liability, fleet, crime, cyber, D&O, employee) is a
material opex line; the PH commercial market has limited capacity and high pricing for catastrophe
and specialty lines (typhoon/flood property, cyber), making a captive a standard risk-financing
tool for large PH groups. The gap analysis (§6) explicitly flagged "captive insurance
underwriting" as a future capability to re-evaluate — this value stream activates that capability.
Total cost of risk benchmarks ~0.5–1.5% of revenue; a captive that retains the predictable layer
and reinsures the catastrophic layer can reduce net cost of risk 10–25%.
