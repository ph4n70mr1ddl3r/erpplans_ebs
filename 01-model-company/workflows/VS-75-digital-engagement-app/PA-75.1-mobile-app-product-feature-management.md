## W5553. Mobile App Store Removal, Policy-Violation Response & Re-Listing Recovery Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Apple App Store or Google Play rejects a pending release, removes/deactivates the live BuildRight app, or takes down listing metadata — App Review rejection, live-app removal for policy violation, or a developer-account action |
| **Frequency** | Release rejections: occasional, a few per year; live-app removal or developer-account action: rare, fewer than one event per several years |
| **Volume** | 1 event × the digital-engagement app population per the VS-75 canon × 2 storefronts (iOS App Store + Google Play per W2647); a live removal halts all new downloads and all update delivery to installed bases on the affected storefront |
| **Owner** | Digital Product Manager |
| **Participants** | IT (mobile engineering), Marketing (customer comms), CRM-Loyalty Manager (W2649 loyalty continuity), Customer Service Representative, CIO, CMO, VP Legal / DPO (privacy-policy findings per W2652) |

### Background

W2647 owns listing upkeep and review response, W2650 performance monitoring, and W659 the ecommerce platform's own incidents — but the store-removal event has no owner ('app store removal'/'policy violation'/'app takedown' appear in zero PA files). Storefront enforcement arrives without negotiation on Apple/Google timelines: downloads halt, updates stop reaching installed apps, and the app decays toward unusable while the appeal runs. The findings arrive in distinct classes with different fix paths — privacy/data-declaration findings (the same SDK/data-flow defects the stores flag often mirror RA 10173 exposure), payment-rule violations, content/metadata findings, and binary defects — so the response must classify before fixing, then appeal with the remediation in hand. This is the W5547/W5548 platform-contingency class applied to the store-distribution layer: the storefront is a supplier whose enforcement can switch the channel off.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Detection & classification**: App Store Connect / Play Console notice or release-pipeline rejection; classify: (a) App Review rejection of a pending release, (b) live-app removal/deactivation, (c) metadata/listing takedown, (d) developer-account action; capture the cited guideline clause and the appeal path/deadline | Digital Product Manager / IT | CIO | < 4 h |
| 2 | **Impact assessment & customer comms**: quantify the affected surface (downloads halted? updates halted? push still functional on installed bases?); publish interim-channel guidance — mobile-web store, hotline, social channels (W948 class) — via push (W2648) while the binary still runs, and through the loyalty desk (W2649 continuity) | Digital Product Manager / Marketing / Customer Service Representative | CMO | < 24 h |
| 3 | **Root-cause fix by class**: (a) privacy/data-declaration finding → fix the privacy manifests and SDK data flows per the W2652 discipline, with the DPO verifying no local RA 10173 issue mirrors it; (b) payment-rule finding → remove or adjust the violating flow; (c) content/metadata finding → listing correction per W2647; (d) binary defect → engineering fix through the paved-road release rings (W5517 class) | IT / Digital Product Manager | CIO | 2–10 days |
| 4 | **Appeal & expedited review**: file the platform appeal with the remediation evidence (diff, policy analysis, corrected declarations); request expedited review where customer impact is live; track daily | Digital Product Manager / IT | CIO | Appeal window |
| 5 | **Re-listing & verification**: app/release restored; verify install, update and deep-link paths, push delivery, and loyalty sign-in across both storefronts; re-verify listing metadata per W2647; resume the release cadence | IT / Digital Product Manager | CIO | 1–3 days |
| 6 | **Prevention register**: add the violated clause to the release checklist (a store-policy gate in the W2647 listing discipline and the W5521-class release calendar); assign the quarterly platform-policy watch; log the event in the channel-risk register feeding the platform-dependency review | Digital Product Manager | CIO | 4 h |

### System Touchpoints
- App Store Connect / Google Play Console notices & release pipeline (links to W2647/W2650 monitoring)
- Privacy-manifest / data-collection declaration tooling (links to W2652)
- Push-notification fallback channel on installed bases (links to W2648)
- Loyalty app-integration continuity (links to W2649)
- Release-calendar policy-gate entry (links to W5521-class change governance)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Channel-switch-off risk**: a live removal silently halts update delivery with no crash to alert anyone — installed apps decay until broken; mitigated by the Step-1 detection SLA and Step-2 impact assessment
- **Wrong-fix risk**: appealing without the remediation, or fixing the binary without the policy analysis, burns the one appeal; mitigated by the Step-3 class-specific fix path sequenced before Step 4
- **Privacy-mirror risk**: the store's privacy finding often flags an SDK/data-flow defect that is also a local RA 10173 issue; mitigated by the DPO verification inside Step 3(a)
- **Comms-vacuum risk**: customers conclude the app is discontinued and migrate; mitigated by Step-2 interim-channel guidance published within 24 hours

### Automation Opportunity
- Release-pipeline gate diffing data-collection declarations and store-policy checklist items before every submission (prevents the Step-3(a)/(c) classes mechanically)
- Storefront-visibility monitor alerting on listing loss across both platforms (replaces manual Step-1 discovery)

### Controls
- CTL-454 (ensure controlled execution — Mobile App Product & Feature Management (PA-75.1).)
- CTL-203 (protect mobile-app customer data & release quality.)
- operational: no appeal is filed without the remediation evidence attached; the violated clause enters the release checklist before cadence resumes

### Time Estimate
Per event: live-removal class ≈ 40–70 staff-hours over the event life (Steps 1–2 ≈ 8–15 in the first 24 hours; Steps 3–6 ≈ 30–55 across weeks); release-rejection class ≈ 4–10 staff-hours each × a few per year. Steady state: the policy watch rides the W2647 review cycle — no incremental headcount.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed by the Digital Product Manager and mobile-engineering bench with Marketing comms support. No incremental headcount.
