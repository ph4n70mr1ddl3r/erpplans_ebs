## 1. Scope

All workflows governing the **people/organization function** of the model company (200 stores,
4 DCs, HQ; ~6,925 employees (active); ~1,200–1,600 new hires/year; ~230 succession-critical roles), across:

- The **People family** — 16 dedicated value streams, **445 workflows**:
  VS-19 Hire-to-Retire (81), VS-83 occupational health (25), VS-84 labor relations (25),
  VS-98 contingent workforce (24), VS-102 total rewards (24), VS-103 HR shared services (24),
  VS-121 talent acquisition (25), VS-123 apprenticeship (24), VS-134 OCM (24),
  VS-141 employee transport (25), VS-144 staff housing (24), VS-150 drug-free workplace (24),
  VS-160 global mobility (24), VS-167 workforce background screening (24),
  VS-169 uniform & PPE issuance (24), VS-183 DTS & TESDA partnership (24).
- **Adjacent streams with people-capability scope**: VS-30.3 document/knowledge (W1484 onboarding
  knowledge path), VS-36 corporate governance (W1721 board committees, W1732 DoA),
  VS-17.4 record-to-report (W9 close, W637 controls testing, W639 rolling forecast),
  VS-21 internal audit (W337 payroll audit, W351 external-audit coordination, W365 workforce audit),
  VS-130 M&A (HR due diligence surface), VS-192 green-fleet/EV (people-impact surface).

## 2. Method

1. **Inventory** every `## W` header in the People family (445 workflows) plus
   people-scoped workflows hosted elsewhere (W1484, W3340 town halls, W1721 committee mechanics).
2. **Map** each capability of a reference full-lifecycle people operating model — learning &
   development platform/content/program layers, talent/succession pipeline development,
   organization design & job architecture, employee-relations case handling, HR technology estate,
   plus the adjacent finance-policy layer the people systems must reconcile against — to the
   owning workflow(s).
3. **Flag** capabilities with no owning workflow, thin-partial coverage, or scattered ownership.
4. **Validate** each candidate gap by keyword search across all PA files; discard false positives
   and adjacents that already own the slice (e.g., 'organization design' candidates collapsed
   into W3305/W3306 job architecture; 'HRIS administration' owned by W3351; 'ATS/CRM stack' owned
   by W3782; 'talent acquisition' wholly owned by VS-121's 24 workflows).

## 4. Gaps Identified

### G1 — Learning-platform (LMS) administration, integration & learning-records layer — **HIGH**

The single most-referenced people system with **no owning workflow anywhere in the inventory**:
'LMS' appears in **58 PA files (101 hits)** and 'e-learning' in 10 more, always as a consumed
platform — W51 delivers through it, W1484 auto-enrolls through it, W683/W1127 track
certifications in it, W64.1 links to it for compliance tracking — but no `## W` header owns the
LMS estate itself. Even a **LMS Administrator** role exists ("system maintenance ~10 hours/month;
absorbed by IT", W1484 staffing note) with no owning workflow.

- **Evidence (dedicated-header hits / defining terms):** 'LMS' **58 PA files / 0 dedicated
  headers**; 'learning management' **11 files / 0 headers**; 'learning platform' **0**;
  'learning records' **0**. The W3351 HRIS workflow and W3782 TA-stack workflow own the
  *adjacent* HR platforms — the LMS is the unowned middle.
- **Why it matters:** the LMS is the *system of record* for statutory training evidence (OSH
  courses under RA 11058 + OSH Standards, forklift certification, food/hazmat handling);
  completion feeds power W72, W3347 and W3349; and onboarding for ~1,200–1,600 hires/year runs
  on it. An unowned integration or retention failure is a compliance and onboarding risk, not an
  inconvenience.
- **Proposed disposition:** 1 workflow in **PA-19.4 (Learning & Development)** — *"Learning
  Platform (LMS) Administration, Integration & Learning-Records Operations"* (platform ops,
  HRIS/BI integrations, SSO, records integrity & custody, release management, vendor/SLA
  governance).

### G2 — Learning-content production, course-catalog & certification-program lifecycle — **MEDIUM-HIGH**

The *content supply chain* beneath every training program: W51 step 2 develops "training
materials" as one sub-duty of the calendar owner, and W1484 owns the onboarding curriculum
slice — but nobody owns instructional design as a capability, the course catalog lifecycle
(create/version/supersede/retire across ~180 courses), the version-lock between SOP revisions
(W186/W1459) and the courses that teach them, localization for provincial stores, or external
content licensing.

- **Evidence:** 'instructional design' **0**; 'course catalog' **0**; 'learning content' **0
  dedicated**; 'content development' hits are marketing/product-content, not training.
- **Why it matters:** staleness is the failure mode — an outdated forklift or hazmat course
  teaches the wrong practice; a superseded POS course degrades W25/W34 execution. The corpus
  already routes SOP governance through W186/W1459; the training-content mirror of that
  governance is missing.
- **Proposed disposition:** 1 workflow in **PA-19.4** — *"Learning-Content Development,
  Course-Catalog & Certification-Program Lifecycle"* (design briefs, SME production, catalog
  & version governance with SOP version-lock, localization/accessibility, assessment standards,
  vendor licenses).

### G3 — Leadership development & management-capability program (HiPo development) — **MEDIUM**

The identification and deployment layers are owned — W178 identifies HiPos in the annual
succession review, W179/W3778 run the cadetship pipeline, W645/W3345 spot pipeline gaps — but
nobody owns the *development* layer between identification and readiness: no leadership
competency model, no cohort program, no first-time-manager transition track, no development-plan
or program-effectiveness ownership.

- **Evidence:** 'leadership development' appears **only** as one line-item in W51's training
  calendar ("(e) leadership development for supervisors and managers" — a semi-annual workshop
  row); 'leadership pipeline' **0**; 'talent review' **4 hits, all incidental** (W682's annual
  analytics step and W567's cross-training feed into W178); W4083's "equip-the-manager" is
  change-program-specific (VS-134), not the standing leadership curriculum.
- **Why it matters:** W178's Volume names ~230 succession-critical roles; internal-promotion
  readiness for 200 Store Manager seats is the company's largest single leadership dependency,
  and first-time-manager failure is a documented DOLE-claim and attrition driver.
- **Proposed disposition:** 1 workflow in **PA-19.4** — *"Leadership Development &
  Management-Capability Program (HiPo Development)"* (competency model, nomination/enrollment
  from W178/W3345 signals, cohort delivery, first-time-manager transition, 360/IDP tracking,
  annual effectiveness review).

### G4 — Accounting policy, technical accounting (PFRS) & new-standard adoption layer — **MEDIUM-HIGH**

'PFRS' appears in **93 PA files (475 hits)**, but every dedicated owner is
*transaction-specific*: VS-157 owns PFRS 15 revenue, VS-148 owns PFRS 16 leases, W407 owns PAS 12
deferred tax, W1875 owns the lease-accounting policy slice. The *policy layer above the
transactions* — the accounting policy manual across the 5 legal entities, technical position
papers for novel transactions, new-standard adoption roadmaps, close/disclosure integration, and
external-audit policy concurrence — has no owner.

- **Evidence:** 'accounting manual' **1** (incidental); 'technical accounting' **0**; 'position
  paper' **4 hits / 0 headers**; 'new standard' **2 hits / 0 headers**; W9 owns close
  *execution*, W637 owns controls *testing*, W351 owns audit *coordination* — none owns policy
  *content* and adoption.
- **Why it matters:** this is the batch-8 ISMS pattern (the management-system capstone above
  well-run operations). With 5 entities, ~1,000+ GL account mappings, a PHP 62.3B P&L and
  SEC/BIR filing obligations (W481), an unowned policy layer surfaces as audit findings,
  late adoptions and entity-level drift — exactly the "maintaining consistent chart of accounts
  … requires constant governance that is often deprioritized" pain PA-17.4 already states for
  the CoA sibling (W288).
- **Proposed disposition:** 1 workflow in **PA-17.4 (FP&A & Reporting)** — *"Accounting Policy,
  Technical Accounting (PFRS) Position & New-Standard Adoption Governance"* (standards radar &
  impact assessments, policy manual maintenance, position papers, close/disclosure integration,
  audit concurrence, training).

**Overall verdict:** the People inventory is deep and well-partitioned — talent acquisition,
compensation/job architecture, payroll/statutory, workforce management, employee relations,
HR shared services and people analytics are all owned at workflow granularity with explicit
boundaries (VS-121 vs VS-19.1 vs VS-103; W3351 HRIS vs W3782 ATS). The residual exposure
concentrated in the **learning platform/content supply chain (G1, G2)** and the **capability
development + reporting-policy layers above operations (G3, G4)** — the platform that everyone
consumes and the layers that sit *above* execution (develop, certify, adopt) rather than
*within* it. Filling the four gaps added **4 workflows (~1% growth to the People family)** and
closed every unowned capability surfaced by this analysis. VS-19 now stands at **81 workflows**
(PA-19.4: 14); VS-17 at **70** (PA-17.4: 19).
