## W45. Store Closure / Relocation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Persistent underperformance, lease expiry non-renewal, market exit, or relocation to better site |
| **Frequency** | Occasional (estimated 2–5 closures/relocations per year as network matures to 300+ stores) |
| **Volume** | 1 store at a time; each takes 2–3 months from decision to final close-out |
| **Owner** | VP Store Operations |
| **Participants** | COO, VP Store Operations, Real Estate (Property Mgmt Inc.), IT, Finance, HR, Supply Chain, Marketing |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Store performance review triggers closure decision: sustained negative EBITDA, declining foot traffic, lease renewal at unfavorable terms, or strategic market exit | VP Store Operations / CFO | CEO | Decision meeting |
| 2 | CEO and Board approve closure/relocation; VP Store Operations appointed as closure lead | CEO / Board | CEO | Board meeting |
| 3 | Property Mgr Inc. negotiates lease termination with landlord; settles any penalties or early termination fees | Property Mgr Inc. | CFO | 2–4 weeks |
| 4 | Supply Planner creates mass inventory redistribution plan: system generates transfer orders from closing store to nearest stores and serving DC; prioritizes by item velocity and destination need | Supply Planner | Supply Planning Manager | 1 week |
| 5 | DC and receiving stores process inbound transfers per standard receiving (W22, W3); closing store picks and ships inventory in waves over 1–2 weeks | Closing Store Staff | DC Supervisor / Store Manager | 1–2 weeks |
| 6 | HR initiates employee action: (a) redeploy high-performers to nearby stores (transfer, not separation), (b) offer voluntary separation to others, (c) process separations per W43 for staff not redeployed; peak: up to 29 separations per store (the full §12.1 store headcount) | HR Assistant / HR Head | CHRO | 2–4 weeks |
| 7 | Marketing executes customer communication plan: notify loyalty members of closure, redirect to nearest store, offer relocation promotion (e.g., extra loyalty points at new nearest store), update store locator on website/app | Marketing | CMO | 2 weeks |
| 8 | Customer Service handles inbound inquiries from trade/corporate accounts; reassigns accounts to nearest store's Sales Rep or Regional Manager | CS Manager / Sales Rep | VP Store Operations | 1 week |
| 9 | AR Clerk reviews and collects any outstanding trade/corporate account balances specific to closing store location | AR Clerk | AR Supervisor | 2 weeks |
| 10 | IT decommissions store infrastructure: POS terminals, network equipment, RF devices (reverse of W16.3); deactivates store location in ERP system; removes from price file and replenishment routing | IT Team | CIO | 1 week |
| 11 | Cost Accountant disposes store fixtures and fixed assets per W39 (disposal/retirement); capitalizes any leasehold improvement write-offs | Cost Accountant | Controller | Per W39 |
| 12 | System deactivates store location master: location status set to "Closed"; no further transactions posted; location excluded from reports and dashboards | IT / Controller | CFO | Automated + 15 min |
| 13 | Controller runs final store P&L and balance sheet reconciliation: all inventory cleared, all AP/AR settled, all assets disposed; final profit/loss on closure recognized | Controller | CFO | 1 day |
| 13a | **Financial close-out checklist** — Controller and Store Manager verify the following location-specific commitments are resolved before final close: (a) petty cash float (PHP 20K) returned to Treasury via bank transfer or armored car; custodian reconciliation completed per W25; (b) gift cards / store credit vouchers issued by closing store — system reissuance to customer's nearest store location with customer notification; unredeemed store credit balance recognized as revenue upon closure; (c) pending special orders (W38) — customer deposits either fulfilled from alternative store/DC or refunded per W38.16 deposit refund process; (d) pending warranty claims (W33) — items at vendor for repair reassigned to customer's nearest store for pickup upon return; (e) pending backorders (W56) — allocation released or redirected to nearest store with customer notification; (f) store-specific vendor contracts (e.g., local maintenance, cleaning) — terminated per W62 with final payment; (g) store-specific service contracts — final invoice verified and paid; (h) **loyalty profile default-store reassignment**: system identifies all loyalty members whose registered default store is the closing store; system automatically reassigns each member's default store to the nearest active BuildRight store based on geographic proximity; affected members notified via SMS/email with new default store information; loyalty points, tier status, and transaction history unaffected by reassignment | Controller / Store Manager / AR Clerk / AP Clerk | CFO | 1 day |
| 13b | **LGU permit retirement**: Regulatory Officer notifies LGU Business Permit and Licensing Office (BPLO) of store closure; files closure notification with final local business tax payment (covering period up to closure date); obtains LGU closure acknowledgment; system updates location master with closure date and LGU retirement confirmation per W54; failure to retire LGU permit may result in continued LBT billing — Regulatory Officer confirms retirement within 30 days of closure | Regulatory Officer | VP Legal | 2–4 hours/location |
| 14 | Post-closure: system retains closed store data for 10-year retention period (BIR); historical data accessible for reporting but location excluded from active operations | System | — | Automated |

**Total closure cycle**: 2–3 months from decision to final close-out

### System Touchpoints
- Mass transfer order generation from closing store to multiple destinations (W45.4)
- Store location deactivation: status change, transaction blocking, removal from active reporting (W45.12)
- Employee redeployment (transfer between locations in same entity) vs. separation processing per W43 (W45.6)
- Fixed asset disposal trigger for store-specific assets per W39 (W45.11)
- Customer account reassignment to new default store (W45.7)
- Trade/AR account collection and reassignment (W45.8–9)
- Final store financial close-out with closure P&L recognition (W45.13)
- Closed location data retention with restricted access (W45.14)
- Financial close-out checklist: petty cash return, gift card/store credit reissuance, special order deposit refund or fulfillment, warranty claim reassignment, backorder release, vendor contract termination per W62, final service invoice verification (W45.13a)
- LGU permit retirement: closure notification to LGU BPLO, final LBT payment, LGU closure acknowledgment, location master update (W45.13b)
- Integration with W25 (petty cash return), W28 (gift card reissuance), W33 (warranty reassignment), W38 (special order deposit refund), W54 (LGU permit retirement), W56 (backorder release), W62 (vendor contract termination)

### Pain Points / Risks
- Employee morale and attrition cascade — announcing closure triggers immediate productivity drop and voluntary resignations from high performers before redeployment can be arranged, leaving the store understaffed during wind-down
- Inventory write-down risk — aggressive redistribution timelines may force markdowns or discounts to move slow-moving inventory quickly, eroding recovery value
- LGU permit retirement delays — failure to retire the business permit within 30 days results in continued local business tax billing; Philippine LGU bureaucracy can delay closure acknowledgment
- Customer loyalty attrition — even with proactive reassignment to nearest store, ~20–30% of closing store's loyalty members become inactive within 6 months of closure

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "review triggers closure decision" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System rule-based auto-approval of "CEO and Board approve closure/relocation" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-generate of "generates transfer orders from closing store to nearest stores and serving DC" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-262 (ensure controlled execution — Store Staffing & People (PA-07.4).)
- operational: VP Store Operations review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
2–3 months total cycle per closure. Key effort concentrations: VP Store Operations ~20 hours over 2–3 months, Supply Planner ~1 week for redistribution plan, HR ~2–4 weeks for redeployment/separation of up to 30 staff, IT ~1 week for decommission, Finance ~1 day for final P&L. Financial close-out checklist (W45.13a) requires 1 full day of combined Controller/Store Manager effort.

### Staffing Implication
- **VP Store Operations**: Leads closure process. ~20 hours per closure spread over 2–3 months.
- **Supply Planner**: Mass redistribution planning adds ~1 week per closure. Absorbed within existing team.
- **HR**: Up to 30 separations + redeployments per closure. Absorbed within existing HR team with advance planning.
- **IT**: 1 week per store decommission. 2–3 IT staff per closure. With 2–5 closures/year, this is ~5–15 staff-weeks. Absorbed within existing IT team.
- **Finance**: Final P&L close-out adds ~1 day per closure. Absorbed.

## W205. Employee Purchase Program & Internal Staff Sales

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Employee wishes to purchase merchandise for personal use |
| **Frequency** | Daily; ~1,000 transactions/month across group |
| **Volume** | Covers all 6,911+ employees |
| **Owner** | Store Manager / HR Manager |
| **Participants** | Employee (Buyer), Cashier, HR (for eligibility) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Eligibility Check**: Employee presents ID at POS; System verifies active employment status and "Monthly Purchase Limit" (e.g., PHP 20,000/month) | Cashier / System | HR Manager | 1 min |
| 2 | **Discount Application**: System applies "Staff Discount" (e.g., Cost + 5% or 15% off SRP) based on product category rules | System | — | Real-time |
| 3 | **Payment**: Employee pays via Cash, Card, or "Payroll Deduction" (W10/W76) | Employee | Cashier | 2 min |
| 4 | **Verification**: Manager signs receipt; confirms items are for personal use (not resale) | Store Manager | — | 1 min |
| 5 | **Audit**: LP Team reviews high-frequency staff purchases for potential abuse (W37) | LP Analyst | VP Legal | Monthly |

### System Touchpoints
- POS Employee Purchase tender type with employee ID verification and active employment status check via HR integration (W205.1)
- Automated staff discount application with configurable category-level rules (cost + 5% or 15% off SRP) (W205.2)
- Monthly purchase limit enforcement per employee (e.g., PHP 20,000/month) with running total tracking (W205.1)
- Payroll deduction integration for employee purchases via salary deduction (W10/W76) (W205.3)
- LP audit dashboard for high-frequency and high-value employee purchase pattern detection (W37) (W205.5)
- Integration with W5B (POS selling — employee purchase workflow), W10 (payroll — salary deduction), W37 (loss prevention — abuse detection), VS-19 HRIS (employee records master)

### Pain Points / Risks
- Resale abuse — employees purchasing items at staff discount for resale to third parties (friends, sari-sari stores, online marketplace) at profit, violating the "personal use only" policy and eroding margin on discounted items
- Manager sign-off bypass — Store Managers rubber-stamping employee purchase receipts without verifying items are for personal use, especially during busy periods when the 1-min sign-off is rushed
- Payroll deduction disputes — employees disputing salary deductions for purchases they claim were unauthorized or for different amounts than expected; resolution requires cross-referencing POS transaction logs with payroll records
- Policy inconsistency across stores — different Store Managers applying different interpretations of what constitutes "personal use" (e.g., purchasing materials for a family member's home renovation), creating fairness complaints and potential HR grievances


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "verifies active employment status and “Monthly Purchase Limit”" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-apply of "applies “Staff Discount”" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews high-frequency staff purchases for potential abuse" (replaces manual Step 5).

### Controls
- CTL-262 (ensure controlled execution — Store Staffing & People (PA-07.4).)
- operational: Store Manager / HR Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
~5 min per employee purchase transaction (1 min eligibility check + 2 min payment + 1 min manager sign-off + 1 min receipt). At ~1,000 transactions/month across 6,911+ employees, total POS time is ~80 hours/month. LP monthly audit: 2–4 hours. HR eligibility verification is automated.

### Staffing Implication
- ~1,000 transactions/month chain-wide (per the Frequency) ÷ 200 stores ≈ 0.2/store/day × ~5 min ≈ ~1 min/store/day of POS time (~80 hours/month chain-wide). Absorbed by existing Cashiers.
- Store Manager sign-off: ~1 min per transaction. LP monthly audit: 2–4 hours chain-wide. No incremental headcount.
- HR handles payroll deduction disputes as part of standard HR operations — estimated 5–10 disputes/month chain-wide.

## W601. Store-Level Daily HR Operations & People Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Start of store business day; ongoing throughout operating hours |
| **Frequency** | Daily per store |
| **Volume** | ~29 employees per store; 200 stores |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Department Supervisors, employees, HR Coordinator (HQ) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Store Manager reviews daily attendance report from biometric/RFID system; identifies absences, late arrivals, and missing punches | Store Manager | — | 10 min |

| 5 | Shift swap requests: Employee A initiates swap with Employee B (same skill set); both confirm; Department Supervisor verifies coverage; Store Manager approves; system updates schedule and attendance rules | Employee | Store Manager | 5 min |

| 7 | System generates daily HR summary: attendance rate, overtime hours, leave usage, open HR action items; auto-submits to Regional Manager HR dashboard | System | — | Automated |
| 8 | Weekly (Fridays): Store Manager reviews upcoming week schedule for coverage gaps; submits schedule change requests to HR Coordinator; confirms weekend staffing adequacy | Store Manager | — | 15 min |

### System Touchpoints
- ERP HR module: attendance integration, leave management, overtime tracking, shift scheduling
- Biometric/RFID system: real-time attendance feed
- Mobile app: Store Manager HR dashboard, employee self-service for leave/swap requests
- Payroll module: daily payroll input accumulation

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Attendance system downtime**: biometric failure requires manual punch entry with supervisor confirmation per W561; mitigated by backup RFID badge scanning
- **Overtime budget overruns**: store-level overtime exceeding monthly allocation triggers Regional Manager review; mitigated by real-time overtime budget tracking in Step 4
- **Inconsistent leave approval**: Store Managers may apply leave policies inconsistently across 200 stores; mitigated by system-enforced policy rules and Regional Manager audit
- **Employee relations sensitivity**: Store Managers may lack HR training for employee relations issues; mitigated by HR Coordinator hotline and escalation protocol to HR Business Partner


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "reviews daily attendance report from biometric/RFID system" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-flag of "For unplanned absences" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-record of "record and payroll input" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-262 (ensure controlled execution — Store Staffing & People (PA-07.4).)
- operational: backup RFID badge scanning; operational: real-time overtime budget tracking in Step 4

### Staffing Implication
- **Store Manager**: ~30 min/day on HR operations (attendance, leave, overtime) + ~15 min/week on schedule review = ~3.5 hours/week. Absorbed within existing role.
- **Department Supervisors**: ~5 min/day on overtime requests and attendance follow-up = ~30 min/week per supervisor. Absorbed within existing role.
- **HR Coordinator (HQ)**: ~2 hours/week reviewing store-level HR reports and handling escalations from 200 stores. Absorbed within existing role.

### Time Estimate
~28–31 min per store-day (attendance review 10 min + absence follow-up 5 min + same-day leave approvals 3 min each + overtime authorization 5 min + shift-swap handling 5 min; employee check-ins 5 min each as needed) ≈ 170–190 hours/store/year ≈ 34,000–38,000 hours/year chain-wide, plus the Friday coverage-gap review 15 min ≈ 13 hours/store/year (daily HR summary automated).

## W602. Store-Level Labor Cost Monitoring & Overtime Budget Control

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Start of pay period (semi-monthly); weekly review trigger |
| **Frequency** | Daily monitoring; weekly review; semi-monthly reconciliation |
| **Volume** | 200 stores; ~5,800 store employees; ~PHP 180M/month store payroll |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Department Supervisors, Regional Manager, HR Coordinator, Finance Analyst |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System calculates daily labor cost flash: total regular hours × hourly rate + overtime hours × premium rate + holiday premium (if applicable) + night differential; compares to daily budget allocation | System | — | Automated |
| 2 | Store Manager reviews daily labor cost as % of daily revenue on morning dashboard; target: labor cost ≤ 12% of store revenue; amber alert at > 14%; red alert at > 16% | Store Manager | — | 5 min |
| 3 | If amber/red alert: Store Manager reviews overtime authorization log (per W601 Step 4) and identifies departments exceeding allocation; implements corrective action (reduce remaining week overtime, adjust schedules) | Store Manager | Regional Manager | 10 min |
| 4 | Weekly (Monday): Store Manager reviews prior week labor cost report with breakdown by department, overtime category (customer-driven vs. operational), and trend vs. prior 4 weeks | Store Manager | — | 15 min |
| 5 | Department Supervisors submit overtime forecast for upcoming week with business justification; Store Manager approves aggregate overtime budget allocation per department | Department Supervisor | Store Manager | 10 min |
| 6 | Semi-monthly: Store Manager reconciles actual payroll (from W10) vs. store labor budget; investigates variances > 5%; submits variance explanation to Regional Manager and Finance Analyst | Store Manager | Regional Manager | 30 min |
| 7 | Finance Analyst consolidates 200-store labor cost flash report for CFO review; identifies top/bottom 10 stores by labor cost %; flags chronic over-budget stores for remediation | Finance Analyst | CFO | 2 hours |
| 8 | Quarterly: Finance and HR review store-level labor budgets against actual; adjust allocation formulas based on store revenue, format size, and seasonal patterns | Finance Analyst | CFO | 4 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Payroll PH (in-house build — fit-gap E5): hours worked, overtime, premium calculations, budget allocation
- ERP Finance module: labor cost GL posting, budget vs. actual reporting
- BI dashboard: store-level labor cost flash, trend analysis, ranking

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Revenue volatility distorts labor cost %**: stores with revenue dips (weather, local events) show elevated labor cost % even with controlled hours; mitigated by tracking absolute hours vs. budget in addition to percentage
- **Philippine holiday complexity**: with 12 regular holidays and 9 special non-working days, holiday premium calculations are complex and error-prone; mitigated by automated holiday calendar in payroll module per HR-011
- **Delayed overtime data**: if overtime approvals are not entered same-day, labor cost flash is inaccurate; mitigated by system requirement for same-day overtime entry in W601


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-calculate of "calculates daily labor cost flash" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "reviews daily labor cost as % of daily revenue on morning dashboard" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "If amber/red alert" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-262 (ensure controlled execution — Store Staffing & People (PA-07.4).)
- operational: tracking absolute hours vs. budget in addition to percentage; operational: automated holiday calendar in payroll module per HR-011; operational: system requirement for same-day overtime entry in W601

### Staffing Implication
- **Store Manager**: ~20 min/day on labor cost review + ~15 min/week on weekly review + ~30 min semi-monthly reconciliation = ~3 hours/week. Absorbed within existing role.
- **Finance Analyst**: ~2 hours semi-monthly on consolidation + ~4 hours/quarter on budget review. Absorbed within existing FP&A role.

### Time Estimate
Daily per-store cycle 5–15 min (labor-cost flash review 5 min + overtime-log review 10 min on amber/red alerts) ≈ 6,100–18,250 hours/year chain-wide; weekly review and overtime forecast 25 min/store ≈ 4,300 hours/year; semi-monthly payroll-vs-budget reconciliation 30 min/store ≈ 2,400 hours/year; Finance consolidation of the 200-store flash 2 hours per cycle and quarterly budget review 4 hours — total ≈ 12,800–25,000 hours/year (per the itemized daily/weekly/semi-monthly components; daily flash calculation automated).

## W603. Store-Level Employee Disciplinary Process & DOLE Due Process Compliance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Employee policy violation, attendance infraction, performance issue, misconduct, or customer complaint against employee |
| **Frequency** | As needed; estimated ~80–120 incidents/month across 200 stores |
| **Volume** | ~0.4-0.6 incidents per store per month |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Department Supervisor, employee, HR Coordinator (HQ), HR Business Partner, witness(es) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Incident occurs or is reported: Department Supervisor or Store Manager documents initial observation with date, time, location, nature of incident, witnesses; system creates disciplinary case record | Dept. Supervisor / Store Manager | Store Manager | 10 min |
| 2 | Store Manager conducts preliminary fact-finding: interviews employee, witnesses, reviews CCTV footage (per W207), reviews system transaction logs if POS-related | Store Manager | — | 30 min |
| 3 | Store Manager classifies offense severity per policy matrix: Minor (tardiness, dress code, minor policy violation), Moderate (insubordination, repeated minor offenses, customer service failure), Severe (theft, fraud, violence, safety violation, gross misconduct) | Store Manager | HR Coordinator | 5 min |
| 4 | **For Minor offenses — Verbal Warning**: Store Manager conducts private counseling with employee; documents verbal warning in system with date, nature, and improvement expectation; system tracks verbal warning count (3 verbal warnings = 1 written warning) | Store Manager | — | 15 min |
| 5 | **For Moderate offenses or repeated Minor — Written Warning**: Store Manager drafts written notice of explanation (NTE) per DOLE two-notice rule; employee given 5 business days to submit written explanation; NTE references specific company policy violated and incident details | Store Manager | HR Coordinator | 30 min |
| 6 | Employee submits written explanation or waives right to respond; Store Manager and HR Coordinator evaluate explanation; if explanation is unsatisfactory, proceed to Step 7 | Employee / HR Coordinator | Store Manager | 15 min |
| 7 | **Notice of Decision (NOD)**: Store Manager issues written decision with disciplinary action: written warning, suspension (1-30 days per severity), or termination recommendation for severe offenses; HR Coordinator reviews for DOLE compliance before issuance | Store Manager | HR Business Partner | 30 min |
| 8 | **For Severe offenses — Preventive Suspension**: If investigation requires more than 5 days, employee placed on preventive suspension (max 30 days per Labor Code) with pay; Store Manager coordinates with HR Business Partner for investigation | Store Manager | HR Business Partner | 15 min |
| 9 | **For termination cases**: HR Business Partner conducts independent review; ensures two-notice rule compliance, just cause per Article 297 Labor Code, and due process documentation; Legal Counsel reviews if employee is regular status; final termination decision by VP Legal & Compliance | HR Business Partner | VP Legal | 60 min |
| 10 | All disciplinary actions recorded in employee master (W292) with immutable audit trail; system flags employees with active disciplinary actions for promotion/transfer eligibility check | System | — | Automated |
| 11 | Monthly: HR Coordinator generates store-level disciplinary summary report; Regional Manager reviews for pattern analysis (specific stores with high incident rates may indicate management or training issues) | HR Coordinator | Regional Manager | 2 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- ERP HR module: disciplinary case management, employee master data, warning tracking
- Document management: NTE/NOD template generation and storage (W255)
- CCTV system: footage retrieval for investigation (per W207)
- POS transaction logs: evidence for POS-related incidents (per W522)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **DOLE due process non-compliance**: failure to follow two-notice rule (NTE → NOD) or denial of opportunity to explain results in illegal dismissal finding; mitigated by system-enforced workflow with mandatory NTE issuance before NOD
- **Inconsistent discipline across stores**: Store Managers may apply different standards; mitigated by policy matrix with offense severity classification and HR Coordinator review in Steps 5 and 7
- **Retaliation claims**: employees may claim disciplinary action was retaliatory for union activity or whistleblower reports (W79); mitigated by HR Business Partner independent review for termination cases
- **Witness cooperation**: witnesses may be reluctant to provide statements against coworkers; mitigated by confidential reporting option and anti-retaliation policy


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "Incident occurs or is reported" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "logs if POS-related" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "documents verbal warning in system with date, nature, and improvement…" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-262 (ensure controlled execution — Store Staffing & People (PA-07.4).)
- operational: system-enforced workflow with mandatory NTE issuance before NOD; operational: policy matrix with offense severity classification and HR Coordinator review in Steps 5 and 7

### Staffing Implication
- **Store Manager**: ~30 min per incident (investigation + documentation); ~10–15 hours/month across all incidents. Absorbed within existing role.
- **HR Coordinator**: ~30 min per incident for compliance review; ~5 hours/month. Absorbed within existing role.
- **HR Business Partner**: ~1 hour per termination case; ~4–6 hours/month for severe offense reviews. Absorbed within existing role.

### Time Estimate
~1.1–2.5 hours per incident depending on severity (documentation 10 min + fact-finding 30 min + classification 5 min + one disposition path — verbal counseling 15 min, written warning 30 min, or termination-track review 60 min + explanation review 15 min + Notice of Decision 30 min + preventive-suspension handling 15 min where applied); at ~80–120 incidents/month chain-wide ≈ 1,000–1,500 incidents/year ≈ 1,050–3,600 hours/year, plus the monthly disciplinary summary 2 hours (employee-master recording automated).

## W609. Store-Level New Employee Buddy System & First-Week Onboarding

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New hire first day at store (following HQ onboarding per W15) |
| **Frequency** | As needed; ~6–8 new hires per store per year per profile §11.4 (~1,200–1,600/year across 200 stores) |
| **Volume** | 1 new hire per event; ~100–130 active buddy assignments at any time (~30-day window on ~1,200–1,600 hires/year) |

| **Participants** | New employee, assigned Buddy (experienced Sales Associate), Department Supervisor, Store Manager |

| 1 | Prior to new hire arrival: Store Manager assigns Buddy from same department — selection criteria: ≥ 6 months tenure, performance rating ≥ satisfactory, completed Buddy training module; system checks Buddy eligibility and current assignment load (max 1 buddy at a time) | Store Manager | — | 10 min |
| 2 | **Day 1**: Buddy meets new hire at store entrance; provides store tour (selling floor, backroom, break room, emergency exits, first aid station, restrooms); introduces to all team members by name and role | Buddy | Dept. Supervisor | 60 min |
| 3 | Buddy guides new hire through department-specific orientation: product locations, top 20 selling SKUs, shelf layout, price tag reading, safety hazards specific to department (heavy items in lumber, chemicals in paint, sharp tools in hardware) | Buddy | Dept. Supervisor | 2 hours |
| 4 | **Days 2-3**: Buddy shadows and is shadowed — new hire observes Buddy handling customer interactions, POS transactions (if cashier), and department tasks; Buddy explains reasoning and shares tips; new hire attempts first customer interactions with Buddy present | Buddy | Dept. Supervisor | Full shift |
| 5 | **Days 4-5**: New hire handles customer interactions independently with Buddy available for questions; Department Supervisor conducts 15-minute check-in to assess comfort level and identify knowledge gaps | New Hire / Dept. Supervisor | Store Manager | 15 min |

### Background

Vendor promodizers (merchandiser demonstrators, product promoters, and brand ambassadors deployed by vendors to BuildRight stores) are a common feature of Philippine big-box retail. With ~800–1,000 vendors, many deploy promodizers to maintain shelf presence, conduct product demonstrations, and influence purchase decisions. W269 covers enterprise-level promodizer management and W449 covers DOLE 174 labor compliance. This workflow addresses the daily store-level coordination challenge: managing multiple promodizers from different vendors operating simultaneously on the sales floor, ensuring they follow BuildRight rules, and preventing conflicts between promodizer activities and store operations.

### Steps

| 3 | **Floor activity monitoring**: during promodizer activity: (a) Department Supervisor periodically checks promodizer activity (every 1-2 hours): (i) promodizer is in assigned area only; (ii) not interfering with customer shopping flow; (iii) not disparaging competing products; (iv) following BuildRight store rules: no loud music, no blocking aisles, no unauthorized promotional materials; (v) wearing required PPE if in paint/chemical department per W237; (b) if violations observed: (i) first offense: verbal warning and correction; (ii) repeated offense: ask promodizer to leave and report to vendor via W269; (iii) serious violation (customer harassment, theft, safety hazard): immediate removal and incident report per W471 | Department Supervisor | Store Manager | 5-10 min per check |

## W1017. Store-Level Scrap Metal & Recyclable Material Collection & Revenue Recognition

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Accumulation of scrap metal, cardboard, and recyclable materials from store operations |
| **Frequency** | Weekly collection per store; daily accumulation |
| **Volume** | ~100–200 kg scrap metal/store/week; ~300–500 kg cardboard/store/week; ~50–100 kg plastic/store/week |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Maintenance/Utility Staff, Scrap Metal Collector (accredited buyer), Finance Analyst (revenue recognition) |

### Background

BuildRight Depot's 200 stores generate significant recyclable waste from daily operations: damaged lumber (off-cuts, broken boards), scrap metal (damaged fixtures, wire offcuts, hardware returns), cardboard packaging (from inbound shipments), and plastic packaging. Philippine recycling infrastructure has matured significantly — accredited junk shops and scrap metal collectors (locally called "bote dyaryo" or "scrap buyers") serve every commercial area and pay market rates for sorted recyclable materials. Current market rates (2026): clean cardboard ~PHP 3–5/kg, scrap steel ~PHP 10–15/kg, scrap copper ~PHP 250–350/kg, aluminum ~PHP 50–70/kg, clean plastic ~PHP 5–8/kg. For a store generating 400–800 kg/week of recyclable material, this represents ~PHP 2,500–6,500/week (≈ PHP 10,000–27,000/month) in potential revenue per store (per the itemized rates and volumes) that is currently lost to improper disposal or untracked collection. Chain-wide, this represents ~PHP 22–65M/year in recyclable material value. Beyond revenue, proper recycling supports BuildRight's ESG commitments per W192 (GHG Emissions Tracking) and W193 (Waste Management & Circular Economy), reduces waste disposal costs per W502, and provides verifiable data for DENR waste diversion reporting per W433.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Daily Recyclable Material Segregation**: During daily operations: (a) Sales Associates and Receiving Clerks segregate waste into designated bins: (i) cardboard bin (flattened boxes, no food contamination), (ii) scrap metal bin (steel, copper, aluminum separated), (iii) plastic bin (clean packaging plastic, no chemical residue), (iv) wood/lumber bin (off-cuts, damaged boards — clean, no paint/treatment), (v) general waste bin (non-recyclable, per W502); (b) bins located at receiving dock and backroom; (c) hazardous materials (paint cans, solvent containers, chemical packaging) never placed in recycling bins — handled per W82 and W236 | All Store Staff | Department Supervisor | Ongoing (5–10 min/day per staff member) |
| 2 | **Weekly Collection & Weighing**: Weekly: (a) accredited scrap collector arrives at scheduled time (registered vendor in ERP per W36); (b) Maintenance Staff and collector weigh each category of recyclable material using calibrated scale at receiving dock; (c) collector issues weigh ticket per category with unit price and total amount; (d) Maintenance Staff verifies weigh ticket accuracy and signs receipt; (e) collector pays cash or issues check per agreed terms; (f) system records collection: material category, weight (kg), unit price, total revenue, collector vendor code | Maintenance Staff / Scrap Collector | Department Supervisor | 30–45 min/week |
| 3 | **Revenue Recording & Cash Handling**: (a) cash received from scrap collector: counted by Maintenance Staff in presence of Department Supervisor; (b) cash deposited in store safe per W541 (Cash Office Operations); (c) system records revenue: debit cash, credit "Other Income — Scrap/Recycling Revenue" per GL account mapping; (d) revenue recognized in period collected per PFRS (realized revenue from recyclable sales); (e) if collector pays by check: check deposited per W89 (Bank Reconciliation) and W764 (Daily Opening Safe Count); (f) monthly scrap revenue reported in store P&L per W67 (Store Performance Review) and W489 (Store Operating Budget) | Maintenance Staff / Department Supervisor | Department Supervisor | 10–15 min/week |
| 4 | **Quarterly Recycling Metrics & ESG Reporting**: Quarterly: (a) system aggregates recycling data: total weight by material category per store per region; (b) total revenue from recycling per store; (c) waste diversion rate = total recycled weight ÷ total waste generated; (d) carbon offset estimate: recycled cardboard saves ~3.5 kg CO2/kg, recycled steel saves ~1.5 kg CO2/kg per DENR conversion factors; (e) metrics included in ESG data collection per W694 (ESG Data Collection & Annual Report Preparation); (f) Store Manager reviews recycling performance in quarterly store performance review per W67; (g) stores with waste diversion rate < 30% flagged for improvement action plan | System / Department Supervisor | Store Manager | 30–60 min/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Waste tracking module with material category and weight logging
- Vendor master for accredited scrap collectors (W36)
- Cash handling and Receivables Revenue Management revenue recognition (W541, W714 — fit-gap A11)
- Store P&L reporting (W67, W489)
- ESG data collection module (W192, W694)
- Waste diversion rate analytics dashboard
- DENR waste reporting integration (W433)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Theft risk**: Scrap metal (especially copper and aluminum) has high street value and is a common theft target; scrap bins must be in secured receiving dock area (not accessible to customers); collector access logged per W581 (Vendor Representative Access); CCTV coverage per W838
- **Accreditation integrity**: Scrap collector must be DENR-accredited per W82 and properly registered as BuildRight vendor per W36; unaccredited collectors may illegally dump materials or resell to unregistered smelters; Finance team should verify collector accreditation annually
- **Revenue accuracy**: Manual weighing creates opportunity for under-weighing (collector benefit) or over-reporting (staff collusion); calibrated scale with digital readout and CCTV at weighing point per W838 mitigates risk; Department Supervisor must co-sign all weigh tickets
- **PFRS revenue recognition**: Scrap revenue is peripheral to BuildRight's core retail business; PFRS 15 requires recognition when collection occurs and payment is received; revenue should be immaterial to financial statements but must be properly recorded for tax compliance (BIR requires declaration of all income)


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "Weekly Collection & Weighing" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "Revenue Recording & Cash Handling" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-generate of "Quarterly Recycling Metrics & ESG Reporting" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-262 (ensure controlled execution — Store Staffing & People (PA-07.4).)
- operational: Department Supervisor review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Staffing Implication
- **Maintenance Staff**: Weekly collection adds ~30–45 min/week; absorbed by existing Maintenance/Utility Staff
- **Department Supervisor**: Revenue recording and quarterly reporting adds ~15–20 min/week + 30–60 min/quarter
- **No incremental headcount**

### Time Estimate
- Daily segregation: 5–10 min/day per staff (absorbed into workflow)
- Weekly collection: 30–45 min/week

## W1030. Multi-Store District Manager Weekly Operations Review & Compliance Audit

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Weekly scheduled review (every Monday); also triggered by significant operational incident |
| **Frequency** | Weekly × ~10–15 district managers (200 stores ÷ ~15 stores per district) |
| **Volume** | 15 stores per district manager per week |
| **Owner** | District Manager (Regional Operations Manager) |
| **Participants** | Store Managers (15 per district), LP Officer, HR Business Partner, Finance Analyst |

### Background

BuildRight Depot's 200 stores are organized into ~13–15 geographic districts (each district covers 13–15 stores within a 50–100 km radius), each led by a District Manager (DM) who reports to the COO via the Store Operations leadership. The DM is the critical management layer between HQ strategy and store-level execution. Each DM is responsible for: store revenue performance, operational compliance (planogram, pricing, cash handling, safety), customer experience scores, labor cost control, shrinkage reduction, and new store ramp-up. Without a structured weekly review cadence, DMs rely on ad-hoc store visits and reactive problem-solving, leading to inconsistent execution across the 200-store network. This workflow defines the DM's weekly review rhythm: Monday morning headquarters review with BI dashboards, Tuesday–Thursday store visits (2–3 stores/day with structured audit checklist), and Friday follow-up and reporting. The DM's weekly review ensures that all 200 stores are inspected at least once every 2–3 weeks (per the 6–9 visits/week district cadence), providing real-time field intelligence to HQ. This workflow is distinct from W67 (Monthly Store Performance Review) which is the store manager's self-review, and W575 (Store-Level Weekly Sales & Operations Review) which is the store's internal team meeting.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monday Morning District Dashboard Review**: DM reviews BI dashboard for all 15 stores in district: (a) **Sales performance**: week-over-week revenue, ATV, units per transaction, sales vs. budget, sales per sqm; red-flag stores > 10% below budget for 2+ consecutive weeks; (b) **Inventory health**: stockout rate by category, overstock alerts, replenishment order fill rate from DC, shrinkage trend; (c) **Customer experience**: customer satisfaction score (daily pulse from W608), NPS trend, complaint count and resolution time per W597; (d) **Labor**: overtime hours, labor cost as % of sales, attendance rate, unfilled positions; (e) **Cash & financial**: cash variance per W958, petty cash balance, bank deposit timeliness per W541; (f) **Compliance**: planogram compliance score (W573), price accuracy (W667), safety incident count (W140); (g) DM identifies 2–3 stores requiring priority visit this week based on performance outliers | District Manager | COO | 60–90 min |
| 2 | **Store Visit & Structured Audit (2–3 stores/day, Tue–Thu)**: DM visits selected stores with standardized audit checklist on tablet: (a) **Store exterior**: parking lot cleanliness, signage condition, cart corral (W1028), garden center appearance (W722), security lighting; (b) **Store entrance**: welcome experience, promotional signage per W262, loyalty program visibility, basket/cart availability; (c) **Selling floor**: planogram compliance (check 3 random aisles per W573), shelf stock level (identify 5 empty shelf positions), pricing accuracy (scan 10 random items per W667), promotional display execution per W262, cleanliness and aisle obstruction; (d) **Backroom**: receiving area organization, damaged goods quarantine area (W91), hazmat storage compliance (W236), cycle count documentation (W6); (e) **Customer service counter**: BOPIS staging area (W11), return processing compliance (W12), queue management; (f) **Cash office**: safe count vs. system per W764, deposit preparation per W541, POS reconciliation documentation; (g) **Safety**: fire extinguisher inspection tags (W758), emergency exit clearance, PPE compliance in lumber yard; (h) **People**: observe 2–3 employee-customer interactions, check staff scheduling board, discuss any HR issues with Store Manager | District Manager | COO | 90–120 min per store |
| 3 | **Store Manager Coaching & Action Plan**: During store visit, DM conducts 15–20 minute coaching session with Store Manager: (a) review Store Manager's weekly self-assessment per W575; (b) discuss top 3 performance gaps identified in dashboard and audit; (c) agree on 2–3 specific action items with deadlines (typically 1–2 weeks); (d) for high-performing stores: recognize achievements, capture best practices for sharing across district; (e) for underperforming stores: develop improvement plan with weekly checkpoints; (f) for new stores (< 6 months old): accelerated coaching per W702 (New Store Opening) milestones; (g) document coaching notes in system for follow-up tracking | District Manager | Store Manager | 15–20 min per store |

| 5 | **Friday Summary Report & HQ Communication**: DM compiles weekly district summary: (a) district sales performance: total revenue, vs. budget, vs. last year, top 3 and bottom 3 stores; (b) audit findings summary: common issues across stores, systemic problems requiring HQ intervention; (c) action items from store visits with status (new/in-progress/completed); (d) resource requests: staffing (W555), capital (W21), maintenance (W47), or vendor issues; (e) competitive intelligence: any competitor activity observed during store visits (new store opening, aggressive pricing per W329); (f) submit report to COO via standardized template; (g) participate in weekly DM conference call with COO and other DMs (30 min): share cross-district learnings, escalate systemic issues, align on priorities | District Manager | COO | 60–90 min |
