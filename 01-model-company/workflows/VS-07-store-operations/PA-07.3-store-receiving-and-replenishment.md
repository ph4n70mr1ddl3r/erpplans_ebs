## W18. Direct Store Delivery (DSD) Receiving

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vendor delivers goods directly to store (bypassing DC) |
| **Frequency** | ~2–3 DSD deliveries per store per month; ~500–600 DSD receipts/month chain-wide |
| **Volume** | ~30% of total inbound goods by value/weight (cement, lumber, sand, gravel are bulky/high-value); ~500–600 DSD receipts/month (~8–10% of total receipt count); ~5–10 lines per receipt |
| **Owner** | Receiving Clerk (Store) |
| **Participants** | Receiving Clerk, Department Supervisor, Vendor Driver, AP Clerk |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Vendor arrives at store receiving dock; presents Delivery Receipt (DR) and PO reference | Vendor Driver | Receiving Clerk | 5 min |
| 2 | Receiving Clerk retrieves PO in system on handheld/tablet | Receiving Clerk | Dept. Supervisor | 2 min |
| 3 | Unload goods; Receiving Clerk scans/verifies each item against PO: SKU, quantity, condition | Receiving Clerk | Dept. Supervisor | 20–45 min |
| 4 | Quality check on sampled items (damage, completeness) | Dept. Supervisor | Dept. Supervisor | 5–10 min |
| 5 | If discrepancy: flag in system; note on DR; notify Buyer | Receiving Clerk | Dept. Supervisor | 5 min if any |
| 6 | Confirm Goods Receipt in system; store inventory increases in real-time | Receiving Clerk | Dept. Supervisor | 5 min |
| 7 | Vendor DR signed and returned; copy retained for AP matching | Receiving Clerk | — | 2 min |
| 8 | Stock Associate moves goods directly to sales floor (no backroom staging for DSD) | Stock Associate | Dept. Supervisor | 15–30 min |
| 9 | AP receives vendor invoice; 3-way match: PO → Store GR → Invoice | AP Clerk | AP Supervisor | Automated / 10 min |

**Total time per DSD delivery**: ~1–2 hours from truck arrival to shelf

### DSD vs. DC-Receiving Differences
- No putaway step — goods go directly to sales floor
- No WMS involved — processed through store-level receiving in POS/ERP
- Smaller average receipt size (~5–10 lines vs. ~15 for DC)
- Vendor driver typically waits for GR confirmation and signed DR
- Common DSD categories: cement, lumber, sand, gravel, large appliances

### System Touchpoints
- PO lookup on store handheld/tablet (W18.2)
- Barcode/RF scanning against PO at store level (W18.3)
- Store-level Goods Receipt posting → inventory update (W18.6)
- DR capture and linking to PO for AP matching (W18.7, W18.9)
- 3-way match including store-level GR (W18.9)
- Real-time inventory visibility for DSD items (W18.6)
- DSD GRNI follow-up: for DSD-related GRNI exceptions at month-end (W9A.2a), AP Clerk contacts the store Receiving Clerk who processed the original GR (W18.3) to confirm received quantities and resolve discrepancies with the vendor
- Shelf-life / expiry capture at DSD receiving: for date-sensitive DSD items (cement, paint, adhesives), Receiving Clerk captures manufacturing date and shelf-life at goods receipt; system records expiry date per batch; near-expiry items flagged for priority sale or markdown (W18)

### Pain Points / Risks
- Vendor driver wait time conflicts — drivers want quick turnaround, but Receiving Clerk may be occupied with DC delivery or BOPIS staging, causing vendor dissatisfaction and delivery schedule disruptions
- Cement and lumber quality verification challenges at store level — no dedicated quality lab; damaged or substandard goods accepted because Department Supervisor lacks time for thorough inspection
- AP 3-way match failures when vendor invoices reference different PO numbers than the DR, or when GR quantities are entered incorrectly under time pressure from waiting drivers
- Shelf-life/expiry risk for cement and paint — if expiry date is not captured accurately at receiving, near-expiry stock reaches the shelf undetected


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-scan of "scans/verifies each item against PO" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-validate of "check on sampled items" (replaces manual Step 4).
- System auto-notify of "notify Buyer" (replaces manual Step 5).

### Controls
- CTL-261 (ensure controlled execution — Store Receiving & Replenishment (PA-07.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
~1–2 hours per DSD delivery from truck arrival to shelf (20–45 min unloading/scanning + 5–10 min quality check + 15–30 min shelf stocking). At ~500–600 DSD deliveries/month across 200 stores (~2–3/store/month per the Frequency), total chain-wide receiving time is ~500–1,200 hours/month. AP 3-way match adds ~10 min per receipt.

### Staffing Implication
- **2 Receiving Clerks per store** (already in staffing model): DSD adds 2–3 additional receiving events per month (~3–6 hours). Combined with DC replenishment receiving (2–3 trucks/week) and BOPIS, the 2 clerks in alternating shifts can handle the load. No additional headcount needed.

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Weekly DSD delivery schedule coordination |
| **Frequency** | Weekly schedule per DSD vendor; ~500–600 DSD deliveries/month across 200 stores |
| **Volume** | ~2–3 DSD deliveries per store per month; each vendor serves multiple stores per region |
| **Owner** | Buyer (coordination); Receiving Clerk (store execution) |
| **Participants** | Buyer, Vendor, Receiving Clerk, Store Manager |

### DSD Vendor Scheduling Process

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Buyer maintains DSD vendor delivery calendar per vendor: assigned stores, delivery frequency, preferred delivery day(s), contact person, special handling requirements | Buyer | Category Manager | 1 hour/vendor/quarter |
| 2 | Vendor or Buyer confirms weekly delivery schedule: which stores, which day, approximate time window, PO references (if applicable) | Buyer / Vendor | Buyer | 30 min/vendor/week |
| 3 | System pushes DSD delivery schedule to affected store Receiving Clerks via handheld/tablet notification; includes expected vendor, PO references, and time window | System | — | Automated |
| 4 | Receiving Clerk plans receiving dock availability around DSD schedule and DC replenishment truck schedule (W4); flags scheduling conflicts to Store Manager | Receiving Clerk | Store Manager | 15 min/day |
| 5 | If vendor misses scheduled delivery: Receiving Clerk reports no-show to Buyer; Buyer follows up with vendor; reschedules within the same week if possible; tracks vendor delivery reliability in vendor scorecard (W44) | Receiving Clerk / Buyer | Category Manager | 10 min/occurrence |

### System Touchpoints (DSD Scheduling)
- DSD vendor delivery calendar per vendor with assigned stores and frequency (W18B.1)
- Weekly delivery schedule communication to store receiving (W18B.3)
- Scheduling conflict alerting when DSD and DC deliveries overlap (W18B.4)
- Vendor no-show tracking feeding into vendor scorecard (W44) (W18B.5)
- Integration with W18 (DSD receiving), W4 (DC replenishment scheduling), W44 (vendor performance)

### Time Estimate
Buyer: ~1 hour/vendor/quarter for calendar maintenance + 30 min/vendor/week for schedule confirmation. Receiving Clerk: 15 min/day planning dock availability. At ~500–600 DSD deliveries/month across 200 stores with multiple vendors, Buyer coordination time is ~4–6 hours/week for all DSD vendors.

### Pain Points / Risks
- Vendor delivery no-shows disrupting store operations — if a cement or lumber vendor fails to deliver on scheduled day, the store may run out of high-demand items before the next scheduled delivery, losing sales
- Dock scheduling conflicts between DSD vendors and DC replenishment trucks — a single receiving dock means vendors wait while DC truck is being unloaded, causing vendor dissatisfaction and schedule cascading
- Buyer single-point-of-failure for DSD scheduling — if the assigned Buyer is unavailable, vendor schedule changes and no-show escalations are delayed, leaving Receiving Clerks without updated information
- Vendor delivery reliability not adequately reflected in scorecards — no-show and late delivery data may not be consistently captured by Receiving Clerks, reducing the effectiveness of W44 vendor performance tracking

### Staffing Implication
- Buyer coordination adds ~4–6 hours/week for DSD scheduling across all vendors. Absorbed within existing Buying team.
- Receiving Clerk spends ~15 min/day planning dock availability around DSD schedule. Absorbed into existing 2 Receiving Clerks per store.
- No incremental headcount. If DSD volume grows significantly (e.g., new vendor categories), additional Buyer support may be needed.

## W86. Planogram Compliance & Store Layout Verification

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly planogram audit schedule; or new planogram release from Merchandising; or post-reset verification after promotional display change |
| **Frequency** | Monthly audit per store (all 200 stores); ad-hoc after planogram resets or new store openings (W16) |
| **Volume** | 200 stores × 2–3 departments per month (per the step-5 rotation) = ~400–600 department-level audits/month; each department contains 50–200 SKUs in the planogram |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (in-store execution); Merchandise Planner (planogram design) |
| **Participants** | Department Supervisor, Stock Associate, Merchandise Planner, Store Manager, Regional Manager |

### Background

Planograms define the visual layout of products on store shelves — which SKUs go where, how many facings, at what height, and in what sequence. Planogram compliance ensures that stores display products according to the approved layout, maximizing sell-through, customer navigation, and brand consistency across all 200 locations. While W63 covers shelf label accuracy and W69 covers price compliance, there is no workflow for verifying that products are physically placed in the correct locations according to the planogram.

### Planogram Design & Distribution

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Merchandise Planner creates or updates planogram per category using planogram design tool: (a) defines shelf layout — bay heights, shelf positions, SKU-to-shelf assignment, number of facings, adjacent product rules, (b) incorporates category management insights from W1 assortment review — new SKUs added, discontinued SKUs removed, space allocation adjusted based on sales velocity, (c) considers fixture type per store format (standard vs. compact stores), (d) generates planogram document per department per store format with visual layout, SKU placement map, and compliance checklist | Merchandise Planner | Category Manager | 4–8 hours/category/update |
| 2 | Category Manager reviews and approves planogram; VP Merchandising approves for major category resets (occurring 2–4 times/year aligned with W1 quarterly cycles) | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1 hour/review |
| 3 | System distributes planogram to stores: Merchandise Planner uploads approved planogram to system; Store Managers and Department Supervisors access planogram view on tablet/terminal; planogram includes visual shelf map, SKU images, facing counts, and before/after photos for resets | System / Merchandise Planner | VP Store Operations | Automated distribution |
| 4 | **Planogram reset execution** (for major category resets): Department Supervisors execute planogram reset during non-business hours (evening or before opening) — Stock Associates remove products from shelves per old layout, reposition shelves if needed, place products per new planogram using tablet visual guide; Stock Associate scan-confirms each SKU placement against planogram using handheld; system records compliance per SKU position | Dept. Supervisor / Stock Associate | Store Manager | 2–4 hours/department/reset |

### Monthly Planogram Compliance Audit

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | System generates monthly planogram audit sample per store: 2–3 departments per month (rotating so all departments are audited over 2–3 months); 20–30 SKU positions per department randomly selected for verification | System | — | Automated (nightly) |
| 6 | Department Supervisor or designated Stock Associate conducts planogram audit: walks assigned department with handheld showing current planogram; for each sampled SKU position: (a) scans shelf location barcode (if location-level barcoding available) or selects position on planogram map, (b) scans item barcode at that position, (c) system compares scanned item to planogram-expected item and facing count, (d) enters result: Correct / Wrong Item / Wrong Position / Out of Stock / Facing Count Mismatch | Dept. Supervisor / Stock Associate | Store Manager | 20–30 min/department |
| 7 | System calculates planogram compliance score per department: (correct positions ÷ total positions audited) × 100; stores must achieve ≥ 90% planogram compliance score | System | — | Automated |
| 8 | Department Supervisor investigates non-compliant positions: (a) wrong item in position — Stock Associate placed wrong SKU during replenishment or reset, (b) out of stock — SKU allocated to position but no inventory available (feeds into W4 replenishment), (c) facing count mismatch — insufficient facings maintained due to low stock or over-crowding from adjacent SKUs, (d) wrong position — SKU present but in incorrect location (often from customer browsing returns) | Dept. Supervisor | Store Manager | 15 min/department |
| 9 | Department Supervisor corrects non-compliant positions immediately during audit (where stock is available): repositions SKUs to match planogram, adjusts facing counts | Stock Associate | Dept. Supervisor | 15–30 min/department |
| 10 | Department Supervisor documents non-compliance exceptions that cannot be resolved immediately (out of stock, fixture issue) with photos and reason code in system; exception flagged for Store Manager review | Dept. Supervisor | Store Manager | 5 min/department |
| 11 | Store Manager reviews planogram compliance dashboard weekly: compliance score per department, trend vs. prior month, top non-compliance reasons; departments below 90% require corrective action plan from Department Supervisor | Store Manager | Regional Manager | 15 min/week |
| 12 | Monthly: Regional Manager reviews planogram compliance scores across all ~33 assigned stores (per VS-177); identifies stores consistently below 90% for targeted coaching; compliance score included in W67 Store Performance Scorecard | Regional Manager | VP Store Operations | 1 hour/month |
| 13 | Quarterly: Merchandise Planner reviews planogram compliance data chain-wide — identifies categories with systematically low compliance across stores (indicating planogram design issues rather than execution issues); feeds into W1 category review for planogram redesign | Merchandise Planner | VP Merchandising | 2 hours/quarter |

### Planogram Compliance for New Store Openings

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | During new store opening (W16 step 10 — receiving and stocking): Department Supervisors execute initial planogram using planogram view on tablet; Stock Associates place every SKU per planogram with scan confirmation; Merchandise Planner or Store Ops representative verifies initial placement before store opens | Dept. Supervisor / Stock Associate | Store Manager | Per W16 (1–2 days) |
| 15 | At new store 30-day post-opening review (W16 T+30): Merchandise Planner reviews initial planogram compliance report for new store; identifies positioning issues caused by local fixture variations or stock availability; adjusts planogram if needed | Merchandise Planner | Category Manager | 2 hours/store |

### System Touchpoints
- Planogram design tool integrated with item master and category management (W86.1)
- Planogram visual layout display on store tablet/terminal with SKU images and facing counts (W86.3)
- Scan-verified planogram reset execution with handheld barcode confirmation (W86.4)
- Monthly planogram audit sampling and compliance scoring per department (W86.5–7)
- Non-compliance exception documentation with photos and reason codes (W86.10)
- Planogram compliance dashboard: score per department, per store, chain-wide trend (W86.11–12)
- Chain-wide planogram analytics: category-level compliance, design vs. execution issue identification (W86.13)
- Integration with W1 (assortment review — planogram redesign trigger), W4 (replenishment — out-of-stock positions), W16 (new store — initial planogram execution), W63 (shelf labels — label matches planogram position), W67 (store performance — compliance KPI), W68 (product discontinuation — remove from planogram), W83 (campaign — promotional display planograms)

### Pain Points / Risks
- Planogram design vs. store reality mismatch — Merchandise Planner creates layouts without visiting every store; fixture variations, column placements, and ceiling heights in leased spaces deviate from the assumed standard layout
- Out-of-stock positions misinterpreted as compliance failures — an empty shelf position is flagged as non-compliant when the real issue is replenishment failure (W4), not planogram execution
- Stock Associate scan fatigue during resets — manually scan-confirming every SKU position during a major category reset is tedious and error-prone, leading to rubber-stamped confirmations without actual verification
- Competitive planogram deviation — stores near strong competitors may intentionally deviate from planogram to emphasize value items or promotional displays, undermining chain-wide consistency

## W109. Store-Level Inventory Receiving & Putaway

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | DC delivery truck arrives at store; or DSD vendor delivery; or inter-store transfer arrives; or customer return processed for restocking |
| **Frequency** | 2–3 DC deliveries per store per week; ~2–3 DSD deliveries per store per month; occasional transfers and returns |
| **Volume** | ~3–5 receiving events per store per week; avg 60–90 order lines per DC delivery (the consolidated-drop canon — ~20–30 transfer orders at the production-measured ~3 lines/order, per data-volumes §1.1); 5–15 lines per DSD |
| **Owner** | Receiving Clerk (store) |
| **Participants** | Receiving Clerk, Stock Associate, Department Supervisor, Store Manager, DC Driver |

### Background

W3 covers DC receiving in detail — barcode-directed putaway, quality inspection, cross-dock flows, and yard management. However, store-level receiving is fundamentally different: (a) no WMS-directed putaway — stores use handheld RF devices or mobile tablets, not full WMS; (b) putaway means moving stock to the sales floor (shelf replenishment) or backroom (overflow), not to bin locations; (c) stores receive from multiple sources (DC delivery, DSD vendor, inter-store transfer, customer return restocking); (d) receiving happens during store operating hours with customers present, unlike DC receiving which is a warehouse-only activity. This workflow fills the gap between DC outbound dispatch (W106) and store shelf availability.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pre-arrival preparation**: System alerts Receiving Clerk of scheduled inbound deliveries for the day (from DC delivery schedule per W106, DSD schedule per W18B, or transfer ETA per W22); Clerk reviews expected receiving volume and coordinates with Stock Associates for putaway support | Receiving Clerk | Store Manager | 10 min/morning |
| 2 | **Truck arrival and dock check**: Driver arrives at store receiving area; Receiving Clerk checks delivery manifest against system-scheduled deliveries; if unscheduled delivery (no matching PO/TO): Clerk checks with Store Manager before accepting | Receiving Clerk / Driver | Store Manager | 5 min |
| 3 | **Unloading**: Receiving Clerk and Stock Associates unload truck; stage goods in receiving area sorted by department (lumber, plumbing, tiles, tools, hardware, paint, appliances); catch-weight items (lumber, wire) measured and verified at unload | Receiving Clerk / Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | 20–45 min/delivery |
| 4 | **Scan receiving against Transfer Order / PO**: Receiving Clerk scans each case/tote/item barcode using handheld RF device against the Transfer Order (DC delivery) or PO (DSD); system displays expected SKU and quantity per line; Clerk confirms or enters actual quantity received | Receiving Clerk | Department Supervisor | 15–30 min/delivery |
| 5 | **Discrepancy handling**: (a) **Shortage** (received less than expected): Clerk enters actual quantity; system posts partial receipt; shortage logged; if > 5% variance: Clerk notifies Store Manager and DC Dispatch per W22.9a; (b) **Overage** (received more than expected): Clerk flags in system; Store Manager decides to accept (updates TO/PO quantity) or reject excess; (c) **Wrong item** (SKU doesn't match TO/PO): Clerk rejects item; Driver takes back or DC Dispatch notified; (d) **Damage**: Clerk documents damage with photos per W91 damage report process; routes to disposition (W93 markdown, W88 RTV, or scrap) | Receiving Clerk | Store Manager | 5 min/discrepancy |
| 6 | **Quality spot-check**: For DSD items (fresh delivery from vendor, no DC quality inspection): Department Supervisor spot-checks 3–5 items per delivery for damage, correct labeling, and shelf-life; DC-delivered items already inspected at W3 AQL checkpoint — no additional store inspection unless visibly damaged | Department Supervisor | Store Manager | 5 min/delivery |
| 7 | **Confirm receipt in system**: Receiving Clerk confirms Goods Receipt in handheld; system posts: (a) store inventory increases (perpetual), (b) in-transit inventory clears (for DC deliveries), (c) inventory ownership at store confirmed (Depot Inc.), (d) receiving timestamp and Clerk ID logged | Receiving Clerk / System | Department Supervisor | 2 min |
| 8 | **Putaway to sales floor (primary)**: Stock Associate moves received goods from receiving staging area to sales floor: (a) system provides shelf location guidance on handheld (aisle/bay) based on planogram (W86); (b) Stock Associate places items on shelf in designated location; scan-confirms shelf placement; (c) for promotional items (W57): Stock Associate places in designated promo display area, not regular shelf; (d) for catch-weight items: Stock Associate places in yard or designated cut-to-length area per store layout; (e) putaway priority: promotional items first, then A-items (fast movers), then B/C-items | Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | 30–60 min/delivery |
| 9 | **Putaway to backroom (overflow)**: If sales floor shelf is full (no space for all received quantity): (a) Stock Associate places excess in backroom stock area; (b) system records backroom location (zone-level, not bin-level — stores do not have bin-level location tracking); (c) backroom inventory tracked separately from floor inventory in system; (d) Stock Associates replenish from backroom to floor during daily shelf restocking (W5A step 7) | Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | 10 min/delivery |
| 10 | **Packaging and waste disposal**: Stock Associate breaks down empty cartons, pallets, and packaging materials; cardboard recycled or compacted; packaging waste disposed per store waste management protocol; wooden pallets stacked for DC truck backhaul on next delivery | Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | 10 min/delivery |
| 11 | **Receiving completion**: Receiving Clerk closes receiving session in handheld; system generates receiving confirmation with: TO/PO number, total lines received, discrepancies noted, receiving completion timestamp; Driver signs delivery receipt (proof of delivery for DC) and departs | Receiving Clerk / Driver | Store Manager | 5 min |

**Total time per DC delivery**: ~1.5–2.5 hours (unloading + scanning + putaway)
**Total time per DSD delivery**: ~30–60 minutes

### Receiving by Source

| Source | Receiving Document | Scan Against | Discrepancy Route | Putaway Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DC delivery** (W4, W106) | Transfer Order | TO lines | W22.9a (transfer discrepancy) | Promo → A-items → B/C-items |
| **DSD vendor** (W18) | Purchase Order | PO lines | W18 discrepancy process | Direct to shelf/yard |
| **Inter-store transfer** (W22) | Transfer Order | TO lines | W22.9a | Per dept priority |
| **Customer return restock** (W12) | Return transaction | Return line items | N/A — already processed | Direct to shelf |
| **RTV backhaul from vendor** (W88) | RTV shipment | RTV record | W88 vendor dispute | To RTV holding area |

### System Touchpoints
- Daily inbound delivery schedule from W106 DC dispatch, W18B DSD schedule, and W22 transfers (W109.1)
- Handheld RF barcode scanning against Transfer Order or Purchase Order with line-level quantity confirmation (W109.4)
- Partial receipt posting with discrepancy logging and auto-notification (W109.5)
- Shelf location guidance from planogram integration (W86) on handheld (W109.8)
- Dual inventory tracking: sales floor vs. backroom at zone level (W109.9)
- Receiving confirmation with proof of delivery for DC truck (W109.11)
- Catch-weight item measurement capture at receiving (W109.3)
- Integration with W4 (replenishment — the orders being received), W18 (DSD — vendor delivery receiving), W22 (transfers — inter-store receiving), W42 (physical inventory — receiving affects count accuracy), W47 (facility — receiving dock maintenance), W86 (planogram — shelf placement guidance), W91 (damage handling), W106 (DC dispatch — proof of delivery loop), W63 (shelf labels — new items may need labels)

### Pain Points / Risks
- Receiving dock bottleneck — with 2–3 DC deliveries per week and 2–3 DSD deliveries per month plus occasional inter-store transfers, the single receiving dock creates scheduling conflicts and driver wait times
- Scan accuracy under time pressure — Receiving Clerks rushing through barcode scanning during high-volume deliveries may confirm quantities without actually scanning each item, leading to receiving discrepancies that propagate into inventory inaccuracy
- Putaway priority conflicts — Stock Associates must prioritize promotional items, then A-items, but urgent BOPIS picking requests and customer service needs compete for the same Stock Associate labor during receiving windows

|---|---|

| **Frequency** | 2–3 replenishment trucks per store per week (DC) + ~2–3 DSD trucks per store per month |
| **Volume** | ~80–120 truck arrivals/day chain-wide (DC ~400–600/week + DSD ~500–600/month + transfers and return pickups) |
| **Owner** | Receiving Clerk |
| **Participants** | Receiving Clerk, Stock Associate, Truck Driver, Security Guard |

### Background

Each BuildRight store receives 2–3 replenishment trucks per week from DCs and ~2–3 DSD deliveries per month from vendors. With 200 stores, that translates to ~80–120 truck movements per day across the chain (DC ~57–86/day + DSD ~20/day + transfers and return pickups). Many stores have limited loading bay capacity (1-2 bays), creating queuing challenges when multiple trucks arrive simultaneously. The loading bay also serves as the customer yard loading area per W438, creating potential conflicts between commercial trucks and customer vehicles. This workflow manages the daily loading bay traffic flow, complementing W585 (DC dock scheduling) which handles the DC side, and W109 (store-level receiving) which handles the goods receipt process.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |

| 2 | **Truck arrival and queuing**: when truck arrives: (a) Security Guard directs truck to loading bay or queue area: (i) if loading bay available: direct to bay; (ii) if loading bay occupied: direct to queue area and assign queue position; (iii) if customer vehicles in yard loading area per W438: prioritize customer safety, hold truck until yard is clear; (b) Security Guard verifies truck identity: driver matches scheduled delivery, license plate matches expected vehicle; (c) for unscheduled/unexpected trucks: Receiving Clerk verifies against open POs or transfer orders before accepting; refuse delivery if no matching order | Security Guard / Receiving Clerk | Store Manager | 5 min per truck |
| 3 | **Bay allocation and turnaround management**: Receiving Clerk manages bay turnaround: (a) target turnaround time: DC replenishment trucks = 45-60 min; DSD trucks = 20-30 min; inter-store transfers = 30-45 min; (b) monitor receiving progress: if truck exceeding target turnaround, investigate bottleneck (missing PO, product discrepancy, short-staffed); (c) if queue is building (2+ trucks waiting): (i) request additional Stock Associate support; (ii) expedite current truck completion; (iii) communicate wait time to queued drivers; (d) for DSD vendors: enforce W18B delivery time window compliance; late arrivals may be rescheduled | Receiving Clerk | Store Manager | Ongoing |
| 4 | **Customer yard conflict management**: loading bay area also serves customer yard loading per W438: (a) separate customer loading zone from commercial truck bay with clear signage and barriers; (b) if customer loading blocks truck access: Security Guard assists with customer vehicle relocation; (c) if commercial truck blocks customer access: expedite truck departure or redirect to alternate bay; (d) ensure pedestrian safety: customers walking through yard area are separated from forklift and truck traffic by barriers and signage | Security Guard / Receiving Clerk | Store Manager | Ongoing |
| 5 | **Daily bay utilization reporting**: at end of day: (a) system generates daily loading bay utilization report: number of trucks received, average turnaround time, peak hour utilization, queue wait times, customer vs. truck conflict incidents; (b) Receiving Clerk reviews for anomalies; (c) report feeds into weekly store operations review per W575; (d) persistent queuing issues escalated to Regional Manager for DC delivery schedule adjustment or facility improvement | Receiving Clerk | Store Manager | 5-10 min |

### Pain Points / Risks

- **DSD vendor non-compliance with time windows**: vendors frequently arrive outside scheduled time windows, disrupting the planned receiving sequence and creating conflicts with DC trucks
- **Driver waiting time disputes**: truck drivers (especially 3PL carriers) may complain about extended wait times, claiming demurrage charges; Receiving Clerk must balance thorough receiving per W109 with turnaround efficiency
- **Security risk during peak receiving**: Security Guard attention is divided between truck management and store perimeter security during peak receiving periods; mitigated by CTL-261 (controlled execution — Store Receiving & Replenishment (PA-07.3))

- CTL-261 (ensure controlled execution — Store Receiving & Replenishment (PA-07.3).)
- operational: physical separation and dedicated customer loading zone

Receiving Clerk: absorbed within existing receiving duties (~10–15 min/day on scheduling + ongoing bay management). Security Guard: ~15–30 min/day on truck direction and yard management. Stock Associates: called for surge support during multi-truck periods. No incremental headcount.

### Background

Construction projects in the Philippines follow a well-defined sequence: (1) site preparation and excavation, (2) foundation and structural work, (3) framing and roofing, (4) plumbing and electrical rough-in, (5) masonry and plastering, (6) tile and flooring, (7) doors and windows, (8) painting and finishing, (9) fixtures and accessories. Each phase requires different materials delivered at specific times. Delivering all materials at project start creates multiple problems: (a) on-site storage space is limited at Philippine residential construction sites (often just the sidewalk or a small staging area); (b) materials left on-site are exposed to theft, weather damage, and deterioration; (c) the customer's cash flow is strained by paying for materials months before they're needed; (d) damaged or lost materials reduce BuildRight's customer satisfaction. Staged delivery — releasing materials in phases aligned with the construction schedule — solves these problems and increases BuildRight's value proposition for project customers. ### Background

BuildRight Depot conducts an annual wall-to-wall physical inventory count at each of its 200 stores, as required by: (a) BIR regulations (physical inventory required for annual inventory list submission per RMC 57-2015, workflow W478); (b) Philippine Financial Reporting Standards (PFRS) requiring inventory valuation verification; (c) BuildRight's internal controls (CTL-21 — cycle counting and the annual inventory count for shrinkage measurement and balance sheet accuracy). The variance (shrinkage) discovered during physical inventory is the basis for: inventory write-off, shrinkage analysis per W845, insurance claim substantiation per W857, and adjustment of loss prevention programs. Each store's physical inventory involves all 29 staff members plus DM supervision and LP observation, and must be completed in a single day (store closed to public) to maintain count integrity. ## W1112. Store-Level Inventory Age-Based Auto-Markdown & Clearance Trigger

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Nightly batch job (inventory aging report), or manual review triggered by category manager |
| **Frequency** | Nightly (automated batch); weekly review by Department Supervisor |
| **Volume** | 35,000 active SKUs × 200 stores = 7,000,000 SKU-location combinations checked nightly |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (store-level execution) |
| **Participants** | Department Supervisor, Stock Associate, Store Manager, Category Manager (pricing approval) |

### Background

BuildRight Depot maintains 35,000 active SKUs across 200 stores, with an inventory turns target of 6–8x per year and a shrinkage target of < 1.5%. Despite replenishment optimization (W4, W2A) and SLOB management (W220), a significant portion of inventory ages beyond healthy thresholds due to: (a) seasonal items that didn't sell through; (b) discontinued items pending full phase-out; (c) localized slow movers specific to a store's demographics (e.g., snow-related items in Mindanao stores, or high-end power tools in provincial locations); (d) overstocks from cancelled projects; (e) display/demo units beyond their useful life. Currently, markdown decisions are reactive — Department Supervisors manually identify slow movers during weekly reviews. An age-based auto-markdown system applies progressive markdowns based on inventory age, ensuring timely price reductions that maximize recovery value before items become unsalable. This complements W93 (markdown & clearance pricing) which handles strategic markdowns, and W220 (SLOB provisioning) which handles accounting provisions. This workflow focuses on the automated trigger and execution mechanism for age-based markdowns at the store level.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## W1139. Store-Level Lumber Yard Inventory Seasonal Rotation & Grade Segregation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly inventory review, or seasonal merchandise transition (W264), or significant lumber delivery receipt |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly full rotation; ongoing daily segregation during receiving |
| **Volume** | ~4,900 lumber & building material SKUs per store; ~200–300 items requiring seasonal rotation |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Lumber & Building Materials) |
| **Participants** | Stock Associate (Lumber Yard), Department Supervisor, Receiving Clerk |

### Background

Lumber and building materials represent 14% of BuildRight's active SKUs (~4,900 items) and are stored primarily in the outdoor/covered lumber yard. Proper inventory management requires both seasonal rotation and grade segregation: (a) **Seasonal rotation**: During the Philippine dry season (December–May), demand shifts toward outdoor construction materials (lumber, plywood, roofing, cement) as construction activity peaks. During the rainy season (June–November), outdoor construction slows, and demand shifts to indoor renovation materials (paint, tiles, fixtures, electrical). Lumber yard inventory should be repositioned accordingly: high-demand seasonal items moved to accessible, front-of-yard positions; off-season items moved to rear storage positions. (b) **Grade segregation**: Lumber is graded by quality (Select, Common #1, Common #2, Construction, Standard, Utility) per Philippine grading standards. Mixed grades in the same stack cause customer confusion, pricing errors at POS, and inventory inaccuracy. Proper segregation ensures customers get the grade they pay for and BuildRight maintains accurate inventory valuation. This workflow complements W42 (physical inventory), W6 (cycle counting), and W1103 (lumber yard rain protection) by addressing the seasonal and quality management aspects of lumber yard inventory.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Quarterly Seasonal Yard Reorganization**: (a) before each quarter, Department Supervisor reviews seasonal demand forecast for lumber & building materials; (b) dry season (Dec–May plan): position dimensional lumber (2×2, 2×3, 2×4, 2×6), plywood (1/4", 3/8", 1/2", 3/4"), GI roofing sheets, cement, and rebar in front-of-yard accessible positions; (c) rainy season (Jun–Nov plan): move indoor renovation materials (moldings, doors, pre-finished panels) to more accessible positions; reduce outdoor lumber front-stocking to minimum display quantities; (d) update yard layout map in ERP for receiving clerk guidance; (e) seasonal reorganization typically requires 2–3 stock associates for 4–6 hours | Department Supervisor / Stock Associates | Store Manager | 4–6 hours (quarterly) |

## W1283. Cement & Building Material Vendor Direct Store Delivery (DSD) Coordination

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled or ad-hoc vendor DSD delivery of bulky construction materials to store |
| **Frequency** | ~2–3 DSD deliveries per store per month; ~500–600 DSD receipts chain-wide per month (per the canon and W1424) |
| **Volume** | ~30% of goods by value delivered DSD (cement, lumber, sand, gravel, rebar, hollow blocks) |
| **Owner** | Store Receiving Clerk |
| **Participants** | Store Receiving Clerk, Department Supervisor, Vendor Driver, Store Manager |

### Background

Unlike DC-supplied merchandise that flows through BuildRight's 4-DC network, ~30% of goods by value are delivered direct-to-store (DSD) by vendors. These are predominantly bulky, heavy, or high-volume construction materials that are uneconomical to double-handle through DCs: cement (bagged and bulk), sand, gravel, hollow blocks, rebar, and large lumber shipments. DSD coordination requires the store to manage its own receiving schedule, verify quantities against PO, handle quality inspection, and post goods receipt — all without DC support. This workflow is critical because DSD items represent some of BuildRight's highest-volume categories (cement alone is ~5% of COGS) and involve heavy, weather-sensitive products.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **DSD delivery schedule management**: Department Supervisor maintains weekly DSD delivery calendar per vendor: (a) cement vendors deliver 2–3x/week per store (high consumption), (b) sand/gravel vendors deliver 1–2x/week, (c) lumber/rebar vendors deliver per PO; system sends automated delivery reminders to vendors 24 hours before scheduled delivery; Store Manager reviews schedule for conflicts (multiple DSD trucks arriving simultaneously, limited receiving dock capacity) | Department Supervisor | Store Manager | 15 min/week |
| 2 | **Pre-delivery preparation**: Before scheduled DSD arrival: (a) Receiving Clerk clears designated unloading area (cement storage area, lumber yard, rebar racks), (b) prepares receiving tablet with expected PO details open, (c) ensures forklift or pallet jack is available for heavy items, (d) verifies scale calibration if vendor delivers by weight (sand, gravel), (e) confirms sufficient stock boys available for manual unloading if no dock-level unloading | Receiving Clerk | Department Supervisor | 15 min |
| 3 | **Vendor arrival & unloading coordination**: Vendor truck arrives; Receiving Clerk: (a) verifies vendor identity and delivery against scheduled PO, (b) directs driver to designated unloading area (cement storage room, lumber yard, or outdoor staging), (c) supervises unloading to ensure proper handling (cement bags not thrown, lumber not dragged, rebar bundles not dropped), (d) for cement: checks bag condition (no torn bags, no hardened cement) | Receiving Clerk | Department Supervisor | 30–60 min |
| 4 | **Quantity verification & quality check**: Receiving Clerk performs quantity count: (a) cement: count bags vs. PO quantity (typically delivered in pallets of 40–50 bags), (b) sand/gravel: measure volume (cubic meters) or weight via scale, (c) lumber: count pieces by dimension and length, verify species and grade, (d) rebar: count bundles and verify diameter/length; quality checks: (e) cement: no lumps or moisture damage (squeeze test), (f) hollow blocks: check for cracks, verify dimensions, (g) rebar: check for excessive rust | Receiving Clerk | Department Supervisor | 20–30 min |
| 5 | **Goods receipt posting**: Receiving Clerk posts goods receipt in ERP via handheld tablet: (a) select PO, (b) enter received quantity per line, (c) note any discrepancies (short shipment, over-shipment, damaged items), (d) capture vendor delivery receipt photo for 3-way match per W7, (e) system updates store inventory in real-time and triggers AP invoice matching | Receiving Clerk | — | 10 min |
| 6 | **Discrepancy resolution**: If quantity discrepancy or quality issue: (a) Receiving Clerk notes on vendor delivery receipt and takes photos, (b) both Receiving Clerk and vendor driver sign annotated delivery receipt, (c) Department Supervisor notified for items >10% variance or quality rejection, (d) for quality rejection: vendor driver takes back rejected items; system records rejected quantity, (e) for quantity variance: post goods receipt for accepted quantity only; AP adjusts PO per actual per W7 | Receiving Clerk / Department Supervisor | Store Manager | 10–15 min |
| 7 | **DSD storage & staging**: Receiving Clerk directs stock associates to place goods in designated storage: (a) cement: indoor storage on pallets (never directly on floor — moisture wicking), stacked max 10 bags high, rotated FIFO (older stock at front), (b) sand/gravel: dumped in designated outdoor bin, (c) hollow blocks: stacked in yard area, (d) rebar: placed in steel racks by diameter; system updates storage location for inventory visibility | Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | 15–30 min |
| 8 | **Monthly DSD vendor performance tracking**: Store Manager compiles monthly DSD vendor scorecard data: (a) on-time delivery rate, (b) quantity accuracy, (c) quality rejection rate, (d) driver cooperation and unloading efficiency; feeds into vendor performance management per W44; flags vendors with chronic issues to Category Manager for sourcing review per W36 | Store Manager | Department Supervisor | 30 min/month |

### System Touchpoints
- DSD delivery scheduling calendar integrated with vendor portal (W1283.1)
- PO management with DSD flag (W2A, W2C)
- Handheld goods receipt processing with photo capture (W1283.5)
- 3-way match for DSD invoices (W7)
- Inventory update with storage location tracking (INV-001)
- Vendor scorecard data feed (W44)
- Weight/volume measurement integration for bulk materials (POS-016, W463)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Unpredictable DSD timing** — vendor DSD trucks often arrive outside scheduled windows due to Philippine traffic, other delivery commitments, or vehicle issues; this disrupts store receiving operations and may conflict with DC delivery schedules
- **Cement moisture damage** — cement is the highest-volume DSD item and extremely moisture-sensitive; bags left in rain or stored on damp floors harden and become unsellable; estimated 1–2% cement shrinkage from moisture damage
- **Weight/volume disputes** — sand and gravel delivered by truckload are measured by volume (cubic meters) or weight; without calibrated scales at store level, disputes arise over actual quantity delivered
- **Heavy lifting injury risk**: cement bags (40 kg), hollow blocks, and rebar require manual handling; high injury risk for receiving staff; compliance with DOLE manual handling limits (max 55 kg for men, per the DOLE OSH Standards as amended by D.O. 252-25 (revised IRR of RA 11058, superseding DO 198-18)) is mandatory per W440
- **Receiving area congestion** — DSD deliveries often arrive during peak store hours when the receiving dock is also used for DC deliveries; scheduling conflicts cause trucks to queue, delaying goods receipt and vendor turnaround

## W1412. Store-Level Receiving Dock Safety Inspection, Material Handling Compliance & Incident Reporting

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Weekly safety inspection schedule; or post-incident investigation; or new employee onboarding to receiving role |
| **Frequency** | Weekly visual inspection; monthly comprehensive inspection; incident-triggered inspection as needed |
| **Volume** | ~200 stores × 52 weekly inspections = ~10,400 inspections/year; ~200 monthly comprehensive inspections/year; ~50–100 incident-triggered inspections/year |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Receiving Clerk, Department Supervisor, Facilities Coordinator (HQ), Safety Officer (regional) |

### Background

Each BuildRight store receives 2–3 trucks per week at the receiving dock, handling merchandise ranging from light hardware items to heavy construction materials (40 kg cement bags, large-format tiles, lumber bundles, appliance boxes). The receiving dock is one of the highest-risk areas in a retail store, with hazards including forklift/pallet jack traffic, heavy lifting, slip/trip hazards from packaging debris, and exposure to outdoor elements (rain, heat) in semi-covered dock areas. DOLE OSH Standards (RA 11058 and its revised IRR D.O. 252-25) require employers to maintain safe workplaces including receiving areas, and BuildRight's incident rate target requires proactive dock safety management.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Weekly visual inspection**: Receiving Clerk walks the receiving dock and backroom area per standardized checklist: (a) floor condition — no standing water, oil, or debris; non-slip matting in good condition; (b) dock leveler/plate — properly secured, no gaps between truck and dock; (c) lighting — adequate illumination (> 200 lux) in receiving area and backroom aisles; (d) MHE condition — pallet jacks and hand trucks operational per W1403; (e) exit routes — unobstructed, clearly marked; (f) PPE availability — safety gloves, steel-toe boots, back support belts available and in good condition; (g) chemical/hazmat receiving area — spill kit accessible, SDS binder present; (h) stacking height — inventory stacked below maximum height (2.4m or 2 pallets high) | Receiving Clerk | Store Manager | 15 min/week |
| 2 | Receiving Clerk enters inspection results in system: Pass / Needs Attention / Hazard — Immediate Action Required; if "Hazard": Receiving Clerk stops receiving operations, restricts access to the hazard area, and immediately notifies Store Manager | Receiving Clerk | — | 5 min |
| 3 | **Monthly comprehensive inspection**: Store Manager conducts detailed inspection with Receiving Clerk: (a) all weekly items plus (b) dock door mechanisms (springs, seals, weather stripping), (c) fire extinguisher accessibility and charge level, (d) electrical panel clearance (1m clearance maintained), (e) pallet rack integrity (no bent uprights, missing pins, or overloaded shelves), (f) waste accumulation (no excessive packaging blocking aisles), (g) receiving dock exterior (drainage clear, no unauthorized access, adequate bollard protection), (h) emergency stop button for dock equipment tested | Store Manager / Receiving Clerk | Regional Manager | 30 min/month |
| 4 | Store Manager documents findings with photographs; creates corrective action items for any "Needs Attention" or "Hazard" items; assigns responsible person and target completion date; system tracks corrective actions to closure | Store Manager | — | 15 min |
| 5 | **Incident reporting**: if any injury, near-miss, or property damage occurs at the receiving dock: (a) Receiving Clerk provides first aid (if trained) and calls Store Manager, (b) Store Manager secures the scene, (c) Store Manager completes incident report in system within 4 hours: date/time, location, person(s) involved, description of incident, immediate cause, contributing factors, photographs, witness statements, (d) if injury requires medical treatment: Store Manager transports employee to nearest clinic/hospital and files DOLE report per W779, (e) if DOLE-reportable: Safety Officer notified for mandatory reporting | Store Manager / Receiving Clerk | Regional Manager | 1–4 hours depending on severity |
| 6 | **Post-incident inspection**: after any receiving dock incident, Store Manager conducts immediate targeted inspection of the specific hazard area; corrects the condition before resuming operations; updates the risk assessment for the receiving dock area | Store Manager | Facilities Coordinator | 30 min |
| 7 | Monthly: Facilities Coordinator aggregates receiving dock safety data across all stores: (a) inspection pass rate by store, (b) corrective action closure rate, (c) incident frequency rate (incidents per 1,000 receiving events), (d) top 5 hazards across the chain; generates receiving dock safety dashboard for VP Store Operations | Facilities Coordinator | VP Store Operations | 2 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Weekly inspection checklist: standardized per dock type (covered dock, open receiving area, yard receiving) with pass/attention/hazard scoring (W1412.1)
- Monthly comprehensive inspection: detailed checklist with photographic evidence upload (W1412.3)
- Corrective action tracking: assignment, target date, closure evidence, overdue alerts (W1412.4)
- Incident reporting form: structured data capture for injury, near-miss, property damage; auto-notifies Regional Manager and Safety Officer (W1412.5)
- Receiving dock safety dashboard: chain-wide pass rate, incident rate, top hazards, corrective action closure rate (W1412.7)
- Integration with W1403 (MHE inspection — equipment safety), W779 (employee injury — workers' compensation), W24.1 (occupational health & safety — overall safety program), W47 (facility maintenance — dock repair), W109 (store receiving — safe receiving procedures), W18 (DSD receiving — vendor truck dock safety)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Dock leveler gap injuries**: the gap between the truck bed and the dock edge is a persistent hazard; workers stepping between truck and dock during unloading can fall or catch limbs; dock leveler maintenance is critical but often deferred
- **Cement bag handling strain**: 40 kg cement bags are manually handled by Receiving Clerks during DSD deliveries; repetitive heavy lifting causes chronic back injuries despite training on proper technique; mechanical aids (cement bag lifters) are not present at all stores
- **Slippery dock floors during rain**: Philippine receiving docks in provincial stores are often semi-covered; rain creates slippery conditions on smooth concrete; non-slip matting and drainage maintenance are ongoing challenges
- **Receiving under time pressure**: trucks arrive with driver wait-time constraints; Receiving Clerks rush to unload, bypassing safe handling procedures (proper stacking height, pallet jack safety) to minimize truck detention time

## W1424. Store-Level DSD Vendor Delivery Performance Monitoring, Scorecard & Compliance Tracking

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Weekly DSD performance review cycle; or monthly vendor scorecard generation; or delivery exception event (late delivery, short shipment, quality rejection) |
| **Frequency** | Weekly exception review; monthly vendor scorecard generation; ongoing per-delivery event logging |
| **Volume** | ~500–600 DSD receipts/month across 200 stores (~3 DSD deliveries/store/month); ~50–80 DSD vendors (cement, lumber, sand, gravel, heavy/bulky items) |
| **Owner** | Supply Planning Manager |
| **Participants** | Supply Planning Manager, Store Manager, Receiving Clerk, Category Manager, Buyer, Vendor Account Manager |

### Background

BuildRight Depot receives ~30% of goods by value through Direct Store Delivery (DSD) — vendors deliver directly to stores rather than through DCs. This is primarily for bulky/heavy items (cement, lumber, sand, gravel) where double-handling through DCs is uneconomical. DSD vendors operate semi-independently: they schedule their own deliveries, use their own trucks, and their drivers interact directly with store receiving staff. This creates a control challenge: (a) DSD deliveries bypass DC quality inspection — the store receiving clerk is the sole quality gate, (b) DSD vendors may deliver late, short-ship, or deliver damaged goods, impacting store availability, (c) DSD vendor performance varies widely (large cement manufacturers are reliable; small local suppliers of sand/gravel may be inconsistent), (d) vendor compliance with delivery windows (per W745 dock scheduling) must be tracked, (e) DSD goods receipt data feeds AP 3-way match per W7 (PO → GR → Invoice). This workflow monitors DSD vendor delivery performance, generates scorecards, and drives vendor compliance improvement.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Per-delivery event logging**: Store Receiving Clerk logs DSD delivery details at goods receipt per W18: (a) delivery vs. PO: (i) on-time (within scheduled delivery window per W745), (ii) late (delivered after window), (iii) early/unannounced (arrived without appointment), (b) quantity accuracy: (i) full delivery (all PO lines received in full), (ii) short shipment (partial delivery), (iii) over-shipment (vendor delivered more than ordered), (c) quality: (i) accepted (no issues), (ii) partial rejection (some items damaged/defective), (iii) full rejection (entire delivery rejected — quality or wrong items), (d) documentation: (i) delivery receipt signed, (ii) truck photos (for damage claims), (iii) discrepancy report filed | Receiving Clerk | Store Manager | 5–10 min/delivery (absorbed in W18) |
| 2 | **Weekly exception review**: Supply Planning Manager reviews weekly DSD exception report: (a) late deliveries: which vendors are consistently late? (threshold: > 20% of deliveries late in the week), (b) short shipments: which vendors are short-shipping? impact on store availability, (c) quality rejections: which vendors have quality issues? what categories are affected? (d) for each exception: Store Manager provides context (vendor communication, local issues) | Supply Planning Manager | VP Supply Chain | 1–2 hours/week |
| 3 | **Monthly vendor scorecard generation**: System generates monthly DSD vendor scorecard: (a) on-time delivery rate (% of deliveries within scheduled window), (b) fill rate (% of ordered quantity delivered), (c) quality acceptance rate (% of deliveries accepted without rejection), (d) invoice accuracy rate (% of DSD invoices matching PO + GR without discrepancy), (e) overall score: weighted average (on-time 40%, fill rate 30%, quality 20%, invoice accuracy 10%), (f) trend: vs. prior month, vs. same month last year, (g) flag: vendors scoring below 80% overall | System / Supply Planning Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2–3 hours/month |
| 4 | **Vendor performance review meeting**: Category Manager and Buyer meet with underperforming DSD vendors (score < 80%): (a) present scorecard with specific delivery exceptions, (b) require vendor corrective action plan: (i) root cause for late deliveries (truck availability, scheduling, distance), (ii) root cause for short shipments (production capacity, driver theft), (iii) root cause for quality issues (packaging, handling, storage), (c) set improvement targets: vendor must achieve 85% score within 60 days or face volume reduction, (d) for chronic underperformers: evaluate alternative DSD vendor per W34 (vendor sourcing) | Category Manager / Buyer | VP Merchandising | 2–3 hours/vendor review |
| 5 | **Annual DSD vendor rationalization**: annually, VP Supply Chain and VP Merchandising review DSD vendor portfolio: (a) total DSD spend by vendor, (b) performance trend (improving/stable/declining), (c) cost comparison: DSD vs. DC-handled (for items that could go either way), (d) vendor consolidation opportunities (reduce from 50–80 DSD vendors to fewer, more reliable partners), (e) terminate underperforming vendors and onboard replacements per W34 | VP Supply Chain / VP Merchandising | COO | 1–2 days/year |

### System Touchpoints
- DSD delivery event logger: per-delivery data capture integrated with W18 goods receipt (W1424.1)
- Weekly DSD exception report: automated aggregation of delivery exceptions by vendor and store (W1424.2)
- Monthly vendor scorecard engine: weighted scoring with trend analysis and benchmarking (W1424.3)
- Vendor corrective action tracker: action plan documentation with follow-up date and resolution tracking (W1424.4)
- Integration with W18 (DSD receiving — data source), W745 (dock scheduling — delivery window compliance), W7 (AP — invoice accuracy), W34 (vendor sourcing — alternative vendors), W109 (store receiving — goods receipt), W1283 (cement DSD coordination)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Small vendor data gaps**: small local DSD vendors (sand/gravel suppliers) may not have EDI or email capability; delivery data must be manually logged by store receiving clerk, introducing data quality risk
- **Store-level inconsistency**: different stores may have different standards for what constitutes "on-time" or "quality rejection" — a lenient receiving clerk may accept deliveries that a strict clerk would reject, distorting vendor scores
- **Vendor retaliation risk**: aggressively enforcing DSD performance on small local suppliers (who may be the only source of sand/gravel in the area) could lead to vendor withdrawal, leaving the store unable to fulfill customer orders; mitigated by CTL-261 (controlled execution — Store Receiving & Replenishment (PA-07.3))
- **Seasonal delivery spikes**: during peak construction season (dry season, Mar–May), DSD volumes increase 30–50% and vendor delivery reliability may drop as their own capacity is strained

### Controls
