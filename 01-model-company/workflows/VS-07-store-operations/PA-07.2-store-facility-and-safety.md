## W47. Store Facility Maintenance & Work Orders

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Equipment failure, scheduled preventive maintenance, safety inspection finding, or store staff report |
| **Frequency** | Continuous; preventive maintenance on fixed schedules per equipment type |
| **Volume** | ~2,000–3,000 maintenance work orders/month across 200 stores + 4 DCs (~10–15 per location per month) |
| **Owner** | Store Manager (store-level); Facilities Coordinator (HQ oversight) |
| **Participants** | Maintenance/Utility staff, Store Manager, Facilities Coordinator, external contractors, Finance |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Issue identified: (a) store staff reports equipment malfunction (POS terminal, paint mixer, lumber saw, forklift, HVAC, lighting, plumbing, roll-up door), (b) scheduled preventive maintenance triggers, (c) safety inspection reveals deficiency | Maintenance / Utility Staff | Store Manager | 5 min (reporting) |
| 2 | Maintenance staff or Store Manager creates work order in system: location, equipment/area, issue description, priority (critical / high / medium / low), photos if applicable | Store Manager / Maintenance | Store Manager | 5 min |
| 3 | System classifies work order: (a) in-store capability — Maintenance/Utility staff can resolve with available tools and parts, (b) requires external contractor (specialized repair: HVAC, electrical, plumbing, elevator/escalator), (c) requires equipment vendor service (POS, forklift, paint mixer under warranty or service contract), (d) requires capex (major repair exceeding PHP 50,000 → route to W21 capex workflow) | System | — | Automated |
| 4a | **In-store resolution**: Maintenance staff performs repair using on-hand parts or petty cash purchase (W25); completes work order with repair description, parts used, and labor time | Maintenance / Utility | Store Manager | Varies (15 min – 4 hours) |
| 4b | **External contractor**: Store Manager or Facilities Coordinator engages pre-approved contractor from approved vendor list; contractor scheduled for site visit; system tracks appointment and SLA | Facilities Coordinator / Store Manager | VP Store Operations | Scheduling: 15 min; repair: varies |
| 4c | **Vendor service call**: Store Manager contacts equipment vendor for warranty or service contract repair; system links to vendor master and warranty records (W33 for warranty process, W21 for asset records) | Store Manager | Facilities Coordinator | 10 min (coordination) |
| 5 | Work order completed: repair verified by Store Manager; work order closed in system with resolution code, actual labor hours, parts cost, and contractor/vendor cost | Store Manager / Maintenance | Store Manager | 10 min |
| 6 | System posts maintenance costs: parts and labor to store maintenance expense GL; contractor invoices routed to AP per W7C (non-PO) or against maintenance contract PO | System | — | Automated |
| 7 | Monthly: Facilities Coordinator reviews maintenance report by location: total cost per store, top issue categories, contractor response time SLA compliance, recurring issues | Facilities Coordinator | VP Store Operations | 2 hours/month |

### Preventive Maintenance Schedule

| Equipment / System | Frequency | Responsible | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| HVAC units | Quarterly filter change; annual service | External contractor | Critical for customer comfort; store sells HVAC — reputation risk if own units fail |
| Fire suppression / extinguishers | Annual inspection per BFP requirement | Certified inspector | Regulatory compliance; non-negotiable |
| Paint mixing station | Monthly cleaning and calibration | Maintenance / Utility | Accuracy affects customer satisfaction |
| Lumber cutting equipment | Weekly blade inspection; monthly sharpening/replace | Maintenance / Utility | Safety-critical; dull blades are injury risk |
| POS terminals and peripherals | Quarterly cleaning and cable check | IT + Maintenance | Cross-reference W48 |
| Roll-up doors / loading docks | Bi-annual servicing | External contractor | Security and receiving operations |
| Electrical panel and generators (if applicable) | Annual inspection | Licensed electrician | Safety compliance |
| Pest control | Monthly treatment | External contractor (pest control vendor) | Health and sanitation |
| Hazardous waste disposal | Per accumulation or quarterly | Licensed DENR-accredited transporter | Regulatory compliance (DENR/EMB) |
| Elevators / escalators (if applicable) | Monthly contractor maintenance; annual DOLE-accredited inspection | Licensed elevator contractor | Regulatory compliance |

### System Touchpoints
- Oracle eAM work order creation with priority classification and photo attachment (W47.2 — fit-gap F5, adopted in-suite)
- Auto-classification of resolution path: in-store, contractor, vendor service, or capex (W47.3)
- Pre-approved contractor vendor list with rate cards and SLA terms (W47.4b)
- Maintenance cost posting to store-level GL (W47.6)
- Preventive maintenance scheduling in Oracle eAM with automated work order generation per equipment calendar (W47 preventive table)
- Maintenance reporting by location, cost, issue category, contractor performance (W47.7)
- Integration with W21 (capex routing for major repairs), W25 (petty cash for small parts), W33 (vendor warranty claims), W39 (asset retirement if equipment beyond repair), W48 (IT equipment maintenance)
- Hazardous waste disposal: paint mixing stations (W5B.3), chemical receiving areas, and lumber treatment areas generate hazardous waste (waste paint, solvents, chemical containers, treated wood offcuts) regulated under DENR DAO 2013-22 (Revised Procedures and Standards for the Management of Hazardous Wastes under RA 6969; superseding the DAO 2004-36 manual); Facilities Coordinator maintains a hazardous waste storage area at each store and DC — clearly labeled, ventilated, and segregated from general waste; waste accumulates in approved containers with proper labeling (waste type, date of first accumulation, hazard class); when accumulation reaches threshold (per DENR guidelines or when the 90-day on-site accumulation limit approaches), Facilities Coordinator arranges pickup by DENR-accredited hazardous waste transporter; transporter provides manifests (manifest system per DENR: 6-copy form tracking waste from generator to transporter to treater/disposer); system records hazardous waste disposal events: waste type, quantity, transporter, manifest number, disposal facility, date; manifest copies scanned and attached to disposal record; quarterly: Facilities Coordinator generates hazardous waste disposal report per location for DENR compliance; annual: DENR hazardous waste generator registration renewal per location (separate from W54 LGU business permit but tracked in the same Regulatory Officer calendar); Facilities Coordinator ensures all locations with paint mixing or chemical operations are registered as hazardous waste generators with DENR-EMB Regional Office; violations carry DENR administrative fines of PHP 10,000–50,000 per day of violation under RA 6969 (plus criminal penalties of imprisonment and a PHP 600–4,000 fine for grave violations)

### Pain Points / Risks
- Contractor availability in provincial/remote stores — limited pool of licensed electricians and HVAC technicians in Tier 3 cities, leading to extended repair times for critical equipment (HVAC, roll-up doors)
- Capex threshold disputes — repairs initially classified as maintenance may exceed PHP 50,000 upon completion, requiring retroactive capex approval (W21) and causing budget classification issues
- Hazardous waste compliance exposure — failure to maintain DENR-accredited hazardous waste disposal documentation per location carries DENR administrative fines of PHP 10,000–50,000 per day of violation under RA 6969; paint mixing stations at all 200 stores generate regulated waste
- Preventive maintenance schedule non-compliance during peak seasons (Q4 holiday, summer construction boom) when store staff prioritize selling over maintenance tasks

- System auto-create of "creates work order in system" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-route of "route to W21 capex workflow" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-260 (ensure controlled execution — Store Facility & Safety (PA-07.2).)
- operational: Store Manager (store-level); Facilities Coordinator (HQ oversight) review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
~10–15 work orders per store per month; each takes 5–15 min to create/classify + variable repair time (15 min to 4 hours depending on severity). Facilities Coordinator spends ~4 hours/month on reporting and contractor management across assigned stores. Annual hazardous waste compliance adds ~2–4 hours per location per quarter.

### Staffing Implication
- **1 Maintenance/Utility per store** (already in model): handles ~10–15 work orders/month including routine preventive tasks. Viable for standard repairs and routine maintenance; relies on external contractors for specialized work.
- **1 Facilities Coordinator** (HQ, within Store Ops team): manages contractor relationships, preventive maintenance calendar compliance, and maintenance cost analysis across 200 stores. This role is now part of the baseline Store Operations team.
- **External contractor network**: each store should have 3–5 pre-approved contractors (electrician, plumber, HVAC technician, general handyman) on retainer or call-out basis. Facilities Coordinator manages the approved list centrally.

- Critical issue response delay if Store Manager is unreachable after hours — the notification system depends on the Store Manager receiving and acting on alerts; if they are asleep or in a dead-signal area, response is delayed until the Facilities Coordinator backup is contacted

## W330. In-Store Emergency Response Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Any emergency event occurring within the store premises that threatens the safety of customers, staff, or the public — including fire, medical emergency, structural failure (e.g., racking collapse, roof leak), bomb threat, civil disturbance, chemical spill (in-store), or severe weather while customers are present |
| **Frequency** | Rare; hopefully < 1 actual emergency per store per year; drills conducted quarterly per W158 |
| **Volume** | 200 stores × ~467 customers/day + 29 staff/store = ~99,000+ people on premises per operating day chain-wide |
| **Owner** | Store Manager (immediate response); Facilities Coordinator (post-incident coordination) |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, all store staff, Security Guard, Facilities Coordinator, Loss Prevention Officer, HR, Legal, local emergency services (PNP, BFP, EMS) |

### Background

W140 covers occupational health and safety incidents affecting employees (worker injury, workplace accident). W49 covers natural disaster/typhoon business continuity at the organizational level. Neither addresses in-store emergencies affecting a mixed population of customers and employees during business hours. BuildRight stores are large-format buildings (8,000–15,000 sqm) containing hazardous materials (paint, solvents, chemicals, lumber), heavy equipment displays, and outdoor yard areas. A structured emergency response protocol is essential for legal compliance (RA 11058, DOLE DO 252-25 (revised IRR of RA 11058, superseding DO 198-18)) and duty of care to customers.

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Panic and crowd management** — 467+ daily customers in a large-format store during an emergency creates stampede risk, especially at limited exit points; elderly and disabled customers require assistance that may not be immediately available; mitigated by the Store Manager (immediate response) and Facilities Coordinator (post-incident coordination) review and approval gate

## W470. Store-Level Rotational Brownout / Power Outage Management Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Advance notification from electric utility of scheduled rotational brownout; or sudden unscheduled power outage |
| **Frequency** | Mindanao (60 stores): ~2–4 events/month per store during dry season (March–May); Visayas (40 stores): ~1–2 events/month; Luzon (100 stores): occasional, primarily during typhoon season; total chain: ~800–1,500 store-outage events/year (per the per-store rates above) |
| **Volume** | 1–30 stores affected per event (rotational brownouts affect specific feeder areas); typical duration: 2–8 hours |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Maintenance / Utility, Cashier, Department Supervisor, Security Guard |

### Background

Mindanao — where BuildRight has **60 stores** and its corporate HQ — experiences regular rotational brownouts due to power generation deficits, especially during the dry season (March–May) when hydroelectric output drops. These are **predictable, recurring operational events** distinct from typhoon-related disasters (W49) or one-time opening delays (W5E). Some electric cooperatives publish rotational schedules; others are unscheduled. Luzon and Visayas stores experience brownouts less frequently but are not immune, especially during typhoon season when grid infrastructure is damaged.

This workflow covers the full lifecycle of managing a **planned or routine** power outage at a store — from preparation through reduced-operations mode to power restoration and reconciliation. It differs from W330 (In-Store Emergency Response) which covers fire, structural, and medical emergencies, and from W5G (Offline POS Recovery) which covers the POS-specific technical recovery.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Brownout schedule monitoring**: Maintenance / Utility staff monitors electric utility advisories (social media, SMS alerts, utility website) for scheduled rotational brownouts in the store's area; for utilities that publish schedules, Maintenance receives schedule weekly and shares with Store Manager; for unscheduled outages, trigger is the power loss itself (step 5) | Maintenance / Utility | Store Manager | 15 min/week during brownout season |
| 2 | **Pre-outage preparation** (for scheduled brownouts): (a) Store Manager confirms generator fuel level ≥ 75%; if below, arranges emergency fuel delivery (maintain relationship with local fuel supplier); (b) Maintenance verifies generator startup (test run for 5 minutes if last test > 2 weeks); (c) Maintenance confirms UPS battery charge for POS terminals (each terminal has ~15 min battery backup for graceful shutdown); (d) Store Manager briefs staff on shift: approximate outage window, which operations will be reduced | Maintenance / Store Manager | Store Manager | 30 min/prep |
| 3 | **Customer pre-notification** (for scheduled brownouts): (a) Store Manager posts "Scheduled Power Maintenance" notice at store entrance; (b) reduced lighting areas marked; (c) PA announcement at start of outage: "We are experiencing a temporary power interruption. Our store remains open. Some areas may have reduced lighting. Thank you for your patience." | Store Manager | — | 15 min |
| 4 | **Load priority plan execution**: Store Manager activates pre-defined load priority for generator power: **Priority 1 (always powered)**: (a) 2 of 3 POS terminals (1 terminal shut down to reduce load), (b) security system and CCTV (non-negotiable — W71 requirement), (c) emergency lighting (fire exits, main aisles), (d) fire alarm and suppression monitoring; **Priority 2 (powered if generator capacity allows)**: (e) customer-facing display lighting (main aisles only — gallery and appliance sections deprioritized), (f) paint mixing station (high customer demand), (g) receiving dock lighting (if trucks scheduled); **Priority 3 (not powered during outage)**: (h) backroom/stockroom lighting (use handheld flashlights), (i) office area (computers shut down), (j) air conditioning (customer discomfort is tolerable for 2–4 hours; staff use portable fans), (k) outdoor yard lighting (unless customer traffic is heavy), (l) non-essential signage | Maintenance / Store Manager | Store Manager | 10 min |
| 5 | **Unscheduled outage response**: When power drops without warning: (a) UPS provides ~15 min battery for POS terminals — Cashier completes current transaction and logs out; (b) Maintenance starts generator (target: within 3 minutes of outage); (c) Store Manager makes PA announcement; (d) Security Guard activates handheld flashlight protocol for dark areas; (e) Department Supervisors assist customers in darker sections (tiles gallery, plumbing aisles) toward better-lit areas; (f) if generator fails to start within 10 minutes, escalate to W5E (Store Opening Delay Procedure) — consider partial store closure | Maintenance / Cashier / Store Manager | Store Manager | 5–10 min |
| 6 | **Sensitive goods protection**: (a) **Paint and chemicals**: Maintenance verifies ventilation continues (exhaust fans may be on generator backup); if ambient temperature in paint mixing area exceeds 35°C, temporarily suspend paint mixing operations; (b) **Appliances**: Demo units on display powered down to prevent voltage surge damage; (c) **Refrigerated items** (if any — limited in hardware retail): check temperature log; if outage exceeds 4 hours, disposition per food safety guidelines; (d) **Lumber yard**: no special action required — outdoor inventory is not power-dependent | Maintenance / Department Supervisor | Store Manager | 15 min |
| 7 | **Extended outage escalation** (beyond 4 hours): (a) Store Manager contacts Regional Manager: report generator fuel status, estimated remaining runtime, store revenue impact; (b) if generator fuel is running low (< 25%): Store Manager arranges emergency fuel delivery or coordinates with neighboring store for fuel sharing; (c) if outage will exceed generator capacity (> 6–8 hours): Store Manager considers early closure per abbreviated W5F procedure; cash secured in safe, POS shut down, building secured; (d) Regional Manager reports extended outage stores to VP Store Operations for pattern analysis | Store Manager / Regional Manager | VP Store Operations | 15 min |
| 8 | **Power restoration protocol**: When utility power is restored: (a) Maintenance switches from generator to utility power (do NOT run both simultaneously — risk of backfeed); (b) generator cooldown period (5 min idle before shutdown per manufacturer spec); (c) IT systems restart sequence: (1) network switch and WiFi access point, (2) POS terminals boot and reconnect, (3) POS sync with central server — validates offline transaction queue, (4) handheld devices reconnect, (5) CCTV system verifies recording resumed; (d) Department Supervisors verify lighting and displays restored; (e) Maintenance logs outage event: start time, end time, duration, generator runtime, fuel consumed, any issues | Maintenance / IT (via phone support) | Store Manager | 15–30 min |
| 9 | **Post-outage reconciliation**: (a) POS system processes offline transaction queue (W5G offline recovery procedure); (b) Store Manager reviews offline transaction report for completeness; (c) Finance: no manual journal entry required if POS sync completes successfully; (d) Maintenance logs generator runtime and fuel consumption for facility cost tracking (W111 utility management); (e) if any data discrepancies from offline POS period: Store Manager flags to IT helpdesk (W48) for investigation | Store Manager / Maintenance | Store Manager | 15 min |
| 10 | **Monthly pattern reporting**: Regional Manager aggregates outage reports from all stores in region: (a) total outage hours per store per month, (b) generator fuel cost per store per month, (c) revenue impact estimate (compare sales during outage hours to same day/hour historical average); (d) stores with chronic outage issues (> 8 hours/month) flagged for capex investment (permanent generator, solar panels per W173, or redundant utility feed); (e) report submitted to VP Store Operations monthly | Regional Manager | VP Store Operations | 1 hour/month |

### System Touchpoints
- POS offline capability and transaction queue (W5G) — transactions during outage stored locally and synced upon reconnection (W470.8c, W470.9a)
- Generator runtime and fuel consumption tracking via Oracle eAM work orders (W47, fit-gap F5) (W470.8e)
- Utility cost tracking integration with W111 (Store Energy & Utility Consumption Management) for generator fuel as utility offset (W470.9d)
- Store closure/reopening status tracking per W5F (abbreviated closure for extended outages) (W470.7c)
- IT helpdesk (W48) for post-outage POS reconciliation issues (W470.9e)
- POS terminal count reduction management — 2 of 3 terminals active during outage; queue management (W470.4a)
- Integration with W5E (opening delay — if outage occurs before opening), W5G (offline POS — technical recovery), W47 (facility maintenance — generator PM), W173 (solar monitoring — solar continues during daytime outages), W330 (in-store emergency — if outage causes a safety incident), W49 (typhoon BC — extended disaster-related outages handled by W49, not this workflow)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Generator maintenance neglect**: Generators at 200 stores require quarterly PM (oil change, filter replacement, load bank testing per W188); if PM is skipped, generators fail to start during outages — the worst possible time; Store Manager must treat generator PM as a W47 priority, not a discretionary expense
- **Mindanao dry season cumulative impact**: During March–May, a single Mindanao store may experience 8–12 brownout days/month (2–4 hours each); cumulative lost revenue and generator fuel cost (~PHP 500–1,000/hour for a 100kVA generator) can reach PHP 50,000–100,000/store/month — PHP 3–6M/month across 60 Mindanao stores
- **Fuel supply chain during widespread outages**: During regional brownouts, all businesses in the area run generators simultaneously; local fuel stations may be unable to meet surge demand or may also be affected by the outage (no electric pumps); Store Managers should maintain minimum 75% fuel level at all times during brownout season
- **POS queue management with reduced terminals**: Reducing from 3 to 2 POS terminals during outage increases average checkout time by ~50%; during peak hours (weekends, sale events), customer queues may become unacceptable — Store Manager may authorize mobile POS (W206) as queue-busting during outage periods
- **Customer safety in reduced lighting**: Darker aisles (tiles gallery, plumbing, electrical) increase slip/trip/fall risk; if a customer injury occurs during a brownout, liability is heightened — W285 (public liability claims) risk increases


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-schedule of "Brownout schedule monitoring" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-test of "test run for 5 minutes if last test > 2 weeks" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-post of "posts" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-260 (ensure controlled execution — Store Facility & Safety (PA-07.2).)
- operational: Store Manager review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Staffing Implication
- **Maintenance / Utility** (1 per store): Absorbs generator operation, fuel management, and utility monitoring. Brownout response is a core duty during dry season. No incremental headcount.
- **Store Manager**: Decision-making on closure, load priority, and escalation. ~30 min/event. Absorbed into existing duties.
- **Regional Manager**: Monthly pattern reporting. ~1 hour/month. Absorbed into existing reporting duties.

### Time Estimate
~2–2.3 hours of store-team effort per outage event (pre-outage preparation 30 min + customer notification 15 min + load-priority execution 10 min + unscheduled-outage response 5–10 min + sensitive-goods protection 15 min + extended-outage escalation 15 min + restoration protocol 15–30 min + post-outage reconciliation 15 min); at ~800–1,500 store-outage events/year (per the Frequency) ≈ 1,600–3,450 hours/year chain-wide, plus brownout-schedule monitoring 15 min/week during season and monthly pattern reporting 1 hour/month.

## W471. Store-Level Security Incident & Police/Barangay Reporting Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Security incident at a store that requires law enforcement involvement or formal documentation: shoplifting apprehension, break-in/burglary, vandalism, customer altercation, employee theft discovery, vehicle break-in in parking area, armed robbery |
| **Frequency** | ~2–5 incidents per store per year requiring formal reporting; ~400–1,000 total incidents/year chain-wide |
| **Volume** | 1 incident per event; some stores in high-crime areas may have 1–2 incidents/month |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Security Guard, LPO, Regional Manager, Facilities Coordinator, HR, Legal, Finance (Insurance) |

### Background

With 200 stores across the Philippines, security incidents requiring police or barangay documentation are a daily operational reality across the chain. W71 covers physical security infrastructure (alarms, CCTV, access control) and W37 covers loss prevention exception reporting and investigation. However, neither covers the post-incident workflow of **documenting and filing reports with Philippine law enforcement** — the Philippine National Police (PNP) and/or the barangay. This is a distinct process because: (a) Philippine law requires police reports for insurance claims and criminal prosecution; (b) barangay blotter entries are required for community-level disputes; (c) DTI may request police reports during consumer complaint investigations (W469); (d) employee discipline cases involving theft require police documentation for due process.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Incident classification**: Store Manager or Security Guard classifies the incident on first report: **Category A — Criminal (requires police)**: (a) armed robbery, (b) burglary/break-in (after hours), (c) shoplifting with apprehension and suspect detention, (d) employee theft discovered (per W37 investigation), (e) assault or violent customer altercation, (f) vehicle break-in in parking lot with property damage; **Category B — Community dispute (barangay)**: (a) non-violent customer altercation, (b) minor property damage dispute, (c) noise or nuisance complaint from neighboring establishment; **Category C — Internal documentation only**: (a) attempted shoplifting (suspect fled, not apprehended), (b) minor vandalism (< PHP 5,000 damage), (c) false alarm activation | Store Manager / Security Guard | Store Manager | 5 min |
| 2 | **Immediate response**: (a) **Category A**: Security Guard secures the scene; if suspect detained, held in store office with CCTV coverage — do not use physical restraint beyond minimum necessary; Store Manager contacts PNP station immediately; if violence or weapon involved, staff safety is priority — do not confront; (b) **Category B**: Security Guard separates parties; Store Manager attempts de-escalation; if parties cannot resolve, proceed to barangay reporting; (c) **Category C**: Store Manager documents incident for internal records; LPO includes in daily exception report (W37) | Security Guard / Store Manager | Store Manager | 5–15 min |
| 3 | **Evidence preservation**: (a) Security Guard ensures CCTV recording of the incident is preserved — marks footage with timestamp, camera ID, and incident description; (b) if physical evidence exists (stolen merchandise recovered, damaged property, weapon), photographed in place before moved; (c) Store Manager identifies and separates witnesses (staff and customers); (d) for employee theft cases: do not tip off the employee until LPO is consulted per W37 investigation protocol; (e) all physical evidence tagged and stored in secure location (store manager's office) | Security Guard / Store Manager | Store Manager | 15–30 min |
| 4 | **Police report filing (Category A)**: (a) Store Manager or designated Security Guard goes to the nearest PNP station to file an incident report (commonly called "blotter entry" at PNP); (b) bring: Store Manager's ID, store business permit (W54), CCTV footage on USB (selected clips only — do not provide full recording without formal request), recovered merchandise/evidence (if applicable), list of witnesses with contact information; (c) PNP officer records the incident in the police blotter — Store Manager requests a copy of the blotter entry (signed and stamped by investigating officer); (d) if suspect is detained: PNP takes custody of suspect and processes per Philippine criminal procedure; BuildRight staff should not detain suspects beyond reasonable time (~30 min max before PNP arrival) | Store Manager / Security Guard | Store Manager | 1–2 hours |
| 5 | **Barangay blotter entry (Category B)**: (a) Store Manager or Security Guard goes to the barangay hall to file a blotter entry for community disputes; (b) barangay tanod records the incident; Store Manager requests a certified copy of the blotter entry; (c) barangay may schedule a conciliation meeting (Katarungang Pambarangay) — mandatory first step for minor disputes before court filing; (d) Store Manager attends conciliation meeting with documentation; settlement recorded by barangay | Store Manager / Security Guard | Store Manager | 30 min–1 hour |
| 6 | **Insurance claim initiation** (if property damage, theft, or robbery): (a) for incidents with estimated loss > PHP 10,000: Store Manager notifies Facilities Coordinator and Finance (Treasury) within 24 hours; (b) Finance initiates insurance claim per W59 (Insurance Policy Lifecycle Management) — requires police report copy, photos, inventory loss valuation, and CCTV footage; (c) for robbery or burglary: insurance adjuster may visit the store for inspection — Store Manager coordinates access and preserves the scene until adjuster arrives (if practical) | Store Manager / Finance | Regional Manager | 30 min + insurance process |
| 7 | **LP investigation handoff**: Store Manager provides all incident documentation to LPO within 24 hours: (a) police/barangay blotter copy, (b) CCTV footage reference (camera ID and timestamp range), (c) witness contact list, (d) physical evidence inventory, (e) incident narrative; LPO incorporates into loss prevention investigation per W37 and enters into investigation case management (W37.10) | Store Manager | LPO / Internal Audit | 15 min |
| 8 | **Employee-related incidents** (if employee involvement): (a) for employee theft or fraud: HR is notified per W37 investigation protocol; employee placed on preventive suspension per Philippine Labor Code (due process); (b) for employee injury during incident: HR initiates incident report per W140 (OHS Incident Management) and coordinates with SSS for emergency medical benefit; (c) for employee as witness: HR documents witness statement; employee's shift is adjusted for police/barangay attendance; (d) all employee-related actions comply with DOLE due process requirements — consult HR before any disciplinary action | Store Manager / HR | HR Head | 30 min |
| 9 | **Incident closure and reporting**: (a) Store Manager closes incident in store incident register with: final classification, police/barangay report reference number, evidence disposition, estimated loss value, insurance claim status (if applicable), employee action (if applicable); (b) Monthly: Store Manager includes security incident summary in monthly store performance review (W67) — count by category, loss value, police/barangay report references; (c) Quarterly: Regional Manager aggregates security incident data across stores; identifies high-incident locations for LP intervention (increased patrols per W171, CCTV upgrades per W207, guard force changes); (d) Annual: LPO includes security incident trends in annual shrinkage report (W37.7) | Store Manager / Regional Manager | VP Store Operations / Internal Audit | 15 min/case + monthly aggregation |

## W497. PWD Accessibility Compliance & Store Facilities Audit

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual store facilities audit cycle; new store opening (W16); store renovation (W96); customer PWD complaint; DOLE/NCDA inspection advisory |
| **Frequency** | Annual (comprehensive audit); event-driven (complaint-triggered or new store) |
| **Volume** | 200 stores; annual audit of all locations; event-driven audits as needed |
| **Owner** | Store Operations — Facilities Standards Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Engineering & Construction (W223), VP Store Operations, VP Legal, NCDA (National Council on Disability Affairs) |

### Background

Republic Act 7277 (Magna Carta for Disabled Persons) and its implementing rules, Batas Pambansa 344 (Accessibility Law), and the National Building Code (PD 1096) require Philippine commercial establishments to provide barrier-free accessibility for persons with disabilities (PWDs). With 200 stores across the Philippines, BuildRight must ensure: (a) accessible parking spaces (nearest to entrance, wider stalls); (b) ramps at store entrances (gradient not exceeding 1:12); (c) accessible restrooms (grab bars, wider doors, lower fixtures); (d) accessible checkout counters (at least one lowered counter per store); (e) clear signage with tactile/Braille elements where required; (f) unobstructed pathways through merchandise aisles (minimum 1.2m clearance); (g) elevator or lift access where stores have multiple levels. Additionally, RA 10754 (expanded PWD benefits) and RA 9994 (Senior Citizens Act) require stores to provide discount privileges (covered by W170 and W432), but the physical accessibility of the store is a prerequisite. This workflow covers the accessibility compliance audit and remediation lifecycle. W47 covers general facility maintenance and W96 covers store renovation, but neither specifically addresses PWD accessibility compliance.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Accessibility Standards Checklist Preparation**: Facilities Standards Manager maintains the PWD Accessibility Standards Checklist aligned with BP 344 and RA 7277 implementing rules: (a) **Parking**: designated PWD spaces per lot size, signage, pavement markings, ramp from parking to entrance; (b) **Entrance**: ramp gradient (max 1:12), handrails, level threshold, automatic or easy-open doors; (c) **Pathways**: minimum 1.2m aisle clearance throughout store, no protruding obstacles above 0.7m height, slip-resistant flooring; (d) **Checkout**: at least one lowered counter (max 0.8m height), accessible queue lane; (e) **Restroom**: at least one accessible toilet (wider door, grab bars, lowered sink, emergency call button); (f) **Signage**: PWD-accessible route signage, tactile floor indicators for blind customers at key decision points; (g) **Service counter**: at least one lowered section at customer service counter; (h) **Emergency egress**: accessible evacuation route, visual fire alarms (for hearing-impaired), designated assembly point accessible by ramp | Facilities Standards Mgr | VP Store Operations | 2 days/annual update |
| 2 | **Annual Store Accessibility Audit**: Facilities Standards Manager conducts annual audit of all 200 stores (can be delegated to Regional Managers with standardized checklist): (a) physical inspection of each store against checklist; (b) photograph and document non-compliance items; (c) rate each store: **Compliant** (all mandatory items met), **Minor Non-Compliance** (remediable within 30 days, no immediate safety risk), **Major Non-Compliance** (requires capital improvement, potential safety risk, or regulatory citation risk); (d) compile audit results into Store Accessibility Compliance Dashboard | Facilities Standards Mgr / Regional Manager | VP Store Operations | 2–3 hours/store × 200 stores = ~400–600 hours total (spread across team) |

- **Customer discrimination liability**: Failure to provide PWD accessibility can result in discrimination complaints under RA 7277, with penalties including fines, imprisonment of responsible officers (rare but possible), and mandatory corrective action orders

W140 covers occupational health and safety (OHS) incidents that are DOLE-reportable and employee-focused. W330 covers emergency response for life-threatening situations (fire, earthquake, bomb threat). W285 covers public liability claims management. W501 covers first aid and medical emergencies. However, there is no workflow covering the daily non-emergency incident and hazard reporting that happens routinely across 200 stores — minor customer slip-and-fall without injury, product spill on aisle, tripping hazard from loose tiles, parking lot pothole, broken display fixture, water leak from ceiling.

These incidents are not emergencies but must be documented for liability protection, pattern analysis, and preventive maintenance. Philippine Consumer Act (RA 7394) and civil liability under the Civil Code require documentation of premises-related incidents. Without structured documentation, repeat hazards go unaddressed, minor incidents escalate into liability claims, and patterns (e.g., same store has 5 slip-and-fall events in rainy season) are not detected for corrective action.

For a hardware retailer with heavy merchandise, construction materials in yard areas, and high customer foot traffic including children accompanying parents, the risk of non-emergency incidents is elevated compared to typical retail. Loose nails, spilled cement dust, wet floors from garden hose area overspray, and tripping hazards from pallet jacks in aisles are daily occurrences that need systematic capture and remediation.

## W1003. Store-Level Paint Mixing Station Daily Cleaning, Calibration & Waste Disposal

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Daily scheduled maintenance (morning before store opening) and end-of-day close |
| **Frequency** | Daily (2 cycles per day: pre-opening and post-closing) × 200 stores |
| **Volume** | 1 paint mixing station per store |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Paint & Finishes) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Paint Specialist), Maintenance/Utility Staff, Environmental Compliance Officer (regional) |

### Background

BuildRight Depot operates a custom paint mixing station in every store (Section 3.1 per-store layout: Paint Mixing Station). Each station contains a computerized color-matching spectrophotometer, tint dispensing machine with 12–16 colorant canisters, paint shaker, sample drying cabinet, and associated plumbing/ventilation. Colorant residue builds up in dispensing nozzles and trays after each day's mixing operations (~40–80 custom paint orders per store per day). If not cleaned daily, dried colorant causes inaccurate tint dispensing (leading to customer color-mismatch complaints), nozzle blockages (requiring costly service calls), and cross-contamination between color batches. The spectrophotometer requires daily white-tile calibration to maintain color accuracy within ΔE < 1.0 (imperceptible color difference). Waste paint and colorant rinse water are classified as hazardous waste under DENR DAO 2013-22 (Revised Procedures and Standards for the Management of Hazardous Wastes under RA 6969) and must be collected in approved containers, stored in the store's hazmat area per W236, and collected by DENR-accredited transporter per scheduled pickup. Failure to maintain the mixing station results in customer complaints, product returns, expensive equipment repair (PHP 15,000–30,000 per service call), and potential DENR citation for improper hazardous waste handling.

## W1005. Store-Level Shelf Stock Rotation & Paint/Chemical Expiry Monitoring

| Field | Detail |

| **Trigger** | Daily scheduled task; also triggered by new batch receipt and product expiry alert |

| **Volume** | ~800–1,200 SKUs monitored per store (paint, chemical, adhesive, consumable categories) |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Paint & Finishes) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Stock Associate rotation), Category Manager (expiry policy) |

### Background

BuildRight Depot carries ~2,800 paint & finishes SKUs, plus ~1,750 adhesives, sealants, solvents, and chemical products across the store network. Unlike hardware items (nails, screws, tools) that have indefinite shelf life, paint and chemical products have finite shelf lives: water-based paint (12–24 months unopened), solvent-based paint (24–36 months), epoxy adhesive (12–18 months), contact cement (12 months), silicone sealant (9–18 months), and PVC solvent cement (12 months). Philippine warehouse-style stores with open ventilation and high ambient temperatures (30–38°C in summer) accelerate product degradation. Selling expired or degraded paint/adhesives leads to customer project failures (paint peeling, adhesive failure, sealant cracking), costly returns, brand damage, and potential consumer safety liability under RA 7394 (Consumer Act). The FIFO (First-In, First-Out) rotation must be rigorously enforced for these categories. This workflow also feeds into markdown optimization (W93, W737) for approaching-expiry products and SLOB provisioning (W220) for expired stock.

## W1008. Store-Level Power Tool Battery Charging Station Safety & Maintenance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Daily scheduled check (morning before store opening) and ongoing monitoring during store hours |
| **Frequency** | Daily × 200 stores; continuous monitoring during store hours |
| **Volume** | 1 charging station per store; ~20–40 batteries charging at any time |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Tools & Hardware) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Power Tool Specialist), Maintenance/Utility Staff |

### Background

BuildRight Depot stores maintain a battery charging station for power tool demonstration units (W1007), cordless display models, and customer-facing battery test/kiosk stations. Each station contains 4–8 multi-bay chargers supporting lithium-ion (Li-ion) and nickel-cadmium (NiCd) batteries from major brands (DeWalt, Makita, Bosch, Milwaukee). Lithium-ion battery fires are a growing safety concern in retail environments — a thermal runaway event in a Li-ion battery can reach 700°C and release toxic hydrogen fluoride gas. Philippine retail stores with limited fire suppression systems and high ambient temperatures (30–38°C) face elevated risk. BFP (Bureau of Fire Protection) Fire Safety Code (RA 9514) requires proper electrical safety measures for battery charging operations. This workflow ensures daily safety checks, temperature monitoring, proper ventilation, and emergency procedures are in place to prevent battery-related fire incidents and ensure BFP compliance.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pre-Opening Charging Station Inspection**: Sales Associate conducts daily check before store opening: (a) visual inspection of all charging bays — no cracked, swollen, or deformed batteries; (b) verify ventilation fan is operational (temperature-sensitive sticker on station showing < 40°C); (c) check charging bay contacts are clean and free of debris; (d) verify fire extinguisher (ABC dry chemical) is present within 3 meters, unobstructed, and inspection current (monthly tag per W758); (e) verify charging station is plugged into surge-protected outlet with correct amperage; (f) log inspection pass/fail in system; if any safety issue found, station powered down until resolved | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 5–8 min |

| 3 | **Continuous Temperature Monitoring**: During store hours: (a) charging station temperature sensor transmits continuous readings to store monitoring dashboard; (b) amber alert at > 40°C — Sales Associate reduces charging load (remove 2–3 batteries) and checks ventilation; (c) red alert at > 50°C — immediate station shutdown, all batteries removed, fire extinguisher on standby, Department Supervisor notified; (d) if any battery shows swelling, hissing, or smoke — activate fire emergency protocol per W330, evacuate immediate area, use fire extinguisher; (e) all temperature alerts logged in system for fire safety compliance records per W758, W806 | System / Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | Continuous (automated) |
| 4 | **Monthly Deep Maintenance & Battery Inventory**: Monthly deep maintenance: (a) power down entire charging station; (b) clean all charging contacts with isopropyl alcohol; (c) inspect charging cables for fraying or damage; (d) test all batteries with brand-specific diagnostic tool (charge capacity, internal resistance); (e) batteries below 70% of rated capacity removed from demo use and disposed per W82 (Li-ion batteries are hazardous waste per DENR); (f) replacement batteries ordered per procurement process; (g) charging station electrical inspection by licensed electrician (annual requirement per BFP); (h) update battery inventory list with current health status per battery | Sales Associate / Maintenance Staff | Department Supervisor | 30–45 min/month |

### Background

Each BuildRight Depot store has 1–2 loading docks at the receiving area where inbound deliveries (DC replenishment trucks, DSD vendor trucks) and outbound customer deliveries (bulky item delivery) are loaded and unloaded. Loading dock equipment includes: hydraulic or mechanical dock levelers (bridge the gap between truck bed and dock floor), dock seals/shelters (weatherproof the truck-to-building connection), dock bumpers (protect building wall from truck impact), dock lights (illuminate truck interior for safe unloading), and overhead doors or rolling gates. ## W1023. Store-Level Post-Typhoon Damage Assessment, Cleanup & Rapid Reopening

### Background

The Philippines experiences an average of 20 tropical cyclones per year, with 8–9 making landfall. BuildRight Depot's 200 stores span Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao — all typhoon-prone regions. A single major typhoon (Signal 3+) can simultaneously affect 30–60 stores, causing roof damage, flooding, power outages, structural compromise to outdoor lumber yards, and inventory loss (especially for cement, adhesives, electrical supplies, and other water-sensitive merchandise). After PAGASA lowers the storm signal, each affected store must execute a structured damage assessment and cleanup before reopening to ensure: (a) employee and customer safety (structural integrity, electrical hazard, standing water); (b) accurate inventory damage quantification for insurance claims (W858) and shrinkage accounting (W92); (c) rapid reopening to capture post-disaster demand surge for plywood, nails, tarps, roofing materials, and waterproofing products — historically a 300–500% demand spike in the 72 hours after a typhoon. Stores that reopen within 24 hours of signal lift capture disproportionate market share during the recovery rebuild period. This workflow is distinct from W576 (preparedness) and W848 (emergency protocol during the event); it governs the post-event recovery phase.

### Steps

## W1025. Store-Level Shelving, Racking & Display Fixture Safety Inspection & Maintenance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly scheduled inspection; also triggered by visible damage, after seismic event, or after forklift/vehicle impact |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly inspection × 200 stores = 800 inspections/year; ad-hoc for incidents |
| **Volume** | ~3,000–5,000 shelving units per store (including gondola end-caps, wall shelving, pallet racking in backroom, outdoor yard racks) |
| **Owner** | Assistant Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Maintenance/Utility Staff, Department Supervisors, Safety Committee Representative, Vendor Service Technician (for specialty fixtures) |

### Background

BuildRight Depot stores use heavy-duty gondola shelving (retail floor), pallet racking (backroom and receiving area), cantilever racks (lumber yard for long materials like pipes, lumber, and steel bars), and specialized display fixtures (appliance displays, tile gallery racks, tool walls). A single BuildRight store with 8,000–15,000 sqm of selling area contains approximately 3,000–5,000 shelving units and fixtures, each supporting loads of 50–500 kg. Philippine building conditions — high humidity (70–90% year-round), salt air in coastal locations, and seismic activity (Philippines sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire with frequent earthquakes) — accelerate corrosion, bolt loosening, and structural fatigue. A shelving collapse in a retail environment risks employee injury, customer injury, merchandise damage, and legal liability. The OSH Standards (materials-handling and storage rules, as amended by DO 252-25) and the National Building Code (PD 1096) require employers to maintain safe structural conditions (the construction-industry DO 13-98 does not apply to store operations per W1015). This workflow ensures systematic inspection and maintenance of all shelving and racking to prevent structural failure.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Quarterly Visual Inspection**: Maintenance/Utility Staff and Department Supervisors conduct systematic walkthrough: (a) **Gondola shelving (retail floor)**: check upright posts for leaning or buckling, shelf clips/bolts for tightness, base plates secure on floor, shelf boards for sagging or cracking, end-cap stability; (b) **Pallet racking (backroom)**: check upright columns for damage (dent, bend, twist — any deformity > 3mm requires replacement), beam connectors locked, safety pins in place, floor anchors secure, rack labeling intact; (c) **Cantilever racks (lumber yard)**: check arms for bending under load, base bolted to concrete pad, no corrosion at weld points, load within rated capacity per arm; (d) **Specialty fixtures**: tile display racks (heavy — verify base anchoring), tool pegboard walls (verify mount integrity), appliance display platforms (verify level and stable); (e) record all findings in shelving inspection module — each unit scanned by location barcode with pass/fail/needs-repair status | Maintenance Staff / Department Supervisors | Assistant Store Manager | 4–6 hours (full store) |
| 2 | **Load Capacity Verification**: For any shelving unit flagged as overloaded: (a) check actual shelf load against rated capacity (rated capacity sticker must be visible on each unit — applied during installation per W225); (b) common overload issues in BuildRight: cement bags on gondola shelves (50 kg bags × 10 bags = 500 kg — exceeds most gondola ratings of 150–200 kg per shelf), tile cartons stacked above maximum height, heavy power tools on upper shelves; (c) re-distribute overloaded shelves immediately: move heavy items to lower shelves or pallet racking; (d) update planogram system (W314) with weight-class restrictions per shelf level — heavy items on bottom shelves only | Department Supervisors / Stock Associates | Assistant Store Manager | 1–2 hours |
| 3 | **Repair & Component Replacement**: For units flagged as "needs-repair": (a) **Minor repairs** (shelf clip replacement, bolt tightening, shelf board replacement): Maintenance Staff completes same day using on-hand spare parts inventory (each store maintains a spare parts kit: shelf clips, bolts, shelf boards, safety pins); (b) **Moderate repairs** (upright post replacement, beam replacement): Maintenance Staff orders replacement parts from facilities vendor per W47 (Store Facility Maintenance); racking temporarily unloaded and tagged "out of service" until repaired (within 5 business days); (c) **Major structural issues** (multiple bent uprights, anchor failure, weld cracking): Assistant Store Manager contacts Regional Facilities Manager and Engineering team per W789 for structural assessment; affected area cordoned off until engineer clears; (d) all repairs logged in system with parts used, labor hours, and repair date; (e) repair costs tracked per store for capital planning per W700 | Maintenance Staff / Vendor Technician | Assistant Store Manager | 1–8 hours depending on severity |
| 4 | **Post-Earthquake Emergency Inspection**: After any seismic event of Magnitude 5.0+ in store's region (PHIVOLCS alert): (a) Store Manager initiates immediate shelving safety check before reopening after evacuation per W330; (b) focused on: upright post alignment (laser level check for plumb), beam connection integrity, anchor bolt condition, any displaced inventory indicating racking movement; (c) any compromised racking unloaded and cordoned off immediately; (d) Engineering team provides remote assessment via photos within 4 hours; (e) if damage is extensive, store may open with restricted access to affected areas | Store Manager / Maintenance Staff | Store Manager | 1–3 hours |
| 5 | **Annual Deep Inspection & Corrosion Assessment**: Once per year (recommended: start of rainy season, June): (a) comprehensive structural inspection of all racking systems by qualified vendor technician; (b) corrosion assessment: measure steel thickness at critical weld points and base plates using ultrasonic thickness gauge; flag any thickness reduction > 15% from baseline; (c) for coastal stores (within 2 km of coastline): accelerated corrosion expected — recommend galvanized or stainless steel replacement components; (d) load testing: verify cantilever rack and pallet racking load capacity with calibrated test weights; (e) seismic readiness: verify all racking is seismically braced per Philippine structural code (NSCP 2015); (f) generate annual shelving condition report per store for capital planning (W21) and insurance (W857) | Vendor Technician / Maintenance Staff | Regional Facilities Manager | 6–8 hours per store |

### System Touchpoints
- Shelving/racking asset register with installation date, rated capacity, location, and manufacturer specifications
- Quarterly inspection checklist module with barcode scanning per shelving unit
- Maintenance work order system (W47) for repair tracking
- Planogram system (W314) with weight-class restrictions per shelf level
- Spare parts inventory module per store
- Facility condition assessment module (W700) for capital planning

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Slow drip of component failure**: Shelving components don't fail catastrophically overnight; they degrade gradually (bolt loosening, corrosion thinning, fatigue cracking); quarterly inspections catch most issues but the 3-month gap means a rapidly degrading component could be missed; high-risk stores (coastal, high-seismic) should inspect critical racking monthly

- **Customer loading heavy items**: Customers in the lumber yard and tile area pull heavy items from cantilever racks themselves; improper pulling technique can destabilize loads; signage and staff assistance (W824) mitigate this risk
- **Replacement parts lead time**: Specialized racking components (upright posts, beam connectors for specific brands) may have 4–8 week lead times from manufacturer; stores should maintain minimum spare parts inventory for the most common failure components

## W1047. Store-Level Rainy Season Floor Safety, Anti-Slip Mat & Entrance Canopy Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Philippine rainy season onset (June–November, per PAGASA); or continuous rainfall for ≥2 hours at store location; or customer slip incident near entrance |
| **Frequency** | Daily during rainy season (June–November); ~180 days/year across the 200-store chain |
| **Volume** | 1 deployment per store per rainy day; ongoing maintenance during rainy season |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Maintenance/Utility Staff, Sales Associates, Customer Service Rep, LP Officer |

### Background

The Philippines experiences intense monsoon rains from June to November, with daily rainfall rates exceeding 50mm/hour during typhoons and southwest monsoon (habagat) events. BuildRight Depot's 200 big-box stores feature large entrance areas (typically 3–4 meter-wide sliding glass doors opening to the selling floor) that become significant slip hazards during rain due to: (a) water tracked in by customers and shopping carts from wet parking lots; (b) rain blown directly into the entrance during wind-driven downpours; (c) wet floor transition from entrance canopy to interior tile/concrete flooring. Philippine slip-and-fall incidents carry significant liability under the Civil Code (Article 2176 — quasi-delict) and represent a customer safety obligation. Each store must deploy entrance safety measures during rainy season as a daily operational protocol. This is distinct from W140 (OHS incident management) which covers post-incident response; this workflow is proactive prevention. It is also distinct from W576 (typhoon preparedness) which is for severe weather events; this workflow covers daily rainy season floor safety management.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rainy Season Preparation (Annual Setup)**: Before June 1 each year, Maintenance/Utility Staff executes annual rainy season readiness: (a) inspect and clean all entrance drainage channels — ensure grates are clear of debris and connected to storm drain; (b) inspect entrance canopy/overhang — no leaks, no accumulated debris on top, gutters clear; (c) inspect and deploy anti-slip floor mats at all entrance points — rubber-backed water-absorbent mats in good condition (replace if worn); (d) verify wet floor sign inventory — minimum 4 "Wet Floor" A-frame warning signs per entrance; (e) verify umbrella bag dispenser (at entrance) is stocked — prevents water dripping throughout store; (f) verify store entrance door seals and weatherstripping are intact; (g) test entrance area lighting — adequate visibility for wet floor hazard identification; (h) system logs rainy season readiness checklist completion with date and Store Manager sign-off | Maintenance/Utility Staff | Store Manager | 60–90 min (one-time annual) |
| 2 | **Daily Rainy Day Activation**: When rain begins (or at store opening if already raining): (a) Maintenance/Utility Staff deploys "Wet Floor" warning signs at all entrance points and at transition zones (entrance to main aisle); (b) lays out anti-slip floor mats at full width of each entrance (ensure mats lie flat, no curling edges); (c) positions umbrella bag dispenser at entrance — customer service representative reminds entering customers to use umbrella bags; (d) assigns a Sales Associate or Customer Service Rep as "entrance monitor" during peak hours (10 AM–2 PM, 4 PM–7 PM) to mop accumulated water and reposition mats as needed; (e) activates entrance canopy rain deflector (if automated) or manually adjusts canopy angle to direct runoff away from entrance; (f) system logs daily rainy day activation with timestamp | Maintenance/Utility Staff / Sales Associates | Store Manager | 10–15 min (per activation) |

BuildRight Depot stores maintain an outdoor/covered lumber yard for bulky building materials (plywood, lumber, cement, hollow blocks, rebar, roofing sheets). ## W1135. Store-Level Daily Temperature-Sensitive Product Monitoring & Quality Check

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Daily opening procedure (W5A), or temperature alert from monitoring device, or product quality concern reported |
| **Frequency** | Daily (200 stores × 365 days) |
| **Volume** | ~30–50 temperature-sensitive SKUs per store |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (relevant department) |
| **Participants** | Stock Associate, Department Supervisor |

### Background

While BuildRight Depot is not a grocery retailer, several product categories are temperature-sensitive and require monitoring to maintain quality and safety: (a) paint and chemicals — extreme heat (>40°C) can cause paint to separate, discolor, or prematurely cure in the can; (b) adhesives and sealants — silicone, epoxy, and construction adhesives degrade in high temperatures and lose bonding strength; (c) PVC pipes and fittings — become brittle when stored in extreme cold or warp under direct sunlight/heat; (d) batteries (power tool, flashlight) — high temperatures reduce battery life and can cause leakage; (e) gardening products (seeds, live plants, fertilizers) — seeds lose viability above 35°C; (f) pest control chemicals — active ingredients degrade at high temperatures; (g) waterproofing membranes — can fuse together in extreme heat. In the Philippines, where ambient temperatures regularly reach 30–38°C and outdoor lumber yard/storage areas can exceed 50°C under direct sun, temperature monitoring is essential for product quality preservation. This workflow ensures daily monitoring of temperature-sensitive products and triggers quality interventions when conditions exceed acceptable ranges.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Daily Temperature Check**: (a) during store opening (W5A), Stock Associate records ambient temperature in key store zones: indoor selling floor, outdoor lumber yard, paint section, garden center, receiving dock, backroom storage; (b) temperature logged in ERP via mobile app or store tablet; (c) automated temperature sensors (if installed) provide continuous monitoring with alert thresholds; (d) focus on high-risk zones: paint mixing station area (should be < 35°C), outdoor garden center (shade structures checked), backroom chemical storage | Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | 5–10 min |
| 2 | **Product Condition Spot Check**: (a) Stock Associate visually inspects temperature-sensitive products: paint cans — check for bulging lids (indicates gas buildup from heat), leaking, or label discoloration; adhesives/sealants — squeeze test for proper consistency (not hardened or runny); PVC pipes — check for warping or discoloration; batteries — check for leaking or corrosion on terminals; garden seeds — check display for heat damage (wilting, mold); (b) any concerns documented with photos in ERP | Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | 10–15 min |
| 3 | **Corrective Action for Temperature Excursion**: (a) if temperature exceeds acceptable range: move affected products to cooler area (indoor backroom with fans/ventilation); (b) for outdoor products: deploy shade structures (awning, shade cloth) over garden center and paint display area; (c) for paint: activate air conditioning or fans in paint section during extreme heat (March–May); (d) products showing quality degradation: quarantine and markdown per W549 (damaged/open-box processing) or disposition per W91 (damaged goods); (e) system logs temperature excursion event for vendor notification (if product quality claim needed) and shrinkage tracking (W845) | Stock Associate / Department Supervisor | Store Manager | 10–20 min |
| 4 | **Seasonal Preparedness & Facility Improvement**: (a) before summer peak (February): verify air conditioning units in paint section are operational; install shade structures in outdoor areas; review product placement — move heat-sensitive items away from direct sunlight and exterior walls; (b) before rainy season (May): check for water leaks that could affect product storage; verify drainage in outdoor areas; (c) facility improvement requests submitted per W47 (store facility maintenance) for permanent solutions: improved ventilation, air conditioning upgrades, shade structure installation | Department Supervisor | Store Manager | 30–60 min (seasonal) |

## W1156. Store-Level Customer Construction Site Safety Equipment Rental & Delivery

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer inquires about safety equipment for a construction project, or customer purchases construction materials that require safety equipment, or pro desk referral for project accounts |
| **Frequency** | ~800–1,200 safety equipment rental transactions/month across all stores |
| **Volume** | 1–20 equipment items per rental |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Tools & Hardware) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate, Department Supervisor, Customer, Delivery Partner (if delivery requested) |

### Background

BuildRight Depot sells safety and PPE products (2% of active SKUs, ~700 SKUs) including hard hats, safety goggles, gloves, harnesses, respirators, and safety shoes. However, many construction safety equipment needs are temporary and project-specific: (a) a homeowner doing a one-time roofing project needs a safety harness for a day, not a lifetime purchase; (b) a contractor starting a new project site needs scaffolding, safety barriers, and fire extinguishers for the project duration (3–6 months) — purchasing all equipment ties up capital; (c) a community building project organized by a barangay needs safety equipment for volunteer workers for a weekend — purchase is wasteful. Offering safety equipment rental alongside sales provides: (a) a new revenue stream (rental fees at 10–15% of purchase price per day, or 25–40% per week); (b) increased customer traffic and project engagement; (c) enhanced safety culture in the community — customers who rent safety equipment are more likely to use it than those who skip it due to purchase cost; (d) a competitive differentiator — no other Philippine hardware retailer currently offers organized safety equipment rental. This workflow manages safety equipment rental from booking through return inspection. It complements W139 (tool rental operations), W1138 (safety gear bundle recommendation), and W1062 (scaffolding rental) by focusing specifically on construction site safety equipment.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rental Booking & Safety Assessment**: (a) customer requests safety equipment rental; (b) sales associate identifies project type and duration: roofing (safety harness, roof brackets, anchor points), scaffolding work (full-body harness, hard hat, safety net), concrete pouring (steel-toe boots, gloves, hard hat), excavation (shoring equipment, gas detector), general construction (hard hat, safety vest, goggles, gloves); (c) associate creates rental agreement in ERP: equipment items, rental duration, daily/weekly rate, deposit amount (50–100% of equipment retail value), delivery option; (d) customer presents valid ID and signs rental agreement with safety acknowledgment: "Customer acknowledges responsibility for proper use of safety equipment and has received basic usage instructions"; (e) deposit collected via POS (refundable upon return of equipment in good condition) | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 10–15 min |

## W1192. Post-Typhoon Store Damage Assessment, Insurance Claim & Rapid Reopening Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | PAGASA lowers typhoon signal below Storm Signal No. 1 for store's area; or NDRRMC declares all-clear for the area |
| **Frequency** | ~3–5 events/year affecting subsets of the 200-store chain; any individual store may be affected 0–2 times/year |
| **Volume** | During a major typhoon: 20–80 stores may be simultaneously affected requiring assessment |
| **Owner** | VP Store Operations |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, VP Store Operations, Maintenance Engineer, Insurance Coordinator, Regional Manager, Facilities Team, CFO (insurance claims > PHP 500K) |

### Background

Following a major typhoon, BuildRight Depot must rapidly assess store damage, secure the premises, file insurance claims, and reopen stores as quickly as possible to serve community rebuilding needs. Every day a store remains closed after a typhoon represents ~PHP 830K in lost revenue (PHP 25M/month ÷ 30 days) plus community hardship. This workflow governs the coordinated post-typhoon response from initial damage assessment through insurance claim filing to rapid reopening.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Post-storm safety clearance**: After PAGASA lowers signal: Store Manager (or designated staff if SM is unavailable) proceeds to store with safety kit (flashlight, hard hat, gloves, first aid); conducts exterior perimeter check for downed power lines, flooding, structural damage; does NOT enter building if structural damage is suspected; contacts VP Store Operations with initial "Go / No-Go" status | Store Manager | VP Store Operations | 1–2 hours/store |
| 2 | **Damage assessment**: If Step 1 is "Go": Store Manager enters store and conducts standardized damage assessment using mobile form: (a) roof integrity (leaks, missing sheets, structural deformation), (b) flooding level and extent (cm measurement at lowest point), (c) structural damage (walls, columns, beams, loading dock), (d) electrical system status (main panel, wiring, outlets, POS terminals), (e) inventory damage (water-damaged merchandise count by category), (f) equipment damage (forklifts, paint mixing machine, compressors), (g) exterior damage (signage, fence, lumber yard cover, parking area); captures photos and video of all damage; uploads to ERP disaster assessment module | Store Manager | VP Store Operations | 2–4 hours/store |
| 3 | **Damage classification and escalation**: VP Store Operations classifies each store: **Green** (minor damage, can open within 24 hours — e.g., minor leaks, no structural concern), **Yellow** (moderate damage, can open in 2–5 days with repairs — e.g., significant roof repair needed, partial flooding), **Red** (major damage, requires engineer assessment, closed > 5 days — e.g., structural compromise, severe flooding > 1 meter, electrical system damage); assigns Maintenance Engineer to Yellow and Red stores | VP Store Operations | COO | 1 hour (all affected stores) |
| 4 | **Insurance claim initiation**: For Yellow and Red stores: Insurance Coordinator files preliminary insurance claim within 48 hours per insurance policy requirements: (a) notifies insurance company via hotline/email, (b) submits preliminary damage assessment report with photos from Step 2, (c) obtains claim reference number, (d) schedules insurance adjuster visit within 5 business days; for claims > PHP 500K: CFO reviews and approves claim before submission | Insurance Coordinator | CFO | 2 hours/store |
| 5 | **Emergency repair prioritization**: Maintenance Engineer prioritizes emergency repairs for fastest possible reopening: (a) safety-critical first (electrical, structural shoring, roof patching), (b) operational-critical second (POS system, lighting, water drainage), (c) cosmetic last (signage, painting); for Green stores: Store Manager handles minor repairs with local contractor; for Yellow/Red: Maintenance Engineer dispatches facilities team or emergency contractor | Maintenance Engineer | VP Store Operations | Varies (4–48 hours) |
| 6 | **Inventory salvage and disposition**: Store Manager separates damaged inventory: (a) water-damaged but sellable at discount → route to W91 (damaged goods disposition) for markdown per W93, (b) unsalvageable (waterlogged cement, swollen wood, rusted hardware) → quarantine for insurance documentation then disposal per W502/W82, (c) undamaged inventory → clean and reshelve; system updates inventory status for affected items | Store Manager / Inventory Accountant | VP Merchandising | 4–8 hours/store |
| 7 | **Controlled reopening**: Once safety-critical and operational-critical repairs are complete: (a) Maintenance Engineer confirms store is safe for occupancy, (b) Store Manager verifies POS system operational, (c) VP Store Operations authorizes reopening, (d) store opens with potentially reduced operating hours or limited product sections; system sends reopening notification to customers via app/SMS per marketing protocol | VP Store Operations | COO | 1 hour/store |
| 8 | **Insurance claim finalization**: After insurance adjuster visit and repair completion: Insurance Coordinator finalizes claim with: (a) final repair cost invoices, (b) inventory loss documentation with supporting GR/PO records, (c) business interruption calculation (days closed × daily revenue average), (d) adjuster report; tracks claim until settlement received; claims > PHP 1M require CFO escalation to insurance broker | Insurance Coordinator | CFO | 4–8 hours/store |

## W1317. Store-Level Generator Backup Power Operations, Fuel Management & Load Shedding Protocol

| **Frequency** | ~1–2 unplanned outage/brownout events per store per month in affected areas (concentrated in the Mindanao dry season; ~800–1,500 chain-wide/year per W470, mitigated by generator backup); ~2–4 scheduled rotational brownouts per month in some areas during dry season |

| **Participants** | Store Manager, Maintenance/Utility Staff, Security Guard, Facilities Coordinator |

### Background

Power reliability in the Philippines varies significantly by location: Metro Manila enjoys relatively stable power (2–4 outages/month, mostly brief), while provincial locations served by electric cooperatives experience frequent rotational brownouts (planned outages to manage limited supply) and unplanned outages (equipment failure, typhoon damage, overloaded distribution). BuildRight's 200 stores are distributed across urban and provincial locations: 30 stores in Metro Manila, 170 stores in provincial areas. Each store requires reliable power for: (a) POS system operation (although POS has offline capability per POS-007, prolonged outages affect real-time inventory sync and customer-facing display), (b) lighting and air conditioning (customer comfort and safety), (c) security systems (CCTV, alarm, access control), (d) paint mixing station and other service equipment, (e) refrigeration for paint and chemicals (temperature-sensitive inventory). It complements W470 (rotational brownout management) by focusing on the physical generator operation and fuel logistics.

## W5536. Missing-Child / Code Adam In-Store Response Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | A parent, guardian, or companion reports a child (under 12) missing inside the store premises; or an unaccompanied, distressed, or apparently lost child is found by staff; or a suspected attempted-abduction is observed |
| **Frequency** | Rare; ~5–15 separations resolved at the service desk weekly chain-wide (minutes, self-resolved); ~2–6 formal Code-Adam activations per year chain-wide |
| **Volume** | 1 store × full team (29 staff/store) × the ~99,000 customers on premises chain-wide per operating day (W330 population canon) — family shoppers with children are a core BuildRight demographic |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, all floor staff (zone search), Front-End/Service Desk (guardian liaison), Security Guard (door control, CCTV), LP Officer, Regional Manager (escalation), PNP (911) & PNP-Women and Children Protection Desk, barangay, VP Legal (evidence/liability), HR (staff trauma support per W717 debrief practice) |

### Background

The corpus covers lost property (W929 lost & found), customer incidents (W285/W501), and security events (W471), but a missing child has no owner — the only corpus hits for "missing child" were shopping-cart child straps (PA-07.3). Big-box retail standard for four decades (the retail Code Adam protocol, named for Adam Walsh) requires a time-boxed, exit-controlled, systematic search with escalation to police — because the first minutes decide outcomes and uncoordinated searches leave yard and backroom zones unswept. RA 7610 (Special Protection of Children) and RA 10821 (Children's Emergency Relief and Protection) frame the duty of care; an uncoordinated response or a child released to the wrong adult creates catastrophic premises-liability and reputational exposure (links to VS-147/VS-14 crisis comms W855). Store layout makes this acute at BuildRight: 8,000–15,000 sqm floors plus outdoor lumber yards (PA-07.3's 500–800 m customer travel paths) with multiple exits and parking access.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Report receipt & Code-Adam activation**: Service Desk takes the guardian's report (child's name, age, clothing, hair/eye color, where/when last seen); Manager announces the activation — coded PA script stating description only (no "missing"/"abducted" wording), all staff stop routine tasks; door alert: front-end holds transactions near exits and positions staff at every customer exit (front, garden, yard, fire exits with alarms) — no child exits unaccompanied until stand-down | Service Desk / Front-End | Store Manager | < 2 min |
| 2 | **Zone search — interior**: assigned staff systematically search their own zones (staff search areas they know): aisle ends, display fixtures, furniture/tent setups, restrooms (call-out check), fitting areas, stockroom, receiving, staff areas, battery/tool kiosks — 5-minute sweep, report found/clear to Manager by radio | Zone staff / Duty Supervisors | Duty Supervisors | 5 min |
| 3 | **Zone search — exterior**: yard/lumber zones, garden center, parking aisles, cart corrals, landscaping; Security sweeps the perimeter and reviews live CCTV (entrances, exits, aisle cameras) from last-seen-time forward | Yard staff / Security Guard | Security Guard | 5–10 min (parallel) |
| 4 | **Found — reunification with verification**: child brought to Service Desk; comfort protocol (second adult present throughout — safeguarding rule); release only to the reporting guardian after identity/description match (and check the custodial-dispute flag: if a custody dispute is known or claimed, do not release — police decide); log time, persons present, and verification basis | Service Desk / Security Guard | Store Manager | 5–10 min |
| 5 | **Not found at 10 minutes — police escalation**: Manager calls 911/PNP; requests PNP-WCPD protocol handling; provides description, CCTV snapshot, last-seen point; store search continues under police direction; guardian stays with a dedicated staff liaison; VP Legal informed; CCTV footage preservation requested per the W471 evidence chain | Store Manager / Security | Store Manager | At the 10-minute mark |
| 6 | **Special branches**: (a) unaccompanied child found → safeguarding rule, two-adult supervision, no release without verified guardian or police, PNP-WCPD if no guardian within 30 min; (b) suspected/attempted abduction or custody grab → do not confront, observe and report, 911 immediately, treat as W471 Category-A crime scene, preserve CCTV, W717/W140 support for involved staff | All Staff / LP / VP Legal | Store Manager | Event-driven |
| 7 | **Stand-down & documentation**: deactivation PA; log the event (times, zones searched, outcome, reunification verification) into the W471 incident record even when resolved internally; guardian thank-you and service-recovery per store practice; debrief within 48 hours — response-time gaps and unswept-zone findings feed the W158 drill rotation (Code-Adam tabletop semi-annually per store) | Duty Supervisors / Manager | Store Manager | 1–2 hrs + debrief |

### System Touchpoints
- Coded PA announcement templates (pre-staged in the PA system; description-only wording per Step 1)
- POS/door alert: exit-hold flag with transaction-hold override at front-end (replaces verbal-only door control)
- CCTV live-view + export request against the W471 evidence chain (preservation hold on the retention window)
- Incident-case record (W471 Category-A class) — even for internally-resolved separations
- Visitor/staff roster (W696 muster data reused for search-staff accounting)
- OLA drill-completion tracking for the semi-annual Code-Adam tabletop (links to W51/W158)

### Time Estimate
Event effort is outcome-scaled and event-driven: an internally-resolved Code-Adam activation ≈ 2–5 staff-hours (29 staff × 4–10 min of search/door-hold, plus desk and manager time); a police-escalated event ≈ 8–20 staff-hours including liaison and documentation. Drill contribution: ~30 min × 29 staff × 2 drills/yr × 200 stores ≈ 5,800 hours/yr absorbed in the existing W158 drill calendar — no incremental headcount.

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Custody risk**: child released to a claiming adult who is not the guardian — the catastrophic failure mode; mitigated by the two-adult safeguarding rule, guardian-identity verification, and the custodial-dispute hold in Step 4
- **Search-gap risk**: yard/backroom/restroom zones unswept while effort concentrates on the sales floor; mitigated by assigned-zone sweep lists in Steps 2–3 and the 10-minute police-escalation clock in Step 5
- **Exit-leak risk**: child leaves through an unmonitored garden/yard exit during activation; mitigated by the all-exits door-alert hold in Step 1
- **Panic-word risk**: PA wording causing customer alarm or tipping an actual abductor; mitigated by the coded description-only script
- **Evidence risk**: CCTV overwritten before police request; mitigated by the W471 preservation hold at Step 5

### Automation Opportunity
- Code-Adam activation button at the service-desk terminal fires the coded PA template, flags all POS lanes with the exit-hold, and starts the 10-minute escalation timer with automatic manager paging (replaces manual Steps 1 and 5 timing)
- Zone-sweep confirmations from staff handhelds render a live searched/unswept map for the Manager (replaces radio roll-call in Steps 2–3)

### Controls
- CTL-260 (ensure controlled execution — Store Facility & Safety (PA-07.2).)
- operational: two-adult rule for any found/unaccompanied child; release only after verified guardian match or police direction
- operational: every activation — including internally-resolved ones — is documented to the W471 incident record

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed by existing store staff (the response mobilizes the same 29-head store team as W330) and the W158 drill calendar for rehearsals. No incremental headcount.

## W5539. Elevator & Escalator Entrapment Response Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Passenger(s) trapped in a stopped elevator car (between floors or doors blocked), or an escalator entrapment event (clothing/shoe caught at comb plate, fall on the unit, handrail incident) |
| **Frequency** | Rare; ~5–15 entrapment stoppages chain-wide per year across the multi-level stores (elevator incidents run ~1–3/unit/yr industry-wide); escalator incidents mostly minor |
| **Volume** | 1 unit × passengers on board (2–15 people) + responding store team; every event involves the licensed elevator-maintenance contractor under the W47 service contract |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Facilities/Maintenance Technician, elevator-maintenance contractor 24/7 emergency line (licensed technician), Security Guard, trained first-aider (W501), EMS (medical cases), DOLE-accredited elevator inspector (post-event, reportable cases), VP Legal (liability/evidence) |

### Background

The corpus owns elevator *maintenance and inspection* — PA-07.2's monthly contractor maintenance and annual DOLE-accredited inspection (W47 facility table), VS-138's hard-services SLA, and the W47 contractor service contracts — but nobody owned the entrapment *event*: passenger communication, the medical-vulnerability triage, the licensed-release rule, and return-to-service. The cardinal rule is that only the licensed technician releases passengers and re-sets the unit — untrained rescue attempts (prying doors, pulling passengers through the car-to-landing gap) are a recurring cause of passenger death in retail elevators. RA 9514-adjacent building-safety duties and the DOLE-accredited inspection regime (Rule 1050-class reporting for injury events per W140) frame the compliance perimeter; entrapment with injury or > 1-hour exposure is documented to the W140/W471 chain. Store layout matters: multi-level BuildRight stores carry passenger/freight elevators and long escalator runs with heavy trolley/cart traffic.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Establish contact & reassurance**: respond to car alarm/interphone or staff report; two-way contact with passengers — keep them calm, instruct all to stand clear of the doors, never attempt to exit; establish count and condition (children, elderly, medical needs, panic) | First responder / Security Guard | Store Manager | < 3 min |
| 2 | **Contractor emergency callout**: call the maintenance contractor's 24/7 line (W47 service contract); log the call time; urban SLA ≤ 60 min on-site, provincial ≤ 120 min; facilities technician assigned to wait at the unit; incident clock started | Facilities Technician | Facilities Coordinator | 5 min |
| 3 | **Medical-vulnerability triage**: continuous interphone check-ins every 10 min; if any passenger reports medical distress (or the projected wait exceeds ~45–60 min with vulnerable passengers): EMS per W501 immediately; if EMS assessment says extrication cannot wait for the contractor → BFP extrication (they have the authority/equipment); Store Manager makes the call and documents it | First Aider / Store Manager | Store Manager | Continuous |
| 4 | **Area control & power discipline**: barricade the landings, keep crowds back, no one touches the machine room, mains/phase-failure switches operated only by the technician; if the car is between floors, door-force attempts are prohibited (the car may shift) | Security / Facilities Technician | Facilities Coordinator | Ongoing |
| 5 | **Licensed release**: contractor technician opens the car and landing doors, verifies car level, and walks passengers out; first-aider offers assessment per W501 (offer even if declined — document); Facilities logs release time | Contractor Technician / First Aider | Store Manager | 10–30 min after arrival |
| 6 | **Escalator variant**: landing stop buttons pressed (each landing end), power isolated by technician only; free the caught item (comb-plate reversal/disassembly is the technician's); injured customers → W501 first aid + W285/W147.3 customer-incident chain; unit stays out of service until the technician re-sets and tests comb plates/handrail | Facilities Technician / First Aider | Store Manager | 30–90 min |
| 7 | **Return-to-service & documentation**: technician's incident report (cause: power phase loss, door sensor, brake, overload) filed to the unit's asset history in W47; unit returns to service only after technician testing; injury/extended-exposure events documented per W140 (employee) and W147.3/W285 (customer) chains; DOLE-accredited inspector notified where the event involves a reportable safety-device failure | Contractor / Facilities Coordinator | Facilities Coordinator | 1–2 hrs |
| 8 | **Recurrence review**: a unit with 2+ entrapments in 12 months triggers an engineering review — modernization vs continued repair via the W21 capex path (elevator modernization > PHP 50K → W21); review results feed the W47 PM program and the contractor SLA review | Facilities Standards Manager | Director, Facilities & Real Estate | Per case |

### System Touchpoints
- Contractor 24/7 emergency-callout log tied to the W47 service-contract record (SLA timing evidence)
- Facility asset history: elevator/escalator unit record with incident reports, technician findings, and PM linkage (links to W47)
- Incident-case records: W140 (employee exposure), W147.3 customer-incident register + W285 claims (customer injury)
- W21 capex request workflow for modernization cases
- Interphone/alarm-system test records in the W1296-class monthly equipment-inspection checklist

### Time Estimate
Event effort is duration-scaled and event-driven: a routine entrapment (contractor within SLA, no injury) ≈ 6–12 staff-hours total (facilities wait + manager + security + documentation); an escalated event (EMS/BFP involvement, injury documentation) ≈ 12–30 staff-hours. At ~5–15 events/yr chain-wide ≈ 30–180 staff-hours/yr, absorbed by existing Facilities/Security/store duty rosters — no incremental headcount.

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Unauthorized-rescue risk**: staff or bystanders pry doors or pull passengers through the gap — the leading cause of elevator fatalities; mitigated by the licensed-release-only rule (Step 5), landing barricades, and door-force prohibition (Step 4)
- **SLA-gap risk**: provincial contractor response beyond 120 min with vulnerable passengers aboard; mitigated by the Step-3 medical triage clock forcing the EMS/BFP decision instead of passive waiting
- **Evidence risk**: no technician report or CCTV preserved when a claim follows; mitigated by the asset-history filing requirement and W147.3 incident chain
- **Recurrence risk**: chronic units returned to service after resets without engineering review; mitigated by the 2-in-12-months modernization trigger in Step 8

### Automation Opportunity
- Elevator interphone/alarm events auto-open an incident case, page Facilities and Security, and display the contractor's 24/7 number and SLA clock (replaces manual Steps 1–2 logging)
- Entrapment-count-per-unit dashboard auto-flags the 2-in-12-months trigger into a W21 modernization review queue (replaces manual Step 8 tally)

### Controls
- CTL-260 (ensure controlled execution — Store Facility & Safety (PA-07.2).)
- operational: passengers are released only by the licensed contractor technician; the machine room and mains switches are technician-only
- operational: medical-vulnerability check-ins run on a documented ≤ 10-minute clock with an EMS/BFP escalation decision point

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed by existing Facilities Technicians, Security, duty managers and the W47 contractor service contracts (emergency callout is a contract deliverable). No incremental headcount.

## W5561. Sustained Water-Service Interruption Response & Store Continuity Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Water-utility notice or sudden loss of piped water service at a store or DC expected to exceed one trading day — service interruption, El Niño rationing, a district main break, or a pressure-loss/boil-water advisory from the utility or LGU |
| **Frequency** | Clustered utility event: dry-season rationing years (El Niño) produce multi-day interruptions across Metro Manila and water-district-served provincial sites; non-drought years see isolated main-break events — plan on the utility-notice lifecycle a handful of times per year chain-wide, with rationing episodes recurring on El Niño cycles |
| **Volume** | 1 event × affected sites (a single main break = 1–3 sites; a rationing episode = every site on the affected district, potentially dozens) × the water-dependent surface (restrooms, the ~200 garden centers' live goods per VS-145, fabrication cooling, cleaning, food-and-beverage concessions per W177) × duration tiers (1–10 days) |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Regional Manager (multi-site events), Facilities/Maintenance (W47), HSE Manager (sanitation decisions, VS-24), Garden Center lead (VS-145 live-goods protection), Store Communications (VS-63 cascade), Procurement (emergency trucked-water sourcing, the W1187 class), LGU health/sanitation office and the utility (VS-166 permit interface), Cash Office (restricted-services tender handling if needed) |

### Background

'Water interruption'/'water rationing' appear in the corpus only as product-selling context — PA-09.2's rainwater-harvesting and filtration advisories cite Manila Water/Maynilad rationing as customer demand drivers. W470 owns the power analog (the rotational-brownout protocol), W1223 generator and extended-power operations, W1387 the flood event (excess water), W692 consumption tracking — nobody owns the absence-of-water event: the sanitation decision law (restroom closure vs portable facilities under the store's Sanitation Permit obligations per VS-166 and DOH/LGU sanitation standards), the live-goods watering priority, emergency trucked-water procurement, customer-facing service-status decisions, or the post-interruption flush and water-quality confirmation before restrooms and food services reopen (pressure loss draws contamination into pipes — the utility's own boil-water advisories exist for exactly this recovery hazard).

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Classify the interruption**: utility notice and restoration estimate; potable vs non-potable availability; affected-site census across the district; duration tier (≤ 1 day / 1–3 days / rationing mode > 3 days) drives every later decision | Store Manager / Facilities | Regional Manager | 1–2 hours |
| 2 | **Sanitation & health decisions**: restroom status — full closure with portable-toilet rental via emergency PO, vs reduced service with enhanced hand-hygiene stations — decided with HSE sign-off under the sanitation-permit obligations (VS-166) and DOH/LGU sanitation standards; document either way | Store Manager / HSE | HSE Manager | 2–4 hours |
| 3 | **Water-dependent service matrix**: garden-center live-goods watering priority per VS-145 (perishable-stock protection, the W1387 inventory-elevation analog); fabrication/cooling services status; cleaning suspended to hygiene minimums; food-and-beverage concessions (W177) suspended where handwashing and dishwashing standards cannot be met | Department Supervisors / Garden Center Lead | Store Manager | 2–3 hours |
| 4 | **Emergency water operations**: trucked-water sourcing and delivery prioritization across affected sites (W1187-class emergency PO with pre-qualified water vendors); on-site storage refill; conservation directives to all departments | Procurement / Facilities | Store Manager | Daily, event-long |
| 5 | **Customer & employee communication**: service-status signage (restrooms closed, services limited) without overpromising restoration times; store-communications cascade per VS-63; drinking-water provision for staff as a welfare duty (VS-19.1-class) | Store Manager / Department Supervisors | Regional Manager | Same day |
| 6 | **Trading-continuity decision**: trade-with-restrictions is the default; partial-closure or closure decisions ride the Regional Manager closure authority (the E-02 custody — W2510 decides, W850 executes) when sanitation standards cannot be maintained; the decision is documented either way | Regional Manager | VP Store Operations (multi-site) | On threshold |
| 7 | **Recovery & flush protocol**: on restoration — system flush per utility/LGU guidance; boil-water advisory handling for any food-related water use; water-quality confirmation before restroom and food-service reopening; sanitation-permit records updated (VS-166) | Facilities / HSE | Store Manager | 4–8 hours |
| 8 | **Duration escalation & after-action**: multi-day events engage the BCP reporting layer (the W848 class) and rationing-mode standing orders (scheduled top-ups, alternate sourcing); after-action review captures utility performance and storage-capacity lessons feeding W692's tracking | Store Manager / Regional Manager / BCP | COO Office (rationing mode) | Post-event |

### System Touchpoints
- Store-facility work-order objects (W47) with a water-event type and restoration timeline
- Emergency PO type with audit trail and post-event reconciliation (the W1187 class)
- Digital-signage and store-communications push for service-status notices (links to W504 and VS-63)
- Sanitation-permit and LGU-inspection records (links to VS-166)
- Garden-center plant-care task lists and loss-capture records (links to VS-145)
- Store water-consumption telemetry where metered (links to W692)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Sanitation-permit exposure**: operating restrooms without water breaches DOH/LGU sanitation standards and the store's own permit conditions; mitigated by Step 2's HSE-gated status decision with portable facilities procured same-day
- **Live-goods loss**: a hot dry-season day without watering destroys the highest-margin perishable stock in the store; mitigated by Step 3's watering-priority matrix ahead of any cleaning or cooling use
- **Contamination-on-return risk**: unflushed lines after pressure loss serve contaminated water on reopening; mitigated by Step 7's flush-and-confirm gate before restrooms and food services reopen
- **Customer-experience shock**: an undisclosed restroom closure discovered mid-visit drives complaints and social-media damage; mitigated by Step 5's proactive signage at entrances and service counters
- **Supply-competition risk**: trucked water is scarce during district-wide rationing; mitigated by Step 4's pre-qualified vendors and LGU/utility coordination for priority retail allocation

### Automation Opportunity
- Utility-advisory subscription feed auto-mapping affected sites from the district roster (replaces manual Step 1 census)
- Storage-tank telemetry with top-up scheduling during rationing mode (replaces manual Step 4 dispatch)
- Signage-template auto-push to affected stores' digital boards on the interruption record (replaces manual Step 5 posting)
- Service-status flags on the store locator/app (links to VS-75) — replaces phone-based customer inquiry handling

### Controls
- CTL-260 (ensure controlled execution — Store Facility & Safety (PA-07.2).)

### Time Estimate
Per single-site event ≈ 4–10 staff-hours on day one (classification, sanitation decisions, sourcing) plus 1–2 hours/day maintenance; rationing episodes ≈ 20–40 staff-hours per affected site per week. The handful-of-events/year cadence puts the chain-wide figure under ~500 staff-hours in an El Niño year, absorbed within store and facilities teams.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed by store management, facilities, and HSE with procurement support; no incremental headcount.
