### System Touchpoints
- Automated IC invoice generation with transfer pricing rules (W14.1)
- Simultaneous IC AP/AR posting across entities (W14.3)
- IC reconciliation report across entity pairs (W14.4)
- IC elimination automation during consolidation (W14.8)
- Consolidated financial statement generation with IC lines eliminated (W14.9)
- Transfer pricing rule maintenance with annual review documentation per BIR RR 02-2013 (W14.1)
- Dual IC framework: BuildRight operates two intercompany models — (1) **Service-based IC** (primary): monthly fees for warehousing (Logistics Inc.), ecommerce fulfillment (Digital Commerce Inc.), rent (Property Mgmt Inc.), and management fees (Holdings Inc.) — no goods change ownership between entities; Depot Inc. owns all merchandise inventory throughout the supply chain; standard DC→Store replenishment (W4) and inter-DC transfers (W22) are intra-entity inventory movements, not IC goods transfers; (2) **Goods-based IC** (rare): if inter-entity goods transfer is needed (e.g., Digital Commerce Inc. purchases goods from Depot Inc. for direct resale, Property Mgmt Inc. purchases building materials for property maintenance), system creates IC sales order and IC purchase order at configured transfer price; IC invoice auto-generated at receipt; IC elimination during consolidation per W9A.13; system supports both models with different GL posting paths (W14)
- IC settlement timing: all IC flows (service-based and goods-based) are settled on a single monthly net settlement date (typically 5th of the following month) as part of W14 step 6; Digital Commerce Inc. remits collected ecommerce payments to Depot Inc. on this same schedule (not daily or weekly), which means Depot Inc. carries up to 30 days of ecommerce revenue as an IC receivable from Digital Commerce Inc.; Treasury accounts for this timing in cash flow forecasting (W30 step 8); if ecommerce GMV grows significantly (Year 2–3 target of PHP 250–350M/month), consider moving ecommerce payment remittance to twice-monthly to reduce IC receivable exposure

### Pain Points / Risks
- **BIR transfer pricing audit exposure**: with 5 related entities and substantial IC flows (rent, warehousing, management fees), the BIR may challenge transfer pricing during examination (W77); if IC pricing is not supported by contemporaneous documentation per RR 02-2013, the BIR can impute income and assess deficiency taxes, surcharges, and compromise penalties
- **IC mismatch blocking consolidation**: even a PHP 1,000 discrepancy between entity pairs blocks automated IC elimination entries; timing differences in IC invoice posting (one entity posts in period X, the other in period X+1) are the most common cause of consolidation delays during W9A close
- **Ecommerce IC receivable buildup**: Digital Commerce Inc. holds up to 30 days of collected ecommerce payments before remitting to Depot Inc.; as ecommerce GMV grows toward PHP 250–350M/month, this creates a PHP 250–350M IC receivable that is effectively an interest-free loan from Depot Inc. to Digital Commerce Inc.
- **Annual transfer pricing review delays**: if the CFO and Controller do not complete the annual IC pricing review during the W26 budget cycle, outdated transfer pricing rules persist into the new fiscal year, creating compliance risk and potential BIR penalties
- **IC loan arm's-length rate compliance**: IC loans must bear interest at or above the BIR-prescribed rate (currently ~12% per annum); if the rate is below threshold, the BIR can impute additional interest income and assess deficiency taxes on the lending entity

### System Touchpoints (Finance Extensions)
- Intercompany loan sub-ledger with auto-interest calculation
- Dividend withholding tax tracking (BIR Form 1601-FQ; intercorporate dividends received from domestic subsidiaries are exempt per CREATE — RA 11534 amended NIRC §27(D)(3) — so final withholding applies only on Holdings' distributions to individual/foreign shareholders)
- Automated IC elimination rules in Consolidation module
- Treasury dashboard showing liquidity across all 5 legal entities

### Pain Points / Risks
- **BIR transfer pricing scrutiny on IC loans**: BIR RR 02-2013 requires IC loan interest rates to be at arm's length; if the rate falls below the BIR-prescribed minimum (currently ~12% per annum), the BIR can impute additional interest income and assess deficiency taxes; CFO must document and defend every IC loan rate during annual review
- **Dividend withholding tax complexity**: subsidiary-to-Holdings intercorporate dividends are exempt from withholding under CREATE (RA 11534, amended NIRC §27(D)(3)); withholding applies only to Holdings' distributions to external shareholders — 10% final withholding tax for Philippine-resident individuals, 15% for resident foreign corporations — remitted quarterly via BIR Form 1601-FQ (W90); if final withholding tax is not remitted on time, penalties accrue at 25% surcharge plus interest; cross-entity dividend cascading (Holdings receives dividends from subsidiaries, then distributes to shareholders) requires careful sequencing to avoid cash being trapped in tax withholding
- **Cash trapped in subsidiaries**: if a subsidiary (e.g., Digital Commerce Inc.) generates significant profit but IC dividend distribution is delayed by Board scheduling or legal documentation (W124), cash is trapped and unavailable for group-level debt service or capex funding; Treasury must plan IC dividend timing 60–90 days ahead
- **IC loan vs. equity classification risk**: Philippine tax authorities may recharacterize long-standing IC loans as equity contributions if there is no demonstrated repayment intent or reasonable repayment schedule; recharacterization triggers different tax treatment and potential penalties; mitigated by standard operating procedures and system-enforced validation rules

## W1201. Intercompany Monthly Settlement Batch Processing & Netting Execution

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly intercompany settlement cycle (5th business day of following month) |
| **Frequency** | Monthly |
| **Volume** | ~100–200 IC transactions/month across 5 entities; ~10 IC relationship pairs (5 entities × 4 counterparty pairs) |
| **Owner** | Chief Accountant |
| **Participants** | Chief Accountant, Controller, Treasury Manager, CFO |

### Background

BuildRight Depot Corporation's 5 legal entities generate significant intercompany (IC) transaction volume: BuildRight Depot Inc. pays rent to Property Management Inc., warehousing fees to Logistics Inc., and e-commerce fees to Digital Commerce Inc. While W14 manages individual IC transaction recording and W612 manages IC rate setting, this workflow governs the monthly settlement batch process — the netting of reciprocal IC balances across all entity pairs, the net cash settlement, and the reconciliation to zero at the consolidated level. Efficient IC settlement minimizes actual cash movement between entities and ensures clean consolidation elimination.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **IC transaction completeness verification**: System runs IC completeness check: (a) all IC transactions for the month must be posted and approved across all 5 entities, (b) system matches each IC receivable (AR-IC) with corresponding IC payable (AP-IC) by entity pair; flags unmatched entries (timing differences, missing entries, amount mismatches) | System / Chief Accountant | Controller | 1 hour |
| 2 | **Unmatched entry resolution**: For any unmatched IC entries from Step 1: Chief Accountant contacts the originating entity's accountant to resolve: (a) timing differences (entity A posted in month, entity B not yet) → entity B records accrual, (b) amount mismatch → investigate and correct, (c) missing entry → identify source and record; all mismatches must be resolved before settlement proceeds | Chief Accountant | Controller | 1–2 hours |
| 3 | **IC balance netting calculation**: System calculates net IC balance for each entity pair: (a) generates IC netting matrix showing gross receivable, gross payable, and net position for each of the 10 entity pairs, (b) identifies opportunities for multilateral netting: e.g., if Depot Inc. owes Logistics Inc. PHP 5M and Logistics Inc. owes Property Management Inc. PHP 2M and Property Management Inc. owes Depot Inc. PHP 3M, system nets to single cash movements, (c) produces proposed settlement schedule with minimum number of cash transfers | System | — | Automated |
| 4 | **Settlement approval**: Chief Accountant presents proposed settlement schedule to Controller for approval; Controller verifies: (a) all IC balances are reconciled (no unmatched entries), (b) netting calculations are correct, (c) settlement amounts are consistent with IC rate agreements per W612; CFO approves settlements > PHP 10M net | Controller | CFO (for > PHP 10M) | 30 min |
| 5 | **Cash settlement execution**: Treasury Manager executes approved cash transfers between entity bank accounts: (a) transfers net payable amounts via intra-bank transfer (BDO to BDO for same-bank entities; CHATS/RTGS for cross-bank), (b) system records: DR: AP-IC / CR: Bank (paying entity) and DR: Bank / CR: AR-IC (receiving entity), (c) ensures each entity pair settles to zero or near-zero balance (residual < PHP 10K due to rounding carried forward) | Treasury Manager | Controller | 1 hour |
| 6 | **Consolidation elimination verification**: System runs consolidation elimination routine: (a) verifies all IC revenue/expense pairs net to zero at consolidated level, (b) verifies all IC receivable/payable pairs net to zero, (c) flags any remaining IC balances for investigation, (d) generates consolidation elimination journal entries | System / Chief Accountant | Controller | 30 min |
| 7 | **Monthly IC settlement report**: Chief Accountant prepares monthly IC settlement summary for CFO: (a) total IC transaction volume by type (rent, warehousing fees, e-commerce fees, management fees), (b) gross vs. net settlement amounts, (c) number of unmatched entries resolved, (d) residual IC balances carried forward, (e) comparison to prior month and budget | Chief Accountant | CFO | 1 hour |

### System Touchpoints
- IC completeness matching engine across 5 entities (W1201.1)

## W1417. Intercompany E-Commerce Platform Per-Order Fee Monthly Calculation, Settlement & Revenue Recognition

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly intercompany settlement cycle; or month-end close deadline |
| **Frequency** | Monthly |
| **Volume** | ~42,900 ecommerce orders/month; per-order fulfillment fee charged by BuildRight Digital Commerce, Inc. to BuildRight Depot, Inc. |
| **Owner** | Controller |
| **Participants** | Controller, E-Commerce Finance Analyst, AP Clerk, AR Clerk, Consolidation Accountant |

### Background

BuildRight's corporate structure separates ecommerce operations into BuildRight Digital Commerce, Inc., which operates the web/mobile platform and collects online payments. However, revenue is recognized by BuildRight Depot, Inc. at fulfillment (pickup or delivery). Digital Commerce Inc. charges Depot Inc. a per-order fulfillment fee and remits collected payments monthly. This creates a complex intercompany settlement: (a) Digital Commerce Inc. collects customer payments for ~42,900 orders/month (~PHP 150M GMV), (b) Digital Commerce Inc. remits collected payments to Depot Inc. monthly, net of its per-order fulfillment fee, (c) the per-order fee must be at arm's length per transfer pricing rules (W612), (d) both the fee revenue (Digital Commerce) and fee expense (Depot) must eliminate in consolidation, (e) cash collected by Digital Commerce but not yet remitted is an intercompany payable. This workflow manages the monthly calculation, settlement, and revenue recognition for this intercompany e-commerce fee arrangement.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monthly order reconciliation**: E-Commerce Finance Analyst reconciles ecommerce orders for the month: (a) total orders placed on platform (Digital Commerce Inc. system) vs. total orders fulfilled (Depot Inc. ERP — goods receipt or BOPIS pickup confirmation), (b) identify unmatched orders: (i) placed but not fulfilled (customer cancelled, abandoned BOPIS pickup per W771), (ii) fulfilled but not recorded on platform (system sync error), (c) reconciled order count = basis for fee calculation | E-Commerce Finance Analyst | Controller | 3–4 hours |
| 2 | **Fee calculation**: E-Commerce Finance Analyst calculates monthly fee: (a) per-order fulfillment fee rate per intercompany service agreement (W1218), e.g., PHP 35/order for BOPIS + PHP 55/order for home delivery, (b) fee = (BOPIS orders × BOPIS rate) + (delivery orders × delivery rate) + ad-hoc services (product photography, content updates), (c) generate IC invoice: Digital Commerce Inc. → Depot Inc. | E-Commerce Finance Analyst | Controller | 1–2 hours |
| 3 | **Cash remittance**: (a) Digital Commerce Inc. remits collected customer payments to Depot Inc.: total GMV collected in the month, (b) net settlement = customer payments collected − IC fulfillment fee, (c) if net settlement is positive: Digital Commerce pays Depot Inc.; if negative (unlikely — fee exceeds collections): Depot Inc. pays Digital Commerce Inc., (d) AP/AR Clerks post IC invoice and payment per W14 (intercompany transactions) | AP Clerk / AR Clerk | Controller | 2–3 hours |
| 4 | **Revenue recognition entries**: Consolidation Accountant posts month-end entries: (a) Depot Inc.: recognizes ecommerce revenue at fulfillment point (Dr. IC Receivable / Cr. Revenue), recognizes IC fee expense (Dr. IC Platform Fee Expense / Cr. IC Payable to Digital Commerce), (b) Digital Commerce Inc.: recognizes platform fee revenue (Dr. IC Receivable from Depot / Cr. IC Platform Fee Revenue), recognizes cost of platform operations (Dr. Platform Operating Expense / Cr. Cash/AP), (c) elimination entries in consolidation: IC platform fee revenue (Digital Commerce) eliminates against IC platform fee expense (Depot) — zero net impact on consolidated P&L | Consolidation Accountant | Controller | 2–3 hours |
| 5 | **Quarterly rate review**: per W612, Controller reviews per-order fee rate quarterly: (a) compare fee rate to market benchmarks (third-party ecommerce platform fees in the Philippines: PHP 20–80/order), (b) verify Digital Commerce Inc. is covering its operating costs + reasonable margin, (c) adjust rates if needed per arm's-length documentation per W1299 | Controller | CFO | 2–3 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Ecommerce order reconciliation module: platform orders vs. ERP fulfillment records matching (W1417.1)
- IC invoice generator: automated monthly IC invoice from reconciled order counts × fee rates (W1417.2)
- IC cash remittance tracker: customer payments collected by Digital Commerce, net settlement calculation, remittance schedule (W1417.3)
- Consolidation elimination entries: IC fee revenue/expense elimination per W14 (W1417.4)
- Integration with W14 (IC transactions & settlement), W612 (IC rate setting), W1218 (IC service agreements), W1299 (transfer pricing documentation), W771 (BOPIS abandoned pickup — order reconciliation input)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Order matching complexity**: at ~42,900 orders/month, even a 0.5% mismatch rate = ~215 orders requiring manual investigation per month; causes delays in fee calculation and IC settlement
- **Timing difference in cash collection**: Digital Commerce Inc. collects payment at order placement, but Depot Inc. recognizes revenue at fulfillment (potentially days later); the float between collection and remittance creates an implicit intercompany loan that must be tracked
- **Transfer pricing scrutiny**: the per-order fee arrangement between Digital Commerce Inc. and Depot Inc. is subject to BIR transfer pricing examination; insufficient documentation or non-arm's-length rates could result in tax adjustments per W1299
- **Refund complexity**: when an ecommerce customer returns an item (online return initiated, processed in-store per W12), the refund flows through Depot Inc. but the original payment was collected by Digital Commerce Inc.; the IC settlement must account for refunded orders


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "recorded on platform" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-calculate of "calculates monthly fee" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-post of "post IC invoice and payment per W14" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-290 (ensure controlled execution — Consolidation & Intercompany (PA-17.2).)
- operational: Controller review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
- Monthly order reconciliation: 3–4 hours
- Fee calculation: 1–2 hours
- Cash remittance: 2–3 hours
- Revenue recognition entries: 2–3 hours
- Total monthly: ~8–12 hours/month
- Quarterly rate review: 2–3 hours/quarter
- Total annual: ~100–150 hours/year

### Staffing Implication
- E-Commerce Finance Analyst: ~8–10 hours/month. Absorbed within existing role.
- Controller: ~2–3 hours/month for review and approval. Absorbed.
- Consolidation Accountant: ~2–3 hours/month for elimination entries. Absorbed per W14.
- No incremental headcount.

## W1496. Intercompany Warehouse Service Fee Dispute Resolution, Rate Review & Quarterly Settlement Agreement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | One entity disputes the intercompany warehouse service fee charged by BuildRight Logistics, Inc.; also quarterly rate review cycle |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly rate review; disputes arising ~2–5 times/year (one register with W14) |
| **Volume** | Monthly intercompany service fees: PHP 50–80M across all DCs (per W1293); disputes typically involve PHP 200K–2M per incident |
| **Owner** | Intercompany Accountant |
| **Participants** | Intercompany Accountant, DC Controller, CFO (Logistics), CFO (Depot), Tax Manager |

### Background

BuildRight's corporate structure creates significant intercompany transaction volume. BuildRight Logistics, Inc. (BRL) operates the 4 distribution centers on behalf of BuildRight Depot, Inc. (BRD), which owns all inventory. BRL charges BRD a monthly warehouse service fee covering: storage (per pallet/sqm), receiving (per receipt), picking (per line item), packing (per order), and distribution (per delivery). These fees must be at arm's-length transfer pricing per BIR Transfer Pricing Guidelines (Revenue Regulation No. 02-2013) and the entities' Transfer Pricing Documentation. Disputes arise when: (a) BRD questions the accuracy of activity-based fee calculations (e.g., number of pallets stored, lines picked); (b) BRL claims costs increased due to volume surges or special handling not captured in the standard rate card; (c) fee calculation methodology disagreements (should overtime labor during seasonal surges be included in the standard rate or charged as a pass-through?).

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monthly intercompany fee calculation & invoicing**: (a) BRL's DC Controller calculates monthly warehouse service fee per DC: (i) storage fee = pallet-days × rate per pallet per day; (ii) receiving fee = number of receipts × rate per receipt; (iii) picking fee = line items picked × rate per line; (iv) packing fee = orders packed × rate per order; (v) distribution fee = deliveries made × rate per delivery × distance zone; (b) calculation supported by WMS (Warehouse Management System) activity logs; (c) BRL generates intercompany invoice; (d) BRD's Intercompany Accountant receives invoice and supporting detail | DC Controller (BRL) | CFO (BRL) | 2–3 days after month-end |
| 2 | **Fee verification & dispute initiation**: (a) Intercompany Accountant (BRD) reconciles BRL invoice against: (i) WMS activity reports (pallet count, receipt count, pick lines, orders, deliveries); (ii) standard rate card per the Intercompany Service Agreement; (iii) prior month trends (are volumes consistent?); (b) discrepancies identified: (i) volume variances > 5% from prior month without corresponding sales change; (ii) rate applied different from agreed rate card; (iii) charges for activities not performed (e.g., distribution fee for a month when BRL used third-party hauler at BRD's cost); (c) if discrepancy found: dispute notice issued to BRL within 10 business days of invoice receipt | Intercompany Accountant (BRD) | CFO (BRD) | 1–2 days |
| 3 | **Dispute investigation & resolution**: (a) both parties review WMS logs and supporting documents together; (b) common dispute causes: (i) WMS data error (pallets counted twice, ghost receipts); (ii) rate card misunderstanding (rate per unit vs. rate per case); (iii) unmetered activities (manual putaway not captured in WMS); (c) resolution options: (i) credit note from BRL for overcharge; (ii) supplementary invoice from BRL for undercharge; (iii) agreement to adjust methodology prospectively; (d) if parties cannot agree: CFO of both entities meet to resolve; (e) resolution documented with root cause and preventive action | Intercompany Accountant (BRD) / DC Controller (BRL) | CFO (both entities) | 1–5 days per dispute |
| 4 | **Quarterly rate card review & adjustment**: Quarterly: (a) Intercompany Accountant prepares rate card review: (i) actual costs incurred by BRL vs. rates charged to BRD; (ii) BRL profit margin on intercompany services (must be within arm's-length range per Transfer Pricing Documentation); (iii) volume trends affecting unit costs (economies of scale); (iv) any new cost components (new DC, new service, labor rate changes); (b) rate adjustments proposed if actual cost recovery deviates > 5% from target; (c) adjusted rate card approved by both VPs Finance; (d) Tax Manager reviews adjusted rates for transfer pricing compliance; (e) updated rate card effective following quarter | Intercompany Accountant / Tax Manager | CFO (both entities) | 1–2 days/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- WMS activity logging: pallet-days, receipts, pick lines, orders, deliveries per DC (W1495)
- Automated intercompany fee calculation engine applying rate card to WMS activity data (W1495)
- Intercompany invoice generation with supporting detail attachment (W1495)
- Dispute management module with notice issuance, investigation tracking, and resolution documentation (W235)
- Quarterly rate card management with version control and approval workflow (W1728)
- Transfer pricing compliance dashboard monitoring BRL margin on intercompany services (W1728)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **WMS data accuracy**: the entire fee calculation depends on accurate WMS activity logging; if warehouse associates skip scanning steps (e.g., manual putaway not logged), volumes are understated and BRL undercharges
- **Transfer pricing audit risk**: BIR may challenge the intercompany pricing arrangement; maintaining robust Transfer Pricing Documentation with benchmarking analysis is essential; mitigated by CTL-290 (controlled execution — Consolidation & Intercompany (PA-17.2))
- **Relationship tension**: disputes between BRL and BRD are inherently political since both entities are under the same holding company but have different P&L responsibilities; CFO mediation required for significant disputes
- **Rate card lag**: if costs increase rapidly (e.g., fuel, minimum wage increase), the quarterly rate card review may lag behind actual cost increases, temporarily squeezing BRL's margin


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of elimination entries to the consolidation journal (replaces manual Step 1 postings).
- System auto-reconcile of "reconciles BRL invoice against" the IC subledger (replaces manual Step 2 matching).
- System auto-assemble of the intercompany documentation pack (agreements, elimination schedules, sign-offs) (replaces manual Step 3 assembly).

### Controls
- CTL-290 (ensure controlled execution — Consolidation & Intercompany (PA-17.2).)
- operational: CFO mediation and documented resolution for significant IC disputes, with outcome logged to the IC dispute register

### Staffing Implication
- Intercompany Accountant: ~2–3 days/month on fee verification + quarterly rate review. Absorbed.
- DC Controller (BRL): ~2–3 days/month on fee calculation. Absorbed.
- Tax Manager: ~0.5 day/quarter on transfer pricing review. Absorbed.
- No incremental headcount.

### Time Estimate
- Monthly calculation & verification: 4–6 days (split between BRL and BRD)
- Per dispute: 1–5 days
- Quarterly rate review: 1–2 days
- **Annual: ~70–100 days total across all involved roles**
