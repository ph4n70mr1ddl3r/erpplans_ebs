## W9. Financial Close & Reporting

### W9A. Month-End Close

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Last calendar day of the month |
| **Frequency** | Monthly |
| **Volume** | 5 entities × 12 months = 60 entity-month closes/year |
| **Owner** | Chief Accountant / Controller |
| **Participants** | Chief Accountant, AP Supervisor, AR Supervisor, Treasury Analyst, Tax Accountant, Cost Accountant, GL Accountant, CFO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Verify all store day-end closings are posted; follow up on any outstanding | Chief Accountant | Controller | 2 hours |
| 2 | AP: Ensure all vendor invoices received before cut-off are entered and matched | AP Supervisor | Controller | 2 hours |
| 2a | GRNI reconciliation: system generates Goods Received Not Invoiced report showing all GRs without matching AP invoices; Finance verifies completeness; if system uses GR/IR clearing account, verify clearing account balance reconciles to open GRNI list; accrue provision for estimated costs where invoices are expected but not yet received | AP Supervisor / Chief Accountant | Controller | 1 hour |
| 3 | AR: Post all AR invoices and payments received through month-end | AR Clerk | Controller | 1 hour |
| 4 | Accruals: Book accrual entries for expenses incurred but not yet invoiced (utilities, freight, rent) | Chief Accountant | Controller | 3 hours |
| 5 | Intercompany: Verify all IC transactions are posted; run IC matching | Chief Accountant | Controller | 2 hours |
| 5a | FX Revaluation: System revalues all open foreign-currency AP and AR balances at month-end BIR exchange rate; posts unrealized FX gain/loss to P&L and FX revaluation reserve to BS; auto-reverses the revaluation entries at start of next period | System / Chief Accountant | Controller | Automated + 30 min review |
| 6 | Inventory: Verify perpetual WAC (weighted average cost) calculations; post adjustments from cycle counts | Cost Accountant | Controller | 2 hours |
| 6a | WAC methodology: system calculates perpetual moving average cost at each goods receipt — new WAC = (prior inventory value + receipt value) ÷ (prior quantity + receipt quantity); COGS at POS uses current WAC at time of sale; at month-end, Cost Accountant verifies WAC accuracy by sampling high-value and high-volume SKUs, reconciles total inventory valuation to GL inventory accounts, and posts any adjustments from cycle counts (W6) or physical inventory (W42) | Cost Accountant | Controller | Part of 2 hours |
| 7 | Fixed Assets: Post monthly depreciation | Cost Accountant | Controller | 30 min |
| 8 | Prepayments: Amortize prepaid expenses (insurance, rent) | Chief Accountant | Controller | 30 min |
| 9 | Bank reconciliation: Match bank statements to GL cash accounts (all bank accounts, all entities) — executed by the entity GL Accountant independent of payment execution per W89 (the roles that initiate, approve or release payment files never reconcile the accounts they pay from); Treasury retains the daily operational auto-match in W30 | GL Accountant | Controller | 3 hours |
| 10 | Run trial balance per entity; review for unusual balances | Chief Accountant | Controller | 1 hour |
| 11 | Post adjusting entries as needed | Chief Accountant | Controller | 1 hour |
| 12 | Generate preliminary financial statements per entity (BS, IS, CF) | System / Chief Accountant | Controller | 30 min |
| 13 | Run intercompany elimination entries for consolidation | Chief Accountant | CFO | 1 hour |
| 14 | Generate consolidated financial statements (5 entities) | System / Chief Accountant | CFO | 30 min |
| 15 | CFO reviews consolidated statements; queries variances | CFO | CFO | 1 hour |
| 16 | Tax Accountant prepares VAT return (BIR 2550M) and withholding tax returns (1601-EQ, 1601-C) | Tax Accountant | CFO | 3 hours |
| 16a | EWT remittance (quarter-end months): Tax Accountant generates EWT remittance file (BIR 1601-EQ) from accumulated Withholding Tax Payable per vendor per ATC; reconciles to vendor remittance list; transmits to BIR via eFPS; system posts remittance (Dr. Withholding Tax Payable / Cr. Cash) | Tax Accountant | CFO | 1 hour |
| 16b | Inventory net realizable value (NRV) review: Cost Accountant runs inventory aging report (days since last sale); for slow-moving items (> 180 days), system compares WAC to estimated NRV (clearance price × sell-through probability); Cost Accountant proposes write-down per SKU where NRV < cost; Controller approves write-downs > PHP 50,000; system posts Dr. Inventory Write-Down Expense / Cr. Inventory Provision (contra-asset); **NRV reversal treatment**: when a written-down item is subsequently sold — (a) system recognizes revenue at selling price (standard), (b) system recognizes COGS at the written-down carrying amount (not the original WAC), (c) difference between original WAC and written-down carrying amount represents the write-down previously recognized; for partial sales of written-down lots, COGS per unit is the written-down unit cost; no additional journal entry is required at point of sale because the provision was already posted; at next quarterly NRV review (W9A.16b), Cost Accountant reassesses remaining written-down items — if NRV has recovered above carrying amount (e.g., market price increase), Cost Accountant may reverse the provision up to the original write-down amount per PAS 2.31 with Controller approval; system posts reversal as Dr. Inventory Provision / Cr. Inventory Write-Down Recovery (P&L); reversal is limited to the original write-down amount per SKU — carrying value cannot exceed original WAC | Cost Accountant / Controller | Controller | 2 hours |
| 16c | Local Business Tax (LBT) per LGU: system generates LBT payment schedule per location (200 stores + 4 DCs + HQ) based on configured LGU calendars (annual or quarterly per LGU); Tax Accountant reviews schedule, validates amounts per LGU rate schedules (typically 1–2% of gross receipts for retail, varies by LGU); processes payments per LGU (online, over-the-counter, or LGU office); system posts payment per location (Dr. Local Business Tax Expense / Cr. Cash); tracks payment status and receipt per location; flags locations with upcoming or overdue LBT payments on monthly dashboard | Tax Accountant | Controller | 4 hours/month (distributed across LGU due dates) |
| 16d | Inventory write-off (obsolete / damaged beyond recovery): distinct from NRV write-down (step 16b) — for items that will never be sold (fully obsolete, expired, irrevocably damaged, or vendor refuses RTV and item has no residual value); Cost Accountant prepares write-off list per SKU with supporting evidence (photos, aging, disposition attempts); tiered approval: Store Manager ≤ PHP 10,000, Controller ≤ PHP 50,000, CFO ≤ PHP 500,000, CEO > PHP 500,000; system posts Dr. Inventory Write-Off Expense (loss) / Cr. Inventory — removes items from inventory register entirely (not a provision — permanent removal); BIR documentation retained: write-off approval, supporting evidence, and physical destruction or disposal certificate | Cost Accountant / Controller | CFO | 1 hour |
| 16e | Credit note / debit note reconciliation: Chief Accountant runs AP and AR credit note aging report — all unapplied credit memos and debit memos listed by entity, vendor/customer, age, and amount; credit memos > 60 days unapplied flagged for investigation; AP Clerk contacts vendor to resolve open AP credits (apply to next invoice, request refund, or confirm as settled); AR Clerk contacts customers to resolve open AR credits (apply to next sale, issue refund, or confirm as settled); unresolved items > 90 days escalated to Controller; summary of unapplied credit notes included in month-end close package | Chief Accountant / AP Clerk / AR Clerk | Controller | 1 hour |
| 17 | Final close: verify the close ran in the suite's sequence — sub-ledger periods (Inventory, PO, AP, AR and Cash Management) are closed and fully transferred and reconciled to GL (bank per W89, fixed assets per W1705, payroll bank file per W10) BEFORE the GL period locks, GL closes last; then lock the GL period; at month-open the new period's sub-ledgers open first (Inventory → PO → AP → AR) and GL last, so no transaction posts to a closed period | Controller | CFO | 15 min |

**Target**: Close within 5 working days of month-end

### W9B. Year-End Close

Additional steps on top of month-end close (December):

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18 | 13th month pay accrual reconciliation | Payroll Accountant | Controller | 2 hours |
| 19 | Physical inventory (wall-to-wall) count and adjustment | Cost Accountant + all store staff | CFO | 1–2 days |
| 20 | Year-end tax provisions and adjustments | Tax Accountant | Controller | 4 hours |
| 21 | Annual income tax return preparation (BIR 1702RT) | Tax Accountant | CFO | 1 week |
| 22 | External audit preparation and coordination | Controller + CFO | CEO | Ongoing (Q1) |
| 23 | SEC annual report preparation | Controller | CFO | 1 week |

### System Touchpoints
- Store day-end posting monitoring (W9A.1)
- GRNI reconciliation report and clearing account balance verification (W9A.2a)
- Accrual and journal entry workflow (W9A.4–8, 11)
- IC matching and elimination automation (W9A.5, 13)
- Inventory valuation engine: perpetual WAC verification (not periodic recalculation); month-end reconciliation of inventory valuation to GL (W9A.6a)
- Bank reconciliation (W9A.9) — by the entity GL Accountant independent of payment execution per W89
- Local Business Tax (LBT) per LGU: per-location LBT calendar, payment tracking and schedule; LGU-specific rate schedules and forms; payment status dashboard with overdue alerting; BIR retention of LBT receipts (W9A.16c)
- Multi-entity financial statement generation, published and distributed to subscribers via Oracle Report Manager — FSG report publishing, scheduling, user-to-value secured distribution (fit-gap H18, batch 27) (W9A.12–14)
- Period lock / close controls (W9A.17) — sub-ledger periods closed and reconciled before GL; GL locks last; new period opens sub-ledgers (Inventory → PO → AP → AR) before GL
- BIR tax form generation (W9A.16)
- EWT remittance file generation per BIR 1601-EQ (quarterly) with per-vendor per-ATC breakdown; eFPS transmission (W9A.16a)
- Inventory NRV review: aging by days-since-last-sale, NRV calculator, write-down journal entry with approval, provision reversal on subsequent sale (PAS 2.31 compliant — reversal limited to original write-down; carrying value capped at original WAC; quarterly reassessment with Cost Accountant and Controller review) (W9A.16b)
- Inventory write-off: complete removal from inventory register (not provision); tiered approval with BIR-compliant documentation (photos, aging, disposition evidence, destruction certificate); distinct from NRV write-down (W9A.16d)
- Credit note / debit note reconciliation: AP and AR credit note aging report with unapplied memos > 60 days flagged; escalation at 90 days; resolution tracking (apply, refund, settle); summary in month-end close package (W9A.16e)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Store day-end posting delays blocking close start**: if any of 200 stores fail to post day-end closings before month-end cut-off, the Chief Accountant cannot begin close activities; store-level posting compliance varies, with provincial stores frequently submitting late
- **GRNI valuation uncertainty during close**: estimating accruals for goods received but not yet invoiced requires judgment; over-accrual understates COGS while under-accrual understates liabilities — both create audit findings
- **Intercompany mismatch blocking consolidation**: unresolved IC discrepancies across 5 entity pairs prevent consolidated statement generation; even small timing differences (PHP 100) block automated elimination entries until manually resolved
- **NRV write-down subjectivity**: determining NRV for slow-moving inventory involves estimating clearance prices and sell-through probabilities; Cost Accountant and Controller may disagree on write-down amounts, especially for seasonal merchandise with uncertain residual value
- **Tax filing deadline compression**: BIR VAT return (2550M) is due by the 20th of the following month, while the close target is 5 working days — this leaves only ~10 days for tax extraction, return preparation, review, and eFPS filing


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "Verify all store day-end closings are posted" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-capture of "entered and matched" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-count of "count, verify clearing account balance reconciles to open GRNI list" (replaces manual Step 2a).

### Controls
- CTL-292 (ensure controlled execution — FP&A & Reporting (PA-17.4).)
- operational: Chief Accountant / Controller review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
- Store day-end verification and follow-up: ~2 hours (day 1)
- AP invoice cut-off and GRNI reconciliation: ~3 hours (day 1)
- AR invoice posting: ~1 hour (day 1)
- Accruals and prepayments: ~3.5 hours (day 2)
- IC matching and FX revaluation: ~2.5 hours (day 2)
- Inventory WAC verification and adjustments: ~2 hours (day 2–3)
- Bank reconciliation (all entities): ~3 hours (day 3)
- Trial balance, adjusting entries, financial statements: ~2.5 hours (day 3–4)
- Consolidation and elimination entries: ~1.5 hours (day 4)
- CFO review and consolidation sign-off: ~1 hour (day 4–5)
- Tax return preparation (VAT, EWT, CWT): ~5 hours (day 4–5)
- Period lock: ~15 min (day 5)
- NRV review, write-offs, credit note reconciliation: ~4 hours (day 4–5)
- Total close effort: ~30–35 hours over 5 working days across 8–10 people

### Pain Points / Risks (Year-End Close — W9B)
- **External audit timeline compression**: BIR and SEC filing deadlines are fixed; if external auditors (W95) identify issues during Q1 fieldwork, the Finance team must resolve queries while also processing Q1 monthly close, creating severe capacity strain on the ~8–10 people already committed to close activities
- **Wall-to-wall physical inventory disruption**: year-end physical count (step 19) requires all 200 stores + 4 DCs to pause or slow operations for 1–2 days; scheduling a chain-wide count during the holiday retail peak in December is impractical, forcing a January count that creates audit window adjustments
- **13th month pay and statutory bonus reconciliation**: PD 851 mandates 13th month pay for all employees; reconciling accruals against actual payments across ~6,932 employees in 5 entities is error-prone, especially for employees who joined or separated mid-year (W43)
- **Multi-entity consolidation complexity**: year-end elimination entries across 5 entities with goods-based IC transfers (W14), IC loans, and IC dividends require careful matching; any unresolved IC mismatch blocks consolidated statement finalization

### Staffing Implication
- **Controller**: Owns the close process. 1 person sufficient with support.
- **1 Chief Accountant**: Handles most close entries. Stretched during close week (~30 hours of close work in 5 days).
- **1 Cost Accountant**: Inventory valuation + fixed assets.
- **1 Tax Accountant**: VAT + withholding monthly; income tax quarterly/annual.
- **Total Finance team (46 per profile §3.3)**: During close week, 8–10 people are heavily involved. The rest of the month is lighter. Current headcount supports this.

## W21. Capital Expenditure (Capex) Request & Approval

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Need identified for asset acquisition (equipment, vehicles, fixtures, IT hardware, store build-out) |
| **Frequency** | ~50–80 capex requests/year |
| **Volume** | Peaks during new store openings (W16) and annual budget cycle |
| **Owner** | Requesting Department Head |
| **Participants** | Requestor, Department Head, Finance (Capex Analyst), CFO, CEO/Board |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Requestor submits capex request: item description, justification, quotations (min. 3), expected useful life, budget code | Requestor | Dept. Head | 30 min |
| 2 | Department Head reviews and approves or returns for revision | Dept. Head | Dept. Head | 15 min |
| 3 | Finance (Capex Analyst) validates against approved annual budget; checks ROI calculation | Capex Analyst | CFO | 30 min |
| 4 | Route for approval per tiered matrix (see below) | System | As per tier | 15–30 min |
| 5 | Approved capex → Purchase Order created (standard PO workflow W2) or direct purchase | Buyer / Requestor | Dept. Head | Per W2 |
| 6 | Goods/asset received; Goods Receipt posted | Receiving Clerk / IT | Dept. Head | Per W3 |
| 7 | Finance capitalizes asset in Fixed Asset module: asset tag, location, depreciation schedule, useful life | Cost Accountant | Controller | 15 min/asset |
| 8 | System begins monthly depreciation per schedule | System | — | Automated |
| 9 | Post-implementation review: actual vs. budgeted cost; in-service date | Capex Analyst | CFO | Quarterly |

### Approval Matrix

| Amount | Approval Required |
|---|---|
| ≤ PHP 100,000 | Finance Manager |
| PHP 100,001 – 500,000 | CFO |
| PHP 500,001 – 5,000,000 | CEO |
| > PHP 5,000,000 | Board of Directors |

### System Touchpoints
- Capex request form with quotation attachments (W21.1)
- Tiered approval workflow (W21.4)
- Budget availability check against annual capex budget (W21.3)
- PO creation from approved capex (W21.5)
- Fixed Asset creation and capitalization (W21.7)
- Depreciation schedule auto-generation (W21.8)
- Capex vs. budget reporting (W21.9)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Quotation gaming for approval threshold avoidance**: requestors may split a PHP 600,000 purchase into two PHP 300,000 requests to stay below the CEO approval threshold (PHP 500,001); without system-level detection of split purchases from the same vendor within a time window, approval tier circumvention goes undetected
- **Capitalization delay for new store build-outs**: during rapid expansion (10–15 new stores/year), hundreds of contractor invoices accumulate against AUC accounts (W276); if the Engineering/Project Manager delays operational handover confirmation (W227), assets remain in AUC without depreciation, understating operating expenses
- **Post-implementation review neglect**: quarterly capex vs. budget reviews (step 9) are often deprioritized in favor of urgent operational issues; without consistent post-implementation discipline, cost overruns on completed projects go undetected and inform future budget estimates inaccurately
- **Fixed asset classification errors**: distinguishing between capitalize (asset) and expense (repair/maintenance) requires judgment; Store Managers may request capex for items that should be expensed (e.g., replacement parts vs. new equipment), inflating the asset base and distorting depreciation schedules


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-submit of "submits capex request" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "reviews and approves or returns for revision" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "checks ROI calculation" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-02 (prevent unauthorized capital expenditure.)
- operational: Requesting Department Head review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
- Capex request preparation: ~30 min/request (requestor)
- Department Head review: ~15 min/request
- Finance validation (budget check, ROI): ~30 min/request
- Approval routing per tier: ~15–30 min/request
- Fixed asset capitalization (post-receipt): ~15 min/asset
- Post-implementation review (quarterly): ~2–4 hours/quarter (Capex Analyst)
- Total capex processing: ~50–80 requests/year × ~1.5 hours avg = ~75–120 hours/year (~6–10 hours/month peak during store openings)

### Staffing Implication
- **1 Capex Analyst** (within Finance): 50–80 requests/year. Each requires ~30 min validation. ~40 hours/year = ~1 day/month. Absorbed by existing Finance team (46 per profile §3.3). May be the Cost Accountant or a dedicated analyst role during peak store-opening periods.

## W26. Annual Budget Preparation & Monthly Variance Review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual budget calendar (typically Q4 for following year) |
| **Frequency** | Annual budget cycle; monthly variance review |
| **Volume** | 5 entities × 200+ cost centers; ~500–800 GL accounts per entity |
| **Owner** | Controller |
| **Participants** | Controller, CFO, Department Heads, Category Managers (merchandising budget), VP Store Operations (store-level budget), HR (payroll budget), IT (capex budget) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CFO issues budget circular with guidelines, assumptions (revenue growth, inflation, headcount plan, new store openings) | CFO | CEO | 1 week |
| 2 | Department Heads prepare department-level budget: opex, headcount, capex requests | Dept. Heads | VP / C-Suite | 2–3 weeks |
| 3 | Category Managers prepare merchandise purchase budget by category (COGS, margin targets, inventory levels) | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1–2 weeks |
| 4 | VP Store Operations prepares store-level P&L budgets (revenue per store, labor, utilities, shrinkage) | VP Store Operations | COO | 1–2 weeks |
| 5 | HR prepares payroll budget (salary adjustments, new hires, statutory increases) | HR Head | CHRO | 1 week |
| 6 | Controller consolidates all department budgets into draft company budget per entity | Controller | CFO | 1 week |
| 7 | CFO and CEO review consolidated draft; negotiate adjustments with Department Heads | CFO / CEO | CEO | 1–2 weeks |
| 8 | Board approves annual budget | Board | CEO | 1 meeting |
| 9 | System loads approved budget per entity, per cost center, per GL account (monthly phasing) | Controller / System | CFO | 2–3 days |
| 10 | Monthly: Controller generates budget vs. actual report per entity and consolidated | Controller | CFO | 2 hours/month |
| 11 | Department Heads review their variances; provide explanations for material deviations (> 10%) | Dept. Heads | VP / C-Suite | 1 hour/month each |
| 12 | CFO reviews consolidated variance report; identifies risks and corrective actions | CFO | CEO | 1 hour/month |
| 13 | Quarterly: if material changes warranted, Controller prepares budget revision for CFO/Board approval | Controller | CFO | 4 hours/quarter |

**Total annual budget cycle**: ~8–10 weeks (Q4)
**Monthly variance review**: ~1 day total effort across Finance and Department Heads

### System Touchpoints
- GL budget entry by department, cost center, GL account with monthly phasing (W26.2–5)
- Consolidation of department budgets into entity and group budgets (W26.6)
- GL budget loading and lock-down after Board approval (W26.9)
- Budget vs. actual reporting with variance % and drill-down (W26.10–11); budgetary-control funds checking on the capex chain (fit-gap F6)
- Budget revision workflow with approval (W26.13)
- Budget availability check during PO and capex creation (links to W2, W21)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Top-down vs. bottom-up budget misalignment**: CFO/CEO revenue growth expectations may not align with Department Heads' operational reality; negotiations during step 7 are contentious, especially for Store Ops (labor costs) and Merchandising (margin targets), leading to budgets that are either unrealistic or built on forced assumptions
- **Budget rigidity during rapid expansion**: opening 10–15 new stores/year (W16) requires frequent budget revisions (step 13); the quarterly revision cadence may not be fast enough to accommodate mid-quarter new store decisions, leading to capex and opex spending without approved budget coverage
- **Cost center proliferation**: with 200 stores, 4 DCs, HQ departments, and 5 entities, the budget structure has 200+ cost centers and 500–800 GL accounts per entity; maintaining consistent chart of accounts and cost center definitions across entities requires constant governance that is often deprioritized
- **Variance analysis superficiality**: Department Heads may provide generic variance explanations ("due to volume increase") rather than root cause analysis; without CFO challenge during monthly reviews, corrective actions are not identified and budget accuracy does not improve year over year


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "headcount plan, new store openings" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-generate of "prepare department-level budget" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "review consolidated draft" (replaces manual Step 7).

### Controls
- CTL-35 (ensure budget adherence.)
- operational: Controller review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
- Annual budget cycle (Q4): ~8–10 weeks total
  - CFO budget circular preparation: ~1 week
  - Department budget preparation: ~2–3 weeks (Dept. Heads)
  - Merchandise purchase budget: ~1–2 weeks (Category Managers)
  - Store-level P&L budgets: ~1–2 weeks (VP Store Operations)
  - Payroll budget: ~1 week (HR)
  - Consolidation and review: ~1 week (Controller)
  - CEO/Board negotiation and approval: ~1–2 weeks
  - System loading: ~2–3 days (Controller)
- Monthly variance review: ~1 day total effort (~2 hours Controller + ~1 hour each Dept. Head × 10–15 departments + ~1 hour CFO)
- Quarterly budget revision: ~4 hours (Controller) + ~2 hours (CFO review)

### Staffing Implication
- **Controller**: Owns the budget process. ~30 hours during annual cycle + ~4 hours/month for variance review.
- **Department Heads**: ~3–5 hours each during annual cycle + ~1 hour/month variance review. Absorbed into existing management duties.
- No additional headcount needed.

## W89. Bank Reconciliation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Bank statements received (monthly paper/electronic from BDO, BPI, Metrobank, Chinabank across 5 entities) |
| **Frequency** | Monthly per bank account per entity; ~210 bank accounts (5 entity main operating accounts, 5 USD import accounts, ~200 store deposit accounts for daily cash collection per W30/W1202, plus DC and payroll accounts) |
| **Volume** | ~3,000–4,000 bank statement lines/month across all accounts |
| **Owner** | GL Accountant |
| **Participants** | GL Accountant, Treasury Analyst, Controller |

### Background

Bank reconciliation is a fundamental financial control ensuring that the general ledger cash balances match actual bank balances. With 5 legal entities, ~25 bank accounts across 4 banks (BDO, BPI, Metrobank, Chinabank), multi-currency (PHP and USD), and high transaction volume from store deposits, vendor payments, payroll crediting, and ecommerce settlement, a structured reconciliation workflow is essential. The reconciliation is prepared by the entity GL Accountant — a role independent of payment execution (the Treasury Analyst and AP Clerk payment-file seats of W320/W1362 hold no reconciliation duty) — so the people who move the cash never clear the account they moved it from; Treasury retains the daily operational auto-match inside W30 cash positioning, which is visibility, not the monthly accounting control. This workflow is distinct from W30 (daily treasury cash position management) which focuses on operational cash visibility; bank reconciliation is the periodic accounting control that validates GL accuracy.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Statement import**: GL Accountant imports electronic bank statements (BDO, BPI, Metrobank, Chinabank formats) into Cash Management (CE, fit-gap A4); for accounts receiving paper statements only, the GL Accountant scans and enters key transactions manually — the statement load sits with the reconciler so no payment-execution role touches reconciliation data | GL Accountant | Controller | 30 min/account |
| 2 | **Auto-matching — system level**: System attempts to auto-match bank statement lines to GL transactions: (a) vendor payment (W7) matched by payment reference and amount, (b) customer receipt (W8) matched by deposit reference, (c) payroll bank file (W10) matched by batch reference and total, (d) intercompany settlement (W14) matched by IC reference, (e) treasury sweeps and transfers (W30) matched by transfer reference, (f) tax payments (W90) matched by BIR payment reference, (g) capex payments (W21) matched by PO/invoice reference | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | **Review auto-matched items**: GL Accountant reviews auto-matched items for accuracy; system displays match confidence score (high: exact reference + amount match; medium: amount match only; low: fuzzy match); high-confidence matches auto-accepted; medium and low reviewed manually | GL Accountant | Controller | 15 min/account |
| 4 | **Manual matching — unmatched items**: GL Accountant investigates unmatched bank statement lines: (a) bank charges and fees (not in GL — post as bank charges expense), (b) deposit discrepancies (store cash deposit variance per W5F), (c) timing differences (checks issued but not yet cleared), (d) unknown items requiring investigation, (e) payment gateway settlement deposits (cross-reference W99), (f) interest income/expense (post as incurred) | GL Accountant | Controller | 30 min/account |

Store Managers need actionable financial visibility to manage their P&L, Regional Managers need comparative store performance, and CFO needs consolidated chain performance. A manually generated store P&L process would require 200 custom reports monthly — impractical without automation. The ERP must auto-generate store P&Ls with standardized cost allocation, enabling consistent comparison across the chain.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System auto-generates store-level P&L after month-end close is finalized (W9A completion trigger): (a) **Revenue**: POS sales by department (Lumber, Plumbing/Electrical, Tiles, Tools/Hardware, Paint, Appliances, Garden, Other) + ecommerce revenue allocated to ship-from-store location + trade account revenue + installation service revenue; (b) **Cost of Goods Sold**: WAC-based inventory cost per item sold, allocated by store; includes landed cost components (freight, duty, insurance) per SKU; (c) **Gross Margin**: revenue minus COGS by department and overall; (d) **Direct Store Operating Expenses**: store labor cost (from payroll W10 allocation), rent (to Property Mgmt Inc. per W14), utilities (electricity, water, internet), store-level maintenance and supplies (W47), store-level LP costs, depreciation of store fixtures and equipment; (e) **Contribution Margin**: gross margin minus direct store operating expenses; (f) **Allocated Overhead**: regional management cost, corporate overhead allocation (IT, HR, Finance, Marketing) allocated by revenue or selling area; (g) **Store Net Income**: contribution margin minus allocated overhead | System | FP&A Manager | Automated (generation ~5 min per store; total ~17 hours for 200 stores — batch processed) |
| 2 | System generates variance analysis: current month vs. prior month, current month vs. same month prior year, current month vs. budget (W26), and year-to-date vs. budget; variances flagged as favorable (green) or unfavorable (red) with percentage deviation; material variances (> 5% of line item or > PHP 100,000) highlighted for investigation | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | FP&A Analyst reviews auto-generated P&Ls for anomalies: negative margins, missing cost allocations, revenue that doesn't reconcile to POS totals (W5B), COGS that doesn't reconcile to inventory movements (W3/W4/W18); investigates and corrects data issues with Controller support | FP&A Analyst | FP&A Manager | 4 hours/month |
| 4 | System generates regional P&L roll-ups: 5 regions (Mindanao, Visayas, Luzon-outside-NCR, Metro Manila, North/Central Luzon per profile §3.1) aggregating individual store P&Ls; includes inter-store comparison metrics: revenue per sqm, gross margin %, labor cost as % of revenue, contribution margin per employee | System | — | Automated |
| 5 | FP&A Analyst publishes store P&L packets: (a) individual store P&L with variance analysis sent to Store Manager and Regional Manager via ERP portal; (b) regional summary with store ranking sent to VP Store Operations; (c) chain consolidated P&L with regional comparison sent to CFO and executive team | FP&A Analyst | FP&A Manager | 2 hours/month |
| 6 | **Store Manager P&L review**: Store Manager reviews their store's P&L within 5 business days of publication; investigates material unfavorable variances; documents explanations for gross margin erosion (markdowns, shrinkage), labor cost overruns (overtime per W1032), or utility spikes; creates corrective action plan for top 3 unfavorable variances | Store Manager | Regional Manager | 1–2 hours/month |
| 7 | **Regional Manager store review**: Regional Manager reviews all store P&Ls in their region (40–60 stores); identifies bottom 5 stores by contribution margin for targeted coaching; schedules monthly P&L review calls with bottom-performing Store Managers | Regional Manager | VP Store Operations | 4–6 hours/month |
| 8 | **Quarterly**: FP&A Manager prepares store P&L trend analysis for CFO QBR (W231): top/bottom quartile stores by contribution margin, stores with 3+ consecutive months of negative contribution margin (flagged for potential closure evaluation), new store ramp-up performance vs. business case (W16), store-level ROI vs. chain average | FP&A Manager | CFO | 8 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Automated store P&L generation engine: revenue (POS, ecommerce, trade), COGS (WAC-based), direct expenses (payroll, rent, utilities, maintenance), contribution margin, allocated overhead, net income (W1405.1)
- Variance analysis engine: current vs. prior month, current vs. same month PY, YTD vs. budget; materiality thresholds configurable per line item (W1405.2)
- Regional P&L roll-up with inter-store comparison metrics: revenue/sqm, gross margin %, labor cost %, contribution margin/employee (W1405.4)
- Store P&L portal: Store Manager and Regional Manager access individual store and regional P&L with drill-down to department and expense category (W1405.5)
- Integration with W9A (month-end close — trigger), W5B (POS — revenue data), W10 (payroll — labor cost allocation), W14 (IC rent — rent expense), W47 (facility maintenance — maintenance expense), W26 (budget — variance comparison), W231 (QBR — quarterly trend analysis), W67 (store performance review — operational KPI feed)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Overhead allocation disputes**: Store Managers may dispute their allocated overhead share (corporate IT, HR, Marketing costs distributed by revenue or sqm); a small store with PHP 15M/month revenue absorbs the same overhead rate as a PHP 35M/month store, making small stores appear less profitable
- **Rent expense distortion**: stores leasing from BuildRight Property Management Inc. at varying rates (older leases vs. newer leases in premium locations) create P&L comparison distortion that is unrelated to store operating performance
- **COGS timing mismatches**: if inventory adjustments, purchase price variances, or inter-DC transfers (W22) are posted after the store P&L is generated, the COGS and margin figures will be inaccurate; FP&A Analyst must verify all inventory transactions are finalized before P&L generation
- **Store Manager financial literacy**: many Store Managers are operations-focused and may struggle to interpret P&L line items, variances, and contribution margin concepts; Regional Manager coaching is essential for P&L to drive action


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "account revenue + installation service revenue" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-generate of "generates variance analysis" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews auto-generated P&Ls for anomalies" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-292 (ensure controlled execution — FP&A & Reporting (PA-17.4).)
- operational: FP&A Manager review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
- System auto-generation: ~17 hours (batch) — automated, no human time
- FP&A anomaly review: ~4 hours/month
- P&L publication and distribution: ~2 hours/month
- Store Manager review: ~1–2 hours/store/month × 200 stores = 200–400 hours/month chain-wide
- Regional Manager review: ~4–6 hours/month × 4 regions = 16–24 hours/month
- Quarterly trend analysis: ~8 hours/quarter
- Total monthly human effort: ~225–430 hours/month (mostly Store Manager review time)

### Staffing Implication
- FP&A Analyst: ~6 hours/month on store P&L review and publication; absorbed within existing FP&A team
- FP&A Manager: ~2 hours/month on review + 8 hours/quarter on trend analysis; absorbed
- Store Managers: ~1–2 hours/month each; absorbed (part of monthly management routine per W67)
- Regional Managers: ~4–6 hours/month each; absorbed
- No incremental headcount

## W1406. Weekly Flash Sales Report, Chain-Wide KPI Dashboard & Executive Performance Summary

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Weekly cadence: every Monday morning, auto-generated from prior week's transaction data |
| **Frequency** | Weekly; 52 weeks/year |
| **Volume** | 1 chain-wide flash report + 4 regional summaries + 200 store-level detail pages per week |
| **Owner** | FP&A Analyst |
| **Participants** | FP&A Analyst, FP&A Manager, VP Store Operations, CMO, CFO, CEO |

### Background

Between monthly store P&Ls (W1405), BuildRight leadership needs weekly visibility into sales performance to detect trends, identify underperforming stores early, and respond to competitive or seasonal shifts in real time. The weekly flash report is a streamlined, action-oriented document focused on revenue, transaction counts, and key retail KPIs — not a full P&L. It serves as the primary early warning system for the executive team, enabling course correction before month-end.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System auto-extracts prior week's (Monday–Sunday) transaction data from POS, ecommerce, and trade account systems: total revenue by store, by region, and by channel; transaction count; average transaction value (ATV); units per transaction (UPT); sales per sqm; sales per employee | System | — | Automated (runs at 6:00 AM Monday) |
| 2 | System calculates key metrics and comparisons: (a) week-over-week revenue change (% and PHP), (b) same-week-prior-year revenue change (% and PHP), (c) week-to-date (WTD) revenue vs. weekly budget target, (d) month-to-date (MTD) revenue vs. monthly budget target, (e) year-to-date (YTD) revenue vs. annual budget target, (f) top 10 and bottom 10 stores by revenue growth vs. prior year, (g) ecommerce revenue as % of total, (h) BOPIS vs. delivery order split, (i) trade/B2B revenue as % of total, (j) promotional revenue as % of total (from active promotions per W83) | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | System generates visual dashboard with: (a) chain-wide revenue trend (13-week rolling chart), (b) regional revenue waterfall (5 store regions per profile §3.1: Mindanao, Visayas, Luzon-outside-NCR, Metro Manila, North/Central Luzon), (c) store heat map (green/yellow/red by performance vs. budget), (d) top 10 / bottom 10 store list, (e) category revenue breakdown (Lumber, Tiles, Plumbing, etc.), (f) ecommerce channel metrics, (g) executive KPI scorecard | System | — | Automated |
| 4 | FP&A Analyst reviews auto-generated flash report for data quality: verifies POS revenue totals reconcile to daily cash/card/e-wallet settlements (W5F, W537, W1268), flags stores with missing or anomalous data (e.g., store showing zero revenue for a day — possible POS system issue), and adds narrative commentary on notable items (weather events, typhoons, holidays, competitor openings) | FP&A Analyst | FP&A Manager | 1 hour/week |
| 5 | FP&A Analyst publishes weekly flash report by 9:00 AM Monday: (a) executive summary email with chain-wide KPIs and 3–5 bullet-point commentary to CEO, CFO, COO, CMO, VP Store Operations; (b) detailed dashboard link in ERP portal for self-service drill-down; (c) regional summaries auto-distributed to Regional Managers | FP&A Analyst | FP&A Manager | 30 min |
| 6 | **Tuesday executive stand-up**: CEO, CFO, COO review flash report in 15-minute stand-up; identify any stores or regions requiring immediate intervention; assign action items (e.g., CMO to investigate declining category, VP Store Operations to visit bottom-10 store) | CEO / CFO / COO | — | 15 min/week |
| 7 | **Thursday follow-up**: FP&A Analyst distributes mid-week update (Wednesday + Thursday partial data) if flash report showed significant variances (> 10% below weekly target chain-wide or any region > 15% below); mid-week update is a simplified 1-page email | FP&A Analyst | FP&A Manager | 30 min (only when triggered) |

### System Touchpoints
- Automated weekly data extraction from POS, ecommerce, and trade account transaction tables (W1406.1)
- KPI calculation engine: WoW, WPLY, WTD vs. budget, MTD vs. budget, YTD vs. budget, ATV, UPT, sales/sqm, sales/employee (W1406.2)
- Visual dashboard: 13-week trend, regional waterfall, store heat map, top/bottom 10, category breakdown, ecommerce metrics, executive scorecard (W1406.3)
- Automated distribution: executive summary email, portal link, regional manager summaries (W1406.5)
- Mid-week update trigger: automated alert when chain-wide or regional variance exceeds threshold (W1406.7)
- Integration with W5B (POS — transaction data), W11/W19 (ecommerce — online revenue), W5F (store closing — daily revenue reconciliation), W83 (campaign — promotional revenue attribution), W1405 (store P&L — monthly context for weekly trends), W67 (store performance — operational KPI alignment)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Typhoon week distortion**: a week with typhoon-related store closures in one region will show a dramatic chain-wide revenue decline that is not representative of underlying performance; narrative commentary must contextualize these events to prevent overreaction
- **POS data latency**: stores operating in offline mode (W5G) may not have all transactions uploaded by the Monday 6:00 AM extraction; flash report may understate the prior week's revenue by 1–2%; automatically reconciled in following week's report
- **Executive attention fatigue**: weekly reports can become routine and ignored if consistently showing "within normal range"; exception-based alerting (automatic email only when variances exceed threshold) mitigates, while full dashboard remains available on-demand
- **Category attribution accuracy**: promotional revenue attribution depends on correct promo SKU tagging at POS (W523); if promotional quick-keys are not set up correctly, promotional revenue is understated and regular revenue is overstated


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "System auto-extracts prior week's" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-calculate of "calculates key metrics and comparisons" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-generate of "generates visual dashboard" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-292 (ensure controlled execution — FP&A & Reporting (PA-17.4).)
- operational: FP&A Analyst review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
- System auto-generation: ~30 min (batch processing + dashboard rendering)
- FP&A Analyst review and commentary: ~1 hour/week
- Publication and distribution: ~30 min/week
- Mid-week update (when triggered): ~30 min (estimated ~10 weeks/year = ~5 hours/year)
- Executive stand-up: ~15 min/week × 3 executives = ~45 min/week
- Total weekly human effort: ~2.5 hours/week for FP&A + ~45 min for executives = ~160 hours/year

### Staffing Implication
- FP&A Analyst: ~2 hours/week on flash report; primary weekly deliverable; absorbed within existing FP&A team
- FP&A Manager: ~30 min/week on review; absorbed
- Executives: ~15 min/week on stand-up; absorbed within executive calendar
- No incremental headcount

## W5531. PFRS 8 Operating-Segment Reporting, CODM Disclosure Package & Segment-Note Production

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | The annual AFS cycle (W9B year-end close feeding the W481 SEC filing calendar); a change in the CODM's operating-review structure (W1653 monthly executive performance review, W231 QBR, W102/W1656 category reviews); a portfolio/structure change (new entity, channel or format); or an external-audit PFRS 8 query (W95/W351) |
| **Frequency** | Annual (AFS cycle); per structural-change event |
| **Volume** | 5 operating entities plus the store/DC/HQ/ecommerce and category views aggregated into the reportable-segment set; 1 annual segment-note disclosure package; ~205-location operating data rolled into the CODM's review structure |
| **Owner** | Corporate Controller |
| **Participants** | CFO (chief-operating-decision-maker), FP&A Manager, Chief Accountant, Segment finance leads (entity accountants), External Audit Team, Corporate Secretary (filing calendar) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **CODM & segment-structure confirmation**: confirm the chief-operating-decision-maker designation (CFO, per board delegation) and the operating segments reflected in the CODM's actual review package — the W1653 monthly executive performance review (entity P&Ls, store/DC/HQ views, ecommerce channel) and the W102/W1656 category margin reviews | Corporate Controller | CFO | ~4 hours/year |
| 2 | **Segment data assembly**: extract per-segment revenue, results on the measure the CODM uses, interest, depreciation & amortization, and capex; build the reconciliation of segment revenue, profit and assets to the consolidated totals per the PFRS 8 reconciliation requirement, tied to the W9B trial balances | FP&A Manager / Chief Accountant | Corporate Controller | ~16 hours/year |
| 3 | **Aggregation & restatement criteria**: document any aggregation of operating segments with similar economic characteristics; where the internal reporting structure changed, restate prior-period segment data and record the basis | Corporate Controller | Corporate Controller | ~4 hours/year |
| 4 | **Entity-wide disclosures**: products/services revenue splits, the single-geography (Philippine) disclosure with major-market context, and major-customer tests (revenue ≥ 10% per customer) against the W107/W3307 concentration data | FP&A Manager | Corporate Controller | inside the Step-2 assembly |

| 6 | **Production & filing**: draft the segment note into the AFS on the W481 filing calendar; cross-check the disclosed segments and measures against the W1657 board governance package for consistency | Corporate Controller | CFO | ~16 hours/year incl. Step-4 drafting |
| 7 | **Change monitoring**: annual refresh trigger review — flag any change in the CODM package, segment composition or measure definition feeding the next cycle | Corporate Controller | Corporate Controller | ≈ 4 hours/year |

### System Touchpoints
- ERP segment dimensions (entity, channel, category, cost-center hierarchy per W1545) feeding the CODM review extracts
- W9B trial balances and W234 consolidation outputs (segment reconciliation to consolidated totals)
- W1653 executive-review package and W1657 board governance package (CODM-measure consistency evidence)
- W481 AFS filing calendar; W95/W351 PBC repository and audit-trail workspace

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Disclosure-compliance risk**: an AFS filed without a PFRS 8-compliant segment note (or with segments inconsistent with the CODM's actual review package) is defective for SEC purposes and exposes the W481 filing to deficiency action; mitigated by the annual structure-confirmation gate (Step 1) and the audit walkthrough (Step 5)
- **Measure-inconsistency risk**: disclosed segment results drifting from the W1653/W1657 management packages invite audit findings and restatement; mitigated by tying the note to the same CODM extracts (Step 2) and the W637 control loop (Step 5)
- **Change-blindness risk**: management-reporting changes (new channel, new entity, format shifts) silently invalidate the segment structure mid-year; mitigated by the Step-7 monitoring trigger review feeding retrospective restatement (Step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- Segment-dimension GL extracts auto-populate the disclosure workbook and the reconciliation to consolidated totals (replaces manual Step 2 assembly).
- Organizational-structure changes auto-flag segment-definition impacts from the entity/channel master (replaces manual Step 7 monitoring).

### Controls
- CTL-292 (ensure controlled execution — FP&A & Reporting (PA-17.4).)
- operational: the segment note may not be drafted from any package other than the CODM's own review extracts
- operational: Corporate Controller sign-off on the reconciliation to consolidated totals before audit delivery

### Time Estimate
Structure confirmation ≈ 4 hours/year; data assembly ≈ 16 hours/year (Step-4 drafting included in Step 6); aggregation/restatement ≈ 4 hours/year; audit walkthrough ≈ 8 hours/year; production & filing ≈ 16 hours/year; change monitoring ≈ 4 hours/year. Total **≈ 52 hours/year** (≈0.03 FTE), absorbed within the corporate accounting and FP&A teams.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed within the Corporate Controller and GL & Consolidation sub-teams as an annual AFS-cycle workstream alongside W9B and W481; no incremental headcount.
