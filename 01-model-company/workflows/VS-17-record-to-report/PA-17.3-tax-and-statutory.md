## W90. Monthly Tax Filing & Statutory Remittance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly tax filing deadlines per BIR calendar; payroll statutory deduction deadlines per SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG schedules |
| **Frequency** | Monthly (VAT, EWT, SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG); quarterly (corporate income tax); annual (1702RT, 1604-C/E) |
| **Volume** | 5 entities × multiple tax types = ~10 monthly filings (2550M, 1601-C per entity) + quarterly cycles (2550Q, 1601-EQ, 1702Q) + annual returns (1702RT, 1604-C/E); plus payroll statutory for 5 entities |
| **Owner** | Tax Accountant |
| **Participants** | Tax Accountant, Controller, Treasury Analyst, Payroll Officer, HR Manager |

### Background

FIN-008 requires BIR tax return generation as a Must Have. W9 (Financial Close) produces the tax data and generates the returns, but the operational process of reviewing, validating, filing, and remitting taxes is a distinct workflow. Philippine tax compliance involves strict monthly deadlines (VAT 2550M by 20th/25th, withholding on compensation 1601-C by 10th), quarterly filings (EWT 1601-EQ by the last day of the month following quarter close, 2550Q, 1702Q income tax), and annual returns (1702RT). Late filing incurs penalties (25% surcharge + 20% interest per annum + compromise penalty, per NIRC Secs. 248–249). This workflow bridges the financial close (W9) to actual regulatory compliance.

### Monthly Tax Filing Cycle

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tax data extraction** (after W9A month-end close day 3–4): Tax Accountant extracts from ERP: (a) VAT data — output VAT (sales) and input VAT (purchases) per entity; sales/purchases split by VAT-able, VAT-exempt, zero-rated; (b) EWT data — withholding tax per vendor per ATC (Alphanumeric Tax Code) from AP (W7.9a); (c) Withholding on compensation — per employee per entity from payroll (W10.4); (d) Local business tax — per LGU per location from W9A.16c | Tax Accountant | Controller | 2 hours/month |
| 2 | **VAT return preparation** (BIR Form 2550M — monthly, or 2550Q — quarterly): (a) Tax Accountant reviews VAT data for completeness: all sales transactions captured, all purchase VAT claimed, intercompany VAT elimination correct per W14; (b) prepares 2550M per entity: gross sales, VAT-able sales, output VAT, total purchases, VAT-able purchases, input VAT, adjustments (prior period corrections), net VAT payable or refundable; (c) validates input VAT claims against supporting documents — BIR-registered sales invoices, official receipts, import entry declarations; (d) cross-references with W9A.16 VAT return schedule | Tax Accountant | Controller | 3 hours/month |
| 3 | **EWT return preparation** (BIR Form 1601-EQ — quarterly): (a) Tax Accountant reviews EWT computations per vendor per ATC; validates against AP records (W7.9a); (b) reconciles total EWT withheld per entity vs. vendor creditable withholding tax certificates (BIR Form 2307) to be issued; (c) at quarter-end, prepares 1601-EQ per entity: total withholding per ATC category, taxes remitted prior quarters, current quarter liability | Tax Accountant | Controller | 2 hours/quarter |
| 4 | **Withholding on compensation** (BIR Form 1601-C): (a) Payroll Officer provides monthly withholding tax per entity from W10 payroll processing; (b) Tax Accountant validates against TRAIN law tax tables and payroll register; (c) prepares 1601-C per entity | Tax Accountant / Payroll Officer | Controller | 1 hour/month |
| 5 | **Statutory remittance — SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG**: (a) Payroll Officer generates monthly contribution schedules per entity from W10: employer share, employee share, total per employee; (b) validates against PRN (Payment Reference Number) from each agency; (c) submits payment via bank transfer or agency portal per deadline (SSS by last day of month, PhilHealth by every 10th, Pag-IBIG by every 10th of the following month) | Payroll Officer | HR Manager | 2 hours/month |
| 6 | **BIR eFPS filing**: Tax Accountant files returns via BIR Electronic Filing and Payment System (eFPS) or eBIRForms for non-eFPS-covered entities: (a) uploads prepared returns (2550M, 1601-EQ, 1601-C); (b) system validates return data against BIR validation rules; (c) receives confirmation and payment reference | Tax Accountant | Controller | 1 hour/month |
| 7 | **Tax payment**: Treasury Analyst processes tax payments via bank: (a) eFPS auto-debit for covered entities; (b) manual bank transfer for non-eFPS entities; (c) verifies payment confirmation against filed return amounts | Treasury Analyst | Controller | 30 min/month |
| 8 | **Tax certificate generation**: Tax Accountant generates and distributes: (a) BIR Form 2307 (creditable withholding tax certificates) to vendors — monthly for top vendors, quarterly for others; (b) BIR Form 2316 (certificate of compensation payment/tax withheld) to all employees — annually or upon separation (W43); (c) alphalist of payees (1604-E) and employees (1604-C) — annual | Tax Accountant | Controller | 2 hours/month |
| 9 | **Tax reconciliation**: Monthly: Tax Accountant reconciles tax liability accounts (VAT payable, EWT payable, WTC payable) per entity: (a) beginning balance + current month liability − payments = ending balance; (b) ending balance must agree with GL (W9A); (c) investigates and resolves discrepancies before next filing cycle | Tax Accountant | Controller | 1 hour/month |
| 10 | **Tax calendar compliance dashboard**: System maintains tax compliance calendar per entity showing: filing deadline, filing status (pending/filed/paid), responsible person, penalty exposure for late filing; Controller reviews weekly | System / Controller | CFO | 15 min/week |

### Quarterly / Annual Filing Additions

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | **Quarterly income tax return** (BIR Form 1702Q): Tax Accountant prepares quarterly corporate income tax return per entity: taxable income, tax due, quarterly installment; validates against quarterly financial statements (W9A) | Tax Accountant | Controller | Quarterly |
| 12 | **Annual income tax return** (BIR Form 1702RT): Tax Accountant prepares annual corporate income tax return per entity; incorporates year-end adjustments from W9B; cross-references with external auditor's tax computations (W95) | Tax Accountant | Controller / CFO | Annual |
| 13 | **Annual information returns** (BIR Forms 1604-C, 1604-E): Tax Accountant prepares and files annual alphalist of employees and payees; reconciles with full-year withholding records | Tax Accountant | Controller | Annual (January) |
| 14 | **Local business tax reconciliation**: Tax Accountant reconciles quarterly LBT payments per LGU (from W9A.16c) against LGU assessment; prepares any variance explanations; LGU assessments reconciled during W54 permit renewals | Tax Accountant | Controller | Quarterly |

### System Touchpoints
- Automated tax data extraction: VAT, EWT, WTC per entity with intercompany elimination (W90.1)
- BIR return generation: 2550M/Q, 1601-EQ, 1601-C, 1702Q/RT, 1604-C/E in BIR-prescribed format (W90.2–4)
- BIR eFPS/eBIRForms integration or export: prepared returns uploadable to BIR systems (W90.6)
- Tax calendar compliance dashboard with deadline tracking and penalty exposure (W90.10)
- Vendor withholding tax certificate (2307) generation and distribution (W90.8)
- Employee withholding tax certificate (2316) generation (W90.8, W43.10)
- Tax liability account reconciliation: VAT payable, EWT payable, WTC payable per entity (W90.9)
- Statutory contribution schedules: SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG with employer/employee split per entity (W90.5)
- Integration with W7 (AP — EWT computation), W8 (AR — VAT on sales), W9 (financial close — tax data source), W10 (payroll — withholding on compensation, statutory contributions), W14 (IC — VAT elimination), W30 (treasury — tax payment processing), W54 (LGU — local business tax), W77 (BIR audit — returns serve as audit support), W95 (external audit — tax provision validation)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **BIR eFPS system downtime near filing deadlines**: eFPS is notoriously unreliable during the last 3 days before filing deadlines (10th for CWT, 20th/25th for VAT; the quarterly 1601-EQ by the last day of the month following quarter close); if eFPS is down and the return is filed late, penalties accrue at 25% surcharge + 20% interest per annum + compromise penalty, regardless of whether the taxpayer attempted to file on time
- **VAT input claim disallowance risk**: BIR may disallow input VAT claims during examination (W77) if supporting documents (BIR-registered sales invoices, official receipts) are incomplete or if the supplier's VAT registration has lapsed; with ~6,715 vendor invoices/month, validating every supplier's VAT status is impractical; mitigated by standard operating procedures and system-enforced validation rules
- **Multi-entity filing complexity and penalty exposure**: 5 entities × 2 monthly filings each (2550M, 1601-C) = 10 monthly filings, plus the quarterly cycle (2550Q, 1601-EQ, 1702Q); missing even one entity's filing triggers penalties; the tax calendar dashboard (step 10) helps but requires constant monitoring
- **EWT ATC misclassification**: with multiple Alphanumeric Tax Codes (WI010, WI020, WC010, etc.), misclassifying a vendor's ATC in the vendor master results in incorrect EWT withholding; correcting this requires amended 1601-EQ filings and re-issuance of vendor 2307 certificates
- **Quarterly income tax estimation uncertainty**: quarterly corporate income tax returns (1702Q) are based on estimated taxable income; if actual year-end income differs significantly, the company faces either underpayment penalties or cash flow impact from large catch-up payments in the final quarter


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "system validates return data against BIR validation rules" (Step 6, already system-executed).
- System auto-track of "System maintains tax compliance calendar per entity" (Step 10, already system-executed).

### Controls
- CTL-291 (ensure controlled execution — Tax & Statutory (PA-17.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
- Monthly tax data extraction (all entities): ~2 hours
- VAT return preparation (5 entities): ~3 hours/month per entity (36 hours per entity per year; ~180 hours/year across the 5 entities)
- EWT return preparation (5 entities): ~2 hours/month
- CWT return preparation (5 entities): ~1 hour/month
- Statutory remittance (SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG): ~2 hours/month
- BIR eFPS filing: ~1 hour/month
- Tax payment processing: ~30 min/month
- Tax certificate generation (2307, 2316): ~2 hours/month
- Tax reconciliation: ~1 hour/month
- Tax calendar compliance monitoring: ~15 min/week
- Quarterly income tax return: ~4 hours/quarter
- Annual returns (1702RT, 1604-C/E): ~20 hours/year

## W217. Senior Citizen & PWD VAT-Exemption Audit & Reporting

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly and Quarterly tax filing deadlines (BIR Form 2550M/Q) |
| **Frequency** | Monthly review; Quarterly reporting |
| **Volume** | ~2–5% of total retail transactions |
| **Owner** | Tax Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Managers (data source), AR Clerk, Controller, BIR |

### Background

Philippine law (RA 9994 and RA 10754) requires 20% discount and VAT-exemption for Senior Citizens and Persons with Disabilities (PWD) on certain goods. This requires rigorous documentation for tax-deductible expense claiming.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Data Consolidation**: System pulls all POS transactions flagged as "SC/PWD Discount" across 200 stores | AR Clerk | Tax Manager | 2 hours |
| 2 | **ID Verification Audit**: Sample review of captured ID numbers (OSCA/PWD ID) and customer signatures in the system to ensure compliance | AR Clerk | Tax Manager | 4 hours |
| 3 | **VAT-Exemption Reconciliation**: Reconcile the "VAT-Exempt" sales column in the Sales Journal against actual discounts given | AR Clerk | Controller | 2 hours |
| 4 | **Report Generation**: Generate "Summary List of Sales" (SLS) and specific SC/PWD discount reports required for BIR filing | Tax Manager | — | 1 hour |
| 5 | **Accounting Entry**: Verify correct posting to "Discounts - SC/PWD" (Operating Expense) and "VAT-Exempt Sales" (Revenue) | Controller | CFO | 30 min |
| 6 | **Document Archival**: Ensure digital/physical copies of SC/PWD booklets and transaction logs are archived for 10 years | Store Manager | Tax Manager | Monthly |

### System Touchpoints
- POS capture of SC/PWD ID and mandatory data fields (W170)
- VAT-Exempt sales reporting in GL
- Automated SLS (Summary List of Sales) generation for BIR
- Archival system for discount documentation

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Fraudulent SC/PWD ID usage**: bogus or borrowed Senior Citizen / PWD IDs used to claim the 20% discount and VAT exemption at POS is a known retail abuse vector in the Philippines; if BIR audit (W77) catches unverified IDs, the VAT-exemption claim is disallowed and penalties are assessed
- **Manual ID verification bottleneck**: cashiers at high-traffic stores cannot always verify SC/PWD ID validity during peak hours; the 2–5% transaction volume (~56,000–140,000 SC/PWD transactions/month) means manual sampling review (step 2) covers only a fraction of total transactions, leaving compliance gaps
- **Discount expense misclassification**: the SC/PWD 20% discount is recorded as an operating expense (not a revenue reduction); if store-level P&L reporting (W35.10) does not properly allocate this expense, store managers appear to have higher operating costs than they control, distorting store performance comparisons
- **BIR audit exposure on VAT-exempt sales**: BIR requires complete documentation (OSCA/PWD ID number, signature, purchase details) for every VAT-exempt transaction; with ~200 stores generating physical SC/PWD purchase booklets, missing or incomplete booklets at even a few locations can trigger a full VAT assessment during BIR examination (W77)


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "Discount" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "review of captured ID numbers" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-reconcile of "Reconcile" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-291 (ensure controlled execution — Tax & Statutory (PA-17.3).)
- operational: Tax Manager review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
Monthly SC/PWD report preparation: ~4–6 hours per reporting period (data extraction across 200+ locations, SC/PWD registry validation, VAT-exemption computation verification, BIR format report generation). Quarterly BIR filing preparation adds ~2 hours per quarter. Annual reconciliation with BIR CAS audit (W216) adds ~1 day.

### Staffing Implication
Monthly reporting is absorbed by the Tax Compliance team (1 Tax Analyst, ~1 day/month). Quarterly and annual cycles require additional review by the Tax Manager. No dedicated headcount required beyond the standing Tax Compliance team.

| **Time Estimate** | 2–4 hours per filing cycle per entity |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Filing Calendar Check**: System alerts Tax Accountant of upcoming filing deadlines per BIR calendar: (a) 2550M — Monthly VAT Return (due 20th of following month), (b) 1601-EQ — Quarterly EWT Remittance Return (due last day of the month following quarter close), (c) 1601-C — Monthly CWT Remittance (due 10th of following month), (d) 2550Q — Quarterly VAT Return, (e) 1702Q — Quarterly Income Tax Return, (f) 1604-C/E — Annual Information Return; alerts triggered 7 days before deadline | System | Controller | Automated |
| 2 | **Data Extraction & Validation**: Tax Accountant runs tax data extraction from ERP: (a) for VAT returns: system pulls sales invoices (output VAT), purchase invoices (input VAT), import entries (VAT on imports per W239), adjustments (SC/PWD VAT-exemption per W217); (b) for EWT returns: system pulls all EWT-deducted vendor payments per ATC code (WI010, WI020, etc.); (c) for CWT returns: system pulls all compensation-related withholding per BIR tax tables; (d) system performs pre-validation: checks for missing TINs, duplicate entries, amount tolerance checks, and late-filed transactions | Tax Accountant | Controller | 1–2 hours |
| 3 | **Return Preparation**: System auto-generates BIR tax return in prescribed format: (a) populated fields from ERP transaction data (sales, purchases, payments, withholding); (b) Tax Accountant reviews auto-populated return against source schedules; (c) manual adjustments for: late-registered transactions, tax credit certificates (TCC), prior-period adjustments, tax relief claims; (d) system validates arithmetic consistency and cross-field validation per BIR form rules | Tax Accountant | Controller | 1–2 hours |
| 4 | **Controller Review**: Controller reviews prepared return: (a) verifies tax liability against GL accounts (VAT payable/receivable, EWT payable, CWT payable); (b) confirms tax credit utilization; (c) approves filing; (d) if adjustments needed, returns to Tax Accountant with comments | Controller | CFO | 30 min |
| 5 | **eFPS Submission**: Tax Accountant logs into BIR Electronic Filing and Payment System (eFPS): (a) selects entity TIN and filing period; (b) uploads generated return file (or manually encodes if eFPS doesn't support batch upload for form type); (c) system validates submission and generates eFPS confirmation reference number; (d) Tax Accountant records confirmation number in ERP against filing record | Tax Accountant | Controller | 30 min |
| 6 | **Payment via AAB**: For returns with tax due: (a) system calculates net tax payable after credits; (b) Tax Accountant initiates payment via eFPS-linked Authorized Agent Bank (AAB): electronic debit from enrolled bank account (BDO, BPI, or Metrobank per FIN-009); (c) for entities not enrolled in eFPS payment: Tax Accountant generates eFPS Payment Form and pays over-the-counter at AAB; (d) Payment Reference Number (PRN) generated and recorded in ERP | Tax Accountant / Treasury Analyst | Controller | 30 min |
| 7 | **Confirmation & Reconciliation**: (a) Tax Accountant downloads eFS (electronic File Saved) confirmation from eFPS; (b) attaches confirmation and PRN to filing record in ERP; (c) system posts tax payment to GL (Dr. VAT/EWT/CWT Payable, Cr. Cash); (d) Treasury Analyst reconciles tax payment against bank statement per W89; (e) Tax Accountant files return in BIR CAS compliance archive per W216 | Tax Accountant / Treasury Analyst | Controller | 30 min |
| 8 | **Quarterly/Annual Reconciliation**: At quarter-end and year-end: (a) Tax Accountant reconciles cumulative monthly filings against quarterly returns; (b) identifies discrepancies (additional transactions booked after monthly filing, classification corrections); (c) files adjustment returns if required per BIR rules; (d) at year-end: reconciles all quarterly filings against annual return (1702RT, 1604-C/E); (e) generates annual tax summary per entity for Controller and CFO review | Tax Accountant | Controller / CFO | 4–8 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- Tax return auto-generation engine per BIR form specifications (2550M/Q, 1601-EQ, 1601-C, 1702Q/RT, 1604-C/E)
- eFPS integration module (direct submission or file export in BIR format)
- Tax calendar with automated deadline alerts (configurable per entity)
- Tax data validation engine (TIN verification, duplicate detection, arithmetic checks)
- Payment gateway integration with AAB (BDO, BPI, Metrobank, Chinabank per FIN-009)
- PRN tracking and reconciliation against GL
- Tax filing archive per BIR CAS requirements (W216) with 10-year retention

### Pain Points / Risks
- **eFPS downtime**: BIR eFPS system frequently experiences downtime near filing deadlines; need contingency for over-the-counter filing with printed returns
- **Manual encoding**: Some BIR forms cannot be batch-uploaded and must be manually encoded in eFPS; time-consuming and error-prone for multi-entity filing
- **Late transactions**: Transactions booked after monthly filing cutoff require adjustment returns; tracking adjustments across periods is complex
- **PRN expiration**: BIR Payment Reference Numbers have limited validity (typically 24 hours for eFPS-generated PRN); missed payment window requires new PRN generation
- **Regulatory changes**: BIR frequently issues new Revenue Regulations affecting form formats, filing deadlines, and computation rules; ERP must support rapid form updates
- **Multi-entity complexity**: 5 entities × multiple form types = ~10 monthly filings plus quarterly/annual cycles (per the Volume); missing one entity's filing incurs penalties and compromise penalties


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "Filing Calendar Check" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-count of "Accountant runs tax data extraction from ERP" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews auto-populated return against source schedules" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-291 (ensure controlled execution — Tax & Statutory (PA-17.3).)

### Time Estimate
- Filing calendar check and deadline monitoring: automated (W260.1)
- Data extraction and validation per filing: 1–2 hours per entity per form type (W260.2)
- Return preparation and review: 1–2 hours per entity per form type (W260.3); ~2–4 hours total per filing cycle per entity including Controller review (W260.4)
- eFPS submission: ~30 min per filing (W260.5); manual encoding for unsupported forms adds ~15–30 min per form
- Payment via AAB: ~30 min per filing with tax due (W260.6)
- Confirmation and reconciliation: ~30 min per filing (W260.7)
- Monthly filing total per entity: ~2–4 hours per form type × 2 monthly forms (2550M, 1601-C; 1601-EQ is quarterly) = ~4–8 hours/entity/month plus the quarterly cycle
- All entities monthly: 5 entities × ~4–8 hours = ~20–40 hours/month across 2 Tax Accountants (per the per-form derivation)
- Quarterly reconciliation (W260.8): 4–8 hours per quarter across all entities
- Annual reconciliation and 1702RT/1604-C/E preparation: ~16–24 hours per entity = ~80–120 hours total across all 5 entities during annual filing season
- Total monthly eFPS effort: ~30–55 hours/month (monthly cycle 20–40 h + amortized quarterly/annual effort; Tax Accountants) + ~2–3 hours (Controller review) + ~1–2 hours (Treasury Analyst payment processing)

### Staffing Implication
- **2 Tax Accountants**: eFPS filing across 5 entities with ~10 monthly filings plus quarterly/annual cycles requires dedicated tax staff; each filing cycle (data extraction, return preparation, review, submission, payment) takes 2–4 hours per form type; monthly effort including amortized quarterly/annual cycles is ~30–55 hours/month — a substantial dedicated workload shared by the 2 Tax Accountants (who also own the W90 preparation cycle)
- **1 Controller**: return review and approval adds ~2–3 hours/month (30 min per filing × 4–5 filings); absorbed within existing Controller duties during filing season (typically the 1st–3rd week of each month)
- **1 Treasury Analyst**: tax payment processing via bank transfer adds ~1–2 hours/month; absorbed within existing Treasury role

## W407. Corporate Income Tax — Computation & Deferred Tax (PAS 12)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarter-end close (interim) or Year-end close (annual) (W9) |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly (interim provision); Annual (final return & deferred tax measurement) |
| **Volume** | 5 legal entities; annual tax filings (1702RT); quarterly tax filings (1702Q) |
| **Owner** | Tax Manager |
| **Participants** | CFO, Controller, Tax Manager, Tax Accountant, External Audit Team (annual) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tax Provisioning**: Tax Accountant computes current income tax liability based on quarterly pre-tax income per entity, adjusting for non-deductible expenses and tax-exempt income | Tax Accountant | Tax Manager | 4 hours |
| 2 | **Temporary Differences Identification**: Tax Accountant identifies temporary differences between accounting (PFRS) and tax carrying values (e.g., depreciation rates, SLOB provisions, bad debt provisions, accrued retirement benefits) | Tax Accountant | Tax Manager | 1 day |
| 3 | **Deferred Tax Calculation**: Tax Manager calculates Deferred Tax Assets (DTA) and Deferred Tax Liabilities (DTL) using the enacted tax rate (e.g., 25% under CREATE Law) | Tax Manager | Controller | 1 day |
| 4 | **PFRS 16 Lease Adjustments**: Compute current and deferred tax adjustments arising from PFRS 16 lease assets (ROU) and liabilities | Tax Accountant | Tax Manager | 4 hours |
| 5 | **Accrual Posting**: Post quarterly tax provision and deferred tax journal entries: Dr. Income Tax Expense (Current/Deferred) / Cr. Income Tax Payable / Cr. Net DTL | Tax Accountant | Controller | 1 hour |
| 6 | **Annual Return Reconciliation**: During year-end (W9B), Tax Manager prepares comprehensive tax reconciliation (PFRS Net Income to Taxable Income); coordinates with external auditors for verification (W95) | Tax Manager | CFO | 2 days |
| 7 | **Filing & Payment**: Tax Accountant uploads the final return via eFPS (W260) and coordinates payment of the balance due | Tax Accountant | Tax Manager | 2 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- Tax compliance and calculation engine
- Fixed Asset module (for book vs. tax depreciation comparison)
- Deferred Tax register
- Integration with W90 (Tax filing), W260 (eFPS filing), and W9A/B (Month/Year-End close)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **CREATE Law complexity**: Changes in tax rates or incentives for specific entities (e.g., Logistics Inc. if registered with BOI/PEZA) require separate tax computations and deferred tax re-measurements
- **BIR audit mismatch**: If the "Tax Recon" is not rigorously maintained, discrepancies between FS and Income Tax Returns (ITR) trigger immediate BIR audit flags (W77)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "Accountant computes current income tax liability based on quarterly pre-tax…" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-calculate of "calculates Deferred Tax Assets" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-post of "posting" (replaces manual Step 5).

### Controls
- CTL-291 (ensure controlled execution — Tax & Statutory (PA-17.3).)
- operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Month-end close process initiation (day 1 of following month), feeds into W9 financial close |
| **Frequency** | Monthly (by day 3 of following month, to meet 5-day month-end close target) |
| **Volume** | 5 legal entities; monthly tax provisions covering: VAT (12% on ~PHP 5.19B monthly revenue), EWT (1–10% on ~PHP 2.5B monthly AP), CWT collection (~PHP 300M/month in customer CWT), percentage tax (3% on VAT-exempt revenue streams), corporate income tax (RCIT 25% / FCIT 20% per CREATE law per PAS 12), local taxes (LGU business tax quarterly); BIR eFPS filing deadlines per tax type |

| **Participants** | Tax Accountant, Chief Accountant, Controller (A for provision approval), AP Supervisor (C for EWT data), AR Supervisor (C for CWT data), Treasury Analyst (C for tax payment funding), External Tax Advisor (C for complex positions) |

### Background

W90 covers monthly BIR tax filing and statutory remittance execution. W260 covers BIR eFPS filing and electronic payment submission. W407 covers corporate income tax computation and deferred tax (PAS 12). W473 covers BIR EIS API transmission. W475 covers CWT 2307 certificate collection. W499 covers percentage tax computation. However, no single workflow covers the end-to-end monthly tax provision process — the computation, review, journal entry posting, and compliance calendar verification that must occur before the financial close is completed on day 5. This workflow fills that gap by ensuring that: (a) tax provisions are computed accurately before financial statements are finalized; (b) VAT input/output reconciliation identifies any unclaimed input VAT or under-remitted output VAT before BIR 2550M filing; (c) EWT remittance per BIR 1601-EQ is reconciled to actual AP withholding; (d) CWT certificates (BIR 2307) are collected from customers for credit against income tax; (e) deferred tax movements are computed per PAS 12; (f) tax provision journal entries are posted before close completion; (g) the BIR compliance calendar is reviewed for upcoming deadlines within the next 30 days. The Philippine tax environment is particularly complex due to: multiple withholding tax rates depending on payee type (corporations 1%, professionals 10%, suppliers of goods 1%, casual employees 5–10%); VAT on importations handled separately from domestic VAT; quarterly income tax returns (1702Q) requiring cumulative provisions; and LGU business tax payments (quarterly) requiring separate accruals.

## W1503. BIR Percentage Tax vs. VAT Threshold Monitoring, Quarterly Tax Regime Evaluation & Registration Adjustment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly rolling 12-month gross sales calculation; quarterly tax regime evaluation; BIR registration update required when crossing VAT threshold (PHP 3M annual gross sales) |
| **Frequency** | Monthly monitoring; quarterly evaluation; per-entity registration adjustment as needed |
| **Volume** | 5 legal entities; BuildRight Depot, Inc. and BuildRight Logistics, Inc. are well above threshold; BuildRight Digital Commerce, Inc. and newer entities may fluctuate near threshold |
| **Owner** | Tax Manager |
| **Participants** | Tax Manager, Intercompany Accountant, CFO, BIR RDO (Revenue District Office) |

### Background

Under Philippine tax law (NIRC as amended by TRAIN Law/RA 10963), businesses with annual gross sales below PHP 3M may elect to pay 3% percentage tax on gross sales instead of 12% VAT. For businesses above PHP 3M, VAT registration is mandatory. While BuildRight's main operating entities (Depot Inc. and Logistics Inc.) are far above the threshold, newer or smaller entities in the corporate structure — particularly BuildRight Digital Commerce, Inc. (which had lower revenue in its early years) — may fluctuate near the PHP 3M threshold. Additionally, Philippine tax law requires mandatory VAT registration within 30 days of exceeding the threshold, with penalties for late registration including back-taxes at 12% VAT plus surcharges and interest. This workflow ensures continuous monitoring of each entity's gross sales trajectory against the threshold and timely BIR registration adjustments.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monthly rolling 12-month gross sales tracking**: Monthly: (a) system calculates rolling 12-month gross sales per legal entity: (i) BuildRight Holdings, Inc. (investment income only — exempt from VAT on certain investment returns); (ii) BuildRight Depot, Inc. (retail sales — well above threshold); (iii) BuildRight Logistics, Inc. (service fees — above threshold); (iv) BuildRight Digital Commerce, Inc. (ecommerce service fees — may be near threshold); (v) BuildRight Property Management, Inc. (rental income — above threshold); (b) for entities near the PHP 3M threshold (within 20%): (i) system projects forward based on current monthly run rate; (ii) if projected to exceed within 3 months: alert sent to Tax Manager; (c) results reported to CFO monthly as part of tax compliance dashboard | System / Intercompany Accountant | Tax Manager | 1 hour/month |
| 2 | **Quarterly tax regime evaluation**: Quarterly: (a) Tax Manager evaluates for each entity near threshold: (i) actual gross sales for the trailing 12 months; (ii) forward 12-month projection based on growth trend; (iii) upcoming large contracts or revenue events that could push the entity over threshold; (iv) whether percentage tax or VAT is more economically advantageous for the entity (for entities below threshold, percentage tax may be cheaper but VAT registration allows input tax claims); (b) recommendation: (i) remain on percentage tax — entity clearly below threshold; (ii) voluntary VAT registration — entity approaching threshold, better to register early and claim input tax credits; (iii) mandatory VAT registration — entity has exceeded or will exceed threshold within the quarter; (c) recommendation presented to CFO for decision | Tax Manager | CFO | 2–3 hours/quarter |
| 3 | **BIR VAT registration application**: If entity crosses threshold or voluntarily registers for VAT: (a) Tax Manager prepares BIR registration update: (i) BIR Form 1905 (Registration Data Update); (ii) certification of gross sales exceeding PHP 3M (or voluntary registration intent); (iii) updated Articles of Incorporation or proof of business registration; (b) submits to the BIR RDO where the entity is registered; (c) BIR processes update (typically 10–15 business days); (d) entity receives updated Certificate of Registration (COR) reflecting VAT status; (e) Tax Manager updates the entity's tax profile in the ERP system: (i) tax classification changed from "Non-VAT" to "VAT"; (ii) sales invoice format updated to include VAT breakdown; (iii) purchasing enabled for VAT input tax tracking; (f) all downstream processes updated: invoicing, purchasing, reporting | Tax Manager / BIR RDO | CFO | 5–10 business days |
| 4 | **Transition period compliance**: During transition from percentage tax to VAT: (a) Tax Manager ensures: (i) all sales from the VAT registration effective date forward include 12% VAT; (ii) existing inventory at time of transition: input tax credit claim filed for VAT paid on inventory purchased before registration (as allowed under BIR rules); (iii) percentage tax returns filed for the final period before VAT registration; (iv) first VAT return filed for the period starting from registration date; (v) books of accounts updated to reflect VAT accounting; (b) any customer-facing documentation updated: (i) official receipts reprinted with VAT details; (ii) sales invoices reformatted; (iii) POS terminals reconfigured to apply VAT | Tax Manager | CFO | 2–3 weeks |
| 5 | **Continuous monitoring & threshold re-evaluation**: (a) Tax Manager monitors for: (i) entities whose gross sales decline below PHP 3M — under current law, once VAT-registered, the entity remains VAT-registered unless it applies for cancellation (complex process rarely pursued); (ii) new BIR issuances that may change the threshold or rules; (iii) proposed tax reforms (e.g., CREATE MORE Act) that may adjust the threshold; (b) if threshold rules change: impact analysis on all 5 entities within 30 days of new law/issuance | Tax Manager | CFO | Ongoing |

### System Touchpoints
- Rolling 12-month gross sales calculator per legal entity with threshold proximity alerting (W1503.1)
- Forward sales projection model with 3-month threshold breach prediction (W1503.1)
- Entity tax profile management module with VAT/non-VAT classification (W1503.3)
- BIR registration document management and RDO correspondence tracker (W1503.3)
- Sales invoice format auto-switching based on entity tax classification (W1503.4)
- POS terminal tax configuration management per store/entity (W1503.4)
- Tax regulatory change alert system (W1503.5)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Retroactive assessment risk**: if BuildRight fails to register for VAT within 30 days of exceeding the threshold, BIR may assess back-VAT from the date the threshold was crossed plus 25% surcharge + 20% interest per annum; for an entity with PHP 5M in unrecognized VAT-able sales, this could mean PHP 1M+ in back-taxes and penalties; mitigated by CTL-291 (controlled execution — Tax & Statutory (PA-17.3))
- **Entity-level revenue attribution**: when BuildRight Digital Commerce collects online payments but revenue is recognized by BuildRight Depot per the intercompany arrangement, determining which entity's gross sales count toward the threshold requires careful tax analysis
- **Multiple RDO management**: different entities may be registered with different BIR RDOs; threshold monitoring and registration updates must be filed at the correct RDO
- **Transition period disruption**: switching from percentage tax to VAT mid-period requires dual reporting, invoice reformatting, and system reconfiguration — all within a tight compliance timeline


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-logof "Monthly: (a) system calculates rolling 12-month gross sales" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-register of "register early and claim input tax credits" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-generate of "prepares BIR registration update" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls

- operational: reconfiguration — all within a tight compliance timeline

- Intercompany Accountant: ~30 min/month on data gathering. Absorbed.
- No incremental headcount.

## W1525. Philippine BIR Computerized Accounting System (CAS) Registration, Permit Renewal & Annual Compliance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | (a) Initial ERP system implementation requiring BIR CAS registration, (b) annual CAS permit renewal, (c) system upgrade or migration requiring re-registration |
| **Frequency** | Initial registration (one-time per system); annual renewal; ad-hoc for system changes |
| **Volume** | 5 legal entities × CAS registration = 5 registrations; annual renewal for all 5 |
| **Owner** | Tax Compliance Manager |
| **Participants** | Tax Compliance Manager, IT ERP Team, Finance Controller, external tax counsel, BIR Revenue District Office (RDO) |

### Background

Under BIR Revenue Regulations No. 11-2018 and related issuances, Philippine businesses using a Computerized Accounting System (CAS) — which includes ERP systems, POS systems, and any computerized bookkeeping — must register the system with the BIR and obtain a Permit to Use (PTU). BuildRight Depot operates 5 legal entities (Holdings, Depot Inc., Logistics Inc., Digital Commerce Inc., Property Management Inc.), each requiring separate CAS registration. The PTU must be renewed annually. Any significant system change (ERP upgrade, new module activation, change in chart of accounts structure, POS system replacement) requires BIR notification or re-registration. Operating without a valid PTU exposes BuildRight to: (a) BIR penalty of PHP 1,000–25,000 per violation, (b) potential disallowance of computerized books as evidence in tax disputes (forcing manual record reconstruction), and (c) reputational risk with BIR auditors who view non-compliance as indicative of broader tax issues.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Initial CAS registration** (for new entity or system): (a) Tax Compliance Manager prepares registration documents per BIR RDO requirements: (i) BIR Form 1907 (Application for Registration of CAS/Components), (ii) system description: name, version, developer, modules (GL, AP, AR, inventory, POS), hardware configuration, data security measures, (iii) sample printouts of all books of accounts (cash receipts journal, cash disbursements journal, sales journal, purchases journal, general journal, subsidiary ledgers), (iv) sample official receipts and sales invoices generated by the system, (v) affidavit of responsible officer attesting to system accuracy and integrity, (vi) sworn statement on data retention and backup procedures; (b) Finance Controller and IT ERP Team provide technical specifications; (c) external tax counsel reviews documentation package | Tax Compliance Manager / IT ERP Team | Finance Controller | 2–3 weeks preparation |
| 2 | **BIR submission and demonstration**: (a) Tax Compliance Officer submits documentation to the BIR Revenue District Office (RDO) where each entity is registered; (b) BIR may require a system demonstration: (i) Tax Compliance Manager and IT ERP Team present system at BIR office, (ii) demonstrate: transaction recording, report generation, books of accounts generation, invoice/receipt generation, data retention and backup; (c) BIR evaluates system against checklist: (i) compliance with Philippine account code structure, (ii) ability to generate required BIR reports, (iii) audit trail integrity (no backdating, no deletion without log), (iv) data retention capability (10 years per NIRC — Sec. 235, as amended by TRAIN RA 10963; BuildRight targets 10 years per A6.5) | Tax Compliance Manager / IT ERP Team | Finance Controller | 1–2 hours demonstration + 2–4 weeks BIR processing |
| 3 | **PTU issuance and implementation**: (a) BIR issues Permit to Use (PTU) for each entity's CAS; (b) Tax Compliance Manager files PTU with entity's BIR registration records; (c) PTU number referenced on all BIR filings going forward; (d) system configuration updated: PTU number displayed on system-generated reports per BIR requirement | Tax Compliance Manager | Finance Controller | 1–2 hours filing |
| 4 | **Annual PTU renewal**: (a) Tax Compliance Manager prepares renewal application 60 days before PTU expiry: (i) updated system description (if any changes), (ii) certification that no unauthorized modifications have been made, (iii) updated sample printouts if report formats changed; (b) submits renewal to RDO; (c) if BIR requests system re-demonstration: coordinates with IT ERP Team; (d) renews PTU and updates records | Tax Compliance Manager | Finance Controller | 1–2 weeks/year |
| 5 | **System change notification**: (a) when IT ERP Team plans system changes that affect CAS: (i) ERP version upgrade, (ii) new module activation (e.g., adding WMS module, POS module replacement), (iii) chart of accounts restructuring, (iv) database migration, (v) change in invoice/receipt format; (b) IT ERP Team notifies Tax Compliance Manager before implementation; (c) Tax Compliance Manager assesses: (i) minor changes (bug fixes, UI updates): no BIR notification needed, (ii) moderate changes (new module, report format change): BIR notification letter within 30 days, (iii) major changes (system replacement, database migration): re-registration per Steps 1–3; (d) external tax counsel advises on classification of change | IT ERP Team / Tax Compliance Manager | Finance Controller | 1–5 days depending on change |
| 6 | **Annual compliance audit**: (a) Tax Compliance Manager performs annual CAS compliance self-check: (i) system continues to generate all required BIR books of accounts accurately, (ii) audit trail is intact (no unauthorized modifications), (iii) data retention meets 10-year requirement, (iv) all 5 entity PTUs are current and valid, (v) any system changes during the year were properly notified to BIR; (b) documents compliance status for Finance Controller review; (c) any gaps remediated before BIR audit exposure | Tax Compliance Manager | Finance Controller | 4–6 hours/year |

### System Touchpoints
- BIR CAS registration document management: templates for Form 1907, system description, sample printout generation (W1525.1)
- System demonstration preparation: report generation, invoice/receipt samples, audit trail display (W1525.2)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly close (W9A) and the annual income-tax-return cycle (W407/W90 calendar); an entity's 4th taxable year beginning (MCIT applicability onset per NIRC §27(E)/§28(A)(e)); a loss year or business-reversal event raising the relief question; or an entity entering the 3-year excess-credit window |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly evaluation per entity; annual computation and return integration; event-driven applicability/relief reviews |

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Gross-income base preparation**: per entity, compute the MCIT base — gross income (gross receipts less sales returns, allowances and discounts, plus cost of goods sold where applicable) — with documentary support for each deduction assembled at the W9A cut-off | Tax Accountant | Tax Manager | 1.5 hours per entity per quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- ERP tax module: gross-income base queries per entity (returns/allowance deductions tagged at invoice level)
- W590 tax-provision worksheet and W407 current-tax computation integration; excess-credit carry-forward register (per-entity)
- W260 eFPS filing workspace and confirmation archive; W256 retention schedule (10-year BIR evidence)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Underpayment-penalty risk**: silently missing the 2% MCIT floor in a low-margin year under-declares the quarterly/annual returns and accrues BIR penalties and interest; mitigated by the quarterly per-entity comparison gate (Steps 1–2) wired into the W590 provision
- **Credit-lapse risk**: excess MCIT credits expire after 3 taxable years and lapse silently when untracked; mitigated by the carry-forward register with 12-month expiry alerts (Step 4)
- **Base-error risk**: the MCIT gross-income base (returns, discounts, allowances) differs from accounting revenue — unsupported deductions are disallowed on examination; mitigated by per-deduction documentation (Step 1) and the W77 examination evidence pack (Step 6)

### Automation Opportunity

- operational: quarterly MCIT-vs-normal-tax comparison signed off by the Tax Manager before the W590 provision posts
- operational: excess-credit register movements require Tax Manager review; lapsed credits require CFO acknowledgment

### Time Estimate
Quarterly evaluations 1.5 hours × 5 entities × 4 quarters = 30 hours/year; annual computations 4 hours × 5 = 20 hours/year; excess-credit register 2 hours × 5 = 10 hours/year; relief budget ≈ 8 hours/year; audit-support papers ≈ 12 hours/year. Total **≈ 80 hours/year** (≈0.04 FTE), absorbed within the existing tax team.
