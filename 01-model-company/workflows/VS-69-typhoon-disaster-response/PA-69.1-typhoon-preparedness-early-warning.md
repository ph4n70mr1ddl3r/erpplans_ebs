## W2502. Typhoon Season Pre-Positioning & Emergency Stock Planning

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | PAGASA typhoon season advisory (June–November) or approaching tropical cyclone |
| **Frequency** | Seasonal (annual pre-positioning June); event-driven per typhoon |
| **Volume** | 1 annual plan; 8–12 event-driven activations per year |
| **Owner** | VP Supply Chain |
| **Participants** | VP Supply Chain, Merchandising, DC Ops, Logistics, Store Ops |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Merchandising identifies emergency merchandise categories: tarps, plywood, nails, waterproofing, flashlights, batteries, water containers, generators, ropes, sandbags; reviews prior year demand curves by region | Merchandising Manager | VP Merchandising | 2 days |
| 2 | Supply Chain calculates pre-position quantities per DC region based on historical typhoon impact zones; coordinates emergency POs with vendors; arranges early shipments to high-risk DCs (Visayas, Bicol) | Supply Planning | VP Supply Chain | 3–5 days |
| 3 | DC Ops allocates dedicated emergency staging areas; ensures stock is segregated and tagged as emergency reserve; Logistics pre-books surge delivery capacity with 3PL partners | DC Ops / Logistics Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2 days |

### System Touchpoints
- Demantra demand planning (fit-gap C5) for surge forecasting (historical typhoon demand patterns)
- Inventory reservation for emergency stock segregation
- PO generation for emergency procurement

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Stockout risk**: If typhoon shifts track after pre-positioning, stock is stranded in wrong DC; mitigated by maintaining flexible inter-DC transfer capability
- **Overstock risk**: Mild typhoon season leads to excess emergency inventory requiring markdown; mitigated by CTL-232 (typhoon-response protocol execution)


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "reviews prior year demand curves by region" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-calculate of "calculates pre-position quantities per DC region based on historical typhoon…" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "Logistics pre-books surge delivery capacity with 3PL partners" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-232 (ensure typhoon-response protocol execution.)
- operational: maintaining flexible inter-DC transfer capability

### Time Estimate
- 30–120 min per occurrence; annual planning phase ~5–7 business days

## W2503. Store-Level Typhoon Readiness Checklist Execution

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | PAGASA Signal No. 1 raised in store region or typhoon within 48-hour projected path |
| **Frequency** | 8–15 activations per store per year (regional variance) |
| **Volume** | Up to 200 stores simultaneously for nationwide typhoons |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, All Store Staff, Regional Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Store Manager receives automated PAGASA alert via ERP dashboard; initiates typhoon readiness checklist: secure outdoor lumber yard, move chemicals to elevated shelving, cover tile displays, sandbag entrances | Store Manager | Regional Manager | 2–3 hours |
| 2 | Staff executes facility checklist: test backup generator, verify fuel levels, charge POS terminals for offline mode, secure cash in safe, backup local POS data | Stock Associate / Cashier | Store Manager | 1–2 hours |
| 3 | Store Manager confirms checklist completion via mobile app; Regional Manager reviews status across affected region; escalates incomplete stores | Store Manager | Regional Manager | 30 min |

### System Touchpoints
- Mobile store operations app for checklist execution and status reporting
- POS offline mode readiness verification
- **Custody & precedence** (per the [Event Custody & Precedence Register](../event-custody-and-precedence-register.md), E-01): the Signal 1 store readiness checklist is the canon; BCP protocol activation/monitoring runs VS-26 W848 with VS-26 W1221's regional supervision layer over it; HSE preparedness activation per VS-24 W1386 (Signal 3, or Signal 2 with forecast intensification); the closure decision (Signal 3+/LGU order) always follows W2510

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Staffing gaps**: Typhoon approaching during shift change or non-operating hours delays execution
- **Communication failure**: Power/internet loss before checklist completion; mitigated by SMS backup alerts


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "Store Manager receives automated PAGASA alert via ERP dashboard" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-test of "test backup generator, verify fuel levels, charge POS terminals for offline…" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews status across affected region" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-232 (ensure typhoon-response protocol execution.)
- operational: SMS backup alerts

### Time Estimate
- 2–4 hours per store per activation

## W2504. DC & Warehouse Typhoon Preparedness Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | PAGASA Signal No. 2 in DC region or typhoon within 36-hour projected path |
| **Frequency** | 4–8 activations per DC per year |
| **Volume** | 4 DCs; up to all 4 simultaneously for super typhoons |
| **Owner** | DC Operations Manager |
| **Participants** | DC Ops Manager, Warehouse Supervisors, Logistics, Safety Officer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | DC Ops Manager activates typhoon protocol: halt outbound shipments to affected stores, expedite any in-transit deliveries, secure dock doors, move ground-level inventory to upper racks or elevated areas | DC Ops Manager | VP Supply Chain | 3–4 hours |
| 2 | Warehouse Supervisors secure hazardous materials (paint, solvents, chemicals) per DENR hazmat protocols; verify fire suppression systems; test emergency lighting and backup power | Warehouse Supervisor / Safety Officer | DC Ops Manager | 2–3 hours |
| 3 | Logistics Manager cancels or reroutes scheduled 3PL deliveries; secures fleet vehicles in covered areas or higher ground; activates driver safety stand-down | Logistics Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- WMS for inventory relocation tracking and hazmat segregation
- Oracle Transportation Execution (in-suite — fit-gap C14) for shipment rerouting and cancellation
- Fleet GPS for vehicle positioning
- **Custody & precedence** (per the [Event Custody & Precedence Register](../event-custody-and-precedence-register.md), E-01/E-04): DC-level preparedness is this workflow's canon (stores run the W2503 checklist); DC power contingency and typhoon fuel logistics chain to W2514; BCP protocol context per VS-26 W848

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Inventory damage**: Flooding at DC3 (Calamba, Laguna — flood-prone) or DC2 (Cebu port area) during severe typhoons
- **Supply chain disruption**: Multi-day DC closure affects replenishment to 50–60 stores


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "verify fire suppression systems" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "Logistics Manager cancels or reroutes scheduled 3PL deliveries" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-436 (ensure controlled execution — Typhoon Preparedness & Early Warning Operations (PA-69.1).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
- 4–8 hours per DC per activation

## W2505. Emergency Merchandise Demand Surge Forecasting

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | PAGASA Signal No. 2+ or post-disaster assessment indicating severe impact area |
| **Frequency** | 6–10 activations per year |
| **Volume** | Regional forecasts for 3–5 regions |
| **Owner** | Merchandise Planner |
| **Participants** | Merch Planner, Supply Planning, Procurement, Finance |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Planner models demand surge using historical typhoon data by region and severity: tarps (+400%), plywood (+300%), nails (+250%), waterproofing (+500%), generators (+200%); adjusts by Signal level and population density | Merchandise Planner | VP Merchandising | 1–2 days |
| 2 | Supply Planning converts forecast to emergency PO recommendations; Procurement contacts vendors for expedited shipments; Finance pre-approves emergency procurement budget (PHP 50M–200M per major typhoon) | Supply Planning / Procurement | CFO | 1 day |
| 3 | System updates emergency stock allocation rules: prioritizes hardest-hit stores, overrides normal ABC allocation, enables automatic replenishment triggers at reduced ROP thresholds | Supply Planning | VP Supply Chain | 4–8 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- Demantra demand planning (fit-gap C5) with typhoon scenario modeling
- Automated PO generation with emergency approval workflow
- Inventory allocation engine with disaster priority rules

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Forecast accuracy**: Each typhoon is unique; overreliance on historical averages may miss category shifts
- **Vendor capacity**: All retailers compete for same emergency supplies simultaneously


### Automation Opportunity
- System rule-based auto-approval of "approves emergency procurement budget" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-update of "updates emergency stock allocation rules" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-436 (ensure controlled execution — Typhoon Preparedness & Early Warning Operations (PA-69.1).)
- operational: Merchandise Planner review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
- 2–4 hours per occurrence for model updates; 1–2 days for full planning cycle

## W2507. Typhoon Emergency Pricing Freeze Activation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | PAGASA Signal No. 2+ or DTI price monitoring alert in affected region |
| **Frequency** | 6–10 activations per year |
| **Volume** | Affects 500–2,000 emergency SKUs across 50–200 stores |
| **Owner** | Pricing Manager |
| **Participants** | Pricing Mgr, Legal, Merchandising, Store Ops |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pricing Manager activates emergency pricing freeze for disaster-essential items (construction materials, basic hardware, emergency supplies) per DTI price control regulations (RA 7581 — Price Act); locks SRP in system for affected region | Pricing Manager | VP Merchandising | 1–2 hours |
| 2 | Legal confirms DTI compliance: no price increases above SRP during declared disaster period; system blocks all promotional markdown overrides and manual price changes for frozen categories | Legal / Pricing Manager | CFO | 1 hour |
| 3 | Store Ops verifies POS price sync across affected stores; confirms price labels on shelf; documents compliance for DTI audit trail | Store Ops / Regional Manager | VP Store Operations | 2–4 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- Pricing engine with emergency freeze toggle per region/category
- POS price enforcement with override blocking
- DTI compliance reporting and audit log

### Pain Points / Risks
- **DTI penalties**: Price freeze violations carry fines of PHP 5,000–1,000,000 per violation under RA 7581
- **Margin erosion**: Extended price freezes during supply cost increases reduce profitability


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-document of "documents compliance for DTI audit trail" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-232 (ensure typhoon-response protocol execution.)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
- 1–3 hours per activation

## W2508. Pre-Typhoon Cash Float & Payment Contingency Setup

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | PAGASA Signal No. 1 in store region |
| **Frequency** | 8–15 activations per year |
| **Volume** | Up to 200 stores simultaneously |
| **Owner** | Treasury Analyst |
| **Participants** | Treasury Analyst, Store Manager, CIT Service, Finance |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Treasury increases cash float for affected stores by 50% to handle surge in cash transactions (GCash/e-wallet outages common during typhoons); coordinates expedited CIT delivery | Treasury Analyst | Treasury Manager | 2–3 hours |
| 2 | Store Manager activates payment contingency: verifies offline POS payment cache, tests manual credit card imprint capability, confirms change fund adequacy | Store Manager | Regional Manager | 1–2 hours |
| 3 | Finance monitors bank branch closures in affected areas; adjusts cash deposit schedules; authorizes extended cash holding at stores with bank holiday overrides | Finance Manager | CFO | 1–2 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- Treasury cash position module with disaster override
- POS offline payment cache management
- CIT scheduling integration with emergency priority

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Cash security**: Increased float during civil disturbance risk period
- **Banking disruption**: Multi-day bank closures in severely affected areas delay deposits


### Automation Opportunity
- System workflow orchestration of "coordinates expedited CIT delivery" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-generate of "imprint capability, confirms change fund adequacy" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-schedule of "Finance monitors bank branch closures in affected areas" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-232 (ensure typhoon-response protocol execution.)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate

## W2509. Staff Safety Notification & Deployment Readiness

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | PAGASA Signal No. 1 in store/DC region or management decision |
| **Frequency** | 8–15 activations per year |
| **Volume** | 6,932 employees across 200 stores, 4 DCs, HQ |
| **Owner** | HR Operations Manager |
| **Participants** | HR Ops Mgr, Store Managers, DC Ops Managers, Safety Officer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | HR activates emergency notification system: SMS blast to all employees in affected region with safety instructions, reporting requirements, emergency contacts; confirms employee location and safety via response tracking | HR Operations Manager | CHRO | 1–2 hours |
| 2 | Store/DC Managers implement staffing contingency: pre-identify skeleton crew for immediate post-typhoon reopening, arrange overnight shelter for essential staff at store/DC if needed, cancel employee travel to affected areas | Store/DC Manager | COO | 2–3 hours |
| 3 | HR tracks employee safety status post-typhoon; activates employee assistance program for affected staff; coordinates emergency salary advances or relief assistance per company policy | HR Operations Manager | CHRO | Ongoing (24–72 hrs post-event) |

### System Touchpoints
- HR emergency notification system with two-way messaging
- Employee self-service portal for safety check-in
- Payroll emergency advance processing module

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Communication blackout**: SMS/phone networks overwhelmed during peak typhoon; mitigated by backup radio communication
- **Employee displacement**: Staff unable to report for days due to flooding or damaged housing


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-track of "HR activates emergency notification system" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-post of "post-typhoon reopening, arrange overnight shelter for essential staff at…" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System workflow orchestration of "coordinates emergency salary advances or relief assistance per company policy" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-436 (ensure controlled execution — Typhoon Preparedness & Early Warning Operations (PA-69.1).)
- operational: backup radio communication

### Time Estimate
- 2–4 hours per activation; post-event tracking 24–72 hours
