## W2670. Multi-Location Business Permit Renewal Calendar

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual renewal season (January) |
| **Frequency** | Annual; ~205 locations across ~150 LGUs (per W54) |
| **Volume** | ~205 locations |
| **Owner** | Legal Compliance Officer |
| **Participants** | Legal Compliance Officer, Store Managers, DC Ops, Legal |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Legal Compliance Officer maintains the master permit calendar: renewal deadlines per LGU (the RA 7160 standard is "on or before Jan 20" but several LGUs vary); tracks mayor's/business permit, BFP FSIC (W2672), barangay clearance (W2673), DENR clearance where applicable, sanitary permit (W2675) | Legal Compliance Officer | VP Legal | Ongoing |
| 2 | Officer initiates renewal 60 days before each deadline: prepares the LGU-specific requirement pack (SEC certificates, prior-year gross receipts for the local business-tax computation, barangay clearance, fire cert, sanitary permit); coordinates with Store Managers on local-submission logistics | Legal Compliance Officer / Store Managers | VP Legal | 60 days before |
| 3 | Store Manager submits to the LGU Business Permit and Licensing Office (BPLO): pays the local business tax (0.5–2% of gross receipts per the LGU tax ordinance — varies by LGU per W2676), obtains the mayor's permit; uploads the issued permit to ERP; Officer confirms all ~205 locations renewed before the Jan 20 deadline | Store Manager / Legal Compliance Officer | VP Legal | January each year |

### System Touchpoints
- Regulatory license/permit repository (links to VS-166 license-portfolio master, VS-88 document control) — the canonical store for issued permits with expiry dates; the W2677 dashboard reads from here
- Location master (links to VS-29 / W254) — each of the ~205 locations (200 stores + 4 DCs + HQ per profile §15.2; off-site lockers/EV chargers tracked separately) tagged with its LGU, barangay, and permit set
- AP/local-tax module (links to VS-79 / W90 tax, VS-17 Financials) — local business tax payment posted against the location's tax accrual

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Deadline-variance risk**: ~50 LGUs do not all use Jan 20 — some are calendar-anniversary, some fiscal — and a single "annual" calendar misses the off-cycle renewals; mitigated by the per-LGU deadline field in the location master (not a single hardcoded date) and the W2677 60/30/expired alert ladder
- **Gross-receipts-dispute risk**: the LGU assesses business tax on declared gross receipts, which BuildRight may compute differently (intercompany, VAT-inclusive vs exclusive); mitigated by the Finance-validated gross-receipts figure (W2676) issued to the Store Manager before submission, not a self-declared number

### Automation Opportunity
- Permit-expiry calendar auto-populated from the issued-permit upload (OCR or structured capture of the expiry date); renewal-pack auto-assembled per LGU template from the location master

### Controls
- CTL-41: applies to permit-document validity — expired permits block active trading status in the location master; operational: VP Legal sign-off on the all-locations-renewed confirmation each January

### Time Estimate
~2 hours/location × ~205 locations + Officer calendar maintenance ~4 hours/month = **~600–700 hours/year** concentrated Nov–Feb (peak), distributed across the Legal Compliance Officer and 200+ Store Managers (~2–3 hours each during renewal season).

## W2671. New Store Business Permit Application

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New store construction approaching completion |
| **Frequency** | Per new store; 10–15/year |
| **Volume** | 10–15 new stores/year |
| **Owner** | Legal Compliance Officer |
| **Participants** | Legal Compliance Officer, Store Opening Team, Legal |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Officer researches the specific LGU's new-business requirements (each LGU's BPLO checklist differs): application forms, SEC/DTI registration certificates, barangay clearance, BFP fire-safety, sanitary, environmental (DENR ECC where required), zoning, occupancy/certificate-of-completion, sign permits (W2674) | Legal Compliance Officer | VP Legal | 3–4 months before opening |
| 2 | Officer coordinates the multi-agency parallel submissions: SEC/DTI (entity-level), LGU BPLO (mayor's permit), BFP (FSIC), City/Municipal Engineering (occupancy), DENR (environmental compliance), barangay (clearance); manages parallel tracks so no single agency is on the critical path to opening | Legal Compliance Officer / Legal | VP Legal | 2–3 months before opening |
| 3 | Officer obtains all permits before opening (a hard gate — the store cannot legally trade without the mayor's permit and FSIC); coordinates final inspections; uploads the full permit set to ERP; sets the renewal reminders (W2670 calendar) for each permit's distinct expiry | Legal Compliance Officer | VP Legal | Before opening |

### System Touchpoints
- Store-opening project module (links to VS-37 / W16 new-store opening, W1652 store project charter) — permit application is a critical-path workstream in the opening plan
- Regulatory license/permit repository (VS-166, VS-88) — the destination for issued permits
- Location master (VS-29 / W254) — new location created with its full permit set at go-live

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Critical-path risk**: a single late permit (often BFP FSIC or occupancy) delays store opening, with committed-marketing and hired-staff costs accruing against an unopening store; mitigated by the parallel-submission approach in step 2 and a 90-day permit-acquisition lead time built into the VS-37 opening schedule
- **LGU-knowledge-gap risk**: BuildRight opens in an LGU it hasn't operated in before and misses a local-only requirement (a community-tax, a specific signage ordinance); mitigated by the VS-166 regulatory-intelligence feed and a pre-opening LGU-engagement visit

### Automation Opportunity
- LGU-requirement checklist template per LGU (built once, reused for every subsequent opening in that LGU); permit-status tracker integrated into the VS-37 opening dashboard

### Controls
- CTL-41: new location cannot trade without the permit set — system-enforced opening gate (locations blocked if permits lapse); operational: VP Legal sign-off that all permits are in hand before the VS-37 soft-opening milestone

### Time Estimate
~40–60 hours per new-store permit acquisition × ~12 openings/year = **~500–720 hours/year** Legal Compliance Officer + Store Opening Team, front-loaded into the 4-month pre-opening window.

## W2672. Fire Safety Inspection & BFP Compliance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual fire safety inspection |
| **Frequency** | Annual per location; ~205 locations |
| **Volume** | ~205 locations |
| **Owner** | Safety Officer |
| **Participants** | Safety Officer, Store Manager, BFP, Facilities |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Safety Officer prepares for the BFP inspection per RA 9514 (Fire Code): fire-extinguisher maintenance and tagging, fire-exit accessibility and clear egress, alarm functionality test, sprinkler/standpipe testing where fitted, emergency-lighting test, fire-drill documentation (links to VS-24 W582), signage (exit, fire-rated doors, occupancy load) | Safety Officer | COO | 1 month before |
| 2 | BFP inspector conducts the inspection: reviews the fire-safety plan, inspects equipment, tests the alarm, verifies exits and signage; issues the Fire Safety Inspection Certificate (FSIC) if passed, or lists deficiencies (Notice of Violation) if failed | BFP Inspector / Safety Officer | COO | Per inspection |
| 3 | Safety Officer addresses any deficiencies within the 30-day cure period; requests re-inspection; obtains the FSIC; uploads to ERP; Store Manager displays the FSIC at the store entrance (regulatory visibility requirement); tracks the FSIC expiry for the W2670 renewal cycle | Safety Officer / Store Manager | COO | 30 days post-inspection |

### System Touchpoints
- Oracle eAM (links to VS-138 / W47 maintenance, fit-gap F5, VS-24 HSE) — fire-equipment maintenance records are the evidence base for the inspection
- Regulatory license/permit repository (VS-166, VS-88) — FSIC stored with expiry; the W2677 dashboard flags FSIC-expiring locations
- HSE/safety module (links to VS-24 / W140 OHS incident, W582 fire-drill) — fire-drill records and incident history

### Pain Points / Risks
- **FSIC-bottleneck risk**: BFP inspection scheduling is LGU-dependent and can slip, risking trade-without-FSIC (a criminal-liability exposure under RA 9514); mitigated by initiating the FSIC renewal 60 days early and documenting the in-progress renewal if the BFP schedule slips past expiry
- **Deficiency-cascade risk**: a failed inspection (e.g., a non-functional alarm panel) requires a part lead-time that exceeds the 30-day cure; mitigated by the pre-inspection self-test in step 1 (catches the failure before the BFP sees it) and a fire-safety-equipment vendor on retainer for expedited repair

### Automation Opportunity
- Fire-equipment maintenance calendar auto-fed to the pre-inspection checklist (flags any overdue test before the BFP inspection is scheduled); FSIC-expiry auto-tracked in the W2677 dashboard

### Controls
- CTL-219: fire-safety equipment tracked as maintained assets; operational: Safety Officer self-test sign-off before requesting the BFP inspection

### Time Estimate
~6–8 hours/location for prep + inspection + deficiency-cure × ~205 locations = **~1,250–1,670 hours/year** Safety Officers (Regional Safety Officers cover ~40 stores each), concentrated in the LGU's inspection window.

## W2673. Barangay Clearance & Community Relations Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Barangay clearance renewal |
| **Frequency** | Annual clearance; ongoing engagement |
| **Volume** | ~205 locations across ~150 barangays |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Legal Compliance Officer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Store Manager maintains the barangay relationship year-round: attends council meetings when invited, participates in barangay clean-up drives and community events, supports the barangay disaster-preparedness program (links to VS-69 typhoon response); the relationship is the lubricant that makes permit and clearance processing straightforward | Store Manager | Regional Manager | Ongoing (~2–4 hours/month) |
| 2 | Store Manager obtains the annual barangay clearance (a prerequisite for the LGU mayor's permit per RA 7160): submits the application, pays the barangay clearance fee, obtains the barangay captain's signature; typically straightforward but can be delayed by local politics or unresolved resident complaints | Store Manager | Legal Compliance Officer | Annual |
| 3 | Legal Compliance Officer tracks clearance status per store; identifies stores with relationship issues (repeated delays, complaints); coordinates with VP Legal for dispute resolution; maintains the barangay-official contact database per location | Legal Compliance Officer | VP Legal | Ongoing |

### System Touchpoints
- Stakeholder/CRM-lite (links to VS-13 / W209 community relationship, VS-104 government affairs) — barangay-official contacts and engagement log per location
- Regulatory license/permit repository (VS-166) — barangay clearance stored as a permit prerequisite for the mayor's permit
- Community-feedback/complaints (links to VS-22 / W209, VS-13 customer feedback) — unresolved complaints are the most common cause of clearance delay

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Political-blockage risk**: a barangay captain delays or denies clearance over an unrelated grievance (a constituent complaint, a request for sponsorship); mitigated by the year-round relationship investment in step 1 and VP Legal escalation path for bad-faith denials
- **Petty-corruption risk**: informal "facilitation fees" solicited at clearance time; mitigated by the ABC anti-bribery policy (links to VS-86.3 / W159 ABC monitoring) and a strict no-fee stance with VP Legal escalation rather than payment

### Automation Opportunity
- Barangay-clearance status tracker (renewal-date + relationship-risk flag) integrated into the W2677 dashboard; barangay-engagement log auto-populated from calendar/visit records

### Controls
- CTL-457 (ensure controlled execution — Multi-LGU Business Permit & License Management (PA-76.1).)
- CTL-457 (ABC / anti-bribery — links to VS-86.3 / W159): no facilitation payments; operational: Regional Manager visibility on any store with a clearance delay >2 weeks

### Time Estimate
~2–4 hours/month Store Manager relationship time + ~1–2 hours/location annual clearance submission × ~205 locations = **~50–100 hours/location-year** Store Manager + Officer tracking ~4 hours/month.

## W2674. Signage & Billboard Permit Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New store opening or signage replacement |
| **Frequency** | Per new store; ad-hoc replacements |
| **Volume** | 10–15 new stores; ~20–30 replacements/year |
| **Owner** | Facilities Manager |
| **Participants** | Facilities Mgr, Store Manager, LGU Engineering, Marketing |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Marketing designs the signage per brand standards; Facilities ensures the design complies with the LGU sign ordinance (each LGU sets size limits, setback requirements, illumination restrictions, and Filipino-language requirements per the approved-national-language policy) | Marketing / Facilities Manager | CMO / COO | Per project |
| 2 | Facilities Manager obtains the sign permit from the LGU Engineering Office: submits the design, structural calculations (wind-load for large signs), electrical plan (for illuminated signs), building-owner consent where leased; pays the permit fee (PHP 5K–50K depending on size and LGU) | Facilities Manager | COO | 2–4 weeks before installation |
| 3 | Signage contractor installs per the approved permit; LGU inspects post-installation; Facilities maintains signage condition and ongoing compliance (illumination, structural integrity); tracks permit renewal where the LGU requires annual sign-permit renewal | Facilities Manager / Contractor | COO | Per project |

### System Touchpoints
- Oracle eAM (links to VS-138 / W47, fit-gap F5) — signage tracked as a maintained asset; condition inspections scheduled
- Regulatory license/permit repository (VS-166) — sign permit stored with the location's permit set
- Brand/asset management (links to VS-14 marketing, VS-29 master data) — brand-standard signage specifications

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Ordinance-violation risk**: a brand-standard sign that violates a local ordinance (too large, too bright, wrong language) is rejected after fabrication, wasting the production cost; mitigated by the LGU-compliance check in step 1 *before* fabrication order
- **Unpermitted-sign risk**: signage installed before the permit issues (to meet an opening date) creates a violation fine and a removal order; mitigated by the sign-permit being a hard gate before installation, sequenced into the VS-37 opening critical path

### Automation Opportunity
- LGU sign-ordinance database (size/setback/language rules per LGU) auto-checked against the proposed design before fabrication

### Controls
- CTL-219: signage tracked as a maintained asset; operational: Facilities Manager sign-off that the permit is issued before the installation contractor mobilizes

### Time Estimate
~8–12 hours per new-store signage project + ~4–6 hours per replacement × ~35 projects/year = **~250–400 hours/year** Facilities Manager + Marketing.

## W2675. Sanitary & Health Permit Compliance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual sanitary permit renewal |
| **Frequency** | Annual per location |
| **Volume** | 200 stores |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Maintenance Staff, LGU Health Office |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Store Manager ensures ongoing sanitary conditions per PD 856 (Sanitary Code): clean restrooms, potable water supply, pest-control program with documented service, waste management (links to VS-73 / W2598), food-handling hygiene where a concessionaire operates | Store Manager / Maintenance Staff | Regional Manager | Ongoing (daily ops) |
| 2 | LGU Health Office conducts the annual inspection: verifies facilities, reviews the pest-control service records, inspects waste practices and restroom condition; issues the sanitary permit if compliant, or cites deficiencies | LGU Health Inspector / Store Manager | Regional Manager | Annual |
| 3 | Store Manager uploads the sanitary permit to ERP; Legal Compliance Officer tracks expiry across 200 stores; ensures renewal before expiry (a lapsed sanitary permit is a trade-suspension risk) | Store Manager / Legal Compliance Officer | COO | Annual |

### System Touchpoints
- Oracle eAM (links to VS-138 / W47, fit-gap F5) — pest-control service records and restroom-maintenance logs are the inspection evidence
- Regulatory license/permit repository (VS-166) — sanitary permit stored with expiry
- Waste management (links to VS-73 / W2598) — waste practices are part of the sanitary inspection

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Concessionaire-contagion risk**: a third-party food concessionaire's hygiene failure (links to VS-156 in-store services) citations BuildRight's sanitary permit even though BuildRight didn't operate the food handl; mitigated by the concessionaire-agreement hygiene SLA and BuildRight's right to inspect/remove
- **Lapse-driven-suspension risk**: a lapsed sanitary permit can trigger an LGU trade suspension, closing the store; mitigated by the W2677 expiry-alert ladder and the renewal initiation 60 days before expiry

### Automation Opportunity
- Sanitary-permit expiry auto-tracked; pest-control service-record completeness auto-checked before the inspection date

### Controls
- CTL-06 (permit validity); operational: Store Manager daily restroom/waste-condition check (part of VS-07 daily ops)

### Time Estimate
~2–3 hours/location annual inspection + upload + ongoing daily sanitary ops absorbed into VS-07 = **~500–700 hours/year** across Store Managers + Legal Compliance Officer tracking.

## W2676. Store Location Tax & Fee Variance Tracking

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New store opening or annual assessment |
| **Frequency** | Per new store; annual review |
| **Volume** | ~205 locations across ~150 LGUs (per W54) |
| **Owner** | Finance Analyst |
| **Participants** | Finance Analyst, Tax Manager, Legal Compliance Officer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Finance Analyst maintains the LGU tax/fee database: local business tax rates (0.5–2% of gross receipts per the LGU tax ordinance under RA 7160), real property tax rates, permit fees (mayor's, fire/sanitary/barangay), community tax rates; updates annually as LGUs amend ordinances | Finance Analyst | Tax Manager | Ongoing (~4 hours/month) |
| 2 | Analyst calculates the location-specific cost: total LGU fees per store per year, comparison across LGUs, impact on store P&L; identifies high-cost LGUs as a site-selection avoidance input (links to VS-20 / W116 site selection) | Finance Analyst | Finance Controller | Annual |
| 3 | Tax Manager incorporates the local-tax figures into store-level budgeting: adjusts P&L projections for local business tax, real property tax, permit costs; ensures the accurate accrual per location so no year-end LGU-tax surprise | Tax Manager / Finance Controller | CFO | Annual |

### System Touchpoints
- Tax master (links to VS-29 / W293 tax & regulatory master data, VS-79 / W90 tax filing) — LGU tax rates and fee schedules per location
- Store-P&L/budgeting (links to VS-17 / W26 budget, VS-101 merchandise financial planning) — local-tax line in the store P&L
- Location master (VS-29 / W254) — each location tagged with its LGU for tax-rate lookup

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Ordinance-change risk**: an LGU amends its tax ordinance mid-year (rate increase, new fee) and BuildRight's accrual understates the liability; mitigated by the VS-166 regulatory-intelligence feed monitoring LGU ordinance changes and the annual refresh in step 1
- **Gross-receipts-mapping risk**: the local business tax base (gross receipts) is defined per LGU and may differ from BuildRight's revenue recognition (intercompany, exempt sales); mitigated by the Tax Manager's documented gross-receipts-reconciliation per LGU before the W2670 declaration

### Automation Opportunity
- LGU tax-rate master auto-applied to each location's P&L; year-over-year fee-delta dashboard surfacing ordinance changes

### Controls
- CTL-457 (ensure controlled execution — Multi-LGU Business Permit & License Management (PA-76.1).)
- operational: local-tax accrual reviewed by Tax Manager quarterly (per VS-79 / W90 indirect-tax); operational: Finance Controller sign-off on the annual store-P&L local-tax line; operational: Finance Controller sign-off on the annual store-P&L local-tax line

### Time Estimate
~4 hours/month database maintenance + ~1 hour/location annual assessment × ~205 locations (≈ 209 hours) + budgeting absorbed into the VS-17 cycle = **~250–350 hours/year** Finance Analyst + Tax Manager.

## W2677. Multi-LGU Compliance Dashboard & Alert Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Continuous monitoring; monthly review |
| **Frequency** | Continuous; monthly dashboard |
| **Volume** | ~205 locations |
| **Owner** | Legal Compliance Officer |
| **Participants** | Legal Compliance Officer, VP Legal, COO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Legal Compliance Officer maintains the compliance dashboard: permit status per location (active / expiring / expired / pending) across all permit types (mayor's, FSIC, barangay, sanitary, sign), deadlines (60/30/0-day countdowns), renewal cost, and an issue log for in-progress disputes or delays | Legal Compliance Officer | VP Legal | 1 day/month |
| 2 | System generates the automated alert ladder: 60 days out → initiate renewal notification to the Store Manager; 30 days out → escalate to VP Legal; expired → critical alert for immediate action and trade-suspension risk flag | System / Legal Compliance Officer | VP Legal | Automated |
| 3 | VP Legal reviews the dashboard monthly: identifies patterns (a slow LGU, a recurring deficiency type), allocates Officer resources to the at-risk renewals, escalates political issues to the CEO; reports the compliance posture quarterly to the Board Audit Committee (links to VS-21 / W120 audit) | VP Legal | CEO | Monthly + quarterly |

### System Touchpoints
- Compliance dashboard (the VS-166 license-portfolio dashboard — the canonical single-pane view across all permit types and all locations)
- Alert/notification engine (links to VS-07 / W571 communication pattern, applied compliance-side) — the 60/30/expired ladder delivered to the right role
- Audit/board reporting (links to VS-21 / W120 audit planning, VS-36 governance) — the quarterly board posture report

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Dashboard-drift risk**: the dashboard shows "active" for a permit that was actually revoked (the LGU revoked it but the revocation wasn't recorded); mitigated by the permit-status reconciliation against the LGU online verification (where available) quarterly
- **Alert-fatigue risk**: too many 60-day alerts (every permit renews annually) desensitizes the recipients; mitigated by tiered severity (60-day is informational, 30-day is action-required, expired is critical) and role-targeted delivery

### Automation Opportunity
- Dashboard auto-populated from the permit repository (no manual status entry); LGU online-verification API integration where the LGU offers it (auto-status-reconcile)

### Controls
- CTL-41: permit-validity is the register control the dashboard surfaces (LGU permit compliance); operational: VP Legal quarterly attestation of the compliance posture to the Board Audit Committee

### Time Estimate
~1 day/month Officer dashboard maintenance + alert handling absorbed + ~2 hours/month VP Legal review = **~180–240 hours/year**, plus the quarterly board report absorbed into VS-21.
