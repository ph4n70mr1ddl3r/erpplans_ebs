### System Touchpoints
- CSR material donation tracking (Inventory deduction with zero-price SO)
- Budget allocation module for cash donations

- Difficulty attributing fair market value to donated near-expiry or slow-moving stock

## W800. Green Building Certification (BERDE/LEED) & Sustainable Store Design Standards

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New store design phase per W223; or major renovation per W226 incorporating sustainability upgrades |
| **Frequency** | Per new store (10-15 per year); per major renovation with sustainability scope |
| **Volume** | Target: all new stores built after 2027 to achieve BERDE Certified level minimum |
| **Owner** | VP for Engineering & Construction |
| **Participants** | Architect, MEP Engineer, ESG Manager, Procurement, Store Operations, BERDE/LEED Assessor |

### Background

The Philippine Green Building Code (referenced in DPWH Memorandum Circular No. 1, series of 2016) encourages green building practices, and BuildRight's ESG commitment per W192 and GOV-008 requires measurable progress on sustainability. BERDE (Building for Ecologically Responsive Design Excellence) is the Philippine national green building rating system administered by the Philippine Green Building Council (PHILGBC). Achieving BERDE certification for new stores demonstrates BuildRight's sustainability leadership, reduces long-term operating costs (energy savings of 15-25%), and aligns with SEC Memo Circular No. 4 ESG disclosure requirements per W694. This workflow ensures new store designs incorporate green building standards and certification is pursued.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **BERDE Certification Decision**: For each new store project, Director, Facilities & Real Estate and ESG Manager decide certification level: (a) BERDE Certified: minimum compliance (target for all new stores); (b) BERDE Silver: enhanced sustainability (target for flagship stores in Metro Manila); (c) BERDE Gold/Platinum: aspirational for landmark stores; (d) consider incremental CAPEX vs. long-term utility savings per W111; (e) register project with PHILGBC for BERDE assessment | Director, Facilities & Real Estate / ESG Manager | CEO | 1-2 days |
| 2 | **Sustainable Design Integration**: During W223 design phase, Architect incorporates BERDE criteria: (a) Energy Efficiency: LED lighting throughout, high-efficiency HVAC (inverter technology), roof insulation, natural ventilation for non-airconditioned areas, solar-ready roof structure per W173; (b) Water Efficiency: low-flow fixtures, rainwater harvesting for garden center irrigation, greywater recycling where feasible; (c) Materials: locally-sourced materials (within 500 km per BERDE criteria), recycled content in non-structural elements, sustainable timber per W195 for lumber display areas; (d) Indoor Environmental Quality: low-VOC paints and adhesives, adequate ventilation, daylighting for showroom areas; (e) Waste Management: construction waste recycling plan per W193, dedicated recycling area in store operations per W167; (f) Sustainable Site: permeable paving for parking per W498, native landscaping, stormwater management | Architect / MEP Engineer | Director, Facilities & Real Estate | Integrated into W223 timeline (+1-2 weeks) |
| 3 | **BERDE Documentation**: Throughout design and construction, compile BERDE submission documentation: (a) energy model showing projected energy consumption vs. baseline; (b) water consumption calculations; (c) material sourcing documentation with distance certificates; (d) indoor air quality management plan; (e) construction waste management plan with diversion targets; (f) commissioning plan for energy systems per W227 | Architect / ESG Manager | Director, Facilities & Real Estate | Continuous during design and construction |
| 4 | **Construction Compliance**: During construction per W225, Project Manager monitors green building compliance: (a) construction waste segregation and recycling per W193 target of 50% diversion; (b) erosion and sedimentation control during site work; (c) indoor air quality management during construction (HVAC protection, material storage); (d) commissioning of energy systems per BERDE requirements integrated into W227; (e) document all sustainable construction practices with photos and records for BERDE submission | Project Manager / Contractor | Director, Facilities & Real Estate | During W225 construction |
| 5 | **BERDE Assessment & Certification**: After store completion: (a) submit BERDE documentation package to PHILGBC assessor; (b) assessor conducts desk review and site verification; (c) address any assessor queries or additional documentation requests; (d) PHILGBC issues BERDE certification rating; (e) if target level not achieved: document gap and remediation plan for future stores; (f) if certified: display BERDE certificate in store per W504 digital signage; (g) publish certification in ESG report per W694 | ESG Manager / Architect | Director, Facilities & Real Estate | 2-3 months post-completion |
| 6 | **Post-Occupancy Monitoring**: During first year of operation, monitor green building performance: (a) actual energy consumption vs. modeled per W111; (b) actual water consumption vs. design target; (c) indoor air quality measurements; (d) occupant comfort survey (staff); (e) report performance to ESG Manager for W694 annual ESG report; (f) if actual performance significantly deviates from model: investigate and correct | Store Manager / ESG Manager | Director, Facilities & Real Estate | 1 year post-opening |

### System Touchpoints

- BERDE criteria checklist integrated into W223 design phase
- Energy modeling software linked to MEP design tools
- Construction waste tracking per W193 with recycling diversion rates
- Utility monitoring per W111 for post-occupancy performance tracking
- ESG data collection per W694 for annual sustainability reporting
- CAPEX budget per W21 with green building premium tracked separately
- Digital signage per W504 for BERDE certificate display

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Green building cost premium**: BERDE-compliant design adds 3-8% to construction CAPEX; payback period through utility savings is typically 5-8 years; Finance may resist incremental cost for provincial stores with lower revenue potential
- **BERDE assessor availability**: limited pool of BERDE-certified assessors in Philippines; scheduling assessment may delay certification by 1-3 months
- **Construction contractor green building experience**: many Philippine contractors lack experience with green building practices; additional supervision required to ensure compliance during construction
- **Performance gap between design and reality**: energy models often predict 20-30% savings that are not fully achieved in operation due to occupant behavior, maintenance quality, and tropical climate extremes
- **Material sourcing challenges**: locally-sourced sustainable materials may not be available in all Philippine regions at competitive prices; import of sustainable materials defeats the local sourcing criterion


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-flag of "flagship stores in Metro Manila" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-capture of "center irrigation, greywater recycling where feasible" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "documentation" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-317 (ensure controlled execution — Social Impact & Governance (PA-25.2).)
- operational: to ensure compliance during construction

### Staffing Implication

- **ESG Manager**: ~4–6 hours per new store project on BERDE coordination; absorbed by existing role
- **Architect**: green building design integration adds ~1–2 weeks to W223 design phase; absorbed by existing role with BERDE reference guide
- **No incremental headcount**.

### Time Estimate

- BERDE certification decision: 1-2 days
- Sustainable design integration: adds 1-2 weeks to W223 design
- BERDE documentation: continuous during construction
- Assessment & certification: 2-3 months post-completion
- Post-occupancy monitoring: 1 year

## W1176. Green Procurement & Sustainable Vendor Certification Program

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual vendor sustainability assessment cycle; or new vendor onboarding per W36; or ESG reporting requirement per W694 |
| **Frequency** | Annual assessment for top-100 vendors by spend; assessment integrated into new vendor onboarding; quarterly progress monitoring |
| **Volume** | ~100 vendors assessed annually (representing ~80% of COGS); assessment integrated into new vendor onboarding (~50–80/year) |
| **Owner** | Sustainability Lead |
| **Participants** | Category Manager, Procurement, Vendor, ESG Manager, VP Merchandising, External Sustainability Auditor |

### Background

BuildRight sources from ~800–1,000 vendors, including ~400 import vendors from countries with varying environmental and labor standards. Green procurement extends W195 (Sustainable Sourcing) by establishing a formal vendor sustainability certification framework with tiered ratings. Philippine regulatory requirements (SEC Memo Circular No. 4 on sustainability reporting, RA 11898 EPR Act) and international best practices (GRI Standards, UN Global Compact principles) require BuildRight to demonstrate supply chain sustainability. Vendors that meet sustainability criteria receive preferential treatment in procurement decisions, longer contract terms, and co-marketing opportunities. Vendors that fail to meet minimum standards receive corrective action plans with timelines for improvement.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sustainability criteria definition**: Sustainability Lead defines assessment criteria across 4 dimensions: (a) **Environmental**: carbon footprint disclosure, energy efficiency practices, waste management, water stewardship, packaging sustainability per W1175, hazardous substance compliance per W236; (b) **Social**: fair labor practices, living wage compliance, workplace safety, no child labor or forced labor (per W160 social compliance audit), diversity and inclusion; (c) **Governance**: anti-corruption policies per W656, ethical business practices, data privacy compliance, regulatory compliance history; (d) **Supply chain transparency**: sub-tier supplier visibility, conflict mineral sourcing, responsible sourcing of raw materials (timber, minerals, chemicals) | Sustainability Lead | VP Merchandising | 1 week |
| 2 | **Vendor self-assessment**: Distribute annual sustainability self-assessment questionnaire to top-100 vendors via vendor portal per W705: (a) structured questionnaire with 40–50 questions across 4 dimensions; (b) evidence requirements: certifications (ISO 14001, ISO 45001, SA8000), environmental permits, labor compliance records; (c) vendors self-rate on 1–5 scale per criterion with supporting evidence; (d) submission deadline: 30 days | Vendor / Procurement | Sustainability Lead | 4 hours/vendor |
| 3 | **Assessment & scoring**: Sustainability Lead evaluates vendor submissions: (a) review self-assessment against evidence; (b) score each dimension on 1–5 scale; (c) calculate overall sustainability score; (d) classify vendors: **Green** (≥ 4.0 overall): certified sustainable, preferred vendor status; **Yellow** (3.0–3.9): meets minimum standards, improvement plan recommended; **Orange** (2.0–2.9): below standard, corrective action plan required within 6 months; **Red** (< 2.0): fails minimum standards, vendor relationship review — potential termination | Sustainability Lead | VP Merchandising | 2 hours/vendor |
| 4 | **On-site verification**: For Green-rated vendors seeking certification and Orange/Red-rated vendors: (a) Sustainability Lead or External Auditor conducts on-site verification visit at vendor facility; (b) verify self-assessment claims against actual practices; (c) inspect: waste management facilities, worker conditions, safety equipment, chemical storage; (d) for import vendors: engage third-party audit firm per W160 for factory audit; (e) verification report with findings and scoring adjustment | Sustainability Lead / External Auditor | VP Merchandising | 1–2 days/vendor |
| 5 | **Corrective action & improvement**: For Yellow/Orange vendors: (a) issue corrective action plan with specific improvement targets and 6-month timeline; (b) provide resources: best practice guides, referral to sustainability consultants; (c) quarterly check-in on progress; (d) re-assessment after 6 months; (e) vendors that improve to Yellow or above retain vendor relationship; vendors that remain Red after 6 months face procurement restrictions | Sustainability Lead / Procurement | VP Merchandising | 2 hours/vendor |
| 6 | **Procurement integration**: Integrate sustainability ratings into procurement decisions: (a) vendor scorecard per W44 includes sustainability dimension (weighted 15%); (b) Green-certified vendors: preferred status, longer contract terms, first consideration for new SKU allocation; (c) Yellow vendors: standard terms, improvement expected; (d) Orange vendors: no new SKU allocation until improvement; (e) Red vendors: progressive phase-out per procurement policy; (f) sustainability score included in bid evaluation per W632 competitive bidding | Procurement / Category Manager | VP Merchandising | Ongoing |
| 7 | **Annual reporting**: Sustainability certification results feed ESG report per W694: (a) % of COGS from Green-certified vendors (target: 40% by Year 3), (b) % of vendors meeting minimum standards (target: 90%), (c) corrective action completion rate, (d) improvement trends | Sustainability Lead | ESG Manager | 4 hours/year |

### System Touchpoints
- Vendor portal per W705 for self-assessment distribution and submission
- Vendor scorecard per W44 with sustainability dimension
- Procurement decision support per W632 with sustainability weighting
- ESG reporting per W694
- Corrective action tracking module
- Factory audit coordination per W160

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Vendor burden**: sustainability assessment adds to existing vendor compliance requirements per W870; vendor fatigue may reduce response rates
- **Self-assessment accuracy**: vendors may overstate sustainability practices; on-site verification per Step 4 is essential but expensive (PHP 50,000–100,000 per audit for import vendors)
- **SME vendor capacity**: local Philippine MSME vendors per W115 may lack resources for formal sustainability programs; overly stringent criteria could exclude small local suppliers
- **Green premium**: sustainable products may carry higher COGS; Category Managers face margin pressure when sourcing from Green-certified vendors vs. cheaper non-certified alternatives
- **Import vendor opacity**: Chinese and Southeast Asian manufacturers may resist transparency on labor and environmental practices; third-party audits help but add cost and delay


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-generate of "footprint disclosure, energy efficiency practices, waste management, water…" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-record of "records" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "review self-assessment against evidence" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-317 (ensure controlled execution — Social Impact & Governance (PA-25.2).)
- operational: Sustainability Lead review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **Sustainability Lead**: ~30% of time on green procurement program (~60 hours/month); absorbed by existing role
- **Procurement**: ~4 hours/month on sustainability integration; absorbed by existing role
- **External Auditor**: engaged for ~20–30 on-site verifications/year

### Time Estimate
- Criteria definition: 1 week/year
- Per vendor assessment: 4 hours (vendor) + 2 hours (Sustainability Lead) = 6 hours
- Per on-site verification: 1–2 days
- Quarterly monitoring: 8 hours/quarter
- **Annual estimate: ~500–600 hours/year**

## W1331. Employee Volunteer Program Management, Tracking & Impact Measurement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual CSR calendar planning; quarterly volunteer event scheduling; or disaster relief activation requiring employee volunteer deployment |
| **Frequency** | ~4–6 organized volunteer events per year (company-wide); ~1–2 local events per store per year; ad-hoc disaster relief deployment |
| **Volume** | Target: 40% employee participation rate (2,700 of 6,911 employees volunteer at least once per year); ~8,000–10,000 volunteer hours/year |
| **Owner** | CSR Coordinator (within Marketing/HR) |
| **Participants** | CHRO, Store Manager (local event coordination), NGO Partner, Department Heads (team participation), Sustainability Lead (impact measurement) |

### Background

BuildRight's CSR program per W194 includes community development initiatives that benefit from employee volunteer participation. Employee volunteering strengthens community relationships, builds team cohesion, enhances employer brand (supporting recruitment per W15 and retention), and generates measurable social impact for ESG reporting per W694. However, managing a volunteer program across 200 stores and 6,911 employees requires structured scheduling, attendance tracking, safety management, and impact measurement. Philippine cultural traditions of "bayanihan" (communal unity) and the company's presence in disaster-prone regions create both opportunity and obligation for organized volunteer deployment, particularly during typhoon and flood relief operations.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Annual volunteer program planning**: CSR Coordinator develops annual volunteer calendar: (a) schedule 4–6 company-wide volunteer events aligned with national events (National Housing Month in October, Earth Day in April, National Volunteer Month in December, BuildRight Foundation anniversary), (b) allocate volunteer event budget: materials, transportation, meals, safety equipment, (c) identify NGO partners for each event (Habitat for Humanity Philippines, Philippine Red Cross, local LGU partnerships), (d) set participation targets by region, (e) obtain CEO and CHRO approval for calendar and budget | CSR Coordinator | CHRO / CEO | 3–5 days |
| 2 | **Event logistics & registration**: For each volunteer event: (a) CSR Coordinator publishes event details via internal communication (company app, email, bulletin board): date, location, activity description, capacity, skills required, safety requirements, (b) employee self-registration via HR portal or volunteer management app, (c) Store Manager approves volunteer time-off for participating employees (ensuring store operations coverage), (d) CSR Coordinator coordinates with NGO partner on activity scope, materials, and safety briefing, (e) arrange transportation for volunteers (company shuttle or travel allowance for provincial events) | CSR Coordinator / Store Manager | CHRO | 3–5 days per event |
| 3 | **Event execution**: (a) CSR Coordinator conducts safety briefing at event site: hazards, emergency procedures, PPE requirements (hard hats for construction events, gloves for cleanup events), (b) employee volunteers execute activity (house building, community cleanup, school renovation, disaster relief packing), (c) document event with photos and videos for internal communication and ESG reporting, (d) collect participant feedback via post-event survey (satisfaction, suggestions) | CSR Coordinator / NGO Partner | CHRO | Event day (4–8 hours) |
| 4 | **Attendance & hours tracking**: (a) CSR Coordinator records attendance and volunteer hours per employee via HR portal: employee ID, event, hours contributed, (b) volunteer hours feed into individual employee records per W51 for training/development tracking, (c) volunteer hours feed into ESG data collection per W694: total volunteer hours, participation rate by gender, participation rate by region, (d) recognize top volunteers in quarterly company newsletter and annual awards per W72 | CSR Coordinator / HR | CHRO | 1–2 hours per event |
| 5 | **Impact measurement**: Sustainability Lead measures social impact of volunteer events: (a) output metrics: houses built, classrooms renovated, families served, trees planted, relief packs assembled, (b) outcome metrics: community satisfaction survey, beneficiary feedback, (c) economic value: volunteer hours × average hourly wage (local wage-proxy methodology per PSA/ILO wage statistics), (d) compile impact data for quarterly ESG dashboard per W1321 and annual sustainability report per W694 | Sustainability Lead / CSR Coordinator | VP Marketing | 2–3 days/quarter |
| 6 | **Disaster relief volunteer deployment**: When a natural disaster (typhoon, earthquake, flood) affects a BuildRight community: (a) CEO authorizes emergency volunteer deployment, (b) CSR Coordinator coordinates with Philippine Red Cross or DSWD for volunteer assignment, (c) Store Manager in affected area organizes local employee volunteers, (d) safety protocol: volunteers deployed only to areas declared safe by LGU, insurance coverage verified per W59, (e) rapid impact reporting: volunteer hours, relief distributed, community impact within 30 days | CSR Coordinator / CEO | CHRO | Per event |

### System Touchpoints

- HR portal with volunteer event registration, attendance tracking, and hours recording
- Volunteer management dashboard: participation rate, hours by department, event calendar
- ESG data collection module per W694: volunteer hours, participation metrics
- Internal communication platform for event promotion and photo sharing
- Integration with W194 (CSR program), W51 (employee development), W72 (performance recognition), W694 (ESG reporting), W1321 (ESG dashboard)

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Volunteer participation inequality**: Metro Manila stores may have high participation while provincial stores with fewer employees and longer hours have lower participation; mitigated by regional event scheduling and local event options
- **Operational coverage**: Store Managers must balance volunteer release with store operations; volunteer events during peak sales periods (weekends, paydays) reduce participation
- **Safety liability**: employee injury during volunteer events raises workers' compensation questions; mitigated by safety briefings, PPE provision, and insurance coverage confirmation per W59
- **Impact measurement difficulty**: social impact (e.g., improved community wellbeing) is harder to quantify than output metrics (houses built); mitigated by tracking both output and outcome indicators

- System auto-log of "logistics & registration" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "document event with photos and videos for internal communication and ESG reporting" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-317 (ensure controlled execution — Social Impact & Governance (PA-25.2).)
- operational: regional event scheduling and local event options; operational: safety briefings, PPE provision, and insurance coverage confirmation per W59; operational: tracking both output and outcome indicators

### Staffing Implication

- **CSR Coordinator**: ~15–20 days/year on volunteer program management. Absorbed within Marketing/HR role.
- **Sustainability Lead**: ~6–8 days/year on impact measurement. Absorbed.
- **Store Managers**: ~2 hours per event for local coordination. Absorbed.
- **No incremental headcount.**

### Time Estimate

- Annual planning: 3–5 days
- Per event logistics: 3–5 days
- Event execution: 4–8 hours per event
- Tracking and reporting: 1–2 hours per event
- Impact measurement: 2–3 days/quarter
- **Annual estimate: ~18–42 days (CSR Coordinator; 3–5 days planning + 4–6 events × 3–5 days logistics, matching the Staffing line) + ~10 days (Sustainability Lead)**

## W1332. Indigenous Peoples (IP) & Cultural Sensitivity Compliance for Store Expansion

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New store site selection per W223 in an area with identified Indigenous Peoples (IP) communities; or LGU consultation revealing IP presence; or NCIP (National Commission on Indigenous Peoples) notification |
| **Frequency** | Estimated 3–5 new store projects per year may intersect with IP areas (particularly in Mindanao, Cordillera, and Palawan) |
| **Volume** | 10–15 new stores per year; ~30% may require IP compliance assessment |
| **Owner** | VP Legal |
| **Participants** | COO, External Affairs Manager, NCIP Representative, IP Community Leader, Director, Facilities & Real Estate, Sustainability Lead |

### Background

The Indigenous Peoples Rights Act (IPRA, RA 8371) requires Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC) from Indigenous Cultural Communities/Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs) before any project affecting their ancestral domains or lands can proceed. BuildRight Depot is expanding across the Philippines, including Mindanao (where Lumad, Subanen, and other IP groups have ancestral domains), the Cordillera region (Igorot communities), and Palawan (Palaw'an and Tagbanwa communities). Failure to comply with IPRA can result in project delays, legal injunctions, NCIP enforcement actions, and reputational damage. This workflow ensures that new store development per W223 and W225 includes IP compliance assessment, community engagement, and FPIC processes where required.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **IP presence screening**: During site selection per W223: (a) External Affairs Manager checks NCIP Ancestral Domain Registry for the proposed site location, (b) checks LGU Comprehensive Land Use Plan for IP community designations, (c) consults with NCIP regional office on any pending or approved Certificates of Ancestral Domain Title (CADT) in the area, (d) if IP presence confirmed or likely: classify site as "IP-sensitive" and trigger FPIC process | External Affairs Manager | VP Legal | 3–5 days per site |
| 2 | **FPIC process initiation**: For IP-sensitive sites: (a) VP Legal coordinates with NCIP to initiate FPIC process, (b) submit project description to NCIP: store location, size, expected employment, economic impact, environmental impact, (c) NCIP determines FPIC requirement level based on impact assessment, (d) NCIP assigns a focal person and facilitates community consultation process | VP Legal / External Affairs Manager | COO | 1–2 weeks |
| 3 | **Community consultation & engagement**: External Affairs Manager conducts community engagement: (a) attend community assembly (organized by NCIP) to present BuildRight's project to IP community leaders and members, (b) present: store concept, employment opportunities for community members, potential impact (noise, traffic, water, land use), community benefit proposals (scholarship program, infrastructure improvement, skills training per W52), (c) respond to community questions and concerns transparently, (d) document all community interactions with NCIP observer present | External Affairs Manager / VP Legal | COO | 2–4 weeks |

## W1333. Stakeholder Engagement Matrix & Materiality Assessment Refresh

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual ESG reporting cycle (Q1); or significant change in stakeholder landscape (new regulator, new community, IPO preparation, major incident); or every 2 years as best practice |
| **Frequency** | Full materiality assessment every 2 years; annual stakeholder engagement matrix update; continuous stakeholder feedback collection |
| **Volume** | ~10–15 stakeholder groups; ~30–40 ESG topics assessed for materiality |
| **Owner** | Sustainability Lead |
| **Participants** | CEO, VP Legal, CFO, COO, CHRO, VP Marketing, External Sustainability Advisor, stakeholder representatives |

### Background

Materiality assessment is the foundation of ESG strategy and reporting: it determines which sustainability topics are most important to BuildRight's stakeholders and most impactful on business performance. SEC Memo Circular No. 4 requires companies to conduct materiality assessment as part of sustainability reporting. W694 covers annual ESG data collection and report preparation, and W1321 covers target setting and progress tracking. This workflow ensures the materiality assessment itself is refreshed regularly — stakeholder priorities change over time, new ESG topics emerge (e.g., AI ethics, biodiversity, just transition), and business strategy evolves. A stale materiality assessment leads to reporting on topics that stakeholders no longer care about while missing emerging topics that could become material risks.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stakeholder identification & mapping**: Sustainability Lead updates stakeholder engagement matrix: (a) identify all stakeholder groups: (i) shareholders/owners, (ii) employees (6,911), (iii) customers (B2C, B2B, trade), (iv) vendors/suppliers (~800–1,000), (v) government/regulators (BIR, DENR, DOLE, SEC, LGU), (vi) communities (200 store locations), (vii) lenders/banks, (viii) insurance partners, (ix) industry associations, (x) media, (xi) NGOs/civil society, (xii) academic institutions; (b) classify stakeholders by: influence (ability to affect BuildRight's operations), impact (degree to which BuildRight affects them), proximity (direct vs. indirect relationship); (c) map stakeholders on a 2×2 matrix: high influence/high impact (priority engagement), high influence/low impact (keep satisfied), low influence/high impact (keep informed), low influence/low impact (monitor) | Sustainability Lead | VP Legal | 2–3 days |
| 2 | **ESG topic universe compilation**: Sustainability Lead compiles comprehensive ESG topic list: (a) environmental: GHG emissions, energy management, water stewardship, waste management, circular economy, biodiversity, packaging sustainability, hazardous materials; (b) social: employee health & safety, labor practices, diversity & inclusion, training & development, community impact, customer safety, supply chain labor standards, data privacy; (c) governance: anti-corruption, risk management, board governance, tax transparency, business ethics, cyber security, AI ethics, regulatory compliance; (d) cross-cutting: climate resilience, disaster preparedness, human rights, economic contribution; (e) reference GRI Standards topics, SASB Retail standards, and Philippine-specific issues (IP rights, disaster resilience, informal worker welfare) | Sustainability Lead / External Advisor | VP Legal | 3–5 days |

## W1479. Disaster Relief & Community Resilience Program Coordination & Emergency Product Distribution

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Natural disaster event (typhoon, earthquake, flooding) affecting BuildRight store communities; or LGU request for emergency building material supply; or DSWD/Red Cross coordination request |
| **Frequency** | ~3–5 major disaster events/year in the Philippines (typhoons); localized flooding events ~10–15/year |
| **Volume** | Per major event: ~50–100 affected stores; emergency product distribution of PHP 5–20M in building materials, tarps, water tanks, tools |
| **Owner** | COO |
| **Participants** | CEO, COO, VP Merchandising, DC Manager, Store Manager, Sustainability Lead, VP Legal, LGU officials, DSWD, Red Cross, Community Leaders |

### Background

The Philippines experiences an average of 20 typhoons per year, with 3–5 causing significant damage to communities where BuildRight operates. BuildRight's product portfolio — building materials, waterproofing, tarps, water tanks, generators, tools, cement, roofing — is exactly what communities need for disaster response and rebuilding. As a major retailer in affected communities, BuildRight has both a social responsibility and a business opportunity to support disaster relief: (a) providing emergency building materials to displaced families, (b) supporting LGU emergency shelter construction, (c) ensuring product availability when demand surges post-disaster, and (d) coordinating with government agencies and NGOs for efficient distribution. This workflow manages BuildRight's disaster relief program from activation through emergency distribution, volunteer coordination, and post-disaster community support — aligning with ESG social impact objectives per W194 and business continuity per W26.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Disaster event activation**: (a) when a typhoon (signal 3+), earthquake (magnitude 5+), or major flooding event is forecast/confirmed: COO activates BuildRight's Disaster Response Protocol; (b) COO assesses: (i) which BuildRight stores and DCs are in the affected path? (ii) pre-position high-demand items at at-risk stores: tarps, plywood, nails, cement, water containers, generators, batteries, flashlights; (iii) coordinate with DC Managers to pre-stage emergency inventory; (c) CEO approves emergency product allocation budget (initial PHP 5M authorization, up to PHP 20M for major events) | COO / CEO | CEO | 2–4 hours |

| 3 | **Community donation & LGU coordination**: (a) COO coordinates with LGU disaster management offices (MDRRMO — Municipal/City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office) in affected areas: (i) offer BuildRight as emergency building material supplier, (ii) donate materials for community shelters and repair of public facilities (schools, health centers), (iii) provide logistics support (BuildRight trucks for relief goods distribution); (b) Sustainability Lead manages donation documentation: (i) itemized list of donated materials with value, (ii) LGU acknowledgment receipts, (iii) photographs for ESG reporting per W1330; (c) VP Legal ensures donations comply with tax deductibility requirements per BIR regulations | COO / Sustainability Lead / VP Legal | CEO | Per event |
| 4 | **Volunteer deployment**: (a) Sustainability Lead activates employee volunteer program per W1331: (i) employees in unaffected stores volunteer for deployment to affected stores (1–3 day assignments), (ii) volunteers assist with: store cleanup, emergency product distribution, community cleanup drives, temporary shelter assembly; (b) HR ensures volunteers are covered by workers' compensation during volunteer deployment per DOLE requirements; (c) Store Manager manages volunteer assignments and logistics (transportation, meals) | Sustainability Lead / HR / Store Manager | COO | Per event |

## W1480. Supplier Diversity Program, Small & Medium Enterprise (SME) Vendor Development & Capacity Building

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual procurement strategy review; or government mandate for SME inclusion in supply chain; or ESG social impact target setting |
| **Frequency** | Annual program planning; quarterly vendor development workshops; monthly SME vendor performance review |
| **Volume** | Target: ≥ 20% of vendor base as MSMEs within 3 years (per W115); currently ~150–200 SME vendors (~20% of the ~800–1,000 base, per the Background) |
| **Owner** | VP Merchandising |
| **Participants** | Category Manager, Buyer, Procurement, Sustainability Lead, Finance (payment terms), SME Vendor, DTI (MSME development programs) |

### Background

The Philippine government through DTI's MSME Development Plan encourages large enterprises to integrate MSMEs (Micro, Small & Medium Enterprises) into their supply chains. BuildRight's vendor base of ~800–1,000 includes ~150–200 Philippine MSMEs — local manufacturers of hardware, fixtures, home décor, garden products, and construction materials. Supporting SME vendors aligns with BuildRight's ESG social impact objectives per W194 and strengthens local economic development in communities where BuildRight operates. However, SME vendors often face challenges that prevent them from effectively supplying a large retailer: (a) inconsistent product quality, (b) inability to meet BuildRight's volume requirements (200 stores), (c) lack of barcode/GS1 compliance per W1345, (d) limited financial capacity to offer payment terms (Net 30), and (e) weak logistics capability for nationwide distribution. This workflow establishes a formal Supplier Diversity Program that identifies, develops, and supports SME vendors to become reliable BuildRight suppliers — creating shared value for BuildRight (unique local products, vendor diversification) and for the SMEs (market access, business growth).

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **SME vendor identification & assessment**: (a) Category Manager identifies product categories suitable for SME sourcing: (i) categories where local products offer unique value (regional tiles, handcrafted fixtures, indigenous materials), (ii) categories where SME sourcing reduces import dependency, (iii) categories where local SMEs exist but are not yet BuildRight vendors; (b) Procurement conducts SME capability assessment: (i) product quality sampling and testing, (ii) production capacity (units/month vs. BuildRight demand), (iii) business registration (DTI/SEC), (iv) tax compliance (BIR registration), (v) GS1 barcode capability per W1345, (vi) logistics capability (direct-to-store or DC delivery) | Category Manager / Procurement | VP Merchandising | 2 hours/vendor |
