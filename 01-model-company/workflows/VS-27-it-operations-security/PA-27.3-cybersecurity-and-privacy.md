## W53. Data Privacy Breach Response

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Suspected or confirmed breach of personal data (customer, employee, or vendor data) as defined by RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012) |
| **Frequency** | Rare; estimated 0–2 incidents/year requiring formal breach response |
| **Volume** | Variable — from a single record exposed to bulk data extraction |
| **Owner** | Data Protection Officer (DPO) |
| **Participants** | DPO, CIO, IT Security, Legal, affected department head, NPC (National Privacy Commission), affected data subjects |

### Background

Under RA 10173 and its Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR), BuildRight Depot (as a PIC — Personal Information Controller) must: (a) notify the National Privacy Commission within 72 hours of discovering a breach involving sensitive personal information affecting 100+ data subjects, (b) notify affected data subjects within a reasonable time, (c) document all breaches regardless of size. BuildRight holds personal data for ~600,000 loyalty members, ~5,200 trade accounts, ~200 corporate accounts, ~6,932 employees, and ~1,000 vendors.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Breach discovered or suspected: IT Security alerts (automated monitoring), employee report, third-party notification, or customer complaint about unauthorized access | IT Security / Any employee | CIO | Immediate |
| 2 | CIO and DPO assess breach severity: (a) scope — how many records, what data types (name, contact, financial, government ID), (b) vector — how the breach occurred (external attack, insider, misconfiguration, lost device, vendor breach), (c) containment status — is the breach ongoing or contained | CIO + DPO | CEO | 1–2 hours |
| 3 | **Containment**: IT Security takes immediate action to stop the breach: isolate affected systems, revoke compromised credentials, block unauthorized access, preserve forensic evidence | IT Security | CIO | As needed |
| 4 | DPO classifies the breach per NPC guidelines: (a) **Notifiable breach** — involves sensitive personal info, affects 100+ data subjects, or involves significant harm; (b) **Non-notifiable breach** — limited scope, no sensitive data, quickly contained | DPO | CEO | 30 min |
| 5 | If notifiable: DPO prepares NPC notification within 72 hours: nature of breach, data subjects affected, data types exposed, measures taken, remedial actions planned; submits via NPC online portal or prescribed form | DPO | CEO | 2–4 hours |
| 6 | DPO prepares data subject notification: clear description of breach in plain language, data exposed, potential harm, mitigation steps data subjects should take, BuildRight contact for inquiries; notification via email, SMS, or website notice depending on breach scope | DPO + Legal | CMO | 2–4 hours |
| 7 | CIO directs forensic investigation: determine root cause, full scope, and whether any data was exfiltrated; engage external forensic firm if needed; preserve all logs and evidence | CIO | CEO | 1–5 days |
| 8 | Legal assesses regulatory and litigation risk: review NPC requirements, evaluate liability exposure, prepare responses to potential regulatory inquiry or customer claims | Legal | CEO | As needed |
| 9 | Affected department head implements immediate operational safeguards: e.g., if payment data breached — reset payment credentials, work with payment gateways; if employee data breached — offer credit monitoring | Dept. Head | CEO | As needed |
| 10 | DPO documents full incident in breach register: timeline, root cause, scope, notifications sent, remedial actions, resolution date; breach register retained for 5 years per NPC requirements | DPO | CEO | 2 hours |
| 11 | Post-incident: CIO and DPO conduct lessons-learned review; update security controls, access policies, and data handling procedures; implement preventive measures; schedule follow-up assessment in 30 days | CIO + DPO | CEO | 2 hours |
| 12 | Quarterly: DPO reviews breach register with CEO and Internal Audit; analyzes trends; reports aggregate breach statistics in annual privacy impact assessment | DPO | CEO | 30 min/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Breach register in system: incident ID, discovery date/time, classification, scope, root cause, notifications, resolution status (W53.10)
- Data access audit trail: system maintains immutable log of all access to personal data (customer records, employee records) with user ID, timestamp, and action; forensic extraction capability for breach investigation (W53.7)
- Data subject notification workflow: system generates notification list of affected data subjects from breach scope analysis; bulk email/SMS capability for notification delivery (W53.6)
- Role-based access enforcement: system enforces minimum-necessary access to personal data; access revocation capability for containment (W53.3)
- Data encryption at rest and in transit: customer and employee data encrypted in database and during transmission; encryption key management with emergency rotation capability (W53.3)
- Data Subject Access Request (DSAR) full lifecycle per RA 10173: (a) **access**: DPO acknowledges DSAR within 72 hours; system generates data package containing all personal data held for the requesting data subject (loyalty profile, transaction history, AR account data, complaint history) in machine-readable format (JSON or CSV) within 30 days; DPO reviews package for third-party data exclusion before release; (b) **rectification**: data subject requests correction of inaccurate personal data; DPO or AR Clerk updates record in system with change log; (c) **erasure / right to be forgotten**: upon verified request, DPO initiates erasure workflow — system anonymizes personal data in active records (replace name, email, phone with "ANONYMIZED-[hash]") while preserving transaction records for BIR 10-year retention requirement (transaction amounts, dates, VAT, and payment methods retained; personal identifiers removed); system flags anonymized records to prevent re-identification; for loyalty accounts with unredeemed points, DPO offers final redemption before erasure; erasure logged with request date, data subject verification method, scope of erasure, and DPO approval; (d) **data portability**: system exports data subject's personal data in structured, machine-readable format (JSON) via DPO-approved download link with 72-hour expiry; (e) **consent withdrawal**: data subject withdraws consent for marketing communications; system updates consent flag in customer master; suppresses customer from all marketing lists, email campaigns, and SMS notifications within 24 hours; transactional communications (order confirmations, warranty notifications) continue as service necessity; (f) DPO reports DSAR metrics quarterly: volume by type, average resolution time, backlog
- Integration with W41 (complaint intake as DSAR channel), W17 (loyalty data — loyalty account erasure), W24 (customer accounts — account closure with erasure), W8 (AR account closure — W8 step 12 references PII anonymization), W15/W43 (employee lifecycle — employee data erasure after 10-year retention), W383 (General IT Security Incident Response)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **72-hour NPC notification deadline**: Tight timeline for breach assessment, scope determination, and formal notification; if breach is discovered on a Friday or during a holiday, DPO and Legal must be available on short notice
- **Data subject notification at scale**: If a breach affects 100,000+ loyalty members, bulk email/SMS notification requires coordination with marketing systems and may trigger customer panic or media attention before BuildRight is ready with public messaging
- **Erasure vs. BIR retention conflict**: Right to be forgotten under RA 10173 conflicts with BIR's 10-year record retention requirement; anonymization approach must be legally defensible and technically robust to prevent re-identification
- **Third-party vendor breaches**: If a SaaS provider (e.g., ecommerce platform, payroll processor) experiences a breach affecting BuildRight data, response depends on vendor cooperation and contractual notification SLAs that may not align with NPC's 72-hour requirement


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-monitor of "Breach discovered or suspected" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-record of "records, what data types" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System rule-based authorization of "unauthorized access, preserve forensic evidence" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-27 (safeguard customer data.)
- operational: Data Protection Officer (DPO) review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
- Breach assessment and containment: 1–2 hours (steps 1–3)
- NPC notification preparation: 2–4 hours (step 5); data subject notification: 2–4 hours (step 6)
- Forensic investigation: 1–5 days (step 7)
- Full incident documentation and lessons learned: 4 hours (steps 10–11)
- DSAR fulfillment: 2–5 days per request (data extraction, review, and delivery)
- Quarterly breach register review: 30 min/quarter

### Staffing Implication
- **1 Data Protection Officer (DPO)**: required by RA 10173 for organizations processing personal data of 1,000+ individuals. BuildRight holds data for 600K+ customers and 6,932 employees. The DPO role is a dedicated position within the Legal & Compliance team (the 14-person roster per profile §3.3 already includes the DPO, regulatory, and AML/MLRO functions).
- No other incremental headcount. Breach response is a cross-functional effort by existing IT, Legal, and management.

| **Frequency** | Daily monitoring; weekly scanning; monthly patching |
| **Volume** | ~1,500 endpoints; 100+ servers; 600 POS terminals; 50+ integrations |
| **Owner** | IT Security Manager |
| **Participants** | Security Analyst, Infrastructure Team, Application Developers, Internal Audit |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Vulnerability Scanning**: Automated tools scan network, servers, and applications for known vulnerabilities (CVEs); generate prioritized risk report | System / Security Analyst | IT Security Mgr | Weekly |
| 2 | **Patch Prioritization**: Review scan results; prioritize "Critical" and "High" vulnerabilities; coordinate with App/Infra teams for testing | Security Analyst | IT Security Mgr | 2 hours/week |
| 3 | **Patch Deployment**: Deploy security patches to OS, middleware, and applications during maintenance windows; verify post-patch health | Infrastructure Team | IT Security Mgr | 4 hours/week |
| 4 | **Endpoint Protection (EDR) Monitoring**: Monitor EDR dashboard for malware, unauthorized access, or lateral movement; triage and isolate suspicious devices | Security Analyst | IT Security Mgr | Daily |
| 5 | **Phishing Simulation**: Conduct quarterly phishing tests for all 6,932 employees; assign remedial training to users who fail | Security Analyst | IT Security Mgr | 4 hours/quarter |
| 6 | **Firewall & WAF Review**: Periodic review of firewall rules and Web Application Firewall (WAF) policies to ensure "least privilege" access | Security Analyst | CIO | Quarterly |

### System Touchpoints
- Vulnerability Scanner (Nessus, Qualys, or similar) (W367.1)
- Endpoint Detection & Response (EDR) Dashboard (W367.4)
- Automated Patch Management System (WSUS, SCCM, etc.) (W367.3)
- Security Information & Event Management (SIEM) for log correlation
- Integration with W132 (software changes), W152 (access management), W53 (breach response)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Patch-window-lag risk**: critical CVEs are exploited in the wild before the monthly patch cycle catches up; mitigated by daily monitoring, weekly scanning, and prioritized Critical/High patching with coordinated testing (steps 1–2)
- **Lateral-movement risk**: EDR alerts triaged late let an intruder pivot from one endpoint to the estate; mitigated by EDR dashboard monitoring with immediate suspicious-device isolation (step 4)

### Staffing Implication
- **1 Security Manager + 1 Security Analyst**: (within planned IT team of 122).
- **No incremental headcount.**


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-scan of "scanning" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "Review scan results" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "verify post-patch health" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-324 (ensure controlled execution — Cybersecurity & Privacy (PA-27.3).)
- operational: IT Security Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Patch cycle 6 hours/week (prioritization 2 hours + deployment 4 hours) ≈ 312 hours/year across ~1,500 endpoints, 100+ servers, and 600 POS terminals; EDR monitoring daily within the security shift; quarterly phishing simulation 4 hours ≈ 16 hours/year; quarterly firewall/WAF rule review (vulnerability scanning itself automated weekly).

## W375. Privileged Access Management (PAM) Operations

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Request for administrative or elevated access; monthly review cycle for high-risk accounts |
| **Frequency** | Daily (provisioning); Monthly (review) |
| **Volume** | ~50–70 privileged accounts (Cloud Admins, DBAs, ERP Admins, Network Admins) |
| **Owner** | IT Security Manager |
| **Participants** | IT Infrastructure, System Admins, Security Analyst, Internal Audit |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Access Request**: IT staff requests temporary or permanent privileged access (e.g., Domain Admin, Cloud Global Admin) with justification | IT Staff | IT Security Mgr | 10 min |
| 2 | **Security Approval**: Security Manager verifies need-to-know and least-privilege; checks for SOD conflicts (W338) | IT Security Mgr | CIO | 15 min |
| 3 | **JIT Provisioning**: System grants Just-in-Time (JIT) access for a limited window (e.g., 4 hours); requires MFA challenge | System | — | Automated |
| 4 | **Session Monitoring**: System logs all actions taken during privileged session (keystrokes, commands); high-risk actions alert Security Analyst | System | Security Analyst | Continuous |
| 5 | **Access Revocation**: Access automatically expires at end of window; permanent accounts require manual de-provisioning upon role change (W152) | System / IT Helpdesk | IT Security Mgr | Automated |
| 6 | **Monthly Privileged Review**: Security Analyst reviews all accounts with administrative rights; verifies users still require access; revokes unused permissions | Security Analyst | IT Security Mgr | 4 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- PAM Tool (CyberArk, Azure PIM, HashiCorp Vault)
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) provider
- SIEM for log correlation and alerting
- Integration with W152 (employee lifecycle) and W338 (SOD review)


### Pain Points / Risks
- **Standing-privilege risk**: permanent admin accounts accumulate across the ~50–70 privileged population; mitigated by Just-in-Time, time-boxed grants with MFA challenge (step 3)
- **Unreviewed-privilege risk**: role changes leave prior admin rights attached to the person; mitigated by monthly high-risk-account review and manual de-provisioning on role change (step 5)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-request of "requests temporary or permanent privileged access" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-validate of "checks for SOD conflicts" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "logs all actions taken during privileged session" (Step 4, already system-executed).

### Controls
- CTL-324 (ensure controlled execution — Cybersecurity & Privacy (PA-27.3).)
- operational: IT Security Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Access requests 25 min each (request 10 min + security approval 15 min) at realized privileged-access volume across ~50–70 accounts (JIT provisioning, session recording, and expiry automated); monthly privileged-access review 4 hours ≈ 48 hours/year.

## W383. IT Security Incident Response (General)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Security alert from SIEM, EDR detection, unauthorized access report, or suspected malware infection |
| **Frequency** | As-needed; estimated 5–10 incidents/month requiring investigation |
| **Volume** | Covers ~1,500 endpoints, ~600 POS terminals, and cloud infrastructure |
| **Owner** | IT Security Manager |
| **Participants** | Security Analyst, IT Infrastructure, Legal (if breach), affected Business Owners |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Triage & Classification**: Verify alert; classify by severity (Low, Medium, High, Critical) and type (Malware, Unauthorized Access, Denial of Service, etc.) | Security Analyst | IT Security Mgr | 30 min |
| 2 | **Containment**: Execute immediate isolation steps: block IP addresses, disable compromised accounts, or isolate infected host from network | IT Security Mgr | CIO | 1 hour |
| 3 | **Investigation & Analysis**: Analyze logs, memory, and disk to determine root cause, extent of lateral movement, and whether PII was touched (trigger W53 if yes) | Security Analyst | IT Security Mgr | 4–8 hours |
| 4 | **Eradication**: Remove the threat: delete malware, close vulnerability, or rebuild compromised system from clean image | Infrastructure Team | IT Security Mgr | 2–4 hours |
| 5 | **Recovery**: Restore services; monitor for recurrence; verify system integrity before full business resumption | Infrastructure Team | CIO | 1–2 hours |
| 6 | **Post-Incident Review**: Document timeline, impact, and lessons learned; update security controls and W381 Knowledge Base | IT Security Mgr | CIO | 2 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- SIEM / Log Management for event correlation and forensic investigation (W383.1, 3)
- EDR Dashboard for endpoint isolation and malware remediation (W383.2, 4)
- Incident ticketing system (W48) with security classification and evidence attachment (W383.6)
- Integration with W53 (Data Privacy Breach Response), W55 (Disaster Recovery), W380 (Alert & Event Management), W381 (Knowledge Management)


### Pain Points / Risks
- **Containment-delay risk**: classification debate delays isolation while malware spreads; mitigated by the severity-classified playbook executing containment (IP blocks, account disables, host isolation) immediately after triage (steps 1–2)
- **Breach-notification risk**: PII exposure identified late blows the NPC 72-hour notification window under RA 10173; mitigated by the investigation step explicitly determining whether PII was touched to trigger the breach workflow (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "Triage & Classification" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-count of "accounts, or isolate infected host from network" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "logs, memory, and disk to determine root cause, extent of lateral movement, and…" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-324 (ensure controlled execution — Cybersecurity & Privacy (PA-27.3).)
- operational: IT Security Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
10.5–17.5 hours per occurrence (per-step effort 30 min + 1 hour + 4–8 hours + 2–4 hours + 1–2 hours + 2 hours) — at As-needed; estimated 5–10 incidents/month requiring investigation ≈ 60–120 occurrences/year ≈ 630–2,100 hours/year.

### Background

BuildRight Depot processes approximately 1 million credit/debit card transactions per month across 600 POS terminals. As a merchant handling card-present transactions, BuildRight must comply with the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI-DSS) v4.0. Non-compliance risks include: fines from card brands (Visa/Mastercard) of USD 5,000–100,000/month, loss of card processing capability (business-threatening), and reputational damage from data breaches. This workflow manages the ongoing PCI-DSS compliance program including the annual Qualified Security Assessor (QSA) audit.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **PCI-DSS scope definition**: IT Security Manager defines PCI-DSS scope annually: (a) Cardholder Data Environment (CDE) includes: all 600 POS terminals, POS network segments, payment gateway integration servers, and any systems that store, process, or transmit cardholder data, (b) identifies segmentation boundaries between CDE and general corporate network, (c) documents data flow diagram showing how card data enters, traverses, and exits the environment | IT Security Manager | CIO | 4 hours |
| 2 | **Self-Assessment Questionnaire (SAQ)**: For locations not requiring on-site assessment: IT Security Manager completes PCI-DSS SAQ (SAQ D for BuildRight as a large merchant with 600 terminals): (a) 12 requirement categories covering network security, access control, encryption, vulnerability management, and security policies, (b) documents compliance evidence for each requirement, (c) identifies any gaps for remediation | IT Security Manager | CIO | 8–16 hours |

| 5 | **Annual QSA on-site audit**: QSA conducts annual on-site audit (typically 3–5 days): (a) interviews IT Security Manager, network team, POS team, and store operations, (b) inspects POS terminals at sampled stores (typically 5–10 stores), (c) reviews network diagrams, firewall rules, access logs, and encryption configurations, (d) observes POS terminal physical security, (e) tests incident response procedures, (f) produces Report on Compliance (ROC) with findings | QSA / IT Security Manager | CIO | 40 hours (IT team) + 3–5 days QSA |

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|

### Background

'Ransomware'/'extortion demand'/'data-destruction' appear in the corpus only as pain points and drill injects — PA-27.2's DR risk names the trap ("a ransomware attack may compromise both primary and DR environments if replication is synchronous; incident response must include isolating the DR environment before failover"), W1330 injects the scenario, W5529 drills the OT variant, and zero dedicated workflow headers own the event. W383 owns host-level incident response (5–10 investigations/month), W55 owns DR failover, W53 the RA 10173 breach notification, W1205 the PCI layer — nobody owns the enterprise class: the extortion decision (a no-payment default that only Board + counsel can depart from), the isolate-DR-before-failover sequencing rule, chain-wide manual trading mode, the privileged forensics engagement, the staged clean rebuild, or the cyber-insurance and listed-company disclosure clocks. This workflow is that single canon; the OT variant rides W5529 and the data-privacy core rides W53's NPC 72-hour clock.

### System Touchpoints
- EDR/SIEM enterprise isolation playbooks with forensic export (extends W383's tooling to the chain-wide kill-switch)
- Immutable/air-gapped backup vault with restore-point integrity attestation (links to W382)
- POS offline-mode flags and floor-limit parameter push (links to W535)
- ERP maintenance/freeze mode and release-calendar hold (links to W5521)
- PAM credential reset and session revocation (links to W375); certificate lifecycle (W377)
- Ecommerce kill-switch and incident banner (links to the W659/W75 estate)
- Incident bridge log, evidence chain, and cyber-insurance claim records (links to VS-26)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Failover-reinfection risk**: declaring failover onto a synchronously-replicated DR environment encrypts the recovery stack with production; mitigated by Step 1's isolate-DR-before-failover rule (the PA-27.2 risk register's own guidance, promoted from pain point to hard sequencing law)
- **Negotiation-legalization risk**: an IT staffer answering, negotiating, or paying an extortion demand creates evidence, sanctions, and escalation traps; mitigated by Step 3's Legal-monopoly demand handling with Board-gated decisions
- **Trading-improvisation risk**: stores individually deciding to close or hand-write tender rules under system loss; mitigated by Step 4's single manual-trading-mode directive under the E-02 closure custody
- **Notification-window risk**: PII or card-data determination arriving late blows the NPC 72-hour and card-brand windows; mitigated by Step 5's retainer-within-12-hours clock under counsel privilege
- **Evidence-destruction risk**: rebuilding before imaging destroys the forensic record that insurance, law enforcement, and litigation all need; mitigated by Step 1's preservation-first holds and the privileged evidence chain
- **Recurrence-blindness risk**: restoring the same flat network that enabled lateral movement; mitigated by Step 8's control retrofit gate before the incident closes

### Automation Opportunity
- SIEM correlation rule that auto-declares the enterprise-crisis threshold (N systems encrypting/locking within a window) and pages the bridge — replaces the manual declaration debate
- One-click enterprise segment: EDR mass-isolation + POS offline-mode push + ecommerce kill-switch in a single privileged action (replaces Step 1/4 manual toggles)
- Automated restore-point integrity attestation from the immutable vault before each rebuild ring (replaces manual Step 6 verification)
- Incident-bridge auto-logging with insurer/notification-clock tickers (replaces manual Step 7 tracking)

### Controls
- CTL-324 (ensure controlled execution — Cybersecurity & Privacy (PA-27.3).)
- operational: no extortion contact outside Legal; dual authorization (W320 rails) on any ransom consideration; DR isolated before failover, evidenced in the runbook; NPC/card-brand notification clocks evaluated within 24 hours of declaration

### Time Estimate
Declaration and first 24 hours ≈ 40–60 staff-hours across the crisis bridge; forensic scoping 2–5 days retainer-led; rebuild 1–4 weeks depending on ring integrity; full-event effort ≈ 300–600 staff-hours over a 2–6-week horizon. The class is rare (zero historical events — readiness rides the W1330/W390 exercise cadence), so no annual volume is claimed.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed by the IT Security bench, IT Infrastructure, and the CIO Office crisis roles with the external IR retainer; no incremental headcount.
