## W55. IT Disaster Recovery & System Failover

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Core ERP system failure exceeding RTO (4 hours per NFR-013), data center outage, cloud service disruption, or cyber attack causing system unavailability |
| **Frequency** | Rare; estimated 0–2 DR events/year requiring failover |
| **Volume** | Affects all ~6,911 employees (~1,500 named ERP users); 600 POS terminals; 200 stores; 4 DCs |
| **Owner** | CIO |
| **Participants** | CIO, IT Infrastructure team, IT Helpdesk, VP Store Operations, CFO, external DR service provider |

### Background

This workflow covers the IT system recovery process, distinct from W49 (typhoon/facility business continuity) which covers physical location response. NFR-013 defines: RPO ≤ 1 hour (max 1 hour of data loss), RTO ≤ 4 hours (max 4 hours of core system unavailability). POS terminals operate offline for 8+ hours per W5G.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | IT Infrastructure monitoring detects or is alerted to system failure: error rates, service health checks, connectivity loss, or user reports of system unavailability | IT Infrastructure / System | CIO | Immediate (automated detection) |
| 2 | IT Infrastructure assesses scope and severity: (a) single module failure (e.g., POS integration down), (b) full ERP outage, (c) data center / cloud region outage, (d) suspected cyber attack (ransomware, DDoS) | IT Infrastructure Lead | CIO | 15–30 min |
| 3 | CIO declares DR event if severity exceeds RTO: notifies CEO, COO, CFO; communicates estimated recovery time; VP Store Operations notifies Regional Managers and Store Managers | CIO | CEO | 15 min |
| 4 | **Stores**: continue selling in offline mode per W5G; POS transactions queue locally for upload upon recovery | Store Manager | VP Store Operations | Automated (W5G) |
| 5 | **DCs**: DC operations shift to manual processing — Receiving Clerks record goods receipts on paper/spreadsheet for batch entry upon recovery; picking and shipping suspended for non-critical orders | DC Manager | Supply Chain Manager | Manual fallback |
| 6 | **Ecommerce**: Digital Commerce platform displays maintenance notification; new orders suspended; in-progress orders held for fulfillment upon recovery | Ecom Team | CMO | 15 min |
| 7 | **Recovery execution**: IT Infrastructure initiates failover to the DR site — the Data Guard twin on the BuildRight data-center estate (on-premises canon A6.2; colocation permitted, no public-cloud hosting): (a) verify DR environment is current (data replication lag ≤ RPO), (b) switch DNS and network routing to DR environment, (c) validate core services: authentication, POS sync, inventory, financial posting, (d) incremental data replay from primary if replication lag exists | IT Infrastructure | CIO | 1–3 hours |
| 8 | If primary system restore (instead of failover): (a) identify root cause, (b) repair or restore from backup, (c) verify data integrity against last known good checkpoint, (d) bring services online sequentially (core first, then ancillary), (e) validate system health | IT Infrastructure | CIO | 2–4 hours |
| 9 | System restored: CIO communicates "all clear" to business; stores begin uploading offline transactions per W5G; DCs batch-enter manual receipts; ecommerce resumes order processing | CIO | CEO | 15 min |
| 10 | **Data integrity verification**: IT runs reconciliation checks: (a) offline POS transactions vs. central inventory, (b) any manual DC entries vs. expected receipts, (c) database transaction logs for completeness, (d) financial posting integrity (no partial journal entries) | IT Infrastructure + Finance | Controller | 1–2 hours |
| 11 | Post-recovery: CIO conducts root cause analysis within 5 business days; documents incident, root cause, recovery actions, data loss (if any), and preventive measures | CIO | CEO | 4 hours |
| 12 | Quarterly: IT Infrastructure conducts scheduled DR failover test during maintenance window; validates RTO and RPO targets; documents test results; updates DR runbook based on findings | IT Infrastructure | CIO | 4 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Automated system health monitoring with configurable alerting thresholds (W55.1)
- DR environment: real-time data replication with RPO ≤ 1 hour (synchronous or near-synchronous); automated or one-click failover capability (W55.7)
- Backup integrity verification: automated checksum validation on backup files; periodic restore testing (W55.8)
- Offline POS transaction upload and reconciliation per W5G (W55.4, 10)
- Incident logging and root cause documentation (W55.11)
- DR test scheduling and results tracking (W55.12)
- DR test failure remediation: if quarterly DR test fails to meet RTO (4 hours) or RPO (1 hour) targets — (a) IT Infrastructure Lead documents the specific failure (failover latency, data replication lag, service recovery sequence, single point of failure); (b) CIO convenes emergency remediation meeting with IT Infrastructure within 5 business days; (c) remediation plan created with specific actions, responsible owners, and target completion date; (d) retest scheduled within 30 days of remediation completion; (e) if retest also fails: CIO escalates to CEO with capital investment request for infrastructure upgrade; (f) all test results (pass and fail) retained in DR test log for audit evidence; (g) External auditor may request DR test results as part of annual audit (W42.19, W55.12)
- Integration with W5G (offline POS), W48 (helpdesk incident management), W49 (physical disaster response — this workflow is system-level, W49 is facility-level)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **RTO of 4 hours is aggressive for full ERP failover**: If the DR environment has replication lag or if sequential service validation is slow, the 4-hour target may be missed; quarterly DR tests may reveal this only under simulated conditions
- **Offline POS transaction reconciliation risk**: Stores accumulate offline transactions during DR events; if recovery takes 4–6 hours across 200 stores, uploading and reconciling thousands of queued transactions can introduce inventory and financial discrepancies; mitigated by periodic reconciliation against source documents
- **Cyber attack complexity**: A ransomware attack may compromise both primary and DR environments if replication is synchronous; incident response must include isolating the DR environment before failover, adding time and uncertainty
- **DR test disruption**: Quarterly failover tests during maintenance windows risk impacting overnight DC operations (receiving, replenishment) if test overruns; scheduling around 24/7 operations is challenging


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "checks, connectivity loss, or user reports of system unavailability" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-capture of "data center / cloud region outage" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-communication of "communicates estimated recovery time" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-323 (ensure controlled execution — Infrastructure & Platform (PA-27.2).)
- operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
- Detection and severity assessment: 15–30 min (steps 1–2)
- DR declaration and stakeholder notification: 15 min (step 3)
- Failover execution: 1–3 hours (step 7); primary system restore: 2–4 hours (step 8)
- Data integrity verification: 1–2 hours post-recovery (step 10)
- Root cause analysis: 4 hours within 5 business days (step 11)
- Quarterly DR test: 4 hours/quarter (step 12)
- Total DR event duration (detection to all-clear): ~2–4 hours target, within RTO of 4 hours (detection 15–30 min + declaration 15 min + failover 1–3 h + verification 15 min)

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount. DR response is executed by existing IT Infrastructure team (part of the planned ~50 IT staff in W48).
- External DR service provider: if BuildRight uses a managed DR service or colocation DR site, the provider's SLA must align with BuildRight's RTO/RPO targets.

| **Frequency** | Continuous monitoring; daily management |
| **Volume** | 200 stores; 4 DCs; 1 HQ; ~200+ SD-WAN nodes; ~400+ ISP circuits (primary/secondary) |
| **Owner** | IT Infrastructure Manager |
| **Participants** | IT Infrastructure Lead, Network Engineers, ISP Account Managers, Store Managers |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monitoring**: System monitors all 200+ nodes via SD-WAN dashboard; tracks latency, packet loss, and circuit health (Primary vs. Secondary) | System | IT Infra Lead | Continuous |
| 2 | **Outage Detection**: If circuit fails, system auto-switches to secondary (e.g., PLDT to Globe or Starlink); generates "Failover Alert" to IT Helpdesk | System | — | Immediate |
| 3 | **ISP Escalation**: Network Engineer contacts ISP for primary circuit restoration; tracks ticket until resolution; monitors SLA credits if uptime falls below the 99.9% SLA | Network Engineer | IT Infra Mgr | 15–30 min |
| 4 | **Store Wi-Fi Management**: Manage guest vs. corporate Wi-Fi; ensure RF devices (WMS) and mPOS have prioritized bandwidth (QoS) | Network Engineer | IT Infra Lead | 1 hour/week |
| 5 | **Hardware Maintenance**: Periodic firmware updates for routers, switches, and APs; physical inspections of network racks in stores during PM (W265) | Network Engineer | IT Infra Mgr | 4 hours/month |
| 6 | **Bandwidth Optimization**: During peak events (12.12, paydays), IT increases bandwidth or implements stricter traffic shaping to protect POS/payment traffic | IT Infra Lead | CIO | 2 hours/event |

### System Touchpoints
- SD-WAN Orchestrator / Dashboard for centralized network management (W366.1-2)
- Network Monitoring System (NMS) with automated alerting (W366.2)
- ISP Vendor Portal for ticket tracking and SLA monitoring (W366.3)
- Integration with W48 (helpdesk tickets for outages), W55 (disaster recovery), W265 (on-site hardware maintenance)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Single-circuit risk**: a store's primary ISP fails while the secondary is misconfigured, taking POS offline; mitigated by SD-WAN health monitoring with automatic failover and a Failover Alert to the IT Helpdesk (steps 1–2)
- **QoS-starvation risk**: guest Wi-Fi crowds out WMS RF guns and mPOS at peak trading hours; mitigated by QoS prioritization for RF and mPOS traffic on store Wi-Fi (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity

### Controls
- CTL-323 (ensure controlled execution — Infrastructure & Platform (PA-27.2).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
Continuous SD-WAN dashboard monitoring within the ITOC shift; ISP outage escalation 15–30 min per circuit event at realized failure volume across ~400+ circuits; store Wi-Fi management 1 hour/week ≈ 52 hours/year; phased firmware maintenance 4 hours/month ≈ 48 hours/year; peak-event bandwidth increases 2 hours/event (12.12, paydays).

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |

| 3 | **Off-site/Immutability**: Ensure backups are replicated to the secondary BuildRight data-center estate (colocation twin) or an "air-gapped" vault to protect against ransomware | System | IT Infra Mgr | Automated |

- Integration with W55 (Disaster Recovery) and W368 (Database/Cloud)

- **Silent-failure risk**: a failed nightly backup unnoticed for days silently shrinks the recovery window; mitigated by daily Backup Success report review with a 4-hour restart SLA for failed jobs (step 2)

- System auto-schedule of "schedule" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "reviews" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "verify data integrity and recovery time" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-323 (ensure controlled execution — Infrastructure & Platform (PA-27.2).)
- operational: IT Infrastructure Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Daily backup-success review 30 min ≈ 182 hours/year; monthly restore verification 2 hours ≈ 24 hours/year; business restoration requests 15–60 min each at realized volume; incremental/full/archive jobs and off-site immutable replication run automated overnight.

| **Volume** | 1 unified ERP system; 600 POS terminals across 200 stores; 4 WMS instances; 1 ecommerce platform; BIR eFPS/EIS integration; bank integrations (BDO, BPI, Metrobank, Chinabank); payment gateways (GCash, PayMaya, credit card processors); ~15–20 critical integration endpoints monitored continuously |
| **Owner** | ERP System Administrator |

### Background

W48 covers IT helpdesk support and incident management. W55 covers disaster recovery and system failover. W257 covers API lifecycle management. W380 covers alert and event management. W382 covers backup and recovery operations. W525 covers POS nightly sync. However, no workflow covers the daily operational health check of the unified ERP system and its critical integrations — the proactive morning ritual that catches issues before stores open, before the first transaction is processed, and before a cascading failure affects 200 stores simultaneously.

BuildRight runs its entire business on a single unified ERP: procurement, merchandising, inventory, POS, WMS, ecommerce, finance, HR, and treasury. The ERP integrates with 600 POS terminals (real-time and offline per W5G), 4 WMS instances (inbound, outbound, putaway, cycle count), the ecommerce platform (product catalog, orders, inventory sync, payment), BIR eFPS (tax filing) and EIS (sales reporting), banking systems (BDO, BPI, Metrobank, Chinabank for payroll, AP, treasury), and payment gateways (GCash, PayMaya, credit card acquirers for POS and ecommerce). Any integration failure can cascade: a broken POS-to-ERP sync means inventory is not updated, which means ecommerce may sell out-of-stock items, which means customer complaints (W41) and refund processing.

The daily health check is a structured, repeatable process that ensures the system is ready for the business day. It catches issues from overnight batch processing (which includes payroll calculations, inventory reconciliation, demand forecasting per W31, and replenishment order generation per W4), identifies integration degradation before it becomes a failure, and provides an early-warning system for the IT team and business stakeholders.

### System Touchpoints
- Environmental telemetry (temperature/humidity/leak detectors) feeding the W380 monitoring canon with event-type auto-classification
- Incident ticket with the environmental class and the staged-repower checklist attached (links to W48/W380)
- Failover-decision record reconciled to the W55 DR declaration log
- Suppression-system status interlock (re-arm certificate recorded before restart checklist opens)
- Vendor SLA callout records (DC facility vendor, VS-138 base-building, fire-protection contractor); equipment-damage claim object to VS-26

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Thermal-runaway risk**: inlet temperature monitored past threshold while the repair is "being arranged"; mitigated by Step 3's act-at-threshold sequence and Step 4's protective-shutdown order
- **Re-energize-on-wet-gear risk**: bus and equipment re-energized before the affected zone is de-energized-and-dried, causing electrocution or cascading failure; mitigated by the power-before-water rule in Step 3
- **Suppression-skip risk**: the room restarted with the suppression system in fault or discharged state — an unprotected room; mitigated by Step 6/7's certified re-arm before the repower checklist opens
- **Premature-failover risk**: a brief cooling blip triggering a full W55 failover and its own recovery burden; mitigated by Step 5's RTO-boundary decision with the basis logged either way
- **Repeat-event risk**: cooling capacity or leak source never right-sized after the fix; mitigated by Step 8's RCA feeding the W376 capacity and capex process

### Automation Opportunity
- Environmental telemetry thresholds auto-open the classified incident and page the on-call chain (replaces manual Step 1 triage)
- Staged-repower checklist gating: restart tasks stay blocked until the suppression re-arm certificate and leak/thermal checks are recorded (replaces manual Step 7 verification)

### Controls
- CTL-323 (ensure controlled execution — Infrastructure & Platform (PA-27.2).)
- operational: failover-boundary decisions logged with their RTO basis; repower only after the documented suppression/leak/thermal checks; RCAs tracked to the capacity process

### Time Estimate
Minor event ≈ 4–12 staff-hours (ITOC, infrastructure, one vendor callout); major event ≈ 40–120 staff-hours plus vendor remediation spread over the recovery week. At ~2–6 events/year chain-wise ≈ 50–150 staff-hours/year, absorbed within the ITOC and infrastructure rosters — no incremental headcount.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed by the ITOC, the IT infrastructure bench, and contracted DC facility/fire-protection vendors; no incremental headcount.
