## W10. Payroll Processing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Semi-monthly payroll calendar (15th and 30th) |
| **Frequency** | 10 payroll runs/month (5 entities × 2) |
| **Volume** | ~6,932 employees total |
| **Owner** | Payroll Manager |
| **Participants** | Payroll Officer, HR Assistant, Department Heads (OT/leave approval), Finance (bank file) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Payroll Officer pulls time & attendance data from biometric/RFID system | Payroll Officer | Payroll Manager | 30 min/run |
| 2 | HR Assistant verifies approved leaves, overtime, and schedule adjustments are posted | HR Assistant | Payroll Manager | 2 hours/run |
| 3 | Payroll Officer validates: basic salary + OT + night differential + holiday pay + allowances | Payroll Officer | Payroll Manager | 1 hour/run |
| 4 | System calculates deductions: SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, withholding tax (TRAIN law), loans, advances | System | — | Automated |
| 5 | Payroll Officer reviews computed payroll for anomalies (negative net pay, unusually high OT) | Payroll Officer | Payroll Manager | 1 hour/run |
| 6 | Payroll Manager reviews and approves payroll register | Payroll Manager | CHRO | 30 min/run |
| 7 | System generates bank file for payroll crediting (BDO, BPI, etc.) | System | — | Automated |
| 8 | Finance/Treasury reviews bank file; transmits to bank | Treasury Analyst | CFO | 30 min/run |
| 9 | System posts payroll journal entries to GL (salary expense, payable, deductions) | System | — | Automated |
| 10 | Payslips generated; distributed via email or employee self-service portal | System | — | Automated |
| 11 | Monthly: generate SSS PRN, PhilHealth contribution, Pag-IBIG contribution files for remittance | Payroll Officer | Payroll Manager | 1 hour/month |
| 11a | Payroll Officer reconciles statutory contribution schedule (per-employee breakdown) to generated remittance file and bank payment confirmation; investigates and resolves discrepancies before remittance deadline; system flags employees with missing or incomplete statutory data | Payroll Officer | Payroll Manager | 30 min/month |
| 11b | Contractual / fixed-term workers: system tracks contract start/end dates and auto-alerts HR Assistant 30 days before expiry; payroll computes pro-rated 13th month pay and statutory benefits per contract duration; end-of-contract settlement computed similar to final pay (W10 step 12) but with different legal basis; if employee converts to regular status, HR Assistant updates employee type in system and payroll adjusts benefit computation accordingly | HR Assistant / Payroll Officer | Payroll Manager | 15 min/employee |
| 12 | Final pay computation (upon employee separation): system calculates pro-rated 13th month pay, converted unused leave credits, less outstanding loans/advances and clearance deductions | Payroll Officer | Payroll Manager | 30 min/employee |
| 13 | System posts final pay as separate payroll run or adjustment; generates final payslip | System | — | Automated |

**Total payroll processing time**: ~6 hours per run per entity. With 5 entities, can be parallelized across 2–3 payroll officers.

### System Touchpoints
- Time & attendance import from biometric system (W10.1)
- Leave and overtime approval workflow (W10.2)
- Philippine payroll computation engine (TRAIN law, SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG tables) (W10.3–4)
- Payroll anomaly detection (W10.5)
- Bank file generation in Philippine bank formats (W10.7)
- GL posting from payroll (W10.9)
- Payslip distribution (W10.10)
- Statutory contribution file generation with PRN (W10.11)
- Statutory remittance reconciliation: per-employee contribution schedule vs. remittance file vs. bank confirmation; discrepancy flagging (W10.11a)
- Contractual/fixed-term worker management: contract date tracking, pro-rated benefit computation, end-of-contract settlement, regularization conversion (W10.11b)
- Agency / manpower contractor worker management: for seasonal and peak-period staffing (Christmas season, bi-monthly sale events, new store openings), BuildRight engages licensed manpower agencies per DOLE Department Order No. 174 (Labor-Only Contracting rules); agency workers are NOT employees of BuildRight entities — they appear in the agency's payroll, not in BuildRight's W10 payroll run; system tracks agency worker headcount separately from regular headcount for workforce planning; Store Manager submits agency staffing request to HR with headcount, duration, and skill requirements; HR coordinates with approved agency partners; agency invoices are processed as non-PO service invoices per W7C with DOLE-compliant documentation (agency service agreement, worker deployment list, attendance records); agency workers are issued temporary POS and access badges with limited system permissions and defined expiry dates; system distinguishes agency hours from regular employee hours for labor cost reporting (agency cost is a contract service expense, not payroll); typical agency worker deployment: 2–5 per store during November–December peak, and 10–15 per new store opening (W16) for the first 2 weeks of operations
- Agency worker access provisioning: Store Manager submits agency worker access request to IT via W48 helpdesk ticket, specifying worker name, agency, assignment duration, and required access level; IT creates temporary system account with predefined "Agency Worker" permission template (POS transaction processing only — no voids, no price overrides, no manager functions, no reports); access badge created with defined expiry date matching deployment end date; system auto-revokes access on expiry date; at end of deployment, Store Manager verifies badge return and IT confirms system deactivation; if deployment extended, Store Manager submits extension request before expiry; system logs all agency worker access with agency name, worker name, store, start/end dates, and permission level; monthly: IT generates agency worker access report showing active, expired, and unreturned badges; unreturned badges flagged for Store Manager follow-up (W10, cross-reference W71 access badge management)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Statutory deduction errors**: SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG contribution tables change periodically; failure to update payroll tables results in under- or over-deduction per employee, requiring manual retroactive adjustments and potential penalties from government agencies
- **Bank file generation failures**: Incorrect bank file formats (BDO, BPI, etc.) or transmission errors can delay salary crediting for thousands of employees, triggering immediate employee complaints and trust erosion
- **Multi-entity reconciliation complexity**: Running 10 payroll cycles/month across 5 legal entities with different TIN registrations creates significant reconciliation overhead; intercompany employee transfers (W43.15) add further complexity to entity-specific payroll totals
- **Negative net pay risk**: When loan deductions, salary advances, and statutory contributions coincide, an employee's net pay may fall below minimum wage; the system must dynamically cap deductions, but manual overrides can bypass this safeguard; mitigated by CTL-13 (separate payroll computation from approval.); CTL-34 (ensure complete tax compliance)
- **Payroll anomaly detection gaps**: With ~6,932 employees, manual review of the payroll register for anomalies (unusually high OT, negative net pay, incorrect deductions) is time-consuming and error-prone; undetected errors cascade into incorrect payslips and bank transfers


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-schedule of "schedule adjustments are posted" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "validates: basic salary + OT + night differential + holiday pay + allowances" (replaces manual Step 3).
- Deduction engine auto-applies SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG/TRAIN withholding, loans, and advances (Step 4, already system-executed).

### Controls
- CTL-13 (separate payroll computation from approval.); CTL-34 (ensure complete tax compliance.)
- operational: must dynamically cap deductions, but manual overrides can bypass this safeguard

### Staffing Implication
- **2–3 Payroll Officers**: 5 entities × 2 runs = 10 runs/month. Each run takes ~6 hours. Total ~60 hours/month of payroll processing. 2 officers can handle this with time for reconciliation and inquiries.
- **1 Payroll Manager**: Approval, oversight, statutory compliance.
- **1–2 HR Assistants**: Leave and OT verification (data entry-heavy during payroll week).
- Fits within the 26-person HR department (profile §3.3).

### Time Estimate
**Per payroll run**: ~6 hours per entity (data validation + computation + review + bank file generation). With 5 entities and bi-monthly runs, total monthly payroll processing time is ~60 hours (5 entities × 2 runs × 6 hours). Quarter-end and year-end runs (13th month, bonus periods) add ~50% additional time per run. Year-end W9B tax reconciliation (W9B) requires an additional 2–3 days of concentrated effort by the Payroll team.

## W280. Court-Ordered Wage Garnishment & Third-Party Deductions

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | HR receives a legal order or authorized request to deduct funds directly from an employee's net pay (e.g., child support, cooperative loan, union dues). |
| **Frequency** | Semi-monthly payroll cycle |
| **Volume** | 50-100 employees affected |
| **Owner** | Payroll Manager |
| **Participants** | HR Admin, Legal, Employee, Third-Party Payee |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Legal/HR receives and verifies the garnishment order or loan agreement. | Payroll Admin | Payroll Manager | 15 min |
| 2 | Payroll Admin configures a recurring deduction wage type in the employee's compensation profile. | Legal Counsel | CHRO | 20 min |
| 3 | System logic applies prioritization rules (e.g., statutory taxes first, court orders second, voluntary loans third) to ensure net pay does not fall below legal minimums. | Payroll Manager | CFO | Automated |
| 4 | Payroll runs; funds are deducted and booked to a specific AP liability account. | Payroll Admin | Payroll Manager | 10 min |
| 5 | Finance remits the consolidated deducted funds to the court, union, or cooperative. | HR Admin | CHRO | 15 min |

### System Touchpoints
- HR Payroll Engine (Deduction Rules)
- General Ledger
- Accounts Payable

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Legal liability for non-compliance**: Failure to implement a court-ordered garnishment on time exposes BuildRight to contempt of court charges, fines, and potential liability for the full garnishment amount; manual tracking of court deadlines is error-prone across 50–100 affected employees
- **Complex deduction prioritization logic**: When an employee has multiple simultaneous deductions (statutory taxes, SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG loans, court garnishment, company loans), the system must apply strict prioritization rules while ensuring net pay does not fall below minimum wage; incorrect prioritization creates legal and employee relations issues
- **Garnishment order changes and terminations**: Court orders may be modified (increased, decreased, or terminated) with varying effective dates; failure to update deduction configurations promptly results in over- or under-deduction requiring manual correction and potential restitution
- **Employee privacy and morale**: Garnishment orders are sensitive legal matters; Payroll Officers with access to garnishment data must maintain strict confidentiality; employees facing wage garnishment may experience financial stress affecting performance and retention


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-file of "Payroll Admin configures a recurring deduction wage type in" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "logic applies prioritization rules" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-count of "account." (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-297 (ensure controlled execution — Payroll & Compensation (PA-19.2).)
- operational: must apply strict prioritization rules while ensuring net pay does not fall below minimum wage

### Time Estimate
- Garnishment order receipt and verification: 15 min/order
- Deduction wage type configuration: 20 min/order
- Payroll deduction processing: Automated per run
- Post-run verification and AP booking: 10 min/employee
- Remittance to court/union/cooperative: 15 min/batch
- **Total per new garnishment setup**: ~60 min; **Ongoing per payroll run**: ~10 min verification per affected employee

### Staffing Implication
- **Payroll Admin**: ~15 min per garnishment setup × 50–100 affected employees = ~12–25 hours/month for initial setups and modifications. Absorbed within existing payroll team.
- **Legal Counsel**: ~20 min per order verification. With 5–10 new orders/month, ~2–3 hours/month. Absorbed within Legal team.
- **HR Admin**: ~15 min per remittance batch, typically 2–4 batches per payroll run = ~2–4 hours/month. Absorbed within HR team.
- **No incremental headcount.**

### Controls
- CTL-297 (ensure controlled execution — Payroll & Compensation (PA-19.2).)

## W643. Final Pay Computation & Separation Settlement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Employee separation (resignation, termination, retirement, end of contract) per W43 |
| **Frequency** | ~100–130 separations/month (~15–20% annual turnover rate) |
| **Volume** | ~5–7 per business day across all entities |
| **Owner** | Payroll Specialist |
| **Participants** | HR Business Partner, Department Head (clearance sign-off), Finance Analyst, Employee |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | HR Business Partner initiates final pay computation trigger in system on separation effective date (or upon receipt of resignation letter for proactive processing); classifies separation type (resignation, termination for just cause, authorized cause separation, retirement, end of contract) | HR Business Partner | HR Manager | 10 min |
| 2 | System auto-computes final pay components: (a) proportionate basic salary through last day, (b) proportionate 13th month pay per PD 851 (1/12 × monthly basic × months worked), (c) cash conversion of unused Service Incentive Leave (minimum 5 days per Labor Code; company policy may grant more), (d) proportionate Sil (if company grants beyond 5 days statutory minimum), (e) overtime/holiday/night differential unpaid in last period, (f) separation pay if applicable (authorized causes per Art. 298–299: redundancy = ½ month per year, retrenchment = 1 month per year, etc.), (g) retirement pay per RA 7641 if qualified (½ month per year of service) | System | Payroll Specialist | Automated computation |
| 3 | Payroll Specialist reviews system computation; validates each component against employee's records: attendance, leave balance, salary history, outstanding loans (W76), prior 13th month payments | Payroll Specialist | Payroll Manager | 30 min |
| 4 | Payroll Specialist computes statutory deductions: (a) withholding tax on final pay per BIR rules (different treatment for resignation vs. retirement vs. termination — retirement pay and separation pay below PHP 90,000 threshold are tax-exempt per TRAIN law Section 32(B)(6)(b)), (b) final SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG contributions for last period, (c) outstanding employee loan deductions, (d) salary advance or cash advance offsets, (e) company property non-return deductions (if authorized per policy) | Payroll Specialist | Payroll Manager | 20 min |
| 5 | Payroll Manager approves final pay computation; validates net pay is non-negative (minimum wage protection does not apply to final pay but net should not be zero unless deductions exceed pay) | Payroll Manager | HR Manager | 10 min |
| 6 | Finance Analyst processes payment: generates bank transfer file for direct deposit; for employees without bank accounts, prepares manual check | Finance Analyst | Finance Manager | 15 min |
| 7 | HR Business Partner coordinates clearance process: ensures all department clearances completed (IT equipment return per W152, company property return, PPE return per W172, confidential document return) before payment release | HR Business Partner | HR Manager | 1–5 days clearance |
| 8 | Payment released within DOLE Advisory 06-2020 timeline: 30 days from separation date for resignation; immediately for termination/retirement; or per specific DOLE/Labor Arbiter order | Payroll Specialist | Payroll Manager | Per timeline |
| 9 | Employee receives final pay with itemized computation document; HR Business Partner obtains signed acknowledgment | HR Business Partner | HR Manager | 15 min |

### System Touchpoints
- Payroll module (final pay computation), tax engine (separation pay tax rules), loan management (W76 outstanding balance), leave management (unused leave balance), employee master (salary history, employment dates)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **DOLE compliance pressure**: 30-day DOLE timeline pressure when clearance delays occur
- **Tax computation complexity**: Complex tax computation for different separation types
- **Formula validation errors**: Separation pay computation errors for authorized causes (different formulas per cause)
- **Arbitration risk**: NLRC disputes when employee contests final pay; mitigated by the Payroll Manager computation review and DOLE 30-day final-pay compliance tracking and separation-pay formula validation per cause type and
- **Loan shortfall**: Outstanding loan offsets that reduce net pay significantly

### Automation Opportunity
- Auto-final-pay computation from separation type, salary history, leave balance, and outstanding loans
- Auto-clearance-status tracking across departments
- Auto-DOLE-deadline tracking with escalation

### Controls
- CTL-297 (ensure controlled execution — Payroll & Compensation (PA-19.2).)
- operational: Payroll Manager computation review; DOLE 30-day final-pay compliance tracking; separation-pay formula validation per cause type; signed employee acknowledgment of final pay

### Time Estimate
- 1–2 hours computation + 1–5 days clearance + payment processing

## W779. Store-Level Employee Injury Incident Reporting & Workers' Compensation Claim Processing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Employee work-related injury or illness |
| **Frequency** | Ad-hoc; ~5–10 workplace injuries per month chain-wide |
| **Volume** | ~60–120 reported workplace injuries/year across 200 stores, 4 DCs, and HQ |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Injured Employee, HR Business Partner, Safety Officer, SSS/EC Claims, DOLE |

### Background
BuildRight Depot's operations involve physical risks: heavy lifting (cement bags, lumber), forklift operations in DCs, ladder use for high shelving, and equipment operation (cutting, mixing). When employees are injured on the job, DOLE requires incident reporting, and the employee is entitled to SSS Employees' Compensation (EC) benefits per PD 626. While W140 covers OHS incident management broadly and W501 covers first aid response, this workflow specifically addresses the employee injury administrative process — from incident documentation through workers' compensation claim filing, modified duty management, and return-to-work processing.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Immediate incident response**: (a) first aid administered per W501, (b) if serious: emergency medical transport coordinated, (c) scene secured per W140, (d) Store Manager notified immediately | First Aider / Supervisor | Store Manager | 10-30 min |
| 2 | **Incident documentation (within 4 hours)**: Store Manager completes incident report: (a) date, time, location of incident, (b) employee details and department, (c) description of what happened, (d) witnesses and statements, (e) injury description and body part affected, (f) immediate cause and contributing factors, (g) photos of scene and equipment involved, (h) first aid or medical treatment provided | Store Manager | Safety Officer | 30-60 min |
| 3 | **Medical treatment and documentation**: Employee seeks medical attention: (a) if minor: company-designated clinic or HMO provider per W642, (b) if serious: nearest hospital emergency room, (c) all medical certificates, treatment records, and receipts collected for SSS EC claim, (d) Store Manager ensures "off-duty" status recorded in time & attendance per W561 | Employee / Store Manager | HR BP | 2-4 hours |

| **Trigger** | Semi-monthly payroll processing cycle (15th and 30th pay periods) |
| **Frequency** | Monthly filing and remittance (contributions deducted from payroll must be remitted to each agency by specified deadlines: SSS by last day of following month, PhilHealth by end of following month, Pag-IBIG by 10th of following month) |
| **Volume** | ~6,932 employees across 5 legal entities (200 stores + 4 DCs + HQ) |
| **Owner** | Payroll Manager |
| **Participants** | Payroll Manager, Payroll Clerks, HR Business Partner, Finance Controller, SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG portal administrators |

### Background

Philippine employers are mandated by law to contribute to three government social insurance programs for every covered employee: (a) SSS (Social Security System — RA 11199): retirement, disability, sickness, maternity, death benefits; employer share ~9.5% of monthly salary credit (MSC), employee share ~4.5%; (b) PhilHealth (Philippine Health Insurance Corporation — RA 11223): national health insurance; employer share ~2.5% of monthly basic salary, employee share ~2.5%; (c) Pag-IBIG Fund (Home Development Mutual Fund — RA 9679): housing loan, savings; employer share 2% of monthly compensation (for employees earning ≤ PHP 1,500/month) or 2% (for those earning > PHP 1,500), employee share 1% or 2%. With ~6,932 employees across 5 legal entities, monthly combined contributions (SSS ~14% of MSC + PhilHealth 5% + Pag-IBIG ~3–4%) reach ~PHP 25–30M. Late remittance incurs penalties (SSS: 2% per month; PhilHealth: 1% per month; Pag-IBIG: 1/10 of 1% per day). Non-remittance is a criminal offense under Philippine law. This workflow ensures timely, accurate deduction, remittance, and reconciliation across all three agencies and five entities.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Payroll deduction computation** (semi-monthly): (a) during payroll processing per W644/1416: Payroll Clerk runs statutory benefits computation: (i) SSS: employee share (4.5% of MSC) and employer share (9.5% of MSC) based on SSS contribution table (tiered by monthly salary credit — 2026 rates); (ii) PhilHealth: employee share (2.5%) and employer share (2.5%) based on monthly basic salary (floor: PHP 10,000, ceiling: PHP 80,000); (iii) Pag-IBIG: employee share (1% or 2% based on monthly compensation) and employer share (2%); (b) system auto-calculates per employee based on: salary, employment status (regular, probationary, seasonal/project — seasonal workers also covered), entity assignment; (c) total deductions per employee reflected on payslip | Payroll Clerk / System | Payroll Manager | Automated during payroll run |
| 2 | **Monthly contribution report generation**: (a) after both semi-monthly payrolls processed (end of month): system generates monthly contribution reports for each agency and each legal entity: (i) SSS Form R-3 (Contribution Collection List): employee names, SSS numbers, MSC, employee share, employer share, total per employee; (ii) PhilHealth ERP (Electronic Premium Remittance) file: employee PhilHealth numbers, monthly basic salary, employee share, employer share; (iii) Pag-IBIG MCR (Member's Contribution Remittance) form: employee Pag-IBIG numbers, monthly compensation, employee share, employer share; (b) Payroll Clerk reviews reports for anomalies: (i) new hires without SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG numbers, (ii) separated employees still appearing in report, (iii) unusually high or low contribution amounts | Payroll Clerk / System | Payroll Manager | 2–3 hours per agency per entity = ~15–20 hours total |
| 3 | **New hire registration** (if needed): (a) for employees without SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG numbers (typically new hires who are first-time workers): (i) HR Business Partner registers employee online via each agency's portal (SSS online registration, PhilHealth electronic registration, Pag-IBIG online membership); (ii) registration typically takes 3–5 business days for number generation; (b) Payroll Clerk flags unregistered employees in contribution report; contributions for unregistered employees are accumulated and remitted retroactively once numbers are issued | HR Business Partner / Payroll Clerk | Payroll Manager | 15–20 min per new hire |
| 4 | **Remittance payment processing**: (a) Payroll Manager authorizes payment to each agency for each entity: (i) SSS: via SSS online payment facility or accredited bank (BDO, Metrobank, etc.); total remittance = employer share + employee share deductions across all employees in entity; (ii) PhilHealth: via PhilHealth electronic remittance or accredited bank; (iii) Pag-IBIG: via Pag-IBIG online payment or accredited collecting partner; (b) Finance Controller reviews and approves fund transfers from each entity's bank account; (c) payments made by deadline: SSS (last day of following month), PhilHealth (end of following month), Pag-IBIG (10th of following month) | Payroll Manager / Finance Controller | CFO | 2–3 hours per entity × 5 entities = ~10–15 hours |
| 5 | **Reconciliation**: (a) Payroll Clerk reconciles: (i) total deductions in payroll system vs. total remitted to each agency (must match to the peso); (ii) per-employee contributions in payroll system vs. per-employee contributions in agency reports; (b) common discrepancies: (i) late new-hire registration causes retroactive adjustment, (ii) mid-month separation causes proration, (iii) salary adjustment effective mid-month causes split contribution; (c) Payroll Clerk resolves discrepancies and files adjusted report with agency if needed | Payroll Clerk | Payroll Manager | 2–4 hours per entity per month |
| 6 | **Quarterly audit and employee statement**: (a) quarterly: Payroll Manager reviews cumulative contributions per employee across all 3 agencies: (i) YTD employee share vs. expected based on YTD salary, (ii) any employees with contribution gaps (unpaid leaves, LWOP exceeding 1 month); (b) generates individual employee contribution certificates on request (e.g., for SSS salary loan application, Pag-IBIG housing loan application); (c) annual: reconcile total contributions against each agency's annual statement of account | Payroll Manager | Finance Controller | 4–6 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Payroll statutory benefits module: auto-calculation of SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG employer and employee shares per current rates and tables (W1527.1)
- Agency report generators: SSS R-3, PhilHealth ERP file, Pag-IBIG MCR per entity (W1527.2)
- Agency portal integrations: SSS online, PhilHealth electronic submission, Pag-IBIG online payment (W1527.4)
- Reconciliation module: payroll deduction totals vs. agency remittance totals, per-employee matching (W1527.5)
- Employee contribution certificate generator: on-demand statement for loan applications (W1527.6)
- Integration with W644/1416 (13th Month Pay — also subject to SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG), W1306 (Multi-Entity Statutory Benefits Consolidation), W816 (Multi-Entity Payroll Consolidation)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Agency portal downtime**: SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG online portals are notorious for downtime, especially during month-end peak filing periods; Payroll Clerks may be unable to submit reports on deadline day, risking penalties — requires early submission (5 business days before deadline) as a buffer
- **Rate and table changes**: government contribution rates and salary credit tables change periodically (SSS contribution rate increases are phased over multiple years); if the payroll system's rate tables are not updated promptly, all contributions compute incorrectly — retroactive corrections are administratively painful
- **Multi-entity complexity**: 5 legal entities = 5 sets of filings per agency per month = 15 monthly filings; if any entity misses a deadline, the penalty applies to that entity's total monthly contribution (not per employee), making the financial impact significant
- **Employee data accuracy**: incorrect SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG numbers (transposed digits, wrong name spelling) cause posting failures at the agency — the employee's contribution is not credited to their account, creating a service issue when the employee needs a loan or benefit claim

- System auto-report of "New hire registration" (replaces manual Step 3).

- operational: Payroll Manager review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

## W5540. Payroll Run Failure, Correction & Emergency Off-Cycle Payment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | A payroll run aborts or is compromised inside the payroll window: validation failure on the payroll register, bank-file (credit/debit-advice) rejection by the disbursement bank, funding shortblock, payroll-system/ERP outage at cut-off, or a disbursement-partner platform outage |
| **Frequency** | ~2–6 failure events chain-wide per year across the 6,932-employee estate (monthly run × 5 entities; most aborts caught at validation and re-run inside the window) |
| **Volume** | 1 run × affected population (a validation abort typically hits 1–3 batches; a bank-file reject or provider outage hits up to all ~6,932 payslips for that entity-run) × statutory remittance deadlines (W1527/W1306) that keep running regardless |
| **Owner** | Payroll Manager |
| **Participants** | Payroll Specialist, HR Shared Services (employee comms per VS-103), Finance Controller (funding release), Treasury (funding/debit account status per VS-18), IT Service Desk & ERP payroll support (system restoration), disbursement bank / payroll funding partner, CHRO (communications), HR Benefits (SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG desk), Audit (post-mortem for material events) |

### Background

W10 owns the payroll processing run, W641 the *planned* off-cycle payment, and W816/W1306 the multi-entity consolidation and statutory remittances — but no workflow owned the *failed* run: the classification decision (re-run vs contingency), the bank-file rejection loop, the emergency-net-pay decision, or the employee-communication surge. The exposure is statutory, not merely operational: Labor Code Art. 103 requires wages paid on time, DOLE money claims follow delayed pay, and the remittance calendar (SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG via W1527, BIR withholding via W2764/W2765-class filings) does not pause because a bank file bounced. With ~6,932 employees across stores and DCs — many unbanked segments paid via cash-card partners — a payday failure also has an immediate floor-level human impact that needs scripted handling. This workflow is the failure canon; W641 remains the mechanic for any approved emergency advance it triggers.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Failure classification & clock**: classify against the payday countdown — (a) validation abort ≥ 48 h before payday → correct & re-run (Step 2); (b) bank-file rejection / provider outage < 48 h → contingency branch (Steps 3–5); (c) funding block (Treasury/debit account issue) → Treasury joint branch (Step 3c); open the event log with timestamps (recovery-time accountability) | Payroll Specialist | Payroll Manager | 30–60 min |
| 2 | **Correct & re-run (inside window)**: isolate failing records (negative net, invalid deduction, missing bank/enrollment per W10 validations), fix master-data or deduction errors, re-run only the failed batches with the normal approval chain intact (CTL-13: computation/approval separation preserved under time pressure); confirm register-to-bank-file control totals | Payroll Specialist / Payroll Manager | Payroll Manager | 2–6 hrs |
| 3 | **Funding & file verification (contingency branch)**: (a) bank-file reject → obtain the bank's reject/error report, regenerate the file per spec, resubmit and obtain acknowledged credit-advice; (b) provider platform outage → invoke the secondary disbursement channel per contract (backup partner or alternate file protocol); (c) funding block → Treasury confirms debit-account funding per the W318 settlement-account structure and releases per Finance Controller sign-off | Payroll Specialist / Treasury / Finance Controller | Finance Controller | 2–12 hrs |
| 4 | **Emergency off-cycle payment decision (< 24 h to payday, credit not assured)**: CHRO + Finance Controller approve an emergency net-pay advance via the W641 off-cycle rails — scope: affected employees, amount (default 50% net, up to 100% for hardship cases), channel (card-partner push or store cash-card load for cash-paid segments under dual-custody cash controls per VS-81 store-cash rules); employees sign the W641 advance agreement (recovery via deduction from the late-arriving original credit) | Payroll Manager / CHRO / Finance Controller | CHRO + Finance Controller (joint) | 4–8 hrs |
| 5 | **Employee communication & floor support**: HR Shared Services issues the ESS banner + SMS/email with facts, cause, and the assured new credit time; store/DC bulletins via the VS-63 store-communication channel; payroll helpdesk surge script (no-pay-confirmation handling); store managers briefed not to improvise promises | HR Shared Services / Store Managers | CHRO | 2–4 hrs |
| 6 | **Statutory protection & reconciliation**: verify remittance calendar still met — SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG (W1527) and BIR withholding (W1306 chain) computed from the corrected register, paid on statutory deadlines with penalty exposure assessed if the failure touches them; when the original credit lands late, reconcile against any Step-4 advances (deduction per agreement), close the register-to-bank reconciliation, and document the event trail for audit | Payroll Specialist / HR Benefits | Payroll Manager | 4–16 hrs |
| 7 | **Post-mortem & control hardening**: within 5 business days — root cause (data, file spec, funding, vendor, infrastructure), run-book update, added pre-run validations, provider SLA review or channel remediation; material events (full-run failure, > 24 h delay, statutory penalty) reported to the Audit Committee via the W81-class write-off/exception reporting path if losses resulted | Payroll Manager / Audit | CHRO | 1–2 days |

### System Touchpoints
- Payroll register validation-error queue with batch-level retry (links to W10 run controls)
- Bank-file (credit advice) generation, transmission log, and bank reject-report intake (reconciliation to control totals)
- Off-cycle payment rails (links to W641) with advance-agreement attachment and recovery-deduction flag
- ESS notification broadcast (banner + SMS/email gateway) and case-surge tagging for the payroll helpdesk (links to VS-103)
- Statutory remittance calendars & penalty tracker (links to W1527/W1306)
- Treasury cash/debit-account status view (links to W318/W556 settlement accounts)

### Time Estimate
Per event: inside-window correction ≈ 2–6 staff-hours; contingency branch ≈ 20–60 staff-hours (Steps 3–6 across payroll, Treasury, HR Shared Services); full-outage with emergency advance ≈ 80–160 staff-hours. At ~2–6 events/yr ≈ 40–360 staff-hours/yr, absorbed within the existing payroll team and HR Shared Services — no incremental headcount.

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Statutory-wage risk**: delayed pay triggers Labor Code Art. 103 exposure and DOLE money claims; mitigated by the classification clock (Step 1), the < 24 h emergency-advance branch (Step 4), and scripted communications
- **Double-payment risk**: emergency advance issued, then the original file also lands → employees overpaid; mitigated by the signed advance agreement with automatic recovery-deduction flag (Step 4) and the Step-6 reconciliation
- **Control erosion under pressure**: bypassing approval separation to re-run fast; mitigated by the explicit CTL-13 preservation requirement in Step 2
- **Remittance-cascade risk**: focus on net pay while SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG/BIR deadlines lapse; mitigated by the Step-6 statutory-calendar check owned alongside the pay fix
- **Reputational risk**: floor-level escalation when stores improvise answers; mitigated by the single-script HR Shared Services communication in Step 5

### Automation Opportunity
- Payroll-run monitor classifies failures from validation/bank-reject codes and auto-selects the run-book branch with the countdown clock displayed (replaces manual Step 1 triage)
- Reject-report ingestion auto-maps error codes to corrective master-data actions and regenerates compliant files (replaces manual Step 3a file surgery)

### Controls
- CTL-297 (ensure controlled execution — Payroll & Compensation (PA-19.2).); CTL-13 (separate payroll computation from approval.); CTL-34 (ensure complete tax compliance.)
- operational: emergency advances ride W641 agreements with documented recovery deductions — never free-standing payouts
- operational: every failure event carries a timestamped event log and a 5-day post-mortem with control-hardening actions

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed within the existing payroll team, HR Shared Services and Treasury desks; surge effort is event-driven and covered by the run-book. No incremental headcount.
