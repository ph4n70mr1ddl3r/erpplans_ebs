## W1698. Monthly Depreciation Run & Journal Posting

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Month-end close process (Day 1 of following month) |
| **Frequency** | Monthly (5 entities × 12 months = 60 runs/year) |
| **Volume** | ~15,000–20,000 active assets depreciated per month |
| **Owner** | Fixed Asset Accountant |
| **Participants** | Fixed Asset Accountant, GL Accountant, Accounting Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Before depreciation run: Fixed Asset Accountant verifies all new assets from the month have been registered per W1690 and all disposals/retirements processed per W1708; system flags assets with missing classification or useful life | Fixed Asset Accountant | Accounting Manager | 2 hours/month |
| 2 | System executes depreciation calculation for all active assets: (a) straight-line depreciation for most categories: monthly expense = (cost − residual value) ÷ useful life in months, (b) declining balance for vehicles: monthly expense = net book value × (rate ÷ 12), (c) depreciation starts from ready-for-use date — partial month calculated pro-rata, (d) fully depreciated assets (net book value = residual value or zero) are skipped, (e) assets on hold or pending disposal continue depreciating until formal disposal date, (f) depreciation calculates in each entity's corporate book — the book of record for the register — and the tax (BIR) book derives from the corporate book per W1704; tax schedules are never maintained independently of the corporate register | System | — | ~5 min computation |
| 3 | Fixed Asset Accountant reviews depreciation run results: (a) total depreciation expense by entity vs. prior month (material variance investigation), (b) new assets added this month — verify depreciation amounts, (c) assets reaching end of useful life this month — flag for disposal review per W1708, (d) any errors or exceptions (negative depreciation, missing parameters) | Fixed Asset Accountant | Accounting Manager | 1–2 hours/month |
| 4 | Accounting Manager approves depreciation run; system posts journal entries: (a) debit: depreciation expense by cost center (store/DC/HQ/department), (b) credit: accumulated depreciation by asset category, (c) entries posted to each entity's GL separately, (d) consolidation elimination not needed (depreciation is entity-specific) | Accounting Manager | CFO | 30 min/month |
| 5 | System updates each asset's net book value; generates depreciation schedule report for the month showing: cost, accumulated depreciation, net book value by asset, category, location, and entity | System | — | Automated |


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "Accountant verifies all new assets from the month have been registered per W1690…" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-calculate of "System executes depreciation calculation for all active assets" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews depreciation run results" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-347 (ensure controlled execution — Depreciation & Financial Reporting (PA-35.2).)
- operational: Fixed Asset Accountant review and approval gate

### System Touchpoints
- Depreciation engine supporting multiple methods (SL, declining balance, units of production) with pro-rata calculation (W1698.2)
- Pre-run validation report flagging incomplete asset records (W1698.1)
- Depreciation run review and approval workflow (W1698.3–4)
- Auto-posting of depreciation journal entries to GL by entity (W1698.4)
- Updated depreciation schedule report (W1698.5)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Depreciation magnitude**: With ~PHP 8–10B in gross fixed assets and average useful life of 8 years, annual depreciation is ~PHP 1.0–1.25B; any error in depreciation run is material
- **Partial month complexity**: New stores opening mid-month require accurate pro-rata depreciation across hundreds of component assets
- **Manual overrides**: System should prevent manual override of depreciation calculations; any adjustment must go through impairment (W1700) or useful life change (W1699) workflows

### Time Estimate
Monthly depreciation cycle ~3.5–4.5 hours (new-asset verification 2 hours + run review 1–2 hours + approval 30 min) ≈ 42–54 hours/year across 60 entity-month runs on ~15,000–20,000 active assets (depreciation calculation and NBV updates automated).

## W1699. Depreciation Method Review & Useful Life Adjustment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual review, physical verification findings (W1706), or significant change in asset usage pattern |
| **Frequency** | Annual review (Q4); ad-hoc as needed |
| **Volume** | ~50–100 useful life adjustments/year |
| **Owner** | Fixed Asset Accountant |
| **Participants** | Fixed Asset Accountant, Accounting Manager, External Auditor |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Triggers for useful life review: (a) physical verification reveals asset in worse/better condition than expected, (b) technology change (e.g., POS system upgrade makes existing terminals obsolete sooner), (c) regulatory change (e.g., new emission standards require fleet replacement), (d) change in usage pattern (store open longer hours → fixtures wear faster), (e) external auditor recommendation | Fixed Asset Accountant | Accounting Manager | Event-driven |
| 2 | Fixed Asset Accountant prepares useful life adjustment proposal: (a) asset(s) affected, (b) current useful life and remaining useful life, (c) proposed new useful life with justification, (d) impact on future depreciation expense (increase/decrease per month), (e) catch-up adjustment needed for prior periods (prospectively per PFRS — no restatement) | Fixed Asset Accountant | Accounting Manager | 1–2 hours/batch |
| 3 | Accounting Manager reviews and approves adjustment; for material changes (aggregate impact > PHP 1M/year): CFO approval required | Accounting Manager / CFO | CFO | 30 min |
| 4 | System updates useful life on affected assets; recalculates remaining depreciation schedule: (a) net book value at adjustment date ÷ remaining new useful life = new monthly depreciation, (b) adjustment applied prospectively from next depreciation run, (c) change documented in asset history with approval reference | System | Fixed Asset Accountant | Automated |
| 5 | External auditor reviews useful life changes during annual audit; confirms PFRS compliance and consistency of application | External Auditor | — | During annual audit |


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "Triggers for useful life review" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-count of "Accountant prepares useful life adjustment proposal" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews and approves adjustment" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-347 (ensure controlled execution — Depreciation & Financial Reporting (PA-35.2).)
- operational: Fixed Asset Accountant review and approval gate

### System Touchpoints
- Useful life adjustment workflow with justification and approval (W1699.2–3)
- Automatic recalculation of depreciation schedule (W1699.4)
- Audit trail for all useful life changes (W1699.4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Income statement impact**: Extending useful life reduces depreciation expense (improves EBITDA); shortening increases it — management bias toward extending; external auditor scrutiny required
- **Batch adjustments**: Technology changes may affect entire asset classes (e.g., all 600 POS terminals); batch adjustment capability needed to avoid processing 600 individual changes

### Time Estimate
Useful-life adjustments 1–2 hours/batch preparation + 30 min approval × ~50–100 adjustments/year in batches ≈ 75–225 hours/year; schedule recalculation automated; external-audit review at the annual audit.

## W1700. Impairment Assessment & Testing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Indicators of impairment: store closure plan, significant decline in asset market value, physical damage, change in use, or annual testing for goodwill/certain intangibles |
| **Frequency** | Event-driven; annual review for stores with consistent losses |
| **Volume** | ~5–15 impairment assessments/year |
| **Owner** | FP&A Manager |
| **Participants** | FP&A Manager, Fixed Asset Accountant, Accounting Manager, CFO, External Auditor |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identify impairment indicators: (a) store-level P&L shows 4+ consecutive quarters of operating loss, (b) store identified for closure or downsizing, (c) natural disaster damage (typhoon, flood), (d) technology obsolescence (old POS system), (e) significant decline in asset replacement cost, (f) regulatory change making asset use illegal or restricted | FP&A Manager | CFO | Event-driven |
| 2 | For identified stores/assets, FP&A performs recoverable amount test per PAS 36: (a) value in use: discounted future cash flows from store operations (revenue projections, cost projections, remaining useful life), (b) fair value less cost of disposal: market comparable or appraisal for real estate, (c) recoverable amount = higher of (a) and (b), (d) if recoverable amount < carrying amount (net book value) → impairment loss = difference | FP&A Manager | CFO | 4–8 hours/store |
| 3 | CFO reviews impairment assessment; if impairment confirmed: (a) Fixed Asset Accountant records impairment loss in GL: debit impairment expense, credit accumulated impairment, (b) asset's carrying amount reduced to recoverable amount, (c) revised carrying amount becomes new basis for future depreciation (over remaining revised useful life) | CFO | CEO / Board (if material) | 1 hour |
| 4 | Disclosures: Accounting Manager ensures impairment loss is properly disclosed in financial statement notes per PAS 36 — description of asset, nature of impairment, amount, method of determining recoverable amount | Accounting Manager | CFO | 2 hours |
| 5 | Annual review: FP&A reassesses impaired assets for possible reversal (PFRS allows reversal for non-goodwill impairments if conditions improve) | FP&A Manager | CFO | 2 hours/year |


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "Identify impairment indicators" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-count of "discounted future cash flows from store operations" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-record of "records impairment loss in GL" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-347 (ensure controlled execution — Depreciation & Financial Reporting (PA-35.2).)
- operational: FP&A Manager review and approval gate

### System Touchpoints
- Impairment indicator dashboard: stores with 4+ quarters of loss, assets approaching end of life with high book value (W1700.1)
- Value in use calculation template with DCF model (W1700.2)
- Impairment journal entry posting with carrying amount adjustment (W1700.3)
- Impairment disclosure report generation (W1700.4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Store closure cascade**: Closing one store may trigger impairment review for nearby stores in the same trade area
- **Judgment intensity**: Value in use calculations require significant management judgment (growth rates, discount rates, terminal values); external auditor will challenge assumptions
- **Timing sensitivity**: Impairment must be recognized as soon as indicator identified; delay until year-end is not acceptable per PFRS

### Time Estimate
~7–11 hours per impairment assessment (PAS 36 recoverable-amount test 4–8 hours/store + CFO review 1 hour + disclosures 2 hours) × ~5–15 assessments/year ≈ 35–165 hours/year; annual reversal reassessment 2 hours.

## W1701. Revaluation of Fixed Assets

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Management decision to adopt revaluation model (vs. cost model) for a class of assets; or significant and sustained change in fair value |
| **Frequency** | Event-driven; if revaluation model adopted, typically every 3–5 years or when material change occurs |
| **Volume** | Rare — only if BuildRight elects revaluation model for land or buildings |
| **Owner** | CFO |
| **Participants** | CFO, Fixed Asset Accountant, External Appraiser, External Auditor |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CFO determines if revaluation model is appropriate: (a) typically applied to land and buildings where fair value is reliably measurable, (b) revaluation model requires regular revaluation of entire asset class (not individual assets), (c) cost model (most common in Philippine retail) is default — no revaluation needed | CFO | Board | Strategic decision |
| 2 | If revaluation adopted: engage accredited external appraiser to determine fair value of asset class (e.g., all store properties, all land); appraiser provides valuation report with methodology, assumptions, and fair value conclusions | External Appraiser | CFO | 2–4 weeks |
| 3 | Fixed Asset Accountant records revaluation adjustment: (a) if fair value > carrying amount: credit revaluation surplus (equity, other comprehensive income), (b) if fair value < carrying amount: debit revaluation loss (P&L, to extent of prior surplus), (c) depreciation recalculated on revalued amount over remaining useful life | Fixed Asset Accountant | CFO | 1–2 days |
| 4 | Ongoing: revaluation must be kept sufficiently current; if asset class is revalued, all assets in that class must be revalued simultaneously | CFO | Board | Every 3–5 years |


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "methodology, assumptions, and fair value conclusions" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-record of "records revaluation adjustment" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-347 (ensure controlled execution — Depreciation & Financial Reporting (PA-35.2).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### System Touchpoints
- Revaluation model configuration per asset class (W1701.1)
- Revaluation adjustment journal entry with OCI or P&L impact (W1701.3)
- Recalculated depreciation schedule on revalued amount (W1701.3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Model choice**: Most Philippine retailers use cost model; revaluation model adds complexity and requires periodic appraisals costing PHP 500K–2M per cycle
- **Equity volatility**: Revaluation surplus increases equity but is unrealized; analysts and lenders may adjust for it
- **Tax implications**: Revaluation surplus is not taxable until realized (asset sold); but BIR may scrutinize

### Time Estimate
Strategic-election workflow: CFO revaluation-model decision, external appraiser engagement over 2–4 weeks, revaluation recording 1–2 days, with revaluation currency maintained every 3–5 years — rare and event-driven (only if the revaluation model is elected for land/buildings); no recurring annual figure.

## W1702. Fixed Asset Register Reconciliation & Reporting

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Month-end close; annual audit preparation |
| **Frequency** | Monthly reconciliation; quarterly detailed review |
| **Volume** | 5 entity registers + 1 consolidated register |
| **Owner** | Fixed Asset Accountant |
| **Participants** | Fixed Asset Accountant, GL Accountant, Accounting Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Monthly: Fixed Asset Accountant reconciles fixed asset sub-ledger to GL: (a) total gross asset value in sub-ledger = GL fixed asset control accounts, (b) total accumulated depreciation in sub-ledger = GL accumulated depreciation accounts, (c) net book value reconciliation: gross − accumulated depreciation = net book value per GL, (d) identify and resolve discrepancies (timing differences, unposted entries, manual adjustments) | Fixed Asset Accountant | Accounting Manager | 4–6 hours/month |
| 2 | System generates monthly fixed asset roll-forward report: (a) opening balance (gross and accumulated depreciation), (b) additions (new assets), (c) disposals (retired assets), (d) depreciation expense for the month, (e) impairment losses, (f) transfers (inter-entity/inter-location), (g) foreign currency translation adjustments (if any), (h) closing balance (gross and accumulated depreciation) | System | Fixed Asset Accountant | Automated |
| 3 | Accounting Manager reviews roll-forward for unusual movements: (a) large additions not matching approved capex list, (b) disposals without proper authorization, (c) depreciation spikes from useful life changes, (d) inter-entity transfer imbalances | Accounting Manager | CFO | 1 hour/month |
| 4 | Quarterly: Fixed Asset Accountant prepares fixed asset summary for CFO: (a) net book value by entity and asset category, (b) capital expenditure vs. budget, (c) depreciation expense vs. budget, (d) disposals and gains/losses on disposal, (e) key metrics: average age of assets by category, asset turnover ratio (revenue ÷ net fixed assets) | Fixed Asset Accountant | CFO | 2 hours/quarter |


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "Accountant reconciles fixed asset sub-ledger to GL" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-generate of "generates monthly fixed asset roll-forward report" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews roll-forward for unusual movements" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-347 (ensure controlled execution — Depreciation & Financial Reporting (PA-35.2).)
- operational: Fixed Asset Accountant review and approval gate; periodic reconciliation against source documents

### System Touchpoints
- Auto-reconciliation of sub-ledger to GL with variance flagging (W1702.1)
- Monthly roll-forward report with all movements (W1702.2)
- Quarterly management report with KPIs (W1702.4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Sub-ledger drift**: Manual journal entries posted directly to GL (bypassing fixed asset module) create reconciliation discrepancies; system should prevent direct GL posting to fixed asset accounts
- **Intercompany elimination**: Consolidated net book value must properly eliminate intercompany asset transfers at group level

### Time Estimate
Monthly sub-ledger-to-GL reconciliation 4–6 hours + roll-forward review 1 hour ≈ 60–84 hours/year across the 5 entity registers; quarterly CFO fixed-asset summary 2 hours ≈ 8 hours/year (roll-forward generation automated).

## W1703. Entity-Level & Consolidated Asset Reporting

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly management reporting; quarterly Board reporting; annual financial statements |
| **Frequency** | Monthly / Quarterly / Annual |
| **Volume** | 5 entity reports + 1 consolidated report per period |
| **Owner** | FP&A Manager |
| **Participants** | FP&A Manager, Fixed Asset Accountant, CFO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System generates entity-level fixed asset reports for each of the 5 entities: (a) BuildRight Holdings Inc. — HQ assets, investments, (b) BuildRight Depot Inc. — stores, POS, fixtures, merchandise-related equipment, (c) BuildRight Logistics Inc. — DC equipment, forklifts, warehouse racking, (d) BuildRight Digital Commerce Inc. — IT servers, development equipment, (e) BuildRight Property Management Inc. — land, buildings, leased property improvements | System | — | Automated |
| 2 | Consolidated fixed asset report: (a) sum of all entity registers, (b) elimination of intercompany asset transfers (selling entity removes, buying entity adds — net zero at group level), (c) elimination of intercompany gains/losses on asset transfers, (d) unified depreciation schedule for group reporting | System | Fixed Asset Accountant | 30 min/month |
| 3 | FP&A prepares Board-level capital asset summary: (a) total net book value and trend, (b) capital intensity ratio (net fixed assets ÷ total assets), (c) asset utilization (revenue ÷ average net fixed assets), (d) capex to depreciation ratio (indicates whether asset base is growing or aging), (e) planned vs. actual capex, (f) store-level asset intensity (net assets per store) | FP&A Manager | CFO | 2 hours/quarter |


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "entity-level fixed asset reports" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-schedule of "schedule for group reporting" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-generate of "prepares Board-level capital asset summary" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-219 (ensure fixed-asset register accuracy.)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### System Touchpoints
- Entity-specific and consolidated fixed asset reporting (W1703.1–2)
- Intercompany elimination automation (W1703.2)
- Board-level capital asset dashboard (W1703.3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Elimination-error risk**: intercompany asset transfers not eliminated (or eliminated leaving the gain/loss in) overstate consolidated net fixed assets and capital intensity; mitigated by the system consolidation step that removes IC transfers and gains/losses at group level (step 2)
- **Register-drift risk**: entity reports generated from unreconciled registers misstate NBV in the Board capital summary; mitigated by report generation off the reconciled asset register with Fixed Asset Accountant review before FP&A roll-up (steps 1–3)

### Time Estimate
Monthly consolidated fixed-asset report 30 min ≈ 6 hours/year; quarterly Board-level capital asset summary 2 hours ≈ 8 hours/year (entity-level report generation automated).

## W1704. Tax Depreciation & BIR Compliance Reporting

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual income tax return preparation (Q1) |
| **Frequency** | Annual (with quarterly estimates) |
| **Volume** | 5 entity tax returns |
| **Owner** | Tax Accountant |
| **Participants** | Tax Accountant, Fixed Asset Accountant, External Tax Advisor |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System maintains parallel tax depreciation schedule per BIR-prescribed rates: (a) buildings: 5% declining balance or straight-line (taxpayer choice), (b) machinery and equipment: varies by type per BIR schedule, (c) vehicles: 20% declining balance (5-year useful life), (d) computers/IT equipment: 20–33.3% per BIR schedule, (e) furniture and fixtures: 10% declining balance (10-year useful life) | System | Tax Accountant | Configured annually |
| 2 | Annual: Tax Accountant generates tax depreciation schedule per entity; compares with book depreciation per W1698; calculates book-tax difference (temporary difference → deferred tax asset or liability per PFRS 112) | Tax Accountant | Tax Manager | 1–2 days/year |
| 3 | Tax depreciation amounts used in annual income tax computation (BIR Form 1702RT); included in tax return supporting schedules; tax depreciation reduces taxable income | Tax Accountant | CFO | Part of annual tax return |
| 4 | BIR audit readiness: Fixed asset register and depreciation schedules maintained for 10-year retention period per BIR requirement; supporting documents (POs, delivery receipts, board resolutions for capex approval) archived and indexed | Fixed Asset Accountant | Accounting Manager | Ongoing |


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-schedule of "schedule per BIR-prescribed rates" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-count of "Accountant generates tax depreciation schedule per entity" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "BIR audit readiness" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-347 (ensure controlled execution — Depreciation & Financial Reporting (PA-35.2).)
- operational: Tax Accountant review and approval gate

### System Touchpoints
- Dual depreciation engine: book (PFRS) and tax (BIR) running in parallel (W1704.1)
- Book-tax difference report with deferred tax calculation (W1704.2)
- Tax depreciation schedule exportable for BIR return preparation (W1704.3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Book-tax differences**: PFRS useful lives often differ from BIR-prescribed rates; maintaining parallel schedules requires dual depreciation functionality
- **BIR audit exposure**: Discrepancies between fixed asset register and tax depreciation claims can trigger BIR assessment; accuracy and documentation are critical
- **New asset categories**: Technology assets (cloud subscriptions, SaaS) may not fit traditional depreciation categories; tax treatment requires careful analysis

### Time Estimate
Annual tax-depreciation schedule generation and book-vs-tax comparison 1–2 days ≈ 10–16 hours/year across the 5 entities; quarterly estimates within reporting; schedules retained 10 years for BIR audit readiness (parallel tax schedule maintained system-side).

## W1705. Capital Work-in-Progress Reconciliation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Month-end close; project milestone completion |
| **Frequency** | Monthly reconciliation |
| **Volume** | ~10–20 active CIP projects at any time (new stores, DC expansion, IT projects) |
| **Owner** | Fixed Asset Accountant |
| **Participants** | Fixed Asset Accountant, Project Manager, FP&A, Accounting Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Monthly: Fixed Asset Accountant reviews all active CIP accounts: (a) costs accumulated to date per project, (b) budget vs. actual cost, (c) project status (on-track, delayed, on-hold), (d) estimated completion date, (e) aging of CIP — projects in CIP > 12 months flagged for review (potential abandoned or stalled projects) | Fixed Asset Accountant | Accounting Manager | 2–4 hours/month |
| 2 | Project Manager confirms project status; provides: (a) % completion estimate, (b) costs incurred but not yet invoiced (accruals needed), (c) remaining budget to complete, (d) expected completion date | Project Manager | COO / CIO | 30 min/project |
| 3 | Fixed Asset Accountant posts month-end CIP accruals for costs incurred but not yet invoiced (contractor progress billing lag) | Fixed Asset Accountant | Accounting Manager | 1 hour/month |
| 4 | When project completes: Fixed Asset Accountant reconciles total CIP costs, performs final review, and triggers asset turnover per W40.3 — converting CIP to completed fixed assets | Fixed Asset Accountant | Accounting Manager | 2–4 hours/project |
| 5 | Abandoned or cancelled projects: CIP costs written off to expense (not capitalized); requires CFO approval; documented with reason for abandonment | Fixed Asset Accountant | CFO | 1 hour/project |


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "reviews all active CIP accounts" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-count of "Accountant posts month-end CIP accruals for costs incurred but not yet invoiced" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-reconcile of "reconciles total CIP costs, performs final review, and triggers asset turnover…" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-347 (ensure controlled execution — Depreciation & Financial Reporting (PA-35.2).)
- operational: Fixed Asset Accountant review and approval gate; periodic reconciliation against source documents

### System Touchpoints
- CIP account dashboard with project-level tracking (W1705.1)
- Budget vs. actual report per CIP project (W1705.1)
- CIP aging report with 12-month threshold flag (W1705.1)
- CIP-to-asset conversion workflow (W1705.4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **CIP accumulation**: Some projects stall (permit delays, contractor issues) and costs accumulate in CIP indefinitely; aging review prevents balance sheet inflation
- **Cost capitalization cutoff**: Determining when a project is "complete" (and ready for depreciation) vs. still in progress requires judgment — especially for phased store openings
- **Intercompany CIP**: If BuildRight Holdings pays for a new store that will be operated by BuildRight Depot Inc., CIP must be tracked in the correct entity

### Time Estimate
Monthly CIP cycle ~8–15 hours (account review 2–4 hours + PM status confirmations 30 min/project × 10–20 active projects + accrual posting 1 hour) ≈ 96–180 hours/year; project completions 2–4 hours/project for reconciliation and capitalization ≈ 60–200 hours/year; abandoned-project write-offs 1 hour/project.
