## W335. Major Capex & Post-Implementation Project Audit

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Completion of a major capital project (e.g., new DC, ERP implementation) |
| **Frequency** | Post-project (Ad-hoc) |
| **Volume** | 2-4 major project audits per year |
| **Owner** | Internal Audit |
| **Participants** | Project Management Office (PMO), Finance, Business Owners |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Select major projects completed in the last 6-12 months for post-implementation review (PIR) | Head of IA | Audit Committee | 1 day |
| 2 | Compare actual project spend against approved Capex budget (W21) | Audit Team | Senior Auditor | 2 days |
| 3 | Evaluate project deliverables against original business case and ROI projections | Audit Team | Senior Auditor | 3 days |
| 4 | Assess project governance, change management effectiveness, and vendor contract adherence | Audit Team | Senior Auditor | 3 days |
| 5 | Document lessons learned and root causes for budget/timeline overruns | Audit Team | Head of IA | 2 days |
| 6 | Issue PIR report to Executive Team and Audit Committee | Head of IA | Head of IA | 1 day |

### System Touchpoints

- ERP Project Accounting module for actual vs. budget tracking
- Fixed Asset module for capitalization review
- EPM / Project Management software for schedule and milestone tracking

### Pain Points / Risks

- Difficulty in quantifying soft benefits or delayed ROI in business cases.
- Defensive posture from project sponsors regarding budget overruns.


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "Select major projects completed in the last 6-12 months" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System rule-based auto-approval of "approved Capex budget" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "Document lessons learned and root causes for budget/timeline overruns" (replaces manual Step 5).

### Controls
- CTL-306 (ensure controlled execution — Specialized Audit Domains (PA-21.3).)
- operational: Internal Audit review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication

2-4 audits per year; requires ~2 weeks of dedicated fieldwork and analysis by a Senior Auditor.

### Time Estimate

~2–3 weeks per review.

## W341. ESG Assurance & Sustainability Compliance Audit

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual reporting cycle (post-Sustainability Report draft) |
| **Frequency** | Annual |
| **Volume** | Audit of Scope 1, 2, and 3 data for 200 stores and 4 DCs |
| **Owner** | Internal Audit / ESG Lead |
| **Participants** | Sustainability Team, Finance, Maintenance, HR, Legal |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Data Source Validation**: Trace GHG emission data (Scope 1 & 2) back to utility bills (W7) and fuel logs (W196) | Auditor | Head of IA | 1 week |
| 2 | **Metric Calculation Review**: Verify that emission factors used are consistent with DOE/DENR and SEC PH guidelines | Auditor | Head of IA | 3 days |
| 3 | **Supply Chain Disclosure Audit**: Verify "Sustainable Sourcing" claims (W195) by sampling vendor audit reports and certifications | Auditor | Head of IA | 1 week |
| 4 | **Social Impact Verification**: Sample CSR spend and community development projects (W194) for evidence of completion and impact | Auditor | Head of IA | 3 days |
| 5 | **SEC Reporting Compliance**: Review draft Sustainability Report against SEC Memo Circular No. 4 requirements | Auditor | Head of IA | 2 days |
| 6 | **Final Assurance Memo**: Issue internal assurance statement to the Board Sustainability Committee | Head of IA | Sustainability Committee | 2 days |

### System Touchpoints

- Utility consumption module (Scope 2 tracking)
- Fleet fuel integration (Scope 1 tracking)
- ESG Dashboard / Scorecard (W192)
- HR Training records (for Social/Human Capital metrics)

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Greenwashing** — Reporting metrics without auditable evidence.
- **Data Fragmentation** — Manual collection of utility data across 200 stores is prone to error.
- **Regulatory Change** — SEC PH requirements for ESG are evolving rapidly.

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "Data Source Validation" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "Metric Calculation Review" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "Supply Chain Disclosure Audit" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-306 (ensure controlled execution — Specialized Audit Domains (PA-21.3).)
- operational: Internal Audit / ESG Lead review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
3.4 weeks elapsed per occurrence (1 week + 3 days + 1 week + 3 days + 2 days + 2 days) — at Annual ≈ 1 occurrence/year.

## W344. Trade Promotion, Rebate & Marketing Spend Audit

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly review of marketing spend |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly |
| **Volume** | Audit of top 10 vendor rebate agreements and top 5 marketing campaigns |
| **Owner** | Internal Audit |
| **Participants** | Merchandising, Marketing, AP, Vendor |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Agreement Audit**: Verify existence of signed vendor rebate contracts for the period | Auditor | Senior Auditor | 1 day |
| 2 | **Rebate Calculation**: Independently calculate rebates based on purchase/sales volume from ERP | Auditor | Head of IA | 2 days |
| 3 | **Collection Audit**: Trace rebate accruals to actual collection or offsetting against AP (W100) | Auditor | Head of IA | 1 day |
| 4 | **Marketing Spend Proof**: Sample "Trade Spend" invoices for proof of execution (e.g., photos of end-caps, print ads) | Auditor | Senior Auditor | 3 days |
| 5 | **Campaign ROI Audit**: Verify that "Bonus Buys" or "Bundles" were correctly configured in POS | Auditor | Senior Auditor | 2 days |
| 6 | **Findings Distribution**: Report leakage (unclaimed rebates) or unauthorized spend to Merchandising | Head of IA | VP Merchandising | 1 day |

### System Touchpoints

- Merchandising Module (Vendor Rebate configurations)
- AP / Vendor Statement Reconciliation (W100)
- Marketing Budget Tracker
- POS Promo Engine logs

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Rebate Leakage** — Failure to claim earned rebates due to poor tracking.
- **Ghost Campaigns** — Paying for marketing services that were never physically executed in-store.
- **Config Errors** — POS promos giving away more discount than intended.

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "Verify existence of signed vendor rebate contracts for the period" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-calculate of "calculate rebates based on purchase/sales volume from ERP" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-collect of "Collection Audit" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-224 (ensure audit-plan execution & remediation.)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
10 days elapsed per occurrence (1 day + 2 days + 1 day + 3 days + 2 days + 1 day) — at Quarterly ≈ 4 occurrences/year.

## W353. Ethical Sourcing & Social Compliance Audit

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vendor onboarding or Annual risk-based cycle |
| **Frequency** | Annual (for high-risk categories/regions) |
| **Volume** | ~20–30 priority vendors (Private Label & Direct Import) |
| **Owner** | Compliance Officer / Internal Audit |
| **Participants** | Procurement, Sustainability Lead, Third-Party Auditors, Vendors |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Risk Profiling**: Identify high-risk vendors based on region, category (e.g., textiles, lumber), and spend | Compliance | Head of IA | 2 days |
| 2 | **Self-Assessment**: Send Social Compliance Questionnaire to vendors | Compliance | Compliance | 1 week |
| 3 | **Audit Selection**: Select vendors for on-site audit or request recent SMETA/BSCI reports | Compliance | Head of IA | 2 days |
| 4 | **On-site Audit**: Conduct (or shadow) audit covering labor practices, safety, and environmental impact | Auditor | Compliance | 2-3 days |
| 5 | **CAP Management**: Issue Corrective Action Plan for findings (e.g., missing fire exits, excessive OT) | Auditor | Compliance | 3 days |
| 6 | **Verification**: Follow up on CAP implementation; terminate vendor if critical violations persist | Procurement | VP Legal | Ongoing |

### System Touchpoints

- Vendor Management Portal (Questionnaire & Report storage)
- Procurement System (Vendor status blocking)
- ESG Dashboard (Social impact metrics)

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Reputational Damage** — Discovery of child labor or forced labor in the supply chain (e.g., modern slavery).
- **Audit Fatigue** — Vendors being over-audited by multiple retailers.
- **Fraudulent Documentation** — Vendors falsifying payroll or safety records.


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-identify of "Identify high-risk vendors based on region, category" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-send of "Send Social Compliance Questionnaire to vendors" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-report of "request recent SMETA/BSCI reports" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-306 (ensure controlled execution — Specialized Audit Domains (PA-21.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication

Handled by Compliance Officer. May require specialized external audit firms for international factory visits.

### Time Estimate

2-3 weeks per vendor audit cycle (prep to CAP issuance).

## W490. Organized Retail Crime (ORC) Investigation & Task Force

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Pattern detection from EBR analytics (W466); law enforcement tip; multi-store incident correlation; industry ORC alert |
| **Frequency** | Event-driven (investigation activation); monthly (task force review); quarterly (industry coordination) |
| **Volume** | ~5–10 active ORC investigations/year; monthly task force meetings |
| **Owner** | Loss Prevention Director |
| **Participants** | LP Investigators, Store Managers, Regional Managers, VP Legal, Security Lead, PNP/CIDG, Industry ORC Alliance |

### Background

Organized Retail Crime (ORC) refers to professional theft rings that systematically steal from retail stores for resale, unlike opportunistic shoplifting handled by standard LP procedures (W466). For BuildRight with 200 stores and high-value merchandise (power tools, copper wire, appliances, branded hardware), ORC manifests as: (a) organized shoplifting rings hitting multiple stores in a region; (b) return fraud (stolen merchandise returned for cash/store credit using fake receipts); (c) employee-assisted theft (collusion between staff and external rings); (d) cargo theft during inter-island transit (W66); (e) vendor delivery short-shipment fraud. ORC is distinct from standard LP (W37, W466) in its scale, sophistication, and cross-store impact. W466 handles individual store-level LP incidents; this workflow handles the coordinated multi-store investigation and law enforcement engagement required for organized rings.

### Steps

### System Touchpoints
- Exception-Based Reporting (EBR) module (W466): pattern detection analytics
- POS Transaction Database: cross-store transaction correlation
- CCTV System (W71): multi-store footage retrieval
- Inventory Shrinkage Reports (W248/W92): loss quantification
- Employee Scheduling System (W34): schedule-incident correlation
- Fleet Telematics (W199): cargo theft investigation
- ORC Case Management System: case files, evidence tracking, law enforcement coordination
- Social Media Monitoring Tools: resale activity detection
- Integration with W37 (loss prevention), W466 (LPAP), W248 (inventory variance), W71 (CCTV), W92 (inventory adjustment), W140 (OHS — if employee injury during incident), W43 (separation — ORC-related termination)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Cross-store evidence correlation difficulty**: With 200 stores using different CCTV systems (potentially different retention periods, different quality), correlating footage of the same individuals across stores in different cities is manually intensive and time-sensitive (30-day online retention per W71)
- **Employee collusion detection**: Internal LP systems (W466) are designed to detect individual employee theft; detecting coordinated collusion between employees at multiple stores requires advanced analytics and confidential investigation techniques that can be perceived as intrusive, creating HR and labor relations friction (W493)
- **Law enforcement capacity**: PNP CIDG has limited capacity for retail theft investigations; low-value individual cases (even if part of an organized ring) may not receive priority attention, reducing prosecution success
- **Social media resale detection**: Stolen BuildRight merchandise resold on Facebook Marketplace, Lazada, or Shopee is difficult to detect systematically; manual monitoring is labor-intensive and reactive
- **Jurisdictional complexity**: ORC rings operating across multiple cities and provinces involve multiple PNP jurisdictions; coordinating a multi-jurisdictional investigation requires significant law enforcement liaison effort


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-monitor of "monitoring for resale of stolen BuildRight merchandise" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-create of "creates ORC case file" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "verify seal integrity, GPS tracking per W199" (replaces manual Step 3).
