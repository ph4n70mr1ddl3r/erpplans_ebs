## W122. Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) Review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly risk committee meeting |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly |
| **Volume** | Top 20 corporate risks reviewed |
| **Owner** | Head of Internal Audit (acting as Risk Officer) |
| **Participants** | Executive Team (C-Level), Board of Directors |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Update Enterprise Risk Register: Financial, Operational, Strategic, Compliance | Head of IA | CEO | 1 week |
| 2 | Assess external risks: Inflation, port congestion, regulatory changes, competition | Executive Team | CEO | Ongoing |
| 3 | Review mitigation plans for top risks (e.g., FX Hedging W80, DR Plan W55) | Head of IA | CFO/CIO | 2 days |
| 4 | Validate Risk Appetite Statement with Board of Directors — confirm thresholds for revenue volatility, FX exposure, inventory shrinkage, and operational downtime remain aligned with strategic direction | Head of IA | Audit Committee | 1 day |
| 5 | Assign mitigation owners for each top-20 risk with measurable KPIs and firm deadlines for next quarter | Executive Team | CEO | 1 day |
| 6 | Generate Risk Heat Map (Impact vs. Likelihood matrix) for visual presentation — categorize risks as Critical (red), High (orange), Medium (yellow), Low (green) | Head of IA | Head of IA | 1 day |
| 7 | Present Risk Dashboard and Heat Map to Board Risk Committee; obtain formal recommendations and sign-off; set up action item tracker for next quarter follow-through | Head of IA | CEO | 2 hours |

### System Touchpoints

- Risk Register module for ERM tracking and heat map generation
- ERP reporting dashboards for financial and operational risk indicators
- FX hedging and treasury module (W80) for financial risk data
- Business Continuity / DR system (W55) for operational resilience metrics
- Board portal for Risk Committee presentation, sign-off, and action item documentation

### Pain Points / Risks

- **SEC revised corporate governance framework** — ERM disclosures must meet enhanced reporting standards for publicly relevant entities.
- **Revised Corporation Code requirements** — Director responsibilities for risk oversight carry personal liability implications. mitigated by the Head of Internal Audit (acting as Risk Officer) review and approval gate
- **Board Audit Committee expectations and independence** — Board may challenge risk appetite thresholds, requiring iterative revisions before sign-off. mitigated by the Head of Internal Audit (acting as Risk Officer) review and approval gate
- **Philippine Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act (RA 3019)** — Corruption risks in government-facing operations (permits, licenses) must be explicitly scored and mitigated.
- Risk appetite thresholds are difficult to quantify for a retail business exposed to typhoon-season disruptions and supply-chain volatility across 200 store locations.
- Mitigation owner accountability can weaken if KPIs are not cascaded into individual performance scorecards.


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-capture of "Enterprise Risk Register" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "Review mitigation plans for top risks" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-validate of "Validate Risk Appetite Statement with Board of Directors — confirm thresholds…" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-305 (ensure controlled execution — Enterprise Risk Management (PA-21.2).)
- operational: Head of Internal Audit (acting as Risk Officer) review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication

Quarterly review cycle. Approximately 1 week of preparation work (risk register updates, heat map generation, data gathering) plus a 2-hour Board Risk Committee presentation. The effort is absorbed by the Head of IA with support from the audit team for data compilation. No dedicated additional headcount required.

### Time Estimate

~1 week of preparation per quarter (risk register update, heat map, mitigation owner assignment, presentation materials). The Board Risk Committee session itself is ~2 hours. Total quarterly effort: ~10 working days (per the step durations), largely absorbed by the Head of IA.

## W123. Fraud Investigation Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Whistleblower tip (W79), LP Exception (W37), or Audit discovery |
| **Frequency** | Ad-hoc |
| **Volume** | ~5–10 investigations/year |
| **Owner** | Head of Internal Audit |
| **Participants** | HR (for employee discipline), Legal (for prosecution), Security |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Intake of report/discovery; initial evidence review (CCTV, ERP logs) | Audit Team | Head of IA | 1–2 days |
| 2 | Form Investigation Committee (IA, HR, Legal) | Head of IA | CEO | 1 day |
| 3 | Detailed Forensic Audit: Transaction tracing, witness interviews | Audit Team | Head of IA | 1–3 weeks |
| 4 | Investigative Report: Document findings, suspects, and loss value | Head of IA | CEO | 3 days |
| 5 | Administrative Hearing / Disciplinary Action | HR Manager | CHRO | 1–2 weeks |
| 6 | Legal Action (if applicable): Filing of criminal/civil cases | Legal Counsel | VP Legal | 1–3 months |
| 7 | Control Gap Remediation: Update process to prevent recurrence | Head of IA | CEO | 1 week |

### System Touchpoints

- ERP activity logs and POS override logs for evidence capture and transaction tracing
- CCTV / security system integration for incident verification
- Whistleblower portal (W79) for case intake and anonymous reporting
- Exception reporting dashboard (shrinkage, voids, high-value returns) for anomaly detection
- Internal Controls Manager (ICM — fit-gap H11) for investigation documentation and case tracking
- HR module for disciplinary action workflows and employee records

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Philippine Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act (RA 3019)** — Procurement fraud involving government officials carries criminal liability for the company and individual officers.
- **Whistleblower protection (W79 integration)** — Inadequate protection can lead to retaliation claims and discourage future reporting.
- **Forensic audit capability in-house vs. external** — The team lacks certified forensic accountants; complex cases require external specialists at significant cost.
- **DOLE involvement in employee disciplinary proceedings** — Procedural errors in administrative hearings can result in illegal dismissal cases before the National Labor Relations Commission (NLRC).
- **NBI coordination for criminal cases** — Criminal referrals to the National Bureau of Investigation require careful evidence handling and may take months to resolve.
- **Board Audit Committee expectations and independence** — High-profile fraud cases attract Board scrutiny and may require direct reporting outside normal channels.
- Evidence spoliation risk if suspects become aware of the investigation before evidence is secured.


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "Intake of report/discovery" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System continuous audit of "Detailed Forensic Audit" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-document of "Document findings, suspects, and loss value" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-305 (ensure controlled execution — Enterprise Risk Management (PA-21.2).)
- operational: Head of Internal Audit review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication

Ad-hoc, with 5–10 investigations per year. Duration varies significantly: 1–3 weeks per investigation for fieldwork, plus potential legal proceedings lasting 1–3 months. An Investigation Committee is formed ad-hoc from Internal Audit, HR, and Legal. The Head of IA leads each investigation. External forensic specialists may be engaged for complex cases, adding cost but not permanent headcount.

### Time Estimate

Each investigation runs 1–3 weeks for forensic fieldwork plus 3 days for the investigative report. Administrative hearings add 1–2 weeks. If legal action is pursued, the external process runs 1–3 months. Total elapsed time per case: 3 weeks (internal only) to 4–5 months (including legal proceedings). With 5–10 cases per year, the team must manage multiple concurrent investigations.

## W1328. Third-Party Risk Assessment & Vendor Criticality Tiering

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual vendor criticality review cycle; new vendor relationship above PHP 10M annual spend; vendor financial distress signal (payment default, news report, credit rating downgrade); or post-incident review revealing over-reliance on a single vendor |
| **Frequency** | Annual full review of top-200 vendors by spend; quarterly monitoring of Critical-tier vendors; ad-hoc for trigger events |
| **Volume** | ~200 vendors reviewed annually (top vendors by spend representing ~85% of COGS); ~20–30 vendors re-tiered per cycle |
| **Owner** | Head of Internal Audit (acting as Risk Officer) |
| **Participants** | Category Manager, Procurement Director, CFO, VP Supply Chain, CIO (for technology vendor dependencies) |

### Background

BuildRight Depot sources from ~800–1,000 vendors, with the top 200 by spend representing approximately 85% of COGS. Several vendors are sole-source or single-source for critical categories (cement, structural steel, electrical cable, plumbing pipe), creating concentration risk. A disruption at a sole-source vendor — due to financial distress, natural disaster, regulatory action, or geopolitical event (particularly for import vendors from China/Taiwan) — could halt sales in entire product categories across all 200 stores. Philippine supply chains face additional risks from typhoon damage, port congestion, and limited logistics alternatives across the archipelago. This workflow systematically assesses third-party risk, assigns criticality tiers, and drives mitigation actions (dual-sourcing, safety stock, alternative qualification) for the most vulnerable vendor relationships. It complements W122 (ERM Review) by providing granular vendor-level risk visibility and W224 (vendor onboarding) by adding risk-based ongoing monitoring.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Vendor spend & dependency analysis**: Procurement extracts annual vendor spend data and category assignment: (a) identify sole-source vendors (only one approved vendor for a category), (b) identify single-source vendors (one primary vendor supplying > 80% of category volume despite alternates existing), (c) calculate concentration ratio: top-5 vendor spend ÷ total COGS, (d) identify vendor-to-store dependency: are all 200 stores equally dependent on the vendor, or do certain regions rely more heavily? | Procurement Analyst | Procurement Director | 2 days |
| 2 | **Financial health assessment**: For vendors above PHP 10M annual spend: (a) request audited financial statements (or BIR-filed financials for Philippine vendors), (b) calculate financial risk indicators: current ratio, debt-to-equity, revenue trend (3-year), profit margin trend, (c) check SEC registration status and any regulatory actions for Philippine corporations, (d) for import vendors: check country risk rating (using World Bank/Economist Intelligence Unit data), sanctions list screening, and political stability indicators, (e) assign financial health score: Green (low risk), Yellow (moderate — declining trend), Red (high — immediate concern) | Procurement Analyst / Finance Analyst | CFO | 3–5 days |
| 3 | **Operational resilience assessment**: For vendors Critical-tiered in the prior cycle plus current sole/single-source candidates: (a) assess geographic concentration: is the vendor's manufacturing facility in a typhoon-prone area, or near a single port?, (b) assess capacity utilization: does the vendor have surge capacity for BuildRight's peak demand (pre-summer, pre-Ber months)?, (c) assess logistics dependency: does the vendor rely on a single shipping route or port that could be disrupted?, (d) assess technology dependency: for IT vendors, does BuildRight have data portability and system access if the vendor fails?, (e) assess regulatory exposure: is the vendor in a heavily regulated industry (DENR permits for cement plants, FDA for paints/coatings) with risk of forced shutdown? | Category Manager / CIO (for IT vendors) | VP Supply Chain | 2–3 days |
| 4 | **Criticality tiering**: Head of IA assigns criticality tier based on spend, dependency, financial health, and operational resilience: **Tier 1 — Critical** (sole-source for essential category OR single-source > 80% OR annual spend > PHP 500M OR vendor failure would stop sales in multiple stores within 2 weeks); **Tier 2 — High** (significant spend PHP 100M–500M, limited alternate sources, category impact if disrupted); **Tier 3 — Moderate** (moderate spend PHP 20M–100M, alternates available but switching takes 30–60 days); **Tier 4 — Low** (spend < PHP 20M, multiple alternates, easy substitution) | Head of IA | VP Supply Chain | 1–2 days |
| 5 | **Mitigation plan development**: For Tier 1 and Tier 2 vendors: Category Manager develops risk mitigation plan: (a) **Dual-sourcing strategy**: identify and qualify at least one alternate vendor per critical category per W224; timeline: 6–12 months for qualification, (b) **Safety stock policy**: increase safety stock for critical sole-source items per W31 to cover 30–60 days of demand, (c) **Contractual protections**: include business continuity clause, disaster recovery commitment, and termination-for-convenience provision in vendor agreements per W688, (d) **Monitoring cadence**: quarterly financial health check for Tier 1, semi-annual for Tier 2, (e) **Escalation protocol**: pre-defined triggers for activating alternate sourcing (vendor misses OTIF for 2 consecutive months, vendor credit rating downgraded, natural disaster at vendor location) | Category Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2–3 days per vendor |
| 6 | **Board risk committee reporting**: Head of IA includes third-party risk summary in quarterly ERM review per W122: (a) number of Critical/High-tier vendors and concentration ratio, (b) key risks and mitigation status for top-10 critical vendors, (c) any trigger events during the quarter and response actions, (d) progress on dual-sourcing initiatives for sole-source categories, (e) recommendation for Board approval if vendor risk acceptance decision required (e.g., accepting sole-source risk due to insufficient alternate quality) | Head of IA | CEO | Absorbed into W122 quarterly cycle |

### System Touchpoints

- Vendor master data with criticality tier, financial health score, dependency ratio, and assessment date
- Spend analytics module for vendor concentration analysis
- Dual-sourcing tracking dashboard with alternate vendor qualification status
- Integration with W122 (ERM review), W224 (vendor onboarding), W688 (contract management), W44 (vendor performance), W31 (safety stock policy)

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Vendor resistance to financial disclosure**: Philippine SME vendors may refuse to share audited financials; mitigated by requiring minimum financial documentation (BIR-filed income statement) as a vendor portal condition per W705
- **Sole-source dependency reality**: for some categories (specialized cement grades, proprietary tool brands), no viable alternate vendor exists; risk acceptance must be formally documented with Board awareness
- **Country risk volatility**: import vendors in politically unstable regions may deteriorate rapidly; quarterly monitoring of Critical-tier import vendors is essential; mitigated by CTL-305 (controlled execution — Enterprise Risk Management (PA-21.2))
- **Mitigation cost**: dual-sourcing increases procurement complexity and may increase unit costs (lower volume per vendor = less leverage); Category Managers must balance risk reduction against margin impact


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-calculate of "calculate concentration ratio" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-validate of "check SEC registration status and any regulatory actions for Philippine…" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "Operational resilience assessment" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-305 (ensure controlled execution — Enterprise Risk Management (PA-21.2).)
- operational: requiring minimum financial documentation (BIR-filed income statement) as a vendor portal condition per W705

### Staffing Implication

- **Head of IA**: ~5–8 days/year on third-party risk assessment governance. Absorbed.
- **Procurement Analyst**: ~5–8 days/year on spend analysis and financial health assessment. Absorbed.
- **Category Managers**: ~2–3 days/year each on mitigation plan development for critical vendors in their categories. Absorbed.
- **No incremental headcount.**

### Time Estimate

- Annual spend and dependency analysis: 2 days
- Financial health assessment: 3–5 days
- Operational resilience assessment: 2–3 days
- Criticality tiering: 1–2 days
- Mitigation plan development: 2–3 days per sole-source/single-source Critical vendor (~10–15 relationships = ~30 days)
- **Annual estimate: ~40 days total effort across all participants (2 + 3–5 + 2–3 + 1–2 + ~30)**

## W1329. Key Risk Indicator (KRI) Monitoring & Automated Alert Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly KRI reporting cycle; or automated alert triggered by KRI threshold breach |
| **Frequency** | Monthly KRI dashboard review; automated daily/weekly threshold monitoring; quarterly KRI calibration |
| **Volume** | ~40–50 KRIs across 8 risk categories (financial, operational, supply chain, compliance, technology, people, reputational, strategic) |
| **Owner** | Head of Internal Audit (acting as Risk Officer) |
| **Participants** | BI Analyst, Department Heads (KRI owners), CFO, CEO |

### Background

W122 establishes the quarterly Enterprise Risk Management review process covering the top 20 corporate risks. However, quarterly review cadence is insufficient for early detection — risks can materialize or escalate between quarterly meetings. Key Risk Indicators (KRIs) are quantifiable metrics that serve as leading indicators of risk exposure, providing early warning before risks crystallize. For example: vendor OTIF decline (leading indicator of supply disruption risk), employee turnover rate increase (leading indicator of people risk), cybersecurity incident frequency (leading indicator of technology risk), and customer complaint escalation rate (leading indicator of reputational risk). This workflow establishes automated KRI monitoring with threshold-based alerts, monthly dashboard review, and quarterly calibration to ensure KRIs remain relevant and thresholds are appropriately sensitive.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **KRI definition & threshold setting**: Head of IA defines KRIs for each of the top 20 corporate risks from W122: (a) for each risk, identify 2–3 leading indicators that predict risk escalation (e.g., Risk: Supply chain disruption → KRIs: vendor OTIF rate, port congestion index, safety stock coverage days), (b) set three threshold levels per KRI: Green (normal — within risk appetite), Amber (elevated — approaching risk limit, requires monitoring), Red (critical — exceeds risk appetite, requires immediate action), (c) define data source, calculation methodology, and reporting frequency for each KRI, (d) examples: **Financial**: days sales outstanding (DSO), current ratio, FX exposure as % of revenue; **Operational**: store revenue variance to plan, inventory turnover, shrinkage rate; **Supply chain**: vendor OTIF rate, import lead time variance, DC throughput; **Compliance**: open audit findings aging, regulatory permit expiry count; **Technology**: system uptime %, cybersecurity incident count, data backup success rate; **People**: employee turnover rate, vacancy rate, training completion %; **Reputational**: CSAT score, social media sentiment, NPS; **Strategic**: new store revenue ramp vs. plan, ecommerce GMV growth rate, market share trend | Head of IA / BI Analyst | CEO | 5–8 days (initial setup); 1 day/year (refresh) |
| 2 | **Automated KRI dashboard configuration**: BI Analyst configures KRI dashboard in BI platform: (a) data feeds from ERP modules (finance, supply chain, HR, POS, IT monitoring), (b) threshold rules with automated color-coding (Green/Amber/Red), (c) trend visualization (3-month rolling, 12-month trend), (d) drill-down capability from KRI to underlying data, (e) automated alert generation: email + dashboard notification when KRI crosses threshold, (f) alert routing: Amber → Department Head (KRI owner), Red → Department Head + VP + Head of IA | BI Analyst | BI Manager | 3–5 days (initial); 1 day/quarter (maintenance) |
| 3 | **Monthly KRI dashboard review**: Head of IA reviews KRI dashboard monthly: (a) status of all 40–50 KRIs — count of Green/Amber/Red, (b) new Amber/Red KRIs since last month — identify emerging risks, (c) KRIs that improved or worsened — trend analysis, (d) for each Amber KRI: confirm Department Head is aware and monitoring, (e) for each Red KRI: confirm action plan is in place with deadline, (f) escalate persistent Red KRIs (> 2 consecutive months) to CEO, (g) generate monthly KRI summary for CFO and CEO | Head of IA | CEO | 2–3 hours/month |
| 4 | **Department Head KRI ownership**: Each Department Head owns specific KRIs and is responsible for: (a) reviewing their KRIs weekly (automated dashboard access), (b) investigating Amber alerts within 5 business days, (c) developing and executing action plans for Red KRIs, (d) reporting corrective action progress to Head of IA monthly, (e) proposing threshold adjustments if KRI is generating excessive false positives or is no longer relevant | Department Heads | VP (respective function) | ~1 hour/week |
| 5 | **Quarterly KRI calibration**: Head of IA reviews KRI effectiveness quarterly: (a) which KRIs provided actionable early warning vs. which were noise?, (b) should any KRIs be added (new risks identified in W122) or removed (no longer relevant)?, (c) are thresholds appropriately calibrated — too sensitive (excessive Amber/Red alerts) or too loose (risks materialize without alert)?, (d) compare KRI alerts to actual risk events — did KRIs predict the risks that materialized?, (e) adjust thresholds and KRI definitions based on calibration findings | Head of IA / BI Analyst | CEO | 1 day/quarter |
| 6 | **Annual KRI effectiveness report**: Head of IA prepares annual KRI effectiveness report for Board Risk Committee: (a) summary of all KRI alerts during the year, (b) prediction accuracy: % of risk events that were preceded by a KRI alert, (c) response effectiveness: time from alert to action plan, (d) calibration changes made during the year, (e) recommendations for KRI framework improvements, (f) integration with W122 annual ERM report | Head of IA | Board Risk Committee | 2 days/year |

### System Touchpoints

- KRI dashboard in BI platform with automated data feeds and threshold monitoring
- Automated alert engine with email/dashboard notifications and escalation routing
- ERP data feeds: finance (DSO, current ratio), supply chain (OTIF, lead time), HR (turnover, vacancy), POS (revenue variance), IT monitoring (uptime, incidents)
- Integration with W122 (ERM review — KRI data feeds quarterly risk assessment), W879 (daily report distribution — KRI alerts distributed), W885 (monthly executive reporting — KRI summary included)

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Alert fatigue**: too many Amber/Red alerts desensitize Department Heads; mitigated by quarterly calibration to reduce false positives and threshold adjustment
- **Data availability for leading indicators**: some KRIs rely on data not captured in current systems (e.g., competitor activity, regulatory change pipeline); mitigated by using proxy indicators and manual assessment where needed
- **KRI gaming**: Department Heads may manage KRI metrics rather than underlying risk (e.g., suppressing customer complaints to keep CSAT KRI green); mitigated by using counter-metrics and Internal Audit spot checks
- **Dashboard complexity**: 40–50 KRIs can overwhelm users; mitigated by role-based views (CEO sees top-10 aggregate, Department Heads see their specific KRIs)


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "methodology, and reporting frequency for each KRI" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-monitor of "Automated KRI dashboard configuration" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "Monthly KRI dashboard review" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-305 (ensure controlled execution — Enterprise Risk Management (PA-21.2).)
- operational: quarterly calibration to reduce false positives and threshold adjustment; operational: using proxy indicators and manual assessment where needed; operational: using counter-metrics and Internal Audit spot checks

### Staffing Implication

- **Head of IA**: ~5–8 hours/month on KRI review and calibration. Absorbed.
- **BI Analyst**: ~1 day/quarter on dashboard maintenance. Absorbed.
- **Department Heads**: ~1 hour/week each on KRI monitoring. Absorbed.
- **No incremental headcount.**

### Time Estimate

- Initial setup: 8–13 days (one-time)
- Monthly review: 2–3 hours
- Quarterly calibration: 1 day
- Annual report: 2 days
- **Ongoing annual estimate: ~80–100 hours/year (Head of IA) + ~50 hours/year (BI Analyst)**

## W1330. Strategic Risk Scenario Planning & War Gaming Exercise

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual strategic planning cycle; or major external event (economic downturn, new mega-competitor entry, regulatory disruption, supply chain shock); or Board request |
| **Frequency** | Annual full scenario planning exercise; quarterly tabletop exercise for highest-impact scenarios; ad-hoc for trigger events |
| **Volume** | 4–6 strategic risk scenarios developed annually; 1–2 tabletop exercises conducted per year |
| **Owner** | Head of Internal Audit (facilitator) |
| **Participants** | CEO, Executive Team, CFO, COO, VP Supply Chain, VP Merchandising, CIO, VP Legal, CHRO |

### Background

Traditional ERM per W122 identifies and rates risks based on likelihood and impact using historical data and expert judgment. However, strategic risks — events that could fundamentally alter BuildRight's business model or competitive position — are inherently difficult to quantify with historical data because they are unprecedented or rare. Examples include: a foreign mega-retailer (e.g., IKEA, Home Depot) entering the Philippine market; a major economic recession reducing construction activity by 30%+; a catastrophic supply chain event (port closure, major vendor bankruptcy) affecting 40%+ of inventory; a regulatory change (e.g., total ban on single-use plastic packaging, new construction materials standards) requiring product reformulation; or a cyberattack compromising customer data for 600,000 loyalty members. Scenario planning and war gaming allow the Executive Team to rehearse responses to these high-impact, low-probability events in a controlled environment, identifying preparedness gaps and pre-positioning response options before the event occurs.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Scenario identification**: Head of IA facilitates scenario identification with Executive Team: (a) review top strategic risks from W122 risk register, (b) identify 4–6 scenarios that would have the highest impact on BuildRight's strategy (revenue decline > 20%, margin compression > 5pp, or existential threat to business model), (c) prioritize scenarios that are plausible within a 3-year horizon, (d) example scenarios for a Philippine hardware retailer: (i) **Mega-competitor entry**: international home improvement retailer opens 20 stores in Metro Manila and Cebu within 18 months, (ii) **Construction recession**: government infrastructure spending cut by 40% + private construction slowdown, (iii) **Import supply shock**: Philippines-China trade tension disrupts 60% of import supply chain, (iv) **Digital disruption**: ecommerce captures 30% of hardware/DIY market within 2 years, (v) **Catastrophic natural disaster**: Category 5 typhoon renders 40 stores inoperable for 30+ days, (vi) **Regulatory disruption**: new building code requires certification for all construction materials, disqualifying 30% of current SKUs | Head of IA / Executive Team | CEO | 1 day |
| 2 | **Scenario development**: For each prioritized scenario, Head of IA develops a detailed scenario brief: (a) scenario narrative: what happens, how it unfolds over 6–12 months, cascading effects, (b) impact assessment: estimated revenue impact, margin impact, cash flow impact, headcount impact, customer impact, (c) current preparedness assessment: what existing capabilities does BuildRight have to respond? (insurance coverage, BCP plans, alternate sourcing, financial reserves), (d) key decision points: what decisions would the Executive Team need to make, and when?, (e) information requirements: what data would be needed in real-time to make those decisions? | Head of IA / Department Heads | CEO | 2–3 days per scenario |
| 3 | **War gaming tabletop exercise**: Head of IA facilitates a half-day tabletop exercise with Executive Team: (a) present scenario brief to participants (no prior exposure to test genuine response capability), (b) inject scenario developments in stages (e.g., "Month 1: Competitor announces 5 store openings in Metro Manila"; "Month 3: Competitor launches aggressive pricing at 15% below BuildRight"; "Month 6: 3 of your Metro Manila stores see 25% revenue decline"), (c) after each inject: participants discuss and decide on response actions, resource allocation, communication strategy, (d) document decisions, decision rationale, and information gaps identified, (e) after exercise: debrief with all participants — what worked, what was missing, what would we do differently? | Head of IA (facilitator) | CEO | Half day per scenario |

## W1541. Key Risk Indicator (KRI) Monthly Monitoring, Threshold Alert & Risk Appetite Dashboard Operations
