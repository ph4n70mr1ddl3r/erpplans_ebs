## W848. Typhoon & Natural Disaster Store Emergency Protocol & Response

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | PAGASA typhoon warning Signal No. 2 or above in store's area; earthquake intensity ≥ V (PHIVOLCS scale); or LGU mandatory evacuation order |
| **Frequency** | Estimated 5–8 activations per year across the chain (Philippines averages 20 typhoons/year; not all affect BuildRight locations) |
| **Volume** | Each activation may affect 20–80 stores depending on typhoon path; DC activations average 2–3 per year |
| **Owner** | COO / Regional Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, DC Manager, LP Officer, IT Security, CHRO, BC Manager, External Emergency Services |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Activate disaster monitoring — BC Manager tracks PAGASA bulletins every 3 hours; classify stores by risk zone (direct path, peripheral, minimal); pre-position emergency response resources | BC Manager | COO | Continuous (from Signal No. 1) |
| 2 | Issue pre-emptive store advisory (at Signal No. 1) — Store Managers activate preparedness checklist: secure outdoor merchandise (lumber, garden center), sandbag entrances in flood-prone locations, verify generator fuel and emergency supplies | Store Manager | Regional Manager | 4 hours |
| 3 | Execute controlled store closure (at Signal No. 3+, LGU evacuation order, or facility safety failure — closure decision per W2510 / VS-69) — complete all customer transactions, secure cash in safe, power down non-essential equipment, deploy window protection, activate security alarm, send staff home with safety advisory | Store Manager | COO | 2 hours |
| 4 | DC emergency protocol — secure all inventory above flood level, activate backup power, secure dock doors, protect hazardous materials, maintain skeleton crew if safe to do so | DC Manager | VP Supply Chain | 4 hours |
| 5 | HQ crisis team activation — establish situation room, activate communication tree (W855), coordinate with all affected stores/DCs, liaise with LGU emergency management | COO | CEO | Continuous |
| 6 | Post-disaster rapid damage assessment (within 24 hours of all-clear) — Store Manager or designated key holder conducts site visit, photographs damage, assesses structural integrity, inventory loss, and equipment condition | Store Manager / LP Officer | COO | 2–4 hours per store |
| 7 | Submit damage report and insurance claim trigger — compile damage assessment, inventory loss estimate, and business interruption impact; notify Insurance Coordinator to initiate claim (per W876) | BC Manager | CFO | 4 hours |
| 8 | Execute store reopening (see W850) — prioritize based on structural safety, inventory availability, staff safety, and community need (BuildRight often serves as recovery supply hub) | COO | CEO | 1–3 days per store |

### System Touchpoints
- **PAGASA Integration** — automated weather alert ingestion and store risk mapping
- **ERP Communication Module** — mass notification, store status tracking, two-way check-in
- **LP/Security System** — alarm monitoring during closure, remote CCTV access
- **Insurance Module** — claim initiation, damage documentation, loss estimation
- **HR System** — employee safety check-in, staff location tracking
- **Finance Module** — business interruption loss calculation, emergency expense tracking
- **Custody & precedence** (per the [Event Custody & Precedence Register](../event-custody-and-precedence-register.md), E-01/E-02/E-08): this protocol owns BCP activation, monitoring, and the CMT (Step 5); Signal 1–2 site preparation executes via the VS-69 W2503 store checklist (with VS-24 W1386 governing HSE preparedness activation), the closure *decision* always follows VS-69 W2510 (this Step 3 executes under its authority), and rapid post-event triage belongs to VS-69 W2518

### Pain Points / Risks
- PAGASA forecasts can change rapidly; premature closure loses sales, delayed closure endangers staff
- Staff may be unable to report for post-disaster assessment due to personal property damage or flooding
- Looters target closed retail stores during disasters — security patrols may be needed
- Structural damage assessment requires licensed engineer; availability is limited after major disasters
- Communication infrastructure (cell towers) may be down — satellite phone backup is essential but expensive
- Supply chain for recovery materials (lumber, roofing, hardware) spikes immediately after disaster — pricing and allocation challenges


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-track of "tracks PAGASA bulletins every 3 hours" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-capture of "Issue pre-emptive store advisory" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-send of "send staff home with safety advisory" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-320 (ensure controlled execution — Crisis Response & Recovery (PA-26.2).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication
- BC Manager: full-time during activation (may last 3–7 days)
- COO: 50% of time during activation
- Store Managers: full-time during closure and reopening
- External: structural engineer on retainer for post-disaster assessment

### Time Estimate
- Per activation: ~40–60 person-hours per affected store over 3–7 days
- **Annual estimate: 6 activations × 50 stores avg × 50 hours = 15,000 person-hours/year**

## W849. IT Disaster Recovery Site Activation & Failover Execution

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Primary data center outage (Davao HQ); or pre-emptive failover before major disaster (Signal No. 3+ in Davao) |
| **Frequency** | Target: never (preventive); actual: estimated 0–2 real activations per year |
| **Volume** | 1 failover event affects entire chain (all 200 stores, 4 DCs, ecommerce, all systems) |
| **Owner** | CIO |
| **Participants** | IT Infrastructure Team, Database Administrator, Network Engineer, Application Support, BC Manager, COO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Declare disaster — CIO confirms primary site failure or pre-emptive trigger; obtain CEO/COO authorization for DR activation | CIO | CEO | 15 min |
| 2 | Activate DR communication — notify all IT staff, Store Managers (via backup communication channel), and BC Manager; switch to satellite/backup network if primary network is affected | CIO | COO | 30 min |
| 3 | Execute failover sequence (automated runbook) — (a) DNS switchover to DR site (Clark or Cebu), (b) promote DR database replicas to primary, (c) activate application servers at DR site, (d) redirect POS terminal connections, (e) re-establish ecommerce endpoint | IT Infrastructure Team | CIO | 30–60 min (target RTO: 1 hour) |
| 4 | Validate system health — run automated smoke tests for all critical systems (POS, WMS, ecommerce, finance, HR); verify data integrity against last replication checkpoint (target RPO: 15 minutes) | Database Administrator | CIO | 30 min |
| 5 | Notify business users — all-clear communication to Store Managers that systems are available; provide reconnection instructions for POS terminals | CIO | COO | 15 min |
| 6 | Monitor DR site performance — continuous monitoring for 72 hours; watch for capacity constraints, latency issues, and data consistency anomalies | IT Infrastructure Team | CIO | Continuous (72 hours) |
| 7 | Plan and execute failback — once primary site is restored, schedule planned failback during low-traffic window; replicate DR data back to primary; validate and switch | IT Infrastructure Team | CIO | 4–8 hours (planned weekend) |

### System Touchpoints
- **DR Site Infrastructure** — standby servers, replicated storage, network failover equipment
- **DNS/CDN** — traffic redirection, geographic failover rules
- **Database Replication** — synchronous (critical) and asynchronous (non-critical) replication
- **POS System** — automatic reconnection to DR endpoint
- **Ecommerce Platform** — CDN-based failover, session persistence
- **Monitoring Platform** — system health dashboard, automated alerting

### Pain Points / Risks
- RPO of 15 minutes means up to 15 minutes of transaction data may be lost; manual reconciliation required for affected transactions
- POS terminal reconnection may require manual intervention at older stores with legacy network equipment
- DR site capacity may not handle full peak load — priority tier system needed (POS > ecommerce > WMS > analytics)
- Failback is riskier than failover — must be carefully planned and tested
- Annual DR testing cost: ~PHP 2–3 million (weekend testing window, external consultant, staff overtime)


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-notify of "notify all IT staff, Store Managers" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "Validate system health — run automated smoke tests for" (replaces manual Step 4).
- System auto-monitor of "Monitor DR site performance — continuous monitoring for 72 hours" (replaces manual Step 6).

### Controls
- CTL-320 (ensure controlled execution — Crisis Response & Recovery (PA-26.2).)
- operational: priority-tier DR peak-load allocation (POS > ecommerce > WMS > analytics); operational: manual POS reconnection runbook for affected transactions at legacy stores

### Staffing Implication
- IT Infrastructure Team: 5 FTE during activation (24-hour rotation)
- Database Administrator: 2 FTE during activation
- 1 DR Site Manager (Cebu or Clark)
- External DR consultant: annual testing engagement

### Time Estimate
- Failover execution: 30–60 min + 30 min validation = 1–1.5 hours (within the 1-hour RTO target)
- Validation: 30 min
- Monitoring: 72 hours (continuous, 5-person rotation)
- Failback: 4–8 hours (planned)
- **Per activation: ~200 person-hours over 72 hours**

## W850. Store Emergency Closure & Reopening Procedure

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Natural disaster (W848), civil disturbance, power outage > 4 hours, structural concern, or government-mandated closure |
| **Frequency** | Estimated 5–10 closures per store per year (most are weather-related half-day closures) |
| **Volume** | ~1,000–2,000 closure events per year chain-wide |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Regional Manager, LP Officer, IT Helpdesk, HR, BC Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Receive and validate closure authorization — Regional Manager issues the closure directive per the W2510 decision matrix (VS-69; VP Store Operations approves multi-store events; COO issues chain-wide directives); Store Manager confirms with regional office; log closure reason and expected duration | Store Manager | Regional Manager | 15 min |
| 2 | Execute in-store closure procedure — (a) announce last call to customers, (b) complete all pending POS transactions, (c) reconcile cash drawers and secure in safe, (d) power down non-essential systems per shutdown checklist, (e) secure all entrances, (f) activate security alarm, (g) verify all staff have safe transport home | Store Manager | Regional Manager | 1–2 hours |
| 3 | Submit closure report to regional office — store ID, closure time, reason, estimated reopening, staff headcount confirmed safe, cash secure status, and any known damage | Store Manager | Regional Manager | 15 min |
| 4 | During closure: maintain remote monitoring — CCTV remote access, alarm system monitoring, LP coordination with security agency for physical patrol if warranted | LP Officer | LP Manager (Regional) | Continuous |
| 5 | Reopening assessment — Store Manager or designated key holder conducts pre-reopening inspection: structural safety, electrical safety, water damage, inventory condition, POS/system functionality, staff availability | Store Manager | Regional Manager | 2–4 hours |
| 6 | Execute reopening — (a) restore systems (POS startup, network verification), (b) cash drawer opening and float distribution, (c) brief staff on any modified procedures, (d) restock damaged/displaced merchandise, (e) reopen to customers | Store Manager | Regional Manager | 2–3 hours |
| 7 | Submit reopening report — time opened, damage summary (if any), sales impact estimate, and any follow-up actions required | Store Manager | Regional Manager | 15 min |

### System Touchpoints
- **ERP Store Operations Module** — closure/reopening status tracking, notification workflow
- **POS System** — shutdown/restart procedure, transaction finalization
- **Security/Alarm System** — remote monitoring, alarm activation/deactivation
- **CCTV System** — remote access for closure monitoring
- **Communication Platform** — staff notification, regional reporting
- **HR System** — staff safety check-in, absence logging
- **Custody & precedence** (per the [Event Custody & Precedence Register](../event-custody-and-precedence-register.md), E-02): the closure *decision* always follows VS-69 W2510 (Regional Manager decides; VP Store Operations approves multi-store events); this workflow governs the closure/reopening *mechanics* and reporting. Frequency reconciliation: the 5–10 closures/store/yr here counts all-cause emergency closures; the typhoon-signal subset (2–5/store/yr) is W2510's canon; reopening chains to VS-69 W2518 (rapid triage) and W1552 (phased governance)

### Pain Points / Risks
- Closure decision timing is critical — too early loses sales, too late endangers staff
- Staff may be personally affected by same disaster; availability for reopening is unpredictable
- Power restoration timeline from utility company is uncertain — generator fuel must be conserved
- Inventory spoilage (garden center plants, mixed paint) increases with closure duration
- Customer dissatisfaction from unexpected closure; proactive communication is essential but difficult when telecom is affected
- Insurance claim documentation must begin during closure — competing priorities for Store Manager


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "log closure reason and expected duration" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-validate of "Execute in-store closure procedure —" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-count of "headcount confirmed safe, cash secure status, and any known damage" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-320 (ensure controlled execution — Crisis Response & Recovery (PA-26.2).)
- operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Staffing Implication
- Store Manager: full-time during closure and reopening
- Regional Manager: oversees 30–40 store closures during regional events
- LP Officer: remote monitoring during closure

### Time Estimate
- Planned closure: 1–2 hours + reopening: 2–3 hours = ~4–5 hours per event
- **Annual estimate: 1,500 events × 5 hours = 7,500 person-hours/year**

## W851. Critical System Recovery & Service Restoration

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | System outage affecting critical business process — POS system down > 15 min, WMS unavailable > 30 min, ecommerce platform down > 30 min, or ERP core unavailable > 1 hour |
| **Frequency** | Estimated 2–4 critical outages per month across the chain |
| **Volume** | ~25–50 critical system recovery events per year; most resolved within SLA |
| **Owner** | CIO / IT Operations Manager |
| **Participants** | IT Infrastructure Team, Application Support, Database Administrator, Store Manager (if POS-related), Business Process Owner |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Incident detection and classification — automated monitoring alert or user report; classify severity: P1 (chain-wide outage), P2 (regional/multi-store), P3 (single store/system); notify appropriate response team | IT Operations | CIO | 5 min |
| 2 | Activate incident response team — for P1: full infrastructure team + CIO; for P2: on-call engineer + application support; for P3: helpdesk + store IT contact | IT Operations | CIO | 10 min |
| 3 | Execute recovery procedure from runbook — follow documented recovery steps for affected system (server restart, failover to standby, database recovery, network rerouting); document actions taken in incident log | IT Infrastructure Team | CIO | 15–60 min (target by severity) |
| 4 | Validate recovery — confirm system functionality via automated smoke test and user acceptance; verify data integrity (no transaction loss); check dependent systems for cascading impact | Application Support | CIO | 15–30 min |
| 5 | Communicate status updates — provide regular updates to affected business users (every 15 min for P1, every 30 min for P2, hourly for P3); issue all-clear when resolved | IT Operations | CIO | 5 min per update |
| 6 | Post-incident analysis — for P1 and P2: conduct root cause analysis within 48 hours; document findings, corrective actions, and preventive measures; update runbook if needed | IT Infrastructure Team | CIO | 2–4 hours |
| 7 | Track SLA compliance — log incident start time, resolution time, and SLA target; report to CIO monthly; escalate recurring issues for infrastructure investment | IT Operations | CIO | 30 min per incident |

### System Touchpoints
- **IT Monitoring Platform** — automated alerting, system health dashboard, SLA tracking
- **ITSM (IT Service Management)** — incident logging, runbook access, escalation workflow
- **ERP System** — recovery target, data integrity validation
- **POS System** — offline mode fallback, transaction queue, resynchronization
- **Communication Platform** — status update distribution, stakeholder notification

### Pain Points / Risks
- POS offline mode can store transactions locally for up to 4 hours but requires resync — risk of data loss if power failure corrupts local storage
- WMS downtime at DCs halts all outbound shipments — cascading delay to store replenishment
- Ecommerce downtime directly impacts revenue and customer satisfaction; social media amplifies outage complaints
- Root cause analysis for intermittent issues is time-consuming and may not yield definitive findings
- Aging infrastructure at older stores increases failure rate — modernization budget constraints


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-notify of "notify appropriate response team" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System workflow notification of "Activate incident response team" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "follow documented recovery steps for affected system" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-320 (ensure controlled execution — Crisis Response & Recovery (PA-26.2).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication
- IT Operations: 24/7 on-call rotation (3 shifts × 2 engineers = 6 FTE)
- Application Support: 2 FTE during business hours, 1 on-call after hours
- CIO: escalation point for P1/P2 incidents

### Time Estimate
- P1 incident: 8–16 person-hours over 2–4 hours
- P2 incident: 4–8 person-hours over 1–2 hours
- P3 incident: 2–4 person-hours over 30–60 min
- **Annual estimate: 5 P1 × 12 hours + 15 P2 × 6 hours + 30 P3 × 3 hours = 240 person-hours/year (60 + 90 + 90)**

## W852. Supply Chain Disruption Business Impact Assessment & Recovery

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Major supply chain disruption — port closure (Manila South Harbor, Cebu, Davao), vendor manufacturing halt, container shipping disruption, or national transportation strike |
| **Frequency** | Estimated 2–4 significant disruptions per year |
| **Volume** | Each disruption may affect 10–50% of inbound supply depending on scope |
| **Owner** | VP Supply Chain |
| **Participants** | Procurement Manager, Warehouse Manager, Merchandising Manager, CFO, BC Manager, VP Store Operations |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Assess disruption scope — identify affected POs (in-transit, pending shipment, at port), affected vendors, affected categories, estimated duration of disruption, and inventory coverage in days-of-supply | Supply Chain Planner | VP Supply Chain | 4 hours |
| 2 | Quantify business impact — estimate lost sales by category, store impact (which locations most affected), customer satisfaction impact, and financial exposure | Supply Chain Analyst | CFO | 4 hours |
| 3 | Activate alternative sourcing — identify backup vendors, authorized substitute SKUs, alternative ports of entry, and expedited air freight options for critical items | Procurement Manager | VP Supply Chain | 8–16 hours (over 2–3 days) |
| 4 | Implement allocation strategy — if supply is constrained, allocate available inventory to stores based on sales velocity, strategic importance, and customer impact | Supply Chain Planner | VP Merchandising | 4 hours |
| 5 | Communicate to Store Managers — provide impacted category list, expected recovery timeline, substitute SKU guidance, and customer messaging for out-of-stock items | VP Supply Chain | COO | 2 hours |
| 6 | Monitor recovery — track alternative vendor performance, port reopening status, container movement, and inventory replenishment rate against normal levels | Supply Chain Planner | VP Supply Chain | Daily until resolved (1–4 weeks) |
| 7 | Post-disruption review — document root cause, response effectiveness, inventory impact, financial cost, and recommendations for supply chain resilience improvement | VP Supply Chain | COO | 4 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- **Supply Chain Planning Module** — disruption scenario modeling, inventory coverage analysis
- **Procurement Module** — alternative vendor identification, expedited PO creation
- **WMS** — inventory allocation, substitute SKU mapping
- **BI Dashboard** — supply chain disruption dashboard, financial impact tracker
- **Communication Platform** — store notification, vendor communication

### Pain Points / Risks
- Alternative vendors may not meet quality or price standards — margin impact
- Air freight for bulky construction materials is prohibitively expensive; may not be viable
- Port closures in Manila affect 60% of import volume — limited alternative routing
- Customer switching to competitors during stock-out — brand loyalty risk
- Vendor force majeure claims may eliminate contractual remedies


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-identify of "identify affected POs" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System rule-based authorization of "authorized substitute SKUs, alternative ports of entry, and expedited air…" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-allocate of "allocate available inventory to stores based on sales velocity, strategic…" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-320 (ensure controlled execution — Crisis Response & Recovery (PA-26.2).)
- operational: VP Supply Chain review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- VP Supply Chain: full-time during disruption
- Procurement Team: all hands on deck for alternative sourcing
- Supply Chain Planners: daily monitoring during recovery

### Time Estimate
- Per disruption: 40–80 person-hours over 1–4 weeks
- **Annual estimate: 3 disruptions × 60 hours = 180 person-hours/year**

## W854. Pandemic/Epidemic Business Continuity Activation & Operations

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | WHO declares pandemic with Philippine impact; DOH declares epidemic alert; or company medical advisor recommends activation |
| **Frequency** | Rare (COVID-19-level events: estimated 1–2 per decade); preparedness maintained continuously |
| **Volume** | 1 activation affects all 200 stores, 4 DCs, and HQ; duration may be 3–18 months |
| **Owner** | CEO / COO |
| **Participants** | Executive Committee, CHRO, BC Manager, CIO, VP Supply Chain, Legal Counsel, External Medical Advisor |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Activate pandemic response plan — CEO convenes crisis management team; declare pandemic operating mode; establish daily situation briefings and weekly strategy reviews | CEO | Board of Directors | 4 hours |
| 2 | Implement workplace safety protocols — mandatory masking, social distancing (store capacity limits), temperature screening, sanitation stations, contact tracing, and isolation rooms at each location | CHRO | COO | 1 week deployment |
| 3 | Activate remote work for HQ/support functions — deploy VPN and collaboration tools for all non-store employees; maintain skeleton crew at HQ for essential on-site functions | CIO | COO | 1 week |
| 4 | Adjust store operations — reduced operating hours, BOPIS/curbside pickup emphasis, contactless payment promotion, queue management, and vulnerable population shopping hours | COO | CEO | Immediate |
| 5 | Manage workforce availability — track employee health status (anonymized), activate cross-training for critical roles, hire temporary staff for increased sanitation, and manage leave policies (quarantine, isolation, caregiving) | CHRO | COO | Continuous |
| 6 | Activate supply chain pandemic protocols — prioritize essential categories (sanitizers, PPE, building materials for home improvement), implement purchase limits for high-demand items, coordinate with vendors for supply continuity | VP Supply Chain | CEO | 1 week |
| 7 | Financial impact management — daily cash flow monitoring, expense reduction activation, government subsidy application (DOLE, SSS), rent renegotiation with landlords, and investor communication | CFO | CEO | Continuous |
| 8 | Deactivation and recovery — transition to post-pandemic operations as DOH guidance permits; phased return to normal operations; employee wellness support program; capture lessons learned | COO | CEO | 1–3 months |

### System Touchpoints
- **HR System** — employee health tracking, contact tracing, leave management, remote work tracking
- **Communication Platform** — daily briefings, employee notifications, customer messaging
- **IT Infrastructure** — VPN capacity scaling, collaboration tools (Zoom, Teams), remote desktop
- **POS System** — contactless payment promotion, purchase limit enforcement, capacity counter
- **Supply Chain Module** — essential category prioritization, purchase limit rules
- **Finance Module** — daily cash flow dashboard, expense tracking, subsidy application tracking

### Pain Points / Risks
- Store employees cannot work remotely — frontline exposure risk is unavoidable
- Pandemic duration is unpredictable — business model sustainability beyond 6 months of reduced operations is uncertain
- Government regulations (IATF resolutions) change frequently — compliance requires rapid policy updates
- Supply chain for pandemic-essential items (masks, sanitizers) may face government price controls and allocation mandates
- Employee mental health and morale deterioration during prolonged crisis
- Customer behavior shift to ecommerce may be permanent — requires accelerated digital investment


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "Activate pandemic response plan — CEO convenes crisis management" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System workflow notification of "contact tracing, and isolation rooms at each location" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-track of "track employee health" (replaces manual Step 5).

### Controls
- CTL-320 (ensure controlled execution — Crisis Response & Recovery (PA-26.2).)
- operational: CEO / COO review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- Crisis Management Team: full-time during initial activation, then weekly cadence
- CHRO: 50% of time on pandemic workforce management
- CIO: 30% of time on remote work infrastructure
- External medical advisor: retained for pandemic duration

### Time Estimate
- Initial activation: 200 person-hours over first 2 weeks
- Ongoing management: 40 person-hours/week for crisis team
- **Per pandemic year: ~2,200 person-hours above normal operations**

## W855. Communication Tree Activation & Crisis Communication Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Any event requiring coordinated communication to multiple stakeholder groups simultaneously — natural disaster, system outage, product recall, workplace safety incident, or PR crisis |
| **Frequency** | 3–5 activations per year (major); 10–15 minor activations |
| **Volume** | Each major activation reaches 6,932 employees, 200 Store Managers, 4 DC Managers, and potentially 600,000 loyalty customers |
| **Owner** | COO / Corporate Communications Manager |
| **Participants** | CEO, CHRO, CIO, Legal Counsel, BC Manager, Store Managers, External PR Agency (for public-facing crises) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Assess communication scope — determine stakeholder groups affected (employees, customers, vendors, media, regulators), message urgency (immediate/same day/next day), and communication channel (SMS blast, email, intranet, social media, press release) | Corporate Communications | COO | 30 min |
| 2 | Draft and approve message — prepare factual, non-speculative message appropriate for each audience; Legal Counsel reviews for liability; CEO approves external communications | Corporate Communications | CEO | 30–60 min |
| 3 | Activate internal communication tree — (a) SMS blast to all Store Managers (target: within 15 min), (b) email to all employees, (c) intranet posting, (d) department head cascade (phone tree for P1 events), (e) employee hotline activation | Corporate Communications | COO | 15–30 min |
| 4 | Activate external communication — (a) customer notification (SMS/email for affected loyalty members), (b) social media post (advisory or statement), (c) vendor notification for supply chain events, (d) press statement for media events, (e) regulatory notification (BIR, DOLE, LGU) as required | Corporate Communications | CEO | 30–60 min |
| 5 | Establish ongoing communication cadence — regular status updates at defined intervals (hourly for P1, every 4 hours for P2, daily for P3) until event resolved | Corporate Communications | COO | Continuous |
| 6 | Monitor social media and feedback — track customer/employee reactions on social media, respond to inquiries, correct misinformation, and escalate negative sentiment trends | Corporate Communications | COO | Continuous during event |
| 7 | Issue closure communication — all-clear notification, summary of event and resolution, thank stakeholders for patience, and provide next steps or resources | Corporate Communications | CEO | 30 min |

### System Touchpoints
- **SMS Blast Platform** — mass notification to employees and customers
- **ERP Communication Module** — internal messaging, Store Manager broadcast
- **HR System** — employee contact database, emergency contact information
- **CRM/Loyalty System** — customer notification, segment-specific messaging
- **Social Media Management Platform** — scheduled posts, sentiment monitoring, response management
- **Press/Media Contact Database** — media distribution list management

### Pain Points / Risks
- SMS blast system reliability during disasters — telecom congestion may delay delivery
- Premature or inaccurate communication can create liability (Legal review is essential but adds delay)
- Social media amplifies negative incidents — response time window is < 1 hour for viral content
- Employee personal social media posts about company incidents can complicate official messaging
- Regulatory notification requirements (BIR, DOLE, DENR) have specific timeframes and formats — non-compliance risk
- Language considerations — communications may need to be in English, Filipino, and regional languages (Bisaya for Mindanao)


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "reviews for liability" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-post of "Activate internal communication tree —" (replaces manual Step 3).

## W1213. Store-Level Flood Response, Water Damage Mitigation & Post-Flood Recovery Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | PAGASA issues flood warning for store area; or store staff observe rising water levels near store perimeter; or local government issues evacuation advisory |
| **Frequency** | 2–4 flood events per year affecting a subset of stores (primarily during June–November typhoon season); individual store may flood once every 2–3 years |
| **Volume** | ~10–30 stores affected per major flood event (out of 200 total) |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, all store staff, Regional Manager, LP Officer, Facilities Maintenance, Insurance Claims Coordinator |

### Background

The Philippines experiences an average of 20 typhoons per year, with associated flooding affecting low-lying areas across all regions where BuildRight Depot operates. Stores in flood-prone locations (near rivers, coastal areas, poorly drained urban zones) face direct flood risk to merchandise, equipment, and infrastructure. BuildRight's merchandise is particularly vulnerable: cement and bagged materials are water-destroyed, lumber warps and rots, electrical items short-circuit, paint degrades, and tiles crack from water pressure. A single flooding event at one store can cause PHP 2–5M in inventory damage plus PHP 1–3M in facility damage. This workflow covers the immediate response during flooding, water damage mitigation, and the recovery process to reopen the store. It complements W848 (typhoon emergency protocol) by focusing specifically on flood/water damage management.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pre-flood alert**: PAGASA warning or Regional Manager alert received; Store Manager activates flood preparedness checklist: (a) move cement, adhesives, and water-soluble items from floor level to upper shelving or pallets (minimum 60cm elevation), (b) deploy flood barriers/sandbags at entrance and receiving dock, (c) cover lumber yard merchandise with tarpaulins, (d) move electrical items, power tools, and appliances from lowest shelves, (e) raise computers, POS terminals, and servers above flood level, (f) secure cash office — move cash, documents, and safe above flood line | Store Manager / All Staff | Regional Manager | 2–3 hours |
| 2 | **During flooding**: if water enters store: (a) Store Manager orders evacuation of staff and customers to safe area, (b) LP Officer activates CCTV recording to capture flood damage for insurance documentation, (c) shut off electrical main breaker to prevent electrocution, (d) if time permits: staff moves highest-value items (power tools, appliances) to upper shelves or mezzanine, (e) Store Manager photographs water line height and affected areas for documentation | Store Manager / All Staff | Regional Manager | Event duration |
| 3 | **Post-flood assessment** (once water recedes and area declared safe): Store Manager and LP Officer conduct damage assessment: (a) photograph all damaged merchandise, equipment, and facility areas with wide-angle and close-up shots, (b) estimate water line height (highest point of water contact), (c) categorize damage: (i) destroyed (cement, adhesives, cardboard packaging, electrical items submerged) — write-off per W92, (ii) damaged but salvageable (lumber with surface water, items in wet packaging) — assess per W91, (iii) unaffected (items above water line), (d) system generates damage report with estimated value | Store Manager / LP Officer | Regional Manager | 4–8 hours |
| 4 | **Emergency merchandise disposition**: (a) destroyed items: system marks as damaged, creates write-off request per W92 (requires Regional Manager approval for amounts > PHP 100K, COO for > PHP 500K); (b) water-damaged items with salvage value: Store Manager coordinates with Merchandising for emergency markdown sale per W93 ("flood sale" — sell water-damaged items at 30–50% discount to recover partial value); (c) contaminated items (paint, chemicals mixed with floodwater): denature and dispose per DENR hazmat procedures per W24.3 | Store Manager / Merchandising | COO | 1–3 days |
| 5 | **Facility restoration**: (a) Store Manager contacts Facilities Maintenance for emergency response: water extraction, dehumidification, mold prevention treatment, (b) electrical system inspection by licensed electrician before power restoration, (c) sanitize all affected floor and shelf surfaces, (d) replace damaged shelving, signage, and fixtures per W1025, (e) POS and IT equipment: IT Helpdesk (W48) inspects and replaces water-damaged terminals and network equipment | Facilities Maintenance / IT Helpdesk | COO | 3–7 days |
| 6 | **Insurance claim initiation**: (a) Store Manager submits flood damage report and photographs to Insurance Claims Coordinator, (b) Coordinator files claim with property insurance provider per W857 within 72 hours, (c) schedule adjuster visit for damage verification, (d) Coordinator tracks claim through settlement per W857/W858 | Insurance Claims Coordinator | CFO | 2 hours initial; ongoing |
| 7 | **Replenishment and reopening**: (a) Supply Planning initiates emergency replenishment for destroyed items, (b) Merchandising adjusts allocation to prioritize flood-affected store, (c) Store Manager conducts pre-opening walkthrough to verify safety and readiness, (d) Regional Manager authorizes store reopening, (e) Marketing coordinates community notification of store reopening | Supply Planning / Store Manager | COO | 3–7 days |
| 8 | **Post-recovery review**: Store Manager documents lessons learned: (a) flood preparedness effectiveness — were sandbags adequate? Were vulnerable items moved in time?, (b) response time — how quickly was staff mobilized?, (c) damage reduction actions that worked, (d) recommendations for facility improvements (drainage, elevated storage, flood barriers); feeds into W856 post-incident review and informs store facility capital plan per VS-20 (W21 capex requests) | Store Manager | Regional Manager | 2 hours |

## W1221. Store-Level Typhoon Preparation, Merchandise Protection & Quick Response Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | PAGASA raises Public Storm Warning Signal (PSWS) #1 or higher for province where BuildRight store is located; or 48 hours before projected typhoon landfall in any operating region |
| **Frequency** | ~8–12 typhoon warning activations per year affecting various regions; each store activates 2–4 times per year on average |
| **Volume** | 50–150 stores may be simultaneously in a typhoon's path per major event |
| **Owner** | Regional Manager |
| **Participants** | Regional Manager, Store Manager, all store staff, COO, LP Officer, Facilities Maintenance, VP Supply Chain |

### Background

The Philippines averages 20 tropical cyclones per year, with 8–9 making landfall. BuildRight Depot's 200 stores span all major regions — from Northern Luzon to Mindanao — meaning virtually the entire store network is exposed to typhoon risk. Unlike floods (localized), typhoons affect entire regions simultaneously, potentially disrupting 50–150 stores at once. The primary risks are: (a) wind damage to store structures (roof, signage, outdoor lumber yard), (b) water ingress through compromised roofs and windows, (c) power outage affecting refrigeration (paint), POS, and security systems, (d) supply chain disruption from road closures and port shutdowns, (e) staff safety during commuting and shelter-in-place. This workflow governs the pre-typhoon preparation, during-typhoon shelter protocol, and post-typhoon rapid assessment and reopening. It complements W848 (natural disaster emergency protocol — corporate-level activation) by focusing on store-level operational execution.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| **Participants** | Regional Manager, Store Manager, Maintenance/Utility, COO, Facilities Manager, Safety Officer |

### Background

The Philippines experiences ~20 typhoons per year, with Signal No. 2+ events requiring store closures. After a typhoon passes, the critical operational challenge is rapidly assessing which stores can safely reopen and in what sequence. Premature reopening risks employee/customer safety; delayed reopening loses revenue and community goodwill (BuildRight is often a source of emergency building materials post-disaster).
