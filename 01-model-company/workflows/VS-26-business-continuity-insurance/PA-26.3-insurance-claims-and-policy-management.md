## W857. Store & DC Property Insurance Claim Filing & Documentation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Property damage event at any store, DC, or HQ — fire, break-in, water damage, equipment failure, or vandalism |
| **Frequency** | Estimated 30–50 property claims per year across 205 locations |
| **Volume** | Average claim value: PHP 50,000–500,000 (minor to moderate damage); major claims (typhoon, fire): PHP 1–10 million |
| **Owner** | Insurance Coordinator / Finance Controller |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, LP Officer, Finance Controller, Insurance Broker, External Adjuster, Legal Counsel |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Incident notification — Store Manager reports property damage to Insurance Coordinator within 24 hours; include initial damage description, estimated value, photos, and whether emergency repairs needed | Store Manager | Insurance Coordinator | 1 hour |
| 2 | Emergency mitigation authorization — for time-sensitive damage (water ingress, structural compromise), Insurance Coordinator authorizes emergency repairs up to PHP 100,000 without adjuster inspection; document with photos before and after | Insurance Coordinator | CFO | 30 min |
| 3 | Compile claim documentation — (a) detailed damage description with cause, (b) photographic evidence (before/after where available), (c) repair estimates from 2–3 contractors, (d) police report (if theft/vandalism), (e) BFP fire investigation report (if fire), (f) inventory damage assessment with replacement cost | Store Manager / LP Officer | Insurance Coordinator | 4–8 hours |
| 4 | Submit insurance claim to broker — complete insurer claim form, attach all documentation, provide loss amount breakdown (building, equipment, inventory, cleanup); submit within insurer's required timeframe (typically 30 days) | Insurance Coordinator | Finance Controller | 2 hours |
| 5 | Coordinate adjuster inspection — schedule insurance adjuster visit, ensure Store Manager availability, provide access to damaged areas and documentation | Insurance Coordinator | Insurance Broker | 2 hours (scheduling) |
| 6 | Negotiate settlement — review adjuster report, dispute undervaluation if applicable, provide supplementary evidence, and agree on settlement amount with insurer | Insurance Coordinator | Finance Controller | 4–8 hours (over 2–4 weeks) |
| 7 | Process settlement — record insurance recovery in finance module, offset against damage loss, and close claim file | Finance Controller | CFO | 2 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- **Finance Module** — claim expense recording, insurance recovery posting, GL impact
- **ERP LP Module** — incident report linking, evidence management
- **Document Management** — claim file storage, photo archive, contractor estimates
- **Inventory Module** — damaged inventory valuation, write-off processing
- **Fixed Asset Module** — building/equipment damage assessment, impairment recording

### Pain Points / Risks
- Claim documentation quality varies significantly by store — provincial stores may lack photographic evidence
- Adjuster availability after major typhoons is limited — claims may take 2–3 months for catastrophic events
- Insurer may dispute cause (wear-and-tear vs. covered peril) — Legal Counsel needed for complex claims
- Emergency repairs must be pre-authorized but delay increases secondary damage — judgment call required
- PHP 100,000 emergency threshold may be insufficient for major water damage at large stores


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-report of "reports property damage to Insurance Coordinator within 24 hours" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-document of "document with photos before and after" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-compile of "Compile claim documentation —" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-321 (ensure controlled execution — Insurance Claims & Policy Management (PA-26.3).)
- operational: Insurance Coordinator / Finance Controller review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- 1 FTE Insurance Coordinator (reports to Finance Controller)
- Store Managers: 4–8 hours per claim for documentation
- External: insurance broker + adjuster per claim

### Time Estimate
- Per claim: 15–25 person-hours over 4–8 weeks
- **Annual estimate: 40 claims × 20 hours = 800 person-hours/year**

## W858. Typhoon, Flood & Natural Disaster Damage Assessment & Insurance Claim

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Natural disaster event (typhoon, flood, earthquake, volcanic eruption) affecting BuildRight locations; triggered by W848 post-disaster rapid assessment |
| **Frequency** | 2–3 catastrophic events per year affecting multiple locations simultaneously |
| **Volume** | Each event may generate 10–50 property claims; catastrophic event (Signal No. 4+) may affect 30–80 stores |
| **Owner** | Insurance Coordinator / BC Manager |
| **Participants** | COO, CFO, Store Managers (affected), LP Officers, External Adjuster Team, Insurance Broker, Legal Counsel, Structural Engineer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Aggregate damage reports from all affected stores — compile W848 rapid assessment results into consolidated damage register; categorize by severity (minor: operational within 24h, moderate: 1–7 days, major: > 7 days or structural concern) | BC Manager | COO | 4–8 hours |
| 2 | Prioritize claim filing order — major structural damage first, then moderate inventory damage, then minor; estimate aggregate claim value and notify insurer of catastrophic event | Insurance Coordinator | CFO | 2 hours |
| 3 | Deploy damage assessment teams — for major claims: engage external structural engineer + insurance adjuster; for moderate claims: LP Officer + Store Manager conduct detailed assessment; for minor: Store Manager self-assessment with photo documentation | BC Manager | COO | 1–3 days (field deployment) |
| 4 | Compile catastrophic event claim package — consolidated claim submission to insurer with: (a) event summary (PAGASA data, affected area map), (b) location-by-location damage detail, (c) aggregate loss estimate with supporting valuation, (d) business interruption calculation (per W860), (e) emergency repair documentation | Insurance Coordinator | CFO | 8–16 hours |
| 5 | Engage loss adjuster for catastrophic event — insurer appoints catastrophic loss adjuster; coordinate multi-site inspection schedule over 2–4 weeks; provide logistics support (transport, access) | Insurance Broker | Insurance Coordinator | 2–4 weeks (elapsed) |
| 6 | Negotiate catastrophic settlement — typically involves interim payments for emergency repairs + final settlement after full assessment; may require mediation if insurer disputes scope or value | CFO | CEO | 4–8 weeks (elapsed) |
| 7 | Process recovery and close claims — record all insurance recoveries, reconcile against actual repair costs, document unrecovered losses, and update property valuations for next renewal | Finance Controller | CFO | 2 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- **Finance Module** — catastrophic loss tracking, interim recovery posting, GL impact
- **ERP LP Module** — consolidated incident register, evidence repository
- **BI Dashboard** — damage heat map, claim status tracker, financial impact summary
- **Fixed Asset Module** — property impairment recording, revaluation
- **Inventory Module** — mass inventory write-off, replacement cost calculation

### Pain Points / Risks
- Catastrophic events overwhelm insurance adjuster capacity — claim resolution may take 3–6 months
- Business interruption calculation requires detailed sales data comparison (current year vs. prior year) — complex documentation
- Aggregate claim value may exceed policy limits — underinsurance risk for catastrophic events
- Multiple simultaneous claim submissions increase documentation errors and omissions
- Provincial stores may lack contractor availability for repair estimates — delays claim filing
- Insurer may invoke policy deductibles per-location, significantly reducing net recovery


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-compile of "compile W848 rapid assessment results into consolidated damage register" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-notify of "notify insurer of catastrophic event" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "Deploy damage assessment teams — for major claims" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-228 (ensure BCP readiness & insurance recovery.)
- operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Staffing Implication
- Insurance Coordinator: full-time during catastrophic event (4–8 weeks)
- CFO: 20% of time during catastrophic event settlement
- BC Manager: coordinates damage assessment teams
- External: structural engineer + loss adjuster + broker (all engaged per event)

### Time Estimate
- Per catastrophic event: 200–400 person-hours over 2–6 months
- **Annual estimate: 2.5 events × 300 hours = 750 person-hours/year**

## W859. Vehicle & Fleet Insurance Claim Processing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vehicle accident, theft, or damage involving company-owned fleet vehicles (delivery trucks, service vehicles, management vehicles) |
| **Frequency** | Estimated 20–30 vehicle claims per year for the owned fleet of ~40 delivery trucks (plus a small service/management vehicle pool) |
| **Volume** | Average claim value: PHP 50,000–300,000 (repair); total loss: PHP 500,000–3,000,000 |
| **Owner** | Fleet Manager / Insurance Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Driver, Fleet Manager, Insurance Coordinator, Finance Controller, External Repair Shop, LTO (Land Transportation Office) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Incident report — driver reports vehicle incident to Fleet Manager within 4 hours; include police report (if third-party involved), photos of damage, location, and circumstances | Driver | Fleet Manager | 1 hour |
| 2 | Vehicle assessment — Fleet Manager arranges vehicle inspection at authorized repair shop; obtain repair estimate; classify as repairable or total loss | Fleet Manager | Insurance Coordinator | 1–3 days (elapsed) |
| 3 | Submit insurance claim — complete vehicle claim form with: (a) police report or barangay blotter, (b) driver's license and vehicle registration, (c) repair estimate or total loss valuation, (d) photos, (e) third-party information (if applicable) | Insurance Coordinator | Fleet Manager | 2 hours |
| 4 | Coordinate repair authorization — insurer approves repair scope and cost; Fleet Manager authorizes repair shop to proceed; arrange replacement vehicle if needed for delivery schedule | Fleet Manager | Insurance Coordinator | 3–5 days (elapsed) |
| 5 | Third-party liability handling — if company vehicle at fault, coordinate with insurer for third-party claim settlement; if third-party at fault, pursue recovery through their insurer | Insurance Coordinator | Legal Counsel | Variable (2–12 weeks) |
| 6 | Process claim settlement — record insurance recovery, post repair expense, and close claim file; update vehicle asset record | Finance Controller | Insurance Coordinator | 2 hours |
| 7 | Driver incident review — Fleet Manager reviews incident with driver; determine preventability; if preventable, apply Fleet safety program consequence (retraining, warning, or suspension) | Fleet Manager | VP Supply Chain | 1 hour |

### System Touchpoints
- **Fleet Management Module** — vehicle record, incident log, maintenance tracking
- **Finance Module** — claim expense, insurance recovery, vehicle asset adjustment
- **Insurance Module** — claim filing, status tracking, settlement recording
- **HR System** — driver incident record, disciplinary action (if applicable)
- **WMS/Delivery Module** — delivery rescheduling during vehicle downtime

### Pain Points / Risks
- Vehicle downtime affects delivery schedule — replacement vehicle cost is often unrecoverable
- Third-party claims in Philippines are slow — court system backlog means 1–3 year resolution
- Driver may be unlicensed or under the influence — policy may not cover; pre-employment verification critical
- Provincial repair shops may not meet insurer's authorized network — requires exception approval
- Total loss settlement may be below vehicle replacement cost — gap coverage recommended


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-report of "report — driver reports vehicle incident to Fleet Manager within 4 hours" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-inspect of "inspection at authorized repair shop" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-submit of "Submit insurance claim — complete vehicle claim form" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-321 (ensure controlled execution — Insurance Claims & Policy Management (PA-26.3).)
- operational: Fleet Manager / Insurance Coordinator review and approval gate; claim register reconciled monthly

### Staffing Implication
- Fleet Manager: 2–4 hours per claim
- Insurance Coordinator: 2 hours per claim
- Finance Controller: 1 hour per claim

### Time Estimate
- Per claim: 8–15 person-hours over 2–8 weeks
- **Annual estimate: 25 claims × 12 hours = 300 person-hours/year**

## W860. Business Interruption Insurance Claim & Loss Documentation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Store or DC closure exceeding 72 consecutive hours due to insured peril; typically triggered alongside W858 (catastrophic property claim) |
| **Frequency** | Estimated 3–5 business interruption claims per year |
| **Volume** | Average claim: PHP 500,000–5,000,000; catastrophic: PHP 10–50 million (multi-store closure) |
| **Owner** | Finance Controller / Insurance Coordinator |
| **Participants** | CFO, BC Manager, Store Manager, External Accountant, Insurance Broker, Loss Adjuster |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identify BI-qualifying events — track all store/DC closures exceeding 72 hours; calculate estimated daily sales loss per location using rolling 12-month average; flag for BI claim consideration | Finance Analyst | Finance Controller | 2 hours per event |
| 2 | Compile BI documentation package — (a) daily sales comparison (closure period vs. same period prior year), (b) payroll expense during closure (continuing costs), (c) fixed costs (rent, utilities, depreciation), (d) mitigation actions taken (BOPIS, curbside pickup), (e) saved variable costs (reduced COGS, lower utility), (f) projected recovery period sales ramp-up curve | Finance Analyst | Finance Controller | 8–16 hours |
| 3 | Engage forensic accountant (for claims > PHP 5 million) — independent verification of loss calculation; adjust for trends, seasonality, and market conditions; prepare accountants' report for insurer | External Accountant | CFO | 1–2 weeks (elapsed) |
| 4 | Submit BI claim to insurer — include property claim reference (W858), detailed loss calculation, supporting financial statements, and projected recovery timeline | Insurance Coordinator | Finance Controller | 4 hours |
| 5 | Negotiate BI settlement — insurer reviews loss calculation methodology, may dispute: (a) base period selection, (b) trend adjustments, (c) saved expense calculations, (d) recovery period assumption; typically requires 2–3 rounds of negotiation | Finance Controller | CFO | 4–8 weeks (elapsed) |
| 6 | Process BI recovery — record insurance recovery as other income; reconcile against actual lost profit; close BI claim file | Finance Controller | CFO | 2 hours |
| 7 | Update BI loss history — maintain claims database for actuarial analysis at next policy renewal; identify patterns (geographic, seasonal) for risk management | Insurance Coordinator | CFO | 2 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- **Finance Module** — sales history, expense analysis, BI loss calculation
- **BI Analytics** — daily sales trend, same-store comparison, recovery curve modeling
- **ERP Communication Module** — closure duration tracking, reopening date confirmation
- **Document Management** — claim file archive, forensic accountant report
- **Insurance Module** — claim tracking, settlement recording

### Pain Points / Risks
- BI claims are the most disputed type — insurers challenge loss calculation methodology extensively
- Saved expense calculation is inherently judgmental — requires forensic accountant for larger claims
- Recovery period assumption is subjective — insurer assumes faster recovery than business projects
- BI policy waiting period (typically 72 hours) means first 3 days are uninsured
- Claims > PHP 10 million require CFO and potentially Board involvement in settlement negotiation
- Policy may have aggregate annual limit — multiple BI claims may exhaust coverage


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-calculate of "calculate estimated daily sales loss per location using rolling 12-month average" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-document of "documentation package —" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-countof "Engage forensic accountant (for claims > PHP 5" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-228 (ensure BCP readiness & insurance recovery.)
- operational: Finance Controller / Insurance Coordinator review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Staffing Implication
- Finance Controller: 30% of time during active BI claim
- Finance Analyst: dedicated to BI calculation for duration
- External forensic accountant: engaged per claim (PHP 200,000–500,000 fee)

### Time Estimate
- Per claim: 40–80 person-hours over 2–4 months
- **Annual estimate: 4 claims × 60 hours = 240 person-hours/year**

## W861. Employee Injury Insurance Claim Coordination & SSS/ECC Filing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Workplace injury or occupational illness affecting any BuildRight employee; triggered by HSE incident report |
| **Frequency** | Estimated 100–150 reportable injuries per year across 6,911 employees |
| **Volume** | ~30–50 claims filed with SSS/ECC per year (lost-time injuries); minor injuries treated on-site without claim |
| **Owner** | HR Manager / Insurance Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Injured Employee, Store Manager, HR Manager, Insurance Coordinator, Company Nurse, SSS Representative, DOLE (for serious incidents) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Injury incident documentation — Store Manager completes incident report within 24 hours: nature of injury, body part, cause, witness statements, first aid administered, and medical referral if needed | Store Manager | HR Manager | 1 hour |
| 2 | Determine reportability — HR Manager classifies as: (a) first aid only (no claim), (b) medical treatment (clinic/hospital visit, possible claim), (c) lost-time injury (claim required), (d) serious incident (DOLE notification required within 24 hours per DO 252-25 (revised IRR of RA 11058, superseding DO 198-18)) | HR Manager | CHRO | 30 min |
| 3 | Facilitate medical treatment — arrange company-designated physician evaluation; ensure Employee's Compensation (SSS/ECC) medical requirements are met from initial visit; document all treatment and costs | Company Nurse | HR Manager | Variable (same day) |
| 4 | File SSS sickness/injury notification — submit the SSS Sickness Notification Form (SIC-01252) within the employer deadline; for EC claims, submit the SSS EC report forms (e.g., B-309 accident/sickness report) with medical certificates | HR Benefits Specialist | HR Manager | 2 hours |
| 5 | Track employee recovery and return-to-work — monitor medical status, coordinate with HSE (W805) for return-to-work assessment, arrange light duty if applicable, and maintain contact with employee during absence | HR Manager | Store Manager | Ongoing (1–12 weeks) |
| 6 | Process SSS/ECC benefit reimbursement — upon claim approval, verify benefit amount, coordinate with payroll for salary differential (if company pays above SSS benefit), and record in HR system | HR Benefits Specialist | Finance Controller | 2 hours |
| 7 | Claim closure and record update — close incident file, update OSH (Occupational Safety and Health) log, contribute to annual HSE statistics, and identify preventive measures | HR Manager | HSE Officer | 1 hour |

### System Touchpoints
- **HR System** — incident reporting, injury tracking, SSS/ECC claim status
- **HSE Module** — workplace injury log, OSH statistics, root cause analysis
- **Finance Module** — medical expense tracking, salary differential calculation
- **Payroll System** — SSS sickness benefit integration, salary continuation processing
- **SSS Portal** — online claim submission, status inquiry

### Pain Points / Risks
- DOLE notification within 24 hours for serious incidents is a strict requirement — non-compliance results in penalties
- Company-designated physician requirement may conflict with employee's preferred doctor
- SSS/ECC claim processing time is 2–6 months — employee may face financial hardship during wait
- Salary differential calculation (company pays 100% for first X days, then SSS benefit + company differential) is complex
- Workplace injury under-reporting by Store Managers to avoid incident rate KPI impact
- Provincial stores may lack nearby company-accredited medical facilities


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-document of "documentation — Store Manager completes incident report within 24 hours" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-report of "reportability — HR Manager classifies" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-file of "File SSS sickness/injury notification — submit the SSS Sickness Notification Form (SIC-01252)" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-321 (ensure controlled execution — Insurance Claims & Policy Management (PA-26.3).)
- operational: HR Manager / Insurance Coordinator review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- HR Benefits Specialist: 3 hours per claim
- HR Manager: 1 hour per claim
- Company Nurse: on-site at HQ, on-call for provincial stores
- Store Manager: 1 hour per incident for initial documentation

### Time Estimate
- Per claim: 8–12 person-hours over 2–6 months (including waiting time)
- **Annual estimate: 40 claims × 10 hours = 400 person-hours/year**

## W862. Insurance Policy Annual Renewal & Coverage Review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — annual renewal cycle (policies typically renewed annually, aligned to company fiscal year) |
| **Frequency** | Annual comprehensive review; quarterly interim adjustments for material changes |
| **Volume** | 1 annual renewal covering ~15–20 insurance policies across all 5 legal entities |
| **Owner** | Insurance Coordinator / CFO |
| **Participants** | CFO, COO, LP Director, Fleet Manager, CHRO, Insurance Broker, External Actuary |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Compile renewal data package — (a) prior year claims history (frequency, severity, recovery rate), (b) current asset register (buildings, equipment, vehicles), (c) payroll data (for workers' comp), (d) revenue data (for BI and liability limits), (e) any new locations or operations | Insurance Coordinator | CFO | 8 hours |
| 2 | Conduct coverage gap analysis — compare current coverage against: (a) new locations opened during year, (b) new vehicle acquisitions, (c) changes in inventory values, (d) new business activities (ecommerce warehouse, installation services), (e) updated risk assessment from BC Manager | Insurance Coordinator | CFO | 4 hours |
| 3 | Obtain competitive quotations — engage insurance broker to obtain quotes from 3–5 insurers; compare coverage terms, premiums, deductibles, and exclusions; prepare comparison matrix | Insurance Broker | CFO | 2 weeks (elapsed) |
| 4 | Evaluate and negotiate — review quotes with CFO and LP Director; negotiate terms with preferred insurer; consider higher deductibles for premium savings on low-frequency risks | Insurance Coordinator | CFO | 1 week |
| 5 | Present renewal recommendation to CFO — summarize coverage changes, premium impact (vs. prior year), claims history analysis, and recommendation for policy selection | Insurance Coordinator | CFO | 2 hours |
| 6 | Execute renewal — obtain CEO/CFO authorization, sign policy documents, process premium payment, distribute certificates of insurance to all locations | Insurance Coordinator | CFO | 4 hours |
| 7 | Update insurance register — record all policy details in insurance tracking system; set renewal reminders; distribute policy summary to all Store Managers and DC Managers | Insurance Coordinator | CFO | 2 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- **Finance Module** — premium payment processing, insurance expense tracking
- **Fixed Asset Module** — asset register for coverage verification
- **ERP LP Module** — claims history extraction
- **HR System** — payroll data for workers' comp calculation
- **Document Management** — policy document storage, certificate distribution

### Pain Points / Risks
- Philippine insurance market has limited capacity for large retail risks — few competitive options
- Premium increases of 10–20% per year are common for property insurance in typhoon-prone areas
- Underinsurance is a chronic risk — inventory values fluctuate seasonally; annual valuation may be stale
- New store openings mid-policy require endorsement — coverage gap if not reported promptly
- Flood and earthquake coverage may require separate policies with high deductibles or exclusions


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-compile of "Compile renewal data package —" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-update of "updated risk assessment from BC Manager" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-generate of "prepare comparison matrix" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-321 (ensure controlled execution — Insurance Claims & Policy Management (PA-26.3).)
- operational: Insurance Coordinator / CFO review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- Insurance Coordinator: 30 hours for annual renewal
- CFO: 8 hours for review and approval
- Insurance Broker: manages quotation process
- External Actuary: engaged every 3 years for independent coverage adequacy review

### Time Estimate
- **Annual renewal: ~60 person-hours over 4–6 weeks**

## W863. Third-Party Liability Claim & Customer Incident Insurance Response

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer or third-party injury/property damage on BuildRight premises; or claim/lawsuit received from third party |
| **Frequency** | Estimated 240–480 third-party incidents per year (20–40/month, per W1566); ~12–20 are formally notified to the insurer each year, of which ~5–10 become defended liability claims (per W1566) |
| **Volume** | Average claim value: PHP 20,000–200,000; major injury lawsuit: PHP 500,000–5,000,000 |
| **Owner** | Insurance Coordinator / Legal Counsel |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, LP Officer, Insurance Coordinator, Legal Counsel, Insurance Broker, External Lawyer (for litigation) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Incident documentation — Store Manager completes third-party incident report within 24 hours: injured party details, incident description, witness statements, photos of scene, first aid provided, and CCTV footage preservation request | Store Manager | LP Manager | 1–2 hours |
| 2 | Notify insurer of potential claim — even if formal claim not yet filed, notify insurer of incident for reservation of rights; include incident report, photos, and CCTV footage reference | Insurance Coordinator | Legal Counsel | 1 hour |
| 3 | Customer follow-up — HR or Store Manager contacts injured party within 48 hours to express concern, offer assistance, and document injury progression; do NOT admit liability or make settlement offers without Legal approval | Store Manager / HR | Legal Counsel | 1 hour |
| 4 | Legal assessment — Legal Counsel reviews incident for liability exposure; determine defense strategy; engage external lawyer if litigation anticipated | Legal Counsel | Insurance Coordinator | 2–4 hours |
| 5 | Formal claim response — if demand letter received, Legal Counsel drafts response (deny liability, offer mediation, or negotiate settlement); coordinate with insurer for settlement authority | Legal Counsel | CFO | 4–8 hours |
| 6 | Settlement or litigation — negotiate settlement within insurer's authority (typically < PHP 500,000); for larger claims, defend through litigation; keep insurer informed at every stage | Legal Counsel / External Lawyer | CFO | Variable (1–24 months) |
| 7 | Claim closure — record settlement or judgment in finance module; update premises safety measures to prevent recurrence; close incident file | Insurance Coordinator | CFO | 2 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- **ERP LP Module** — incident report, evidence management, CCTV footage preservation
- **Finance Module** — settlement payment, legal expense tracking
- **Insurance Module** — claim notification, status tracking, settlement recording
- **Legal Matter Management** — case tracking, deadline management, document repository
- **HSE Module** — premises safety improvement tracking

### Pain Points / Risks
- Social media amplification of customer injury incidents — reputation risk management required
- Store employees may inadvertently admit liability at scene — front-line training critical
- Philippine courts are sympathetic to consumer-plaintiffs — settlement is often more cost-effective than litigation
- Medical cost documentation from claimants may be inflated — requires verification
- Slipping hazard claims (wet floors during rainy season) are the most common and are largely preventable with proper mat placement and signage
- Premises liability is a Store Manager KPI — may discourage incident reporting


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-document of "documentation — Store Manager completes third-party incident report within 24…" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-file of "filed, notify insurer of incident for reservation of rights" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System workflow notification of "contacts injured party within 48 hours to express concern, offer assistance,…" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-321 (ensure controlled execution — Insurance Claims & Policy Management (PA-26.3).)
- operational: Insurance Coordinator / Legal Counsel review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- Insurance Coordinator: 4 hours per claim
- Legal Counsel: 8–20 hours per claim (more for litigation)
- External Lawyer: retained for litigation cases
- Store Manager: 2 hours per incident

### Time Estimate
- Per claim: 15–40 person-hours over 1–12 months
- **Annual estimate: 7 claims × 25 hours = 175 person-hours/year**

## W864. Insurance Claim Recovery, Settlement & Accounting Entry

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Insurance claim settlement received from insurer; or final claim denial |
| **Frequency** | 40–60 settlement events per year across all claim types |
| **Volume** | Average settlement: PHP 50,000–500,000; catastrophic: PHP 5–50 million |
| **Owner** | Finance Controller |
| **Participants** | Insurance Coordinator, CFO, External Auditor (for material settlements) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Receive settlement offer — Insurance Coordinator receives insurer's settlement offer letter; review against claim amount for acceptance or dispute | Insurance Coordinator | Finance Controller | 1 hour |
| 2 | Verify settlement calculation — Finance Controller reconciles: (a) claim amount vs. offer, (b) deductible application, (c) co-insurance adjustment, (d) underinsurance penalty (average clause), (e) salvage credit (if applicable); calculate net recovery | Finance Controller | CFO | 2–4 hours |
| 3 | Approve settlement — for amounts < PHP 500,000: Finance Controller approves; PHP 500,000–5,000,000: CFO approves; > PHP 5,000,000: CEO/CFO approves | Finance Controller | CFO / CEO | 1 hour |
| 4 | Process settlement payment — record insurance recovery in GL: debit cash/bank, credit insurance recovery (other income) or receivable; offset against original loss entry; process any deductible write-off | Finance Controller | CFO | 1 hour |
| 5 | Reconcile claim file — ensure total claim costs (repairs, replacements, lost inventory) equal insurance recovery + deductible + unrecovered balance; post unrecovered balance to appropriate expense account | Finance Controller | CFO | 1 hour |
| 6 | Close claim file — update insurance register, archive claim documentation per retention policy (10 years for BIR), and update claims history database for actuarial analysis | Insurance Coordinator | Finance Controller | 1 hour |

## W1565. Annual Insurance Portfolio Review, Coverage Gap Analysis & Market Benchmarking for Policy Renewal

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual insurance renewal cycle (typically 60–90 days before policy expiration); or significant business change (new store format, new service offering, major acquisition); or major claim event revealing coverage gap |
| **Frequency** | Annual comprehensive review; quarterly monitoring of coverage adequacy |
| **Volume** | ~15–20 insurance policies across 5 legal entities covering: property (200 stores + 4 DCs + HQ), general liability, product liability, workers' compensation, motor vehicle (fleet), business interruption, cyber liability, professional liability, directors & officers, and cargo/transit |
| **Owner** | CFO |
| **Participants** | CFO, Insurance Coordinator, COO, VP Legal & Compliance, External Insurance Broker, External Actuary, CEO (final approval) |

### Background

BuildRight Depot's insurance portfolio covers ~PHP 15–20B in insured assets (200 stores, 4 DCs, inventory, equipment) and ~PHP 62B in annual revenue across 5 legal entities. The Philippine insurance market offers policies through local insurers (often with international reinsurance backing) with premiums influenced by typhoon risk (Philippines averages 20 typhoons/year), earthquake risk (Pacific Ring of Fire), flood risk, and fire risk. Annual premium spend is estimated at PHP 150–250M across all policies. An annual portfolio review ensures: (a) coverage keeps pace with BuildRight's growth (10–15 new stores/year means new assets to insure), (b) policy terms are market-competitive ( Philippine insurance market is competitive — annual benchmarking can save 5–15% on premiums), (c) coverage gaps are identified before a claim exposes an uninsured loss, and (d) deductibles and limits are appropriately calibrated to BuildRight's risk appetite.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Current portfolio inventory**: Insurance Coordinator compiles complete insurance portfolio inventory: (a) all active policies: insurer, policy number, coverage type, insured amount, premium, deductible, policy period, key exclusions; (b) insured assets: all 200 stores (replacement cost per W1445 construction cost), 4 DCs, HQ, inventory value (WAC per model company profile Section 6.3), fleet (owned vehicles), equipment; (c) claims history: 3-year rolling claims by policy type, frequency, and severity; (d) loss ratio (claims paid ÷ premiums) by policy type — loss ratio > 60% may trigger premium increase at renewal | Insurance Coordinator | CFO | 8 hours |
| 2 | **Coverage gap analysis**: CFO and VP Legal identify coverage gaps: (a) new stores opened during the year: are they added to property policy or require separate coverage?; (b) new business activities: ecommerce delivery per W19 (are third-party delivery partners covered?), tool rental per W139 (customer liability), installation services per W795 (workmanship/warranty liability); (c) emerging risks: cyber liability (customer data breach per RA 10173), climate change (increasing typhoon intensity), supply chain disruption (business interruption coverage for import delays); (d) intercompany coverage: are all 5 legal entities properly covered? intercompany transactions per W14 may create coverage complexities; (e) regulatory requirements: DOLE-mandated workers' compensation, LGU-required liability insurance for business permits per W54 | CFO / VP Legal | CEO | 4–6 hours |
| 3 | **Market benchmarking**: External Insurance Broker conducts market benchmarking: (a) obtain competitive quotes from 3–5 insurers per policy type; (b) compare: (i) premium rates (per PHP 1,000 of coverage), (ii) deductible options, (iii) coverage limits and sub-limits, (iv) key exclusions and endorsements, (v) insurer financial strength rating (Philippine market: assess AM Best or local rating agency ratings); (c) identify potential savings: if current insurer is > 10% above market average, negotiate or switch; (d) evaluate alternative structures: (i) higher deductibles for lower premiums (self-insure smaller losses), (ii) blanket coverage vs. scheduled per-location (blanket may be more efficient for 200 locations), (iii) captive insurance feasibility (if BuildRight reaches sufficient scale) | External Insurance Broker / CFO | CEO | 20–30 hours |
| 4 | **Renewal negotiation**: CFO negotiates renewal with incumbent insurer or transitions to new insurer: (a) present loss history and risk mitigation measures (W848 BCP, W1450 earthquake protocol, W927 fire safety) to demonstrate risk quality; (b) negotiate: (i) premium rate (target: ≤ market average), (ii) deductible per risk type (balance: lower deductible = higher premium but more predictable costs), (iii) coverage enhancements (remove or reduce key exclusions), (iv) multi-year policy (2–3 year terms lock in rates); (c) VP Legal reviews policy wording for coverage triggers, conditions, and exclusions; (d) CEO approves final policy terms and premium for policies > PHP 5M annual premium | CFO / VP Legal / External Broker | CEO | 10–15 hours |
| 5 | **Policy implementation & communication**: (a) Insurance Coordinator distributes policy documents to all stakeholders: (i) Store Managers — certificate of insurance for their location (required for LGU business permits per W54), (ii) COO — claims procedure quick reference, (iii) VP Legal — full policy documentation, (iv) CHRO — workers' compensation procedure; (b) update ERP insurance module with new policy terms, premium schedules, and claim contact information; (c) calendar next renewal date with 90-day advance preparation trigger | Insurance Coordinator | CFO | 4–6 hours |

### System Touchpoints

- Insurance portfolio management module — policy inventory, asset mapping, premium tracking, claims history (W1565.1)
- Coverage gap analysis tool — asset register vs. insured values with gap identification (W1565.2)
- Market benchmarking database — premium rate comparisons by policy type and insurer (W1565.3)
- Policy document management — centralized repository with version control and renewal tracking (W1565.5)
- Integration with W862 (annual insurance renewal), W857 (claims filing), W864 (claims recovery), W54 (business permits), W14 (intercompany)

- **Philippine insurance market capacity**: for catastrophic risks (typhoon, earthquake), Philippine insurers rely heavily on international reinsurance; after major catastrophes (e.g., Typhoon Odette 2021), reinsurance capacity tightens and premiums spike; maintain multi-year policies where possible
- **Valuation accuracy**: property insurance requires accurate replacement cost valuation; BuildRight's stores are warehouse-format (relatively simple construction) but costs vary by location (Metro Manila construction costs are 30–50% higher than Mindanao); annual valuation updates per W1445 construction cost estimates are essential

## W1566. Store-Level Slip-and-Fall, Customer Injury & Third-Party Liability Incident Response & Claims Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer injury reported at store (slip-and-fall, falling merchandise, equipment injury, parking lot incident); or third-party property damage claim; or customer allegation of injury without immediate evidence |
| **Frequency** | ~20–40 customer injury incidents/month chain-wide (~0.1–0.2 incidents/store/month); ~5–10 result in liability claims/year |
| **Volume** | Majority are minor (bruises, minor cuts requiring first aid only); ~10–15% require medical treatment beyond first aid; ~2–5% result in formal liability claims or litigation |
| **Owner** | Store Manager (immediate response); VP Legal (claim management) |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Customer Service Rep, VP Legal, Insurance Coordinator, LP Officer, Maintenance/Utility, External Legal Counsel (for litigation), Customer |

### Background

With 2.8M monthly customer transactions across 200 stores and large warehouse-format layouts featuring heavy merchandise (cement bags, lumber, tiles, appliances) stacked on high shelves, customer injury incidents are inevitable. The most common types: (a) slip-and-fall on wet floors (rainy season water ingress, spillage in paint or garden section), (b) falling merchandise (improperly stacked lumber, tiles shifting on high shelves), (c) parking lot incidents (vehicle-pedestrian, loading zone accidents per W1537), (d) forklift/pallet jack incidents in customer-accessible areas, (e) cuts from sharp merchandise (sheet metal, glass, broken tiles). Philippine law provides for tort liability, and the Civil Code allows injured parties to claim damages. Without a structured incident response protocol, BuildRight faces: (a) increased liability exposure (failure to document properly weakens legal defense), (b) missed insurance claim deadlines, and (c) reputational damage from viral social media posts about the incident. This workflow establishes the end-to-end process from immediate incident response through claims resolution.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Immediate incident response** (within minutes): (a) Store Manager or nearest staff member: (i) ensure customer safety — remove hazards, stop the activity causing injury; (ii) provide first aid from store first aid kit per W140 (OHS); (iii) if injury appears serious (broken bone, head injury, heavy bleeding): call emergency medical services (911 or local hospital ambulance); (iv) do not move a customer with suspected spinal injury; (v) Store Manager notifies LP Officer for immediate scene documentation; (b) for minor injuries (bruise, small cut): offer first aid and water; ask customer if they would like to sit down; (c) for all injuries: Store Manager personally attends to the customer — shows empathy and concern; do not admit fault or liability | Store Manager / LP Officer | COO | 10–30 min |
| 2 | **Scene documentation** (within 1 hour): LP Officer conducts thorough scene documentation: (a) photographs: (i) hazard that caused injury (wet floor, fallen merchandise, damaged flooring), (ii) surrounding area (wide angle shots showing context), (iii) warning signage present or absent; (b) CCTV footage: extract and preserve footage covering the incident location from 15 minutes before to 15 minutes after the incident; (c) witness statements: name, contact number, and brief description of what they saw from 2–3 witnesses (staff and other customers); (d) incident report: date, time, location (specific area of store), customer name and contact, description of what happened, customer's account of events, staff account, first aid provided, whether emergency services called; (e) Store Manager signs incident report | LP Officer / Store Manager | COO | 30–60 min |
| 3 | **Customer follow-up** (within 24 hours): (a) Store Manager calls the injured customer: (i) express genuine concern for their well-being, (ii) ask about their medical status, (iii) offer to reimburse reasonable medical expenses (doctor visit, x-ray, medication) — this is a goodwill gesture, not an admission of liability (VP Legal approves reimbursement threshold: up to PHP 10,000 at Store Manager level, PHP 50,000 at COO level); (b) document customer's account of the incident and current condition; (c) if customer indicates intent to pursue legal claim: VP Legal notified immediately | Store Manager / VP Legal | COO | 15–30 min |
| 4 | **Insurance notification** (within 48 hours for incidents involving medical treatment): Insurance Coordinator submits preliminary incident report to general liability insurer: (a) incident details from Step 2; (b) customer information; (c) preliminary assessment of potential liability; (d) early notification preserves BuildRight's right to claim even if the incident does not develop into a formal claim; (e) insurer assigns claim adjuster for incidents involving medical treatment | Insurance Coordinator | VP Legal | 30 min/incident |
| 5 | **Formal claim management** (if customer files claim or lawsuit): VP Legal manages formal claim process: (a) engage External Legal Counsel for claims exceeding PHP 100,000 or involving litigation; (b) legal defense strategy: (i) review all documentation (incident report, CCTV, witness statements, maintenance logs for the hazard area), (ii) assess liability: was BuildRight negligent? (failure to maintain safe premises, inadequate warning signage, improper merchandise stacking), (iii) assess damages: medical expenses, lost income, pain and suffering (Philippine courts award lower damages than US courts, but awards can still reach PHP 500K–5M for serious injuries); (c) settlement vs. litigation decision: (i) for clear BuildRight liability with moderate damages: settle within insurance deductible to avoid litigation costs, (ii) for disputed liability or excessive demands: defend through litigation; (d) coordinate with insurer per W857 for claim payment | VP Legal / External Counsel / Insurance Coordinator | CEO (for claims > PHP 1M) | Variable (weeks to months) |
| 6 | **Corrective action & prevention**: After every customer injury incident: (a) Store Manager implements immediate corrective action: (i) wet floor: deploy additional mats and warning signs, fix leak source, (ii) falling merchandise: restack properly, review shelf load limits per W47 (store facility maintenance), install shelf safety clips, (iii) parking lot: repair pothole, improve lighting, re-mark pedestrian zones per W1537; (b) COO reviews corrective action adequacy; (c) LP Officer includes incident in monthly loss prevention report per W538 for chain-wide pattern analysis: (i) incident frequency by type and store, (ii) identify stores with above-average incident rates for targeted safety inspections | Store Manager / LP Officer / COO | COO | 1–2 hours/incident |

### System Touchpoints

- Incident reporting module — standardized digital incident report form with photo upload and CCTV extraction workflow (W1566.2)
- Customer injury follow-up tracker — call log, medical reimbursement tracking, and escalation alerts (W1566.3)
- Insurance claim notification system — automated preliminary report to insurer within 48-hour window (W1566.4)
- Legal case management — claim tracking, litigation timeline, and settlement documentation (W1566.5)
- Corrective action tracking — root cause analysis, remediation steps, and verification (W1566.6)
- Integration with W140 (employee safety incidents — parallel process), W853 (insurance claims), W538 (loss prevention reporting), W1537 (parking lot management), W503 (facility maintenance), W631 (first aid)

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Social media amplification**: injured customers or bystanders may film the incident and post on social media before Store Manager can respond; VP Marketing monitors social media per W1560 for viral incident posts; crisis communications per W1562 activated if necessary
- **Fraudulent claims**: some customers may stage slip-and-fall incidents to claim damages; CCTV footage review and LP Officer investigation are essential for fraud detection; maintain chain-of-custody for CCTV evidence
- **Inconsistent scene documentation**: Store Managers under stress may fail to complete thorough scene documentation; digital incident report form with mandatory fields and photo prompts mitigates
- **Medical reimbursement as de facto admission**: offering to pay medical expenses is a goodwill gesture, but the customer's lawyer may interpret it as an admission of liability; VP Legal provides clear guidelines: "BuildRight is concerned about your well-being. As a gesture of goodwill, we can assist with immediate medical expenses. This offer does not constitute an admission of liability."

- System rule-based auto-approval of "approves reimbursement threshold" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls

- operational: incident form enforces mandatory fields and photo prompts before submission; LP Officer verifies scene-documentation completeness

- LP Officer: ~30–60 min per incident for scene documentation. Absorbed.
