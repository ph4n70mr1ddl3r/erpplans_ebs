## W847. Business Continuity Plan Annual Review & Update

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — annual (Q1 each year); or ad-hoc triggered by material organizational change (new store format, DC addition, IT platform migration) |
| **Frequency** | Annual formal review; quarterly interim updates for material changes |
| **Volume** | 1 comprehensive plan revision per year covering all 205 locations (200 stores + 4 DCs + HQ per W1204) |
| **Owner** | Business Continuity Manager / COO |
| **Participants** | CIO, CFO, CHRO, VP Supply Chain, LP Director, Legal Counsel, Department Heads |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Distribute BCP self-assessment questionnaire to all department heads — assess current recovery capabilities, identify new single points of failure, validate RTO/RPO targets, and confirm contact lists | BC Manager | COO | 1 week (elapsed) |
| 2 | Conduct business impact analysis (BIA) update — reassess critical business processes, maximum tolerable downtime, financial impact per hour of outage, and interdependency map for all 5 legal entities | BC Manager | COO | 2 weeks (elapsed) |
| 3 | Review and update IT disaster recovery plan — verify DR site readiness (Clark DC for Luzon, Cebu DC for Visayas, Davao DC for Mindanao), test data replication integrity, update system inventory and recovery sequences | CIO | COO | 1 week |
| 4 | Update store emergency response procedures — incorporate lessons from prior year incidents, update emergency contact directories (PNP, BFP, Red Cross, LGU), verify emergency supply stock levels | BC Manager | COO | 1 week |
| 5 | Validate communication tree — update all-staff contact database, test SMS blast system, verify satellite phone inventory for areas with unreliable cellular coverage | CHRO | COO | 3 days |
| 6 | Present updated BCP to Executive Committee for approval — key changes, cost implications, identified gaps, and investment recommendations | BC Manager | CEO | 2 hours |
| 7 | Distribute updated BCP materials — digital copies to all Store Managers, physical emergency binders to all 200 stores and 4 DCs, and updated intranet portal | BC Manager | COO | 1 week |

### System Touchpoints
- **ERP Document Management** — BCP version control, distribution tracking
- **HR System** — employee contact database, communication tree
- **IT DR Platform** — replication status dashboard, DR site health monitoring
- **Communication Platform** — SMS blast system, satellite phone inventory
- **BI Dashboard** — prior year incident summary, recovery time actuals vs. targets

### Pain Points / Risks
- BCP review is often deprioritized during peak business periods (Q4 holiday season)
- Store Manager turnover means emergency binder holders may not be current
- IT DR testing is expensive and requires weekend downtime; may be skipped to avoid sales impact
- Philippine telecom infrastructure is vulnerable during disasters — backup channels essential
- Insurance coverage gaps may emerge between annual reviews as operations expand


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "validate RTO/RPO targets, and confirm contact lists" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-update of "update — reassess critical business processes, maximum tolerable downtime,…" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "Review and update IT disaster recovery plan — verify DR site readiness" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-319 (ensure controlled execution — BCP Planning & Testing (PA-26.1).)
- operational: Business Continuity Manager / COO review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- 1 FTE Business Continuity Manager (reports to COO)
- Department Heads: ~20 hours each for annual self-assessment
- IT Team: ~40 hours for DR plan update and testing
- External consultant: engaged every 2 years for independent BCP assessment

### Time Estimate
- BC Manager: ~160 hours over 6 weeks (annual)
- Department Heads: ~20 hours each × 15 departments = 300 hours
- IT Team: ~40 hours for DR review
- **Total annual effort: ~500 person-hours**

## W853. Business Continuity Plan Tabletop Exercise & Drill Execution

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|

| **Frequency** | 1 full exercise/year, 2 tabletops/year, 4 IT DR tests/year |

| **Owner** | BC Manager |
| **Participants** | Executive Committee, CIO, COO, CHRO, CFO, VP Supply Chain, LP Director, Store Manager representatives |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Design exercise scenario — based on realistic threat (typhoon Signal No. 4 hitting Visayas, 7.2 magnitude earthquake in Mindanao, ransomware attack on ERP, or pandemic resurgence); define inject points and expected responses | BC Manager | COO | 8 hours |
| 2 | Prepare exercise materials — scenario briefing document, inject cards (escalating events), evaluation rubric, participant roles, and communication simulation plan | BC Manager | COO | 4 hours |
| 3 | Conduct tabletop exercise — walk through scenario with Executive Committee and department heads; discuss decision points, communication protocols, resource allocation, and escalation procedures | BC Manager | CEO | 3–4 hours |
| 4 | For full exercise: simulate operational response — activate communication tree (simulated), conduct mock store closure/reopening at 2 volunteer stores, test IT DR failover, and simulate supply chain rerouting | BC Manager | COO | 1 full day |
| 5 | Evaluate exercise performance — assess decision speed, communication effectiveness, plan adequacy, resource availability, and interdepartmental coordination | BC Manager | COO | 2 hours |
| 6 | Document findings and recommendations — identify plan gaps, training needs, resource shortfalls, and system improvements; assign corrective action owners and deadlines | BC Manager | COO | 4 hours |

- Exercise findings may not be acted on if not tied to accountability and budget

- Scenarios may be too theoretical — participants may not take them seriously

- External evaluator: engaged for full exercise (1 day)

### Time Estimate

## W1204. Store-Level Business Continuity Plan (BCP) Annual Update, Tabletop Exercise & Certification

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual BCP review cycle (Q1 each year); or triggered by major incident at any store, new store opening, or significant operational change |
| **Frequency** | Annual per store; 200 stores reviewed on rolling schedule (~40/month over 5 months in Q1–Q2) |
| **Volume** | 200 store-level BCPs + 4 DC-level BCPs + 1 HQ BCP = 205 total |
| **Owner** | Business Continuity Manager |
| **Participants** | BC Manager, Store Manager, VP Store Operations, IT Disaster Recovery Lead, Insurance Coordinator, Regional Manager |

### Background

Each of BuildRight Depot's 200 stores, 4 DCs, and HQ must have an actionable Business Continuity Plan (BCP) that enables rapid response and recovery from disruptions including typhoons, earthquakes, flooding, fire, extended power outage, system failure, and supply chain disruption. While W49 and W158 cover corporate-level disaster response and BCP testing, this workflow governs the annual update and certification of individual location-level BCPs, ensuring each Store Manager knows their specific response procedures, communication chains, and recovery priorities.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **BCP template update**: BC Manager reviews and updates the standardized store-level BCP template: (a) updates contact lists (regional management, emergency services, insurance hotline, ACS contact, bank branch), (b) updates escalation procedures, (c) updates recovery time objectives (RTO): POS operations ≤ 4 hours, full store operations ≤ 24 hours, normal operations ≤ 72 hours, (d) incorporates lessons learned from incidents in the past year, (e) distributes updated template to all Store Managers | BC Manager | VP Store Operations | 8 hours (template update) |
| 2 | **Store Manager BCP review**: Each Store Manager reviews and customizes the BCP template for their specific location: (a) identifies location-specific risks (flood-prone area, earthquake zone, high-crime area), (b) lists local emergency contacts (barangay captain, nearest fire station, nearest hospital, police station), (c) documents alternative operating procedures (reduced POS terminals, manual operations per W580, limited product categories), (d) identifies pre-approved local contractors for emergency repairs, (e) documents cash float emergency procedure if armored car cannot access store | Store Manager | Regional Manager | 2 hours/store |
| 3 | **Tabletop exercise execution**: BC Manager facilitates annual tabletop exercise for each store (can be conducted in batches of 10–15 stores via video conference): (a) presents a scenario (typhoon Signal No. 4, fire in backroom, 48-hour system outage, flooding), (b) Store Manager walks through their BCP response step-by-step, (c) BC Manager evaluates response quality: communication timeliness, escalation appropriateness, resource awareness, customer/safety prioritization, (d) identifies gaps and assigns corrective actions | BC Manager / Store Manager | VP Store Operations | 1 hour/store |
| 4 | **Gap remediation**: For gaps identified in Step 3: (a) Store Manager updates BCP to address gaps within 2 weeks, (b) BC Manager reviews updated BCP and confirms closure, (c) critical gaps (e.g., no emergency power plan, no communication backup) require immediate remediation within 48 hours | Store Manager / BC Manager | Regional Manager | 1 hour/store |
| 5 | **BCP certification**: BC Manager issues annual BCP certification for each location that: (a) BCP is current and location-specific, (b) Store Manager has completed tabletop exercise, (c) all gaps from tabletop exercise are remediated, (d) emergency contact lists are verified; certification status tracked in BCP dashboard; uncertified stores flagged for VP Store Operations attention | BC Manager | VP Store Operations | 15 min/store |
| 6 | **New store BCP onboarding**: For each new store opening (10–15/year): BC Manager conducts dedicated BCP onboarding session with the new Store Manager during the pre-opening period (per W16/W702); tabletop exercise conducted within 30 days of opening; initial BCP certification issued upon successful tabletop completion | BC Manager | VP Store Operations | 3 hours/new store |

### System Touchpoints
- BCP document management system with template distribution and version control (W1204.1)
- Location-specific risk mapping with GIS data (flood zones, fault lines, crime data) (W1204.2)
- Tabletop exercise evaluation scoring and gap tracking (W1204.3–4)
- BCP certification tracking dashboard with status per location (W1204.5)
- New store BCP onboarding checklist integrated with W16/W702 (W1204.6)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Store Manager turnover impact**: with ~15–20% annual turnover, trained Store Managers leave and new ones may not be familiar with their location's BCP; mitigated by BCP onboarding for new Store Managers (W16) and requiring BCP review as part of new SM orientation
- **Tabletop exercise fatigue**: conducting tabletop exercises for 200 stores annually is resource-intensive; mitigated by batch video conference sessions (10–15 stores at a time) and alternating detailed vs. abbreviated exercises for low-risk locations
- **BCP document staleness**: contact lists, procedures, and risk assessments become outdated quickly; mitigated by mandatory annual update in Step 2 and trigger-based updates (staff turnover, phone number changes)
- **Execution gap between plan and reality**: a well-documented BCP does not guarantee effective execution under stress; mitigated by realistic scenario-based tabletop exercises and periodic live drills (W158)


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "reviews and updates the standardized store-level BCP template" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-document of "documents alternative operating procedures" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-assign of "assigns corrective actions" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-228 (ensure BCP readiness & insurance recovery.)
- operational: BCP onboarding for new Store Managers (W16) and requiring BCP review as part of new SM orientation; operational: batch video conference sessions (10–15 stores at a time) and alternating detailed vs. abbreviated exercises for low-risk locations; operational: mandatory annual update in Step 2 and trigger-based updates (staff turnover, phone number changes)

### Staffing Implication
- **BC Manager**: ~20 hours/week during Q1–Q2 (BCP review season) = ~400 hours/year. Absorbed within existing role.
- **Store Managers**: ~3 hours/year each for BCP review and tabletop exercise = ~600 hours/year total across 200 stores. Absorbed within existing duties.
- **VP Store Operations**: ~2 hours/week during BCP review season for certification oversight. Absorbed within existing role.
- **No permanent incremental headcount**.

### Time Estimate
Annual program: BCP template update 8 hours + per-location cycle 4.25 hours (Store Manager BCP review 2 hours + tabletop exercise 1 hour + gap remediation 1 hour + certification 15 min) × 205 locations ≈ 870 hours/year on the Q1–Q2 rolling schedule (~40/month); new-store BCP onboarding 3 hours × 10–15 openings ≈ 30–45 hours/year.

## W1322. IT Disaster Recovery (DR) Failover Test Execution, Validation & Recovery Time Assessment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly IT DR testing calendar; or ad-hoc triggered by major system incident, IT infrastructure change, or regulatory audit requirement |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly full DR failover test (4×/year); monthly component-level tests for critical modules (POS, inventory) |
| **Volume** | 4 full failover tests + 12 component tests per year |
| **Owner** | CIO |
| **Participants** | CIO, Infrastructure Team, Applications Team, IT Security, BC Manager, COO (observer for quarterly tests) |

### Background

BuildRight's 200 stores, 4 DCs, 600 POS terminals, ecommerce platform, and 6,911 users depend on continuous ERP system availability. The model company profile specifies 99.9% uptime for POS and 99.5% for back office. The BCP (W847) includes IT DR planning and W853/158 cover general BCP drills. However, IT disaster recovery testing is a specialized technical exercise distinct from business-level tabletop exercises: it requires actually failing over production systems to the DR site, validating data integrity, measuring recovery time against RTO targets, and failing back. For a retailer processing 2.8M POS transactions/month, even a 4-hour ERP outage during business hours represents ~PHP 280K per store in lost revenue (on the PHP 840K/day POS downtime-cost canon, §12.3) — ~PHP 56M chain-wide. This workflow governs the quarterly IT DR failover test — a critical technical assurance activity that validates BuildRight's ability to recover from catastrophic system failure.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pre-test planning**: CIO prepares DR test plan: (a) selects test scope: full failover (all systems) or component failover (POS only, inventory only, ecommerce only, financials only); (b) defines test window: typically Saturday midnight to Sunday 6 AM to minimize business impact; (c) identifies RTO targets to validate: POS ≤ 4 hours, inventory ≤ 6 hours, ecommerce ≤ 4 hours, financials ≤ 8 hours; (d) defines RPO targets: data loss ≤ 15 minutes (near-real-time replication); (e) notifies all stakeholders: Store Managers, DC Supervisors, ecommerce team, Finance — "system maintenance window" communication; (f) prepares rollback plan in case DR failover fails | CIO | COO | 4–6 hours |
| 2 | **Pre-failover data snapshot**: Infrastructure Team captures baseline data: (a) record current transaction volumes (POS, ecommerce, AP/AR); (b) take database timestamp snapshot for data integrity comparison; (c) document current system performance metrics (response times, concurrent users, batch job status); (d) verify DR site replication is current: replication lag must be < 5 minutes before proceeding | Infrastructure Team | CIO | 1 hour |
| 3 | **Failover execution**: Infrastructure Team executes failover: (a) primary site: gracefully stop all application services; (b) activate DR site: (i) promote DR database to primary, (ii) start application services on DR infrastructure, (iii) update DNS/load balancer to route traffic to DR site, (iv) verify network connectivity from stores and DCs to DR site; (c) start timer: record exact failover start time for RTO measurement; (d) monitor system health: CPU, memory, disk I/O, database connections, replication status | Infrastructure Team | CIO | 2–4 hours |
| 4 | **Functional validation**: Applications Team validates all critical functions at DR site: (a) **POS**: process test transactions at 2–3 volunteer stores (cash sale, card payment, return, price lookup, inventory check); verify near-real-time inventory sync; (b) **Ecommerce**: place test BOPIS order and test delivery order; verify inventory reservation and order confirmation; (c) **Inventory**: verify real-time stock levels match pre-failover snapshot; process test goods receipt and transfer order; (d) **Procurement**: verify PO creation and approval workflow; check vendor portal accessibility; (e) **Finance**: verify GL posting, AP invoice processing, AR statement generation; (f) **HR/Payroll**: verify employee self-service portal and time attendance access; (g) **DC/WMS**: verify RF gun connectivity and pick/pack/ship processing; (h) record any functional failures or degraded performance | Applications Team | CIO | 2–3 hours |
| 5 | **Performance validation**: Infrastructure Team measures DR site performance against production baselines: (a) POS transaction processing time: target < 3 seconds per transaction per model company profile; (b) report generation: target < 30 seconds for standard reports; (c) concurrent user capacity: verify DR site supports ≥ 500 concurrent users (50% of peak per NFR-028); (d) batch job processing: verify scheduled batch jobs (nightly inventory sync, daily sales posting) execute within normal time windows; (e) database query performance: compare average query response time to production baseline | Infrastructure Team | CIO | 1 hour |
| 6 | **Data integrity verification**: Applications Team compares post-failover data to pre-failover snapshot: (a) record count comparison for critical tables (inventory, orders, customers, GL entries); (b) verify no data loss: all transactions recorded between last replication and failover are present at DR site; (c) verify referential integrity: no orphaned records or broken cross-references; (d) calculate actual RPO: time difference between last replicated transaction and failover time | Applications Team | CIO | 1 hour |
| 7 | **Failback execution**: After validation: (a) Infrastructure Team executes failback to primary site: (i) replicate DR site data back to primary, (ii) stop DR site services, (iii) promote primary back to active, (iv) update DNS/load balancer, (v) verify connectivity; (b) verify all systems operational at primary site; (c) resume normal operations; (d) verify replication from primary to DR site resumes normally | Infrastructure Team | CIO | 2–3 hours |
| 8 | **Post-test report**: CIO prepares DR test report: (a) test scope and objectives; (b) timeline: planned vs. actual failover and failback times; (c) RTO achievement: actual vs. target for each system; (d) RPO achievement: actual data loss (if any) vs. target; (e) functional test results: pass/fail by system; (f) performance test results: DR vs. production baseline; (g) data integrity verification results; (h) issues encountered: any failures, errors, or unexpected behavior; (i) recommendations: improvements to DR architecture, procedures, or training; (j) report submitted to COO and BC Manager for integration into BCP per W847 | CIO | COO | 4 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- DR site infrastructure: server compute, storage replication, networking, DNS management (W1322.3, 7)
- Production monitoring dashboard extended to DR site for performance comparison (W1322.5)
- Automated data integrity comparison tool: pre/post-failover record count and transaction verification (W1322.6)
- POS test transaction module for store-level DR validation (W1322.4)
- DR test report template with RTO/RPO tracking and trend analysis (W1322.8)
- Integration with W847 (BCP review), W853 (BCP drill), W695 (IT incident management), W376 (capacity planning)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Failover during test causing actual outage**: if failover fails, stores cannot process transactions until failback completes; mitigated by Saturday midnight test window and rollback plan; worst-case scenario: stores switch to offline POS mode per W580 for up to 4 hours
- **Data replication lag**: if replication lag exceeds RPO target at time of failover, data loss occurs; mitigated by near-real-time synchronous replication for critical databases and pre-failover replication lag check (Step 2)
- **Test fatigue**: quarterly tests at midnight require IT team overtime; mitigated by rotating test responsibilities across Infrastructure Team members
- **Production-like performance at DR**: DR site may be sized for emergency operation (lower spec) but not production-equivalent performance; performance degradation during DR operation is acceptable for short periods but must be documented and communicated to users
- **Failback risk**: replicating data from DR back to primary introduces a second failover risk; mitigated by thorough pre-failback verification and staged approach


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "validate" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-record of "record current transaction volumes" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-route of "route traffic to DR site" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-228 (ensure BCP readiness & insurance recovery.)
- operational: Saturday midnight test window and rollback plan; operational: near-real-time synchronous replication for critical databases and pre-failover replication lag check (Step 2); operational: rotating test responsibilities across Infrastructure Team members

### Staffing Implication
- **CIO**: ~10–12 hours per quarterly test (planning + execution + reporting). Absorbed.
- **Infrastructure Team**: ~8–10 hours per test (evening/weekend). Compensated per overtime policy per W10.
- **Applications Team**: ~4–6 hours per test (functional validation). Absorbed.
- **No incremental headcount.**

### Time Estimate
- Pre-test planning: 4–6 hours
- Pre-failover snapshot: 1 hour
- Failover execution: 2–4 hours
- Functional validation: 2–3 hours
- Performance validation: 1 hour
- Data integrity verification: 1 hour
- Failback: 2–3 hours
- Post-test report: 4 hours
- **Per test**: ~17–22 hours total effort; elapsed time: 8–12 hours (midnight to morning)
- **Annual**: ~70–90 hours across 4 quarterly tests + 12 component tests (~24 hours) = ~95–115 hours

## W1323. Supply Chain Disruption Simulation & Alternate Sourcing Activation Drill

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual BCP testing cycle (aligned with W847/W853); or triggered by actual near-miss disruption event (port congestion, supplier bankruptcy, typhoon-induced logistics failure) |
| **Frequency** | Annual full simulation; semi-annual tabletop scenario for supply chain team |
| **Volume** | 1 full simulation + 1 tabletop per year |
| **Owner** | Supply Planning Manager |
| **Participants** | Supply Planning Manager, Category Managers (5), Buyers (10 per §13.1), DC Supervisors (4), Logistics Manager, VP Merchandising, COO, Procurement Manager |

### Background

W729 covers actual supply chain disruption rapid response and W852 covers disruption business impact assessment. W847/853 cover general BCP review and tabletop exercises. However, BuildRight's supply chain is uniquely vulnerable due to: (a) Philippine archipelago geography requiring inter-island shipping, (b) 40% import dependency from Asia-Pacific suppliers, (c) 400–600 TEU/month import volume, (d) typhoon season (Jun–Nov) disrupting both port operations and inter-island logistics, (e) concentration risk from top 20 vendors accounting for 45% of COGS. A supply chain disruption simulation tests the organization's ability to activate alternate sourcing, reroute logistics, and maintain store replenishment when primary supply paths fail. Unlike a general BCP tabletop, this simulation involves detailed operational decision-making: which vendors to activate, which DCs to reroute through, how to prioritize limited inventory across 200 stores, and how to communicate with customers about availability.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Scenario design**: Supply Planning Manager designs simulation scenario based on realistic Philippine supply chain risks: (a) **Scenario A — Port congestion**: Manila port congested for 30 days (as occurred in 2014, 2018); import containers delayed; DC3 (Laguna) and DC4 (Clark) unable to receive import shipments; (b) **Scenario B — Primary vendor failure**: top vendor (accounting for 8% of COGS) suddenly ceases operations due to factory fire or financial collapse; 300+ SKUs affected; (c) **Scenario C — Typhoon logistics disruption**: Signal No. 5 typhoon closes Visayas ports for 7 days; DC2 (Cebu) unable to receive or dispatch; 40 Visayas stores cut off from supply; (d) **Scenario D — Raw material shortage**: global cement or steel shortage causes 30% price increase and 50% availability reduction for key construction materials; scenario selected annually by rotation; Supply Planning Manager prepares inject cards with escalating developments over simulated 30-day period | Supply Planning Manager | VP Merchandising | 2 days |
| 2 | **Simulation kickoff**: Supply Planning Manager briefs participants: (a) presents initial scenario inject (e.g., "Day 1: News reports Manila port congestion; 200 BuildRight containers stuck at anchorage; ETA unknown"); (b) participants organized by function: Merchandising/Buying (sourcing), Supply Planning (allocation), Logistics (rerouting), DC Operations (receiving/dispatch), Finance (cost impact); (c) each function has access to their normal ERP data and tools; (d) simulation runs in real-time compressed format: each simulated "day" = 30 minutes of real time; total simulation: 15 simulated days in ~8 hours | Supply Planning Manager | COO | 30 min briefing |
| 3 | **Alternate vendor activation drill**: Buyers must identify and activate alternate vendors for affected categories: (a) search vendor master for pre-qualified alternate vendors per W224; (b) contact alternate vendors for emergency pricing and availability (simulated via role-play or scripted responses); (c) Buyers who have maintained vendor relationships per W36 get faster response times; (d) Category Manager approves alternate vendor selection based on price, quality, and lead time tradeoffs; (e) Procurement issues emergency POs per W60 expedited order process; (f) Finance confirms budget authorization per W489 for emergency procurement above normal thresholds | Buyers / Category Manager / Procurement | VP Merchandising | 2–3 simulated days |
| 4 | **Logistics rerouting drill**: Logistics Manager must reroute shipments around disrupted nodes: (a) if port congested: identify alternate ports (Subic, Batangas) and arrange drayage to DCs; (b) if DC inaccessible: redirect shipments to alternate DC and plan inter-DC transfer per W22; (c) if inter-island routes closed: assess air freight for critical items; evaluate inventory redistribution from unaffected stores; (d) coordinate with 3PL carriers per W1207 for capacity; (e) Logistics Manager documents rerouting decisions and estimated cost impact vs. normal logistics cost | Logistics Manager | COO | 3–4 simulated days |
| 5 | **Store allocation prioritization**: Supply Planning Manager must allocate limited inventory across 200 stores: (a) identify stores with lowest weeks-of-supply in affected categories; (b) prioritize: (i) high-volume flagship stores, (ii) stores with active B2B project commitments (W58), (iii) stores in high-competition trade areas where stock-outs drive customer defection; (c) activate store-to-store transfers per W22 for stores with excess inventory in affected categories; (d) communicate allocation decisions to Regional Managers; (e) adjust replenishment parameters per W31 to stretch available inventory | Supply Planning Manager | VP Merchandising | 3–4 simulated days |

## W1393. Store-Level IT Disaster Recovery, POS System Failover & Manual Operations Procedure

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Store POS system failure — complete outage (all terminals down), network connectivity loss to HQ/data center, or regional internet outage affecting multiple stores |

### Background

BuildRight's POS system processes ~2.8M transactions/month across 200 stores. Without POS, the store cannot scan items, calculate totals, process payments, or update inventory. Manual operations must be activated within 15 minutes to avoid customer walkouts. Each store maintains a pre-configured manual operations kit and Store Managers are trained annually on the procedure.

| 1 | **Outage detection and reporting**: (a) Cashier or Store Manager notices POS system unresponsive on all terminals; (b) Store Manager checks: is it a single terminal (W206) or all terminals? is internet connectivity light on router green or red? is the issue local (store only) or regional (nearby stores also affected — check WhatsApp group)? (c) Store Manager calls IT Help Desk and reports complete POS outage; IT creates incident ticket with priority "P1 — Store POS Complete Outage" | Store Manager | — | 5 min |
| 2 | **IT diagnosis and ETA** (within 10 minutes): IT Help Desk checks: (a) is the store's internet connection up? (ping test); (b) is the ERP/data center accessible from other stores? (c) is there a known regional ISP outage? (d) provides Store Manager with estimated recovery time: (i) < 30 min (ISP blip, auto-reconnect expected), (ii) 30 min–2 hours (requires IT intervention), (iii) > 2 hours (major outage — activate manual operations) | IT Help Desk | IT Ops Manager | 5–10 min |

- Manual operations kit: pre-printed price list auto-generated weekly from current price master, manual sales slip templates, credit card imprint machines (W1393.4)

## W1450. Earthquake Response Protocol, Structural Assessment & Store Reopening Safety Verification

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | PHIVOLCS earthquake alert: Intensity V (Strong) or higher in any BuildRight location area; or store/DC structural damage reported by staff; or LGU-mandated building inspection order |
| **Frequency** | Significant earthquakes (Intensity V+) affecting BuildRight operations: estimated 2–4 events per year in the Philippines (seismically active region on the Pacific Ring of Fire) |
| **Volume** | Each event may affect 5–50+ stores depending on epicenter location and depth; Mindanao (active fault lines near Davao HQ and DC1) and Metro Manila (West Valley Fault) are highest risk areas |
| **Owner** | COO / Facilities Engineer |
| **Participants** | COO, Facilities Engineer, Store Manager, DC Manager, BC Manager, LP Officer, Local Structural Engineer (contracted), CHRO, IT Security |

### Background

The Philippines is located on the Pacific Ring of Fire and experiences frequent earthquakes. The West Valley Fault runs directly through Metro Manila (30 BuildRight stores), and active fault systems exist near Davao (HQ + DC1 + 60 Mindanao stores) and across the Visayas. A major earthquake (Magnitude 7+) in Metro Manila could damage multiple BuildRight stores simultaneously, disrupt DC3 operations, and impact hundreds of thousands of customers. Unlike typhoons and volcanic eruptions, earthquakes have zero advance warning — the response begins immediately after the event. This workflow governs BuildRight's earthquake response: immediate safety (evacuation and personnel accounting), rapid structural assessment, and phased reopening based on engineering safety certification.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Immediate earthquake response** (during/within minutes of shaking): (a) Store Manager/DC Manager executes "Duck, Cover, Hold" protocol for all personnel and customers, (b) when shaking stops: initiate evacuation per building evacuation plan — all personnel and customers assemble at designated outdoor assembly point (parking lot, away from building, power lines, and outdoor lumber yard), (c) Store Manager accounts for all staff and any customers who were in the building — report missing persons to emergency services, (d) provide first aid for injuries (stores have first aid kits per W631), (e) secure building perimeter — no re-entry until structural assessment is complete | Store Manager / DC Manager | COO | 15–30 min |
| 2 | **Rapid visual structural assessment** (within 1–2 hours of earthquake): (a) Facilities Engineer or trained LP Officer conducts rapid external visual assessment of each affected store/DC: (i) visible structural damage: cracked walls, collapsed roof, leaning structure, ground settlement, (ii) utility hazards: gas leaks (smell), electrical hazards (downed power lines), water main breaks, (iii) fallen merchandise/shelving inside (visible through windows), (iv) outdoor area: lumber yard collapse, fence/wall collapse, (b) classify each building: (i) GREEN — no visible damage, likely safe for rapid reopening, (ii) YELLOW — minor visible damage, requires engineering inspection before reopening, (iii) RED — significant visible damage, unsafe, do not enter until professional structural engineering assessment complete, (c) report classification to COO for chain-wide status dashboard | Facilities Engineer / LP Officer | COO | 30–60 min per site |
| 3 | **Professional structural engineering inspection** (within 24–72 hours for YELLOW/RED buildings): (a) COO engages contracted structural engineers (pre-qualified and on retainer for rapid deployment), (b) engineer conducts detailed inspection: (i) foundation and structural frame integrity, (ii) roof connection points, (iii) wall cracks (measure width — > 5mm may indicate structural compromise), (iv) floor slab condition, (v) dock door alignment (DCs), (vi) fire suppression system integrity, (c) engineer issues written safety certification: (i) SAFE for immediate reopening, (ii) SAFE WITH RESTRICTIONS (e.g., "no heavy merchandise on top shelves"), (iii) UNSAFE — requires repair before occupancy, (d) Buildings rated UNSAFE: COO plans repair project per W96 (renovation), including insurance claim per W853 | Structural Engineer / COO | COO | 4–8 hours per building |
| 4 | **Phased store reopening**: COO authorizes reopening based on safety certification: (a) GREEN buildings: reopen immediately (after internal cleanup — fallen merchandise, shelf resets), (b) YELLOW buildings cleared as SAFE: reopen after engineering-required fixes (e.g., re-anchoring shelves, patching walls), (c) RED buildings: remain closed until repaired and re-certified; Store Manager communicates closure to customers via social media and redirects to nearest open BuildRight store, (d) DC reopening: Logistics Manager reroutes deliveries to alternative DC if primary DC is RED; activate inter-DC transfer per W22 to replenish stores from unaffected DCs | COO | CEO | Ongoing |
| 5 | **Post-earthquake operational recovery** (T+1 day to T+2 weeks): (a) inventory damage assessment: identify merchandise damaged by falling, crushing, or contamination, (b) IT systems check: verify POS, network, and payment systems operational at reopened stores, (c) supply chain recovery: assess vendor and logistics partner impact; activate alternative sourcing per W763 if primary vendors are earthquake-affected, (d) HR: check on employee safety and welfare; activate employee assistance program for affected staff, (e) insurance: initiate claims for structural damage and inventory loss per W853 | COO | CEO | 1–2 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- Earthquake alert integration — PHIVOLCS real-time earthquake information API (W1450.1)
- Building safety status dashboard — chain-wide store/DC status tracking (GREEN/YELLOW/RED) (W1450.3)
- Structural engineer deployment tracker — contractor scheduling and certification management (W1450.3)
- Integration with W848 (disaster protocol), W850 (store closure), W853 (insurance), W852 (supply chain disruption), W22 (inter-DC transfer)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Simultaneous multi-store impact**: a major Metro Manila earthquake could damage 20–30 stores simultaneously, overwhelming Facilities Engineer capacity for rapid assessment; pre-qualifying multiple structural engineering firms for surge capacity is essential
- **DC infrastructure vulnerability**: DCs have high-bay racking systems that are vulnerable to seismic damage; racking collapse in a DC endangers workers and destroys inventory; seismic racking restraints are a critical capital investment
- **Customer and employee panic**: strong earthquakes cause significant psychological distress; Store Managers must be trained to manage evacuation calmly and provide clear post-earthquake communication to staff and customers
- **Utility disruption**: earthquakes frequently disrupt electricity, water, and telecommunications for days; stores must be prepared to operate without utilities (generator per W913, manual POS per W1393)

## W5571. Tsunami & Storm-Surge Coastal-Intrusion Response Protocol (PHIVOLCS Advisory / PAGASA Storm-Surge Warning)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | PHIVOLCS tsunami advisory or warning covering any BuildRight coastal site (including the near-field case: a felt earthquake at a coastal site is itself the warning); or a PAGASA significant storm-surge forecast (≥ 2–3 m class) covering a site's coastline in the current typhoon track |
| **Frequency** | ~60 of ~205 sites sit in coastal municipalities; PAGASA surge warnings accompany the strongest typhoons (several advisory episodes per year chain-wide, most affecting 1–5 sites); tsunami warnings affecting BuildRight sites are rare — distant-basin advisories a few times per decade, a near-field generating quake once in the planning horizon |
| **Volume** | 1 event × the affected coastal sites (1–15 stores per surge episode; a near-field tsunami closes an entire coastal band) × the evacuation/hardening move × the documented all-clear gate before re-entry |
| **Owner** | Business Continuity Manager / COO (protocol); Store Manager (site execution); Regional Manager (coastal-band supervision) |
| **Participants** | Security Guard, HSE Manager (VS-24), Store Manager, DC Manager, Regional Manager, BC Manager, Facilities (W650 make-safe), VS-69 execution chain where the surge rides a typhoon (W2503-class readiness), PNP/BFP/LGU DRRM Office, Structural Engineer (retainer, post-intrusion), Corporate Communications (W855/W134) |

### Background

'tsunami' appears in zero PA files and 'storm surge' only as customer-advisory context (PA-09.2 sells flood-resilient materials; the enterprise risk register maps surge exposure for siting) — W1450's quake protocol ends at structural assessment and explicitly does not govern water, W1387 owns rainfall/riverine flooding (freshwater, forecast by rainfall, not by coastal water level), and W847's BCP names hazards generically — nobody owned the coastal-intrusion class: the quake-is-the-warning law (a near-field tsunami arrives in 10–30 minutes; a strong shaking that makes standing hard at a coastal site means self-evacuate upward immediately, before any official message), the move-up-not-out rule (where horizontal time is insufficient, vertical evacuation to the store's upper level beats flight into traffic), the no-return law (no re-entry until PHIVOLCS cancels the warning — surges and tsunami waves re-arrive for hours, and most casualty stories are returns), the surge-ladder discipline (a lower typhoon signal with a 3 m surge forecast can be more dangerous to a coastal site than a higher signal without one — the surge forecast, not the signal number, gates pre-emptive closure riding the E-02 custody), and the saltwater distinction (salt intrusion into generators, switchgear, HVAC and elevators is a corrosion event beyond the W1387 freshwater cleanup — equipment re-energized before desalination fails weeks later). This workflow is the single canon for that event class — the W1449/W1450 natural-hazard family's coastal-water counterpart, gating re-entry on documented all-clear.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monitor & classify**: the DC command desk and coastal-region managers track PHIVOLCS tsunami messages and PAGASA storm-surge advisories; a felt quake at a coastal site (shaking Intensity IV+ or difficulty standing) triggers Step 2 immediately without waiting for an official message; official advisories classify each site: advisory (prepare), warning 1–3 m (evacuate shoreline-exposed zones), warning > 3 m (full site evacuation) | BC Manager / Store Manager | Business Continuity Manager / COO | Continuous; 5–15 min on event |
| 2 | **Evacuation / move-up decision**: horizontal evacuation to the assembly point above surge lines where time allows; vertical move-up (customers and staff to the upper sales level/mezzanine) where it does not; PWD/senior assistance per the W4400 protocols; garden center and lumber-yard staff clear the open-air yard first | Store Manager / Security Guard | Regional Manager | 10–30 min |
| 3 | **Pre-arrival hardening (time-allowing)**: kill power at the main where the water path is confirmed; secure the LPG/propane cage (VS-175) and chemical aisles (W1312-class) against flotation and mixing; move critical records and portable tills up; generator fuel topped (W1223) only where the unit sits above surge line | Store Manager / HSE Manager | Store Manager | 15–40 min |
| 4 | **Accountability & shelter**: assembly/upper-level headcount with the visitor log; comms ring to the Regional Manager and the CMT (W855 activation for multi-site events); no staff re-entry for any reason during the warning window | Store Manager / Security Guard | Regional Manager | During event |
| 5 | **All-clear gate**: re-entry only after PHIVOLCS cancels the tsunami message (or the surge episode recedes with the LGU DRRM all-clear) AND a walk-down finds no structural undercut, no live utility hazard, and no contaminated standing water beyond the W1312-class response; the gate decision is documented per site | Regional Manager / Facilities | Business Continuity Manager / COO | At all-clear |
| 6 | **Salt-intrusion triage**: any equipment that contacted surge water (generators, switchgear, HVAC, elevators per W5539-class vendor callouts, POS lanes) is de-energized, photographed and scheduled for professional desalination/inspection before re-energization; elevator and escalator units get a licensed-technician release check | Facilities / Store Manager | Business Continuity Manager / COO | 1–5 days |
| 7 | **Merchandise, claim & closure interface**: damaged-stock documentation feeds the W876 claim chain (VS-26); where the surge rode a typhoon, the event merges into the VS-69 recovery chain (W2518/W1552 assessment) with this protocol owning the coastal-water specifics; closure/reopening decisions ride the E-02 custody (W2510/W850) | Insurance / Store Manager | Regional Manager | Per recovery |
| 8 | **After-action & retrofit**: event timeline reviewed against the classification ladder; site surge-readiness items (yard elevation, cage anchoring, generator placement) join the resilience-capex backlog; BCP (W847/W1204) and drill rotation updated | BC Manager / HSE Manager | Business Continuity Manager / COO | 1–3 weeks post-event |

### System Touchpoints
- PHIVOLCS/PAGASA advisory feed integrated into the DC command-desk monitoring (W1450-class alert integration)
- Site hazard-tier register (coastal sites flagged with surge exposure class)
- Closure/reopening flags on the site file riding the E-02 custody (links to W2510/W850)
- Facility work orders with a salt-corrosion flag gating re-energization (links to W650/W47)
- Claim records to VS-26 (W876) with surge-water damage type; BCP update records (links to W847/W1204)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Return-too-early risk**: staff and customers re-entering between wave arrivals is the classic tsunami casualty pattern; mitigated by Step 5's documented all-clear gate with no staff re-entry during the warning window
- **Signal-ladder misread risk**: treating a modest typhoon signal as the measure of danger while the surge forecast is the coastal threat; mitigated by Step 1's classification of sites by surge forecast, not signal number alone
- **Warning-gap risk**: distant-basin overnight advisories arriving with no felt-quake cue; mitigated by the command desk's continuous PHIVOLCS/PAGASA monitoring duty
- **Salt-corrosion risk**: panels, generators and elevator controllers re-energized after desalination-worthy contact and failing weeks later; mitigated by Step 6's de-energize-inspect-then-repower sequence
- **Coupled-event confusion risk**: surge flooding managed as generic W1387 flood, losing the salt-intrusion and no-return specifics; mitigated by the Step 7 merge rule that keeps this canon's coastal-water duties inside the typhoon chain

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-open of the site evacuation checklist on a matching advisory tier from the PHIVOLCS/PAGASA feed (replaces manual Step 1 dispatch)
- Reopening checklist interlock: site closure flags stay latched until the documented all-clear gate is recorded (replaces manual Step 5 verification)

### Controls
- CTL-319 (ensure controlled execution — BCP Planning & Testing (PA-26.1).)
- operational: all-clear gate decisions documented per site before re-entry; re-energization only after the desalination/inspection record; surge-readiness items tracked to the capex backlog

### Time Estimate
Per surge episode ≈ 20–60 staff-hours per affected site spread over the event and the recovery week (hardening, accountability, triage, documentation); at several advisory episodes per year mostly touching 1–5 sites ≈ 150–600 staff-hours/year chain-wide, absorbed by coastal-region rosters. Drill upkeep ≈ 1–2 hours per coastal site per year inside the W1204 BCP rotation — no incremental headcount.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed by the coastal-region store/DC benches, the BC Manager and Facilities; contracted structural engineers per event as needed; no incremental headcount.
