## W3577. Application Portfolio Management & Rationalization

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual portfolio review; strategy/roadmap change; acquisition/decommission |
| **Frequency** | Annual + on change |
| **Volume** | Enterprise application portfolio (~ERP + ~20–40 surrounding applications) |
| **Owner** | Enterprise Architects |
| **Participants** | Enterprise Architects, Application Owners, Business Capability Owners, IT Operations (links to VS-27), ITAM (links to VS-99), Finance (links to VS-17.4), Procurement (links to VS-34), BI (links to VS-28.1) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Enterprise Architects/Application Owners maintain the application portfolio inventory: each application's business capability, owner, business value, cost (TCO links to VS-99.3/W3588), technology/health, risk, usage, integrations (links to W3579), and lifecycle stage; reconcile to discovery/CMDB (links to VS-99/VS-27.2) and SaaS estate (links to VS-99.2) | Enterprise Architects / Application Owners | Chief Enterprise Architect | Continuous + annual |
| 2 | Enterprise Architects assess each application on a TIME matrix (Tolerate / Invest / Migrate / Eliminate) using business value vs. technical-health: identify duplicates/overlaps, under-used applications, end-of-life risks, consolidation opportunities, and cloud/SaaS migration candidates; produce the rationalization roadmap | Enterprise Architects / Finance | Chief Enterprise Architect | Annual |
| 3 | Enterprise Architects/IT Leadership execute the rationalization roadmap via the PMO (links to VS-112/W3546), retire applications (W3578), consolidate/merge duplicates, and report portfolio health/cost/reduction to governance (W3592) and finance (links to VS-17.4); refresh the portfolio annually | Enterprise Architects / IT Leadership / PMO | CIO | Annual + ongoing |

### System Touchpoints
- Application inventory/repository (links to W3575); discovery/CMDB (links to VS-99/VS-27.2); SaaS estate (links to VS-99.2); integration map (links to W3579); finance/TCO (links to VS-17.4/W3588); PMO (links to VS-112)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Portfolio-bloat risk**: Duplicate/overlapping applications inflate cost and integration complexity; mitigated by TIME rationalization
- **Orphaned-application risk**: Unowned applications with no business owner; mitigated by ownership discipline and retirement


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-capture of "Enterprise Architects/Application Owners maintain the application portfolio…" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-identify of "identify duplicates/overlaps, under-used applications, end-of-life risks,…" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-report of "report portfolio health/cost/reduction to governance" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-569 (ensure controlled execution — Application Portfolio, Integration & Solution Architecture (PA-113.2).)
- operational: TIME rationalization; operational: ownership discipline and retirement

### Time Estimate
- Annual + ongoing

## W5516. Best-of-Breed Product Lifecycle Management, Vendor Release Intake & Exit Reserves

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vendor release notification; contract signature/renewal; annual TPRM reassessment; QBR cycle; vendor health/EOL signal |
| **Frequency** | Per vendor release + monthly product health + quarterly/annual lifecycle cycles |
| **Volume** | ~9 vendor-managed products (vendor commodities incl. foundation-model API contracts, the already-built POS/ecommerce/loyalty platforms, and the in-suite EBS currency program) × ~6–12 vendor releases/product/year ≈ 54–108 release intakes/year; carried by 4 Vendor PM seats (WLI ×2, SSP ×1, CCP ×1) plus the CIO Office secretariat |
| **Owner** | Vendor Product Manager (owning domain team, per managed product) |
| **Participants** | Vendor Product Managers (WLI ×2, SSP ×1, CCP ×1), CIO Office vendor-portfolio analyst (Register & exit-reserve records), domain QA & release analysts (regression pack), SEC TPRM analyst (links to VS-161), IAP (contract catalog & consumer tests — links to W3579), INFRA (staging rings), Procurement/Legal (links to VS-34/VS-100.3), Tier & Control Board (escalation), application lifecycle owner (links to W3578) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Vendor Product Manager run release intake: vendor releases land in the owning team's staging ring and pass the intake checklist plus the domain regression pack (Tier-1 workflow coverage mandatory) before production — the same standard the in-suite EBS currency (RUP/CPU) cycle meets; a release may be deferred one cycle, never two | Vendor PM / QA & release analyst | Owning team IT PO | 4–8 hours/release |
| 2 | Vendor PM/IT PO track the upgrade-currency KPI — at most one major version behind vendor current per product; a product two versions behind is a Tier & Control Board escalation with a funded remediation plan | Vendor PM | Owning team IT PO | Monthly check |
| 3 | Procurement/Legal/Vendor PM verify the required contract clauses at signature and each renewal: Philippine data-residency/processing terms (RA 10173), statutory-readiness warranty where applicable, exit/transition assistance, data-export in open formats, and price-escalation caps | Procurement / Legal / Vendor PM | Owning team IT PO | Per signature/renewal |
| 4 | SEC/TPRM keep every managed vendor risk-tiered with tier-1 annual reassessment and audit-report review (links to VS-161/W4729); continuous-monitoring feeds (financial, cyber, performance) watch vendor health and EOL (links to W4734) | SEC TPRM analyst | SEC lead | Annual + continuous |
| 5 | CIO Office accrue each replaceable vendor dependency's exit reserve at every QBR (a set percentage of license cost, held centrally) and disclose reserves in the QBR FinOps pack — a vendor exit is never unfundable; the in-house platforms are forever by doctrine, so their resilience is a DR/run-cost question, not an exit question (links to VS-135) | Vendor-portfolio analyst / FinOps | CIO | Quarterly |
| 6 | Vendor PM/CIO Office manage product health: SLA attainment, integration latency vs IAP budget, roadmap intelligence, and the Register's re-evaluation triggers (links to W5515); sunset decisions route through W3578 application retirement and the SIB (W5515) | Vendor PM / Vendor-portfolio analyst | Owning team IT PO | Monthly + per trigger |

### System Touchpoints
- Vendor staging rings & the domain regression pack (Tier-1 coverage mandatory per release)
- Upgrade-currency dashboard (vendor version vs current, per managed product)
- Exit-reserve ledger (CIO Office central bucket; disclosed in the QBR FinOps pack — links to VS-135)
- TPRM reassessment & continuous-monitoring platform (links to VS-161/W4729/W4734)
- IAP consumer-driven contract tests for product-to-product flows (links to W3579)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Upgrade-debt risk**: deferred vendor releases accumulate until a product is two major versions behind, stacking security and integration exposure; mitigated by the defer-one-never-two rule and the Tier & Control Board escalation at two behind
- **Untested-exit risk**: exit clauses and data-export rights go unexercised until the day they are needed; mitigated by funded exit reserves accrued at every QBR, open-format export clauses, and transition-assistance terms verified at signature and renewal

### Automation Opportunity
- Intake checklist auto-scores each vendor release against the regression-pack result and the product's upgrade-currency position (replaces manual Step 1–2 collation).
- Exit-reserve accrual auto-computed from license spend at each QBR (replaces manual Step 5 calculation).

### Controls
