## W3569. Enterprise Architecture Strategy, Framework & Governance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual IT/business strategy (links to VS-33); CIO/ExCo cycle |
| **Frequency** | Annual + on major change |
| **Volume** | Enterprise-wide |
| **Owner** | Chief Enterprise Architect / CIO |
| **Participants** | Chief Enterprise Architect, CIO, Business Capability Owners, IT Leadership (links to VS-27/VS-30/VS-99), Strategy (links to VS-33), PMO (links to VS-112), Security (links to VS-27.3), Data Office (links to VS-28.2) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Chief Enterprise Architect/CIO define the EA strategy and framework: EA charter and scope (business, data, application, technology, security architectures), the EA framework/method adoption (TOGAF or equivalent adapted), the link to business strategy and capability model (links to VS-33), and the governance operating model (board, authorities, cadence links to W3571) | Chief Enterprise Architect / CIO | CIO | Annual |
| 2 | Chief Enterprise Architect/Leadership establish the EA function and governance: EA team and roles, the Architecture Review Board (ARB links to W3571), the principles/standards/reference-architecture program (W3570), the technology-standards/approved-list (W3572), and the integration to the PMO (links to VS-112/W3546) and IT operations (links to VS-27) | Chief Enterprise Architect / IT Leadership | CIO | Annual |
| 3 | CIO/Chief Enterprise Architect ratify the EA strategy and governance with ExCo (links to VS-33/VS-36), secure the EA investment (links to VS-40/W3588), communicate the mandate to the organization, and report EA posture to the board (links to VS-36) and audit (links to VS-21) | CIO / Chief Enterprise Architect | CEO | Annual |

### System Touchpoints
- Strategy (links to VS-33); governance (links to VS-36); PMO (links to VS-112); IT operations/security (links to VS-27); ITAM (links to VS-99); repository (links to W3575); capex/finance (links to VS-40)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **No-mandate risk**: EA function without executive mandate is ignored; mitigated by board-ratified charter and ARB authority (W3571)
- **Framework-over-engineering risk**: Heavy framework with no business value; mitigated by pragmatic, capability-aligned scope


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-capture of "Enterprise Architect/CIO define the EA strategy and framework" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "Chief Enterprise Architect/Leadership establish the EA function and governance" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-post of "posture to the board" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-144 (ensure architecture standards & exception control.)
- operational: board-ratified charter and ARB authority (W3571); operational: pragmatic, capability-aligned scope

### Time Estimate
- 4–6 weeks annual

## W3571. Architecture Review Board & Solution Architecture Review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New initiative/project intake (links to VS-112/W3546); significant change request |
| **Frequency** | Per project + recurring ARB cycle |
| **Volume** | All in-flight and proposed initiatives |
| **Owner** | Architecture Review Board (ARB) |
| **Participants** | ARB (Chief Enterprise Architect + Domain Architects + Security links to VS-27.3 + Data links to VS-28.2), Project/Solution Architects, PMO (links to VS-112), Sponsor, IT Operations (links to VS-27.1) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Solution Architect/Project prepare the solution-architecture artifact for the initiative: business context, requirements, proposed solution (applications, integration, data, deployment, security), alignment to principles/reference-architectures (W3570) and standards (W3572), alternatives considered, and risks/assumptions; submit to ARB | Solution Architect / Project | Chief Enterprise Architect | Per initiative |
| 2 | ARB review the solution architecture: principle/standard conformance (W3570/W3572), reuse vs. build/buy, integration approach (W3579), data implications (W3581), security posture (W3583), operational readiness (links to VS-27.1), and total cost (links to W3588); decision — approve, approve-with-conditions, revise, or reject; capture conditions and waivers (W3573) | ARB | Chief Enterprise Architect | Per initiative |
| 3 | ARB/Solution Architect track conformance to the approved architecture through delivery (links to VS-112/W3554), capture deviations and waivers (W3573), update the repository (W3575) and reference architectures (W3570) with learnings, and feed maturity/CI (W3576) | ARB / Solution Architect | Chief Enterprise Architect | Initiative life |

### System Touchpoints
- PMIS/intake (links to VS-112/W3546); repository (links to W3575); standards (links to W3572); security (links to VS-27.3); operations (links to VS-27.1); data (links to VS-28.2)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Rubber-stamp risk**: ARB approves without rigor; mitigated by clear criteria and quorum
- **Bottleneck risk**: ARB backlog stalls projects; mitigated by tiered review (light for minor, full for major) and cadence

- System auto-review of "review the solution architecture" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-track of "track conformance to the approved architecture through delivery" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-568 (ensure controlled execution — Enterprise Architecture Framework, Standards & Governance (PA-113.1).)
- operational: clear criteria and quorum; operational: tiered review (light for minor, full for major) and cadence

### Time Estimate
- Per initiative + recurring ARB

## W5517. SEP Paved Road & Engineering Standard Governance for Built Products

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New built-product service starting (golden-path bootstrap); ring expansion; security-gate result; quarterly QBR cycle |
| **Frequency** | Continuous + per release ring + quarterly reporting |
| **Volume** | 2 built products (OMO, TPS) × ~6–12 services each; 100% of new services start on the golden path minus ARB-recorded exceptions; every deploy rides the ring model (~weekly per squad) |
| **Owner** | Head of Engineering (SEP) |
| **Participants** | Head of Engineering, SEP DevEx engineers, AppSec engineer (dotted to SEC lead), QA automation lead, build SRE, OMO/TPS Tech Leads & squad PMs, ARB (exception records — links to W3573), INFRA SRE (PRR co-owner), SEC GRC cell (Tier-1-touching changes), IAP (contract-first integration — links to W3579), DP (data contracts) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | DevEx bootstrap every new service from the SEP golden-path templates — repo layout, CI/CD, observability, IaC; any deviation requires an ARB-recorded exception (W3573), and paved-road adoption (100% minus recorded exceptions) is reported at QBR | DevEx engineers / Squad Tech Lead | Head of Engineering | 0.5–1 day/service bootstrap |
| 2 | SEP enforce the engineering standard: trunk-based development with feature flags (no long-lived branches); contract-first integration — every cross-product call is an IAP-published contract with consumer-driven contract tests, no direct database access (links to W3579); data contracts on every DP-published dataset | SEP QA-automation lead / IAP / DP | Head of Engineering | Continuous |
| 3 | Squads deploy on the ring model — internal ring → canary stores/DCs → fleet — with automated SLO-burn rollback gates, independent of the ERP monthly train; the AppSec engineer runs the security gates: SAST + dependency scan on every merge, DAST before each ring expansion, SBOM per release, secrets never in code — and can block a ring expansion | Squad engineers / AppSec engineer | Head of Engineering | Per deploy |
| 4 | SEP + INFRA run the production readiness review before the first fleet ring: on-call runbook, SLOs, capacity model, DR posture — typhoon-season resilience is a launch criterion, not a follow-up | SEP build SRE / INFRA SRE | Head of Engineering | 8–16 hours/service |
| 5 | Head of Engineering report squad DORA targets at QBR — deployment frequency ≥ weekly, change lead time < 1 week, MTTR < 4 hours, change-failure rate < 15% — alongside product outcome KPIs; changes touching Tier-1 workflows carry Tier & Control Board sign-off (links to W1409/W391) | Head of Engineering / SEP | CIO (QBR) | Quarterly |

### System Touchpoints
- SEP golden-path service templates (repo layout, CI/CD, observability, IaC provisioning)
- Ring-deployment infrastructure (internal → canary → fleet) with automated SLO-burn rollback gates
- AppSec gate suite — SAST, dependency scan, DAST, SBOM, secrets scanning
- Contract-testing harness against IAP-published contracts (links to W3579)
- DORA dashboards & the ARB exception register (links to W3573)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Paved-road-drift risk**: squads assemble private toolchains that fragment delivery and security posture; mitigated by golden-path templates, the ARB-exception record, and QBR adoption reporting (100% minus exceptions)
- **Fleet-first risk**: a change reaches the fleet without canary evidence or security gates; mitigated by automated SLO-burn rollback gates, the AppSec block right on ring expansion, and the production-readiness review as a launch criterion

### Automation Opportunity
- Golden-path bootstrap auto-provisions repo, CI/CD, observability and IaC from the service template (replaces manual Step 1 assembly).
- DORA metrics auto-aggregated from pipeline and incident telemetry (replaces manual Step 5 collation).

### Controls
- CTL-568 (ensure controlled execution — Enterprise Architecture Framework, Standards & Governance (PA-113.1).)
- operational: 100% golden-path starts — deviations only via ARB-recorded exception (W3573)
- operational: security gates — SAST + dependency scan per merge, DAST before ring expansion, SBOM per release; AppSec block right
- operational: production readiness review (SEP + INFRA) before first fleet ring

### Time Estimate
Production readiness reviews ~8–16 hours/service × ~12–24 first-fleet services/year ≈ 96–384 hours/year; exception reviews ~2–4 hours/exception × ~6–12 exceptions/year ≈ 12–48 hours/year; quarterly DORA/adoption reporting ~4–6 hours/quarter × 4 quarters ≈ 16–24 hours/year. Total **≈ 124–456 hours/year** (≈0.06–0.22 FTE), absorbed within the sized SEP team.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed within the sized SEP platform team (Head of Engineering + DevEx ×2 + AppSec + QA-automation lead + designer + build SRE, OM §5.3/§9.1); the standard is enforced by the paved road, not added headcount.
