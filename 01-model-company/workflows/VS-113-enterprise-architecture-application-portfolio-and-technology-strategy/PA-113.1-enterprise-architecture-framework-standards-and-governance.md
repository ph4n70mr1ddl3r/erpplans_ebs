### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | DevEx bootstrap every new service from the SEP golden-path templates — repo layout, CI/CD, observability, IaC; any deviation requires an ARB-recorded exception (W3573), and paved-road adoption (100% minus recorded exceptions) is reported at QBR | DevEx engineers / Squad Tech Lead | Head of Engineering | 0.5–1 day/service bootstrap |
| 2 | SEP enforce the engineering standard: trunk-based development with feature flags (no long-lived branches); contract-first integration — every cross-product call is an IAP-published contract with consumer-driven contract tests, no direct database access (links to W3579); data contracts on every DP-published dataset | SEP QA-automation lead / IAP / DP | Head of Engineering | Continuous |
| 3 | Squads deploy on the ring model — internal ring → canary stores/DCs → fleet — with automated SLO-burn rollback gates, independent of the ERP monthly train; the AppSec engineer runs the security gates: SAST + dependency scan on every merge, DAST before each ring expansion, SBOM per release, secrets never in code — and can block a ring expansion | Squad engineers / AppSec engineer | Head of Engineering | Per deploy |
| 4 | SEP + INFRA run the production readiness review before the first fleet ring: on-call runbook, SLOs, capacity model, DR posture — typhoon-season resilience is a launch criterion, not a follow-up | SEP build SRE / INFRA SRE | Head of Engineering | 8–16 hours/service |
| 5 | Head of Engineering report squad DORA targets at QBR — deployment frequency ≥ weekly, change lead time < 1 week, MTTR < 4 hours, change-failure rate < 15% — alongside product outcome KPIs; changes touching Tier-1 workflows carry Tier & Control Board sign-off (links to W1409/W391) | Head of Engineering / SEP | CIO (QBR) | Quarterly |

### System Touchpoints
- SEP golden-path service templates (repo layout, CI/CD, observability, IaC provisioning)
- Ring-deployment infrastructure (internal → canary → fleet) with automated SLO-burn rollback gates
- AppSec gate suite — SAST, dependency scan, DAST, SBOM, secrets scanning
- Contract-testing harness against IAP-published contracts (links to W3579)
- DORA dashboards & the ARB exception register (links to W3573)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Paved-road-drift risk**: squads assemble private toolchains that fragment delivery and security posture; mitigated by golden-path templates, the ARB-exception record, and QBR adoption reporting (100% minus exceptions)
- **Fleet-first risk**: a change reaches the fleet without canary evidence or security gates; mitigated by automated SLO-burn rollback gates, the AppSec block right on ring expansion, and the production-readiness review as a launch criterion

### Automation Opportunity
- Golden-path bootstrap auto-provisions repo, CI/CD, observability and IaC from the service template (replaces manual Step 1 assembly).
- DORA metrics auto-aggregated from pipeline and incident telemetry (replaces manual Step 5 collation).

### Controls
- CTL-568 (ensure controlled execution — Enterprise Architecture Framework, Standards & Governance (PA-113.1).)
- operational: 100% golden-path starts — deviations only via ARB-recorded exception (W3573)
- operational: security gates — SAST + dependency scan per merge, DAST before ring expansion, SBOM per release; AppSec block right
- operational: production readiness review (SEP + INFRA) before first fleet ring

### Time Estimate
Production readiness reviews ~8–16 hours/service × ~12–24 first-fleet services/year ≈ 96–384 hours/year; exception reviews ~2–4 hours/exception × ~6–12 exceptions/year ≈ 12–48 hours/year; quarterly DORA/adoption reporting ~4–6 hours/quarter × 4 quarters ≈ 16–24 hours/year. Total **≈ 124–456 hours/year** (≈0.06–0.22 FTE), absorbed within the sized SEP team.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed within the sized SEP platform team (Head of Engineering + DevEx ×2 + AppSec + QA-automation lead + designer + build SRE, OM §5.3/§9.1); the standard is enforced by the paved road, not added headcount.

### System Touchpoints
- Customization register (CIO Office secretariat) — decision records, sign-offs, budget consumption by class
- Architecture repository & decision log (links to W3575) — ARB records and conditions
- Capability sourcing register (links to W5515) — confirms no build product is assigned to the gap
- Change management & CAB (links to W132/W1409) — approved conditions carried into delivery
- Internal-control register — the control-mapping appendix and post-change evidence path
- TBM/FinOps cost model (links to VS-135/W4100) — three-year build-and-run verification

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Ladder-skip risk**: a team proposes an extension without documenting the standard, configuration and personalization attempts, so the gate rubber-stamps a gap that the delivered product already closes; mitigated by the returned-not-assessed rule at Step 1 and the ARB's authority to deny back to the standard tier
- **Budget-erosion risk**: caps are exceeded one exception at a time until the estate accretes rather than trades, leaving every future patch a manual merge; mitigated by the paired-retirement commitment at Step 3 and the quarterly budget-consumption report to the SIB
- **Undocumented-object risk**: an extension ships without its record, so nothing downstream knows the object exists or who re-verifies it at patch time; mitigated by the register entry at Step 6 as the ship gate and the quarterly inventory audit (links to W5576)

### Automation Opportunity
- Ladder-evidence completeness auto-checked against the proposal template before the assessment slot opens (replaces manual Step 1 screening).
- Budget consumption by class auto-computed from the register and rendered against the caps in the decision pack (replaces manual Step 3 tallying).
- Quarterly SIB report auto-drafted from the register's decisions, cycle times and budget state (replaces manual Step 6 collation).

### Controls
- CTL-568 (ensure controlled execution — Enterprise Architecture Framework, Standards & Governance (PA-113.1).)
- operational: no extension without an approved Customization Decision Record naming its controls, owner and retirement trigger
- operational: extension budget enforced by class — exceeding a cap requires CIO sign-off and a paired retirement commitment
- operational: product-modifying changes are inadmissible — the gate may approve extension, personalization, localization and interface classes only

### Time Estimate
CDR authoring and admissibility assessment ~10–16 hours/CDR × ~15–25 CDRs/year ≈ 150–400 hours/year; ARB decision slot ~1 hour/CDR × ~15–25 CDRs/year ≈ 15–25 hours/year; quarterly SIB register report ~3 hours/quarter × 4 quarters ≈ 12 hours/year. Total **≈ 177–437 hours/year** (≈0.09–0.21 FTE), absorbed within the CIO Office.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed within the sized CIO Office (Head of EA + solution architects, OM §5.3/§9.1); the gate formalizes the admissibility judgement the ARB already makes per initiative — no incremental headcount.

## W5577. Extension De-Customization, Retirement & Budget Reclamation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | A vendor release ships equivalent standard function; a register row's usage falls below threshold for two consecutive quarters; a process-mining finding shows the forked path costs more than the gap; a Philippine statute or format change supersedes a localization component; a module re-tiering re-scopes the in-suite footprint |
| **Frequency** | Trigger-driven; retirement review at the quarterly business review; standing target of at least one completion per half-year |
| **Volume** | ~6–10 fired retirement triggers/year against the standing inventory, converting to ~2 completed retirements/year at the steady-state extension budget |
| **Owner** | Head of Enterprise Architecture (retirement backlog owner) |
| **Participants** | Head of Enterprise Architecture, Owning PO & BPO (retirement execution), ARB (initiates retirement on a process-mining finding — links to W3571), Application Manager (links to W132), ERP System Administrator (links to W495), Finance Controller (accounting-adjacent rows), SIB (quarterly review), Internal Audit (links to VS-21) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Head of EA monitor the register's retirement triggers against vendor release content, usage telemetry, process-mining findings (links to W3577/W3592), Philippine regulatory change and in-suite footprint decisions (links to W5515); a fired trigger opens a retirement assessment rather than a discussion | Head of EA | CIO | Continuous |
| 2 | Owning PO & BPO assess the fired trigger: does the standard function reach parity on the workflows and controls the extension carries, what data migrates out, which interfaces and reports depend on the objects, and what the cutover window costs; the assessment ends in a retirement plan with a date or a documented reason the trigger does not apply this cycle | Owning PO & BPO | Head of EA | 4–8 hours/trigger |
| 3 | Head of EA set the retirement clock on a parity trigger — the owning team has two quarters to adopt the standard function and retire the extension; a slip is escalated to the SIB with the run cost the estate keeps paying, not renegotiated silently | Head of EA | CIO | Per fired trigger |
| 4 | Owning Team IT PO execute the retirement through the standard delivery rails (links to W132/W1409): migrate data out, re-point dependent interfaces and reports, re-map the affected controls to their standard-function evidence path, drop the objects, and confirm the drop in the next environment diff (links to W5576) | Owning Team IT PO / Application Manager | Head of EA | 24–60 hours/extension |
| 5 | Head of EA close the register row as retired, reclaim its budget slot by class, release any paired-retirement commitment the gate booked against it (links to W5575), and report the completion at the quarterly review with the run cost and patch-merge effort the estate stopped carrying | Head of EA | CIO | 2 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Customization register (CIO Office secretariat) — retirement triggers, clocks, closure state and reclaimed budget slots
- Vendor release content and roadmap (links to W495) — the parity trigger source
- Process-mining and portfolio analytics (links to W3577/W3592) — fork-cost findings that initiate retirement
- Change management & CAB (links to W132/W1409) — the retirement execution rail
- Object-inventory audit (links to W5576) — confirms the objects are gone, not merely deactivated
- Internal-control register — control re-mapping to the standard function's evidence path

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Permanent-temporary risk**: an extension outlives the gap it closed because nobody owns retiring it, so every future patch pays a merge cost for a function the product now ships; mitigated by the registered retirement trigger on every row and the two-quarter clock at Step 3
- **Control-orphan risk**: the objects are dropped before the controls they carried are re-mapped, leaving an evidence gap the next assessment finds instead of the retirement plan; mitigated by the control re-mapping inside Step 4 and the closure gate at Step 5
- **Zombie-object risk**: a row is closed administratively while its objects remain in the schema, so the budget is reclaimed against an estate that never shrank; mitigated by confirming the drop in the environment diff before closure

### Automation Opportunity
- Parity triggers auto-matched from vendor release content against the register's declared gap statements (replaces manual Step 1 scanning).
- Usage telemetry auto-compared to the below-threshold rule per row across consecutive quarters (replaces manual Step 1 tracking).
- Budget slots auto-reclaimed on confirmed closure and rendered against the caps in the gate's decision pack (replaces manual Step 5 bookkeeping).

### Controls
- CTL-568 (ensure controlled execution — Enterprise Architecture Framework, Standards & Governance (PA-113.1).)
- operational: every register row carries a retirement trigger; a row without one cannot be approved at the gate
- operational: a parity trigger starts a two-quarter retirement clock, and a slip is escalated to the SIB with its carried run cost
- operational: a row is closed only after the environment diff confirms its objects are gone and its controls are re-mapped

### Time Estimate
Retirement assessment ~4–8 hours/trigger × ~6–10 fired triggers/year ≈ 24–80 hours/year; retirement execution ~24–60 hours/extension × ~2 completions/year ≈ 48–120 hours/year; quarterly retirement review ~2 hours/quarter × 4 quarters ≈ 8 hours/year. Total **≈ 80–208 hours/year** (≈0.04–0.10 FTE), absorbed within the CIO Office and the owning product teams.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed within the sized CIO Office and the rows' owning teams (Head of EA + owning team IT POs, OM §5.3/§9.1); retirement work replaces the patch-merge and run cost the retired extension was already consuming — no incremental headcount.
