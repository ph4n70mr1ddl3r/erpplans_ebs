## W695. Emergency Response & Evacuation Protocol Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual drill schedule; post-incident protocol update; new store opening; regulatory requirement (BFP fire safety inspection) |
| **Frequency** | Semi-annual evacuation drills; quarterly protocol review; continuous protocol maintenance |
| **Volume** | 200 stores, 4 DCs, HQ — each requiring location-specific emergency response plans; ~205 emergency response protocols maintained |
| **Owner** | Safety Officer |
| **Participants** | Store Managers, DC Managers, HR, COO, Local Emergency Services (BFP, barangay), External Safety Consultant |

### Background

BuildRight operates 200 stores and 4 DCs across the Philippine archipelago, exposed to multiple emergency scenarios: earthquakes (Philippine fault zone, Mindanao earthquakes), typhoons (average 20 typhoons/year per PAGASA), fires (BFP-reported commercial fire incidents), flooding (especially during monsoon season), and workplace violence/robbery incidents. DOLE requires documented emergency response plans for all workplaces with >10 employees, and BFP requires semi-annual fire drills as a condition of Fire Safety Inspection Certificate (FSIC) renewal. This workflow manages the lifecycle of emergency response protocols across all locations.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Location-specific emergency plan development**: Safety Officer develops emergency response plan template covering: (a) fire evacuation routes and assembly points; (b) earthquake duck-cover-hold procedure and post-earthquake building assessment; (c) typhoon/flood response (sandbagging, stock elevation, early closure decision per W576); (d) hazmat spill containment per W238; (e) active threat/robbery response protocol; (f) medical emergency response (first aid, AED use, ambulance coordination); (g) bomb threat procedure; (h) power failure response per W470; each plan customized to location layout, staffing, and local emergency service contacts (nearest fire station, hospital, police station, barangay disaster coordinator) | Safety Officer | COO | 1-2 days per new location |
| 2 | **Emergency team appointment**: for each location, Store/DC Manager appoints emergency response team: (a) Floor Wardens (per department/zone — guide evacuation); (b) Assembly Point Coordinator (headcount at assembly area); (c) First Aid Responders (trained per W655 first aid certification); (d) Fire Marshal (fire extinguisher and suppression system operation); (e) Communication Officer (contacts emergency services and HQ); post appointments in system with contact details; update when personnel change | Store/DC Manager | Safety Officer | 2 hours per location; updated as needed |
| 3 | **Semi-annual evacuation drill**: per BFP requirement: (a) Safety Officer schedules drill calendar (avoid peak business hours; alternate between announced and unannounced); (b) conduct drill: activate alarm, execute evacuation, assembly point headcount, all-clear and re-entry; (c) drill evaluation: evacuation time (target <5 minutes for full store evacuation), headcount accuracy (target 100%), identified bottlenecks (blocked exits, malfunctioning alarms); (d) document drill in system with photos, evaluation scores, and corrective actions | Safety Officer / Store Manager | COO | 2-3 hours per drill per location |
| 4 | **Post-drill corrective action**: for any deficiencies identified during drill: (a) blocked or obstructed emergency exits → clear immediately, install monitoring signage; (b) malfunctioning fire alarms or emergency lighting → maintenance request per W188 with emergency priority; (c) emergency team members absent or untrained → schedule makeup training per W655; (d) evacuation time exceeds target → investigate bottleneck and modify floor plan or routing | Store Manager | Safety Officer | 1-5 days per corrective action |
| 5 | **Protocol update trigger**: update emergency response plans when: (a) post-incident review identifies protocol gaps (after any actual emergency event); (b) store layout changes (renovation, new department, fixture rearrangement); (c) new regulatory requirements (BFP, DOLE circulars); (d) new store opening per W702; (e) semi-annual drill identifies needed changes; (f) annual plan review | Safety Officer | COO | 2-4 hours per update |
| 6 | **Emergency communication system testing**: quarterly, IT tests emergency communication cascade: (a) mass notification to all 200 stores via SMS, email, and mobile app; (b) verify message delivery rate (target >95% within 5 minutes); (c) test two-way communication (store confirms receipt and reports status); (d) integrate with W685 BC plan communication protocols | IT Operations | Safety Officer | 2 hours/quarter |
| 7 | **Annual emergency preparedness audit**: Safety Officer conducts annual audit per W141 framework: (a) verify all locations have current emergency response plans on file; (b) verify drill completion (2 drills/year per BFP requirement); (c) verify emergency equipment inspection (fire extinguishers monthly per BFP, AED battery check, first aid kit replenishment); (d) verify emergency team appointments are current; (e) compile audit report for COO and CHRO | Safety Officer | COO | 1-2 weeks |

### System Touchpoints

- Emergency response plan document management per W255
- Drill scheduling and documentation module
- Emergency communication system (mass notification, two-way status reporting)
- Maintenance request integration per W188
- Training management integration per W655
- BC plan integration per W685
- BFP compliance calendar per W506
- Location master data (floor plans, exits, assembly points)

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Drill fatigue and complacency**: 200 stores conducting semi-annual drills creates logistical burden; store staff may treat drills as routine without genuine engagement
- **Multi-floor and multi-exit complexity**: larger BuildRight stores may have complex layouts with multiple exits, loading docks, and mezzanine levels requiring detailed evacuation planning
- **Barangay coordination**: emergency response in the Philippines relies heavily on barangay (local village) disaster risk reduction councils; BuildRight stores must coordinate with local barangay officials who have varying levels of disaster preparedness
- **Night shift coverage**: DCs operating night shifts have reduced staffing and may lack trained emergency team members during low-attendance periods
- **Post-disaster protocol execution**: after a major typhoon or earthquake, standard evacuation protocols may be insufficient (building structural damage, flooded roads, overwhelmed local emergency services)


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-route of "routes and assembly points" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-count of "headcount at assembly area" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "document drill in system with photos, evaluation scores, and corrective actions" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-314 (ensure controlled execution — Emergency Preparedness (PA-24.2).)
- operational: Safety Officer review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication

- **Safety Officer**: ~4–6 hours/month on drill coordination and protocol maintenance. Absorbed within existing role.
- **Store/DC Managers**: ~2–3 hours/drill (2 drills/year) + 2 hours/year on protocol review. Absorbed within existing role.
- **IT Operations**: ~2 hours/quarter on communication system testing. Absorbed within existing role.
- **No incremental headcount**.

### Time Estimate

- Plan development (new location): 1-2 days
- Semi-annual drill: 2-3 hours per location per drill
- Post-drill corrective action: 1-5 days
- Annual audit: 1-2 weeks
- Communication system test: 2 hours/quarter

## W696. Contractor & Visitor Safety Induction & Access Control

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Contractor arrives at BuildRight location for work; vendor representative visits for meeting; government inspector arrives for inspection per W658; delivery driver not on approved list; job applicant or visitor enters operational area |
| **Frequency** | Continuous (~100–200 contractor/visitor entries/day across all locations) |
| **Volume** | ~25,000–50,000 contractor/visitor entries/year; major contractors: construction/renovation crews, IT installers, maintenance technicians, cleaning services, vendor merchandisers |
| **Owner** | Store/DC Manager (location-level); Facilities Manager (HQ) |
| **Participants** | Security Guard, Contractor/Vendor, Safety Officer, Department Supervisor |

### Background

BuildRight's 200 stores, 4 DCs, and HQ receive continuous visits from contractors, vendors, government inspectors, and other third parties. W187 covers general contractor safety orientation, but this workflow addresses the day-to-day operational process of inducting and controlling access for all non-employee visitors to BuildRight locations — including verifying safety qualifications, issuing access credentials, monitoring activities, and ensuring proper checkout. DOLE requires that employers ensure workplace safety for all persons on premises, including contractors and visitors.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pre-arrival registration**: for planned contractor/vendor visits: (a) host department registers visitor/contractor in system: name, company, purpose, expected duration, areas to access, host employee; (b) for contractors performing physical work: system verifies contractor has valid safety orientation record per W187 and required certifications per W655 (e.g., working at heights, electrical safety); (c) system generates visitor/contractor pass with access level: General (office/retail areas only), Operational (warehouse/DC floor with escort), Restricted (requires Safety Officer escort + specific permit) | Department Supervisor | Store/DC Manager | 10-15 min |
| 2 | **Arrival check-in**: Security Guard verifies: (a) visitor/contractor identity (government ID); (b) matches pre-registration or registers walk-in visitor; (c) issues color-coded visitor badge with photo, access level, and expiry time; (d) for contractors: verify PPE compliance (hard hat, safety shoes, high-visibility vest per W187 requirements); (e) for government inspectors: follow W658 inspection response protocol; (f) captures entry timestamp in visitor log | Security Guard | Store/DC Manager | 5-10 min |
| 3 | **Safety briefing**: for visitors entering operational areas (DC floor, construction zones, maintenance areas): (a) Security Guard or designated staff provides 5-minute safety briefing: emergency exits, assembly point, restricted areas, PPE requirements, prohibited activities (photography without authorization per data privacy); (b) for contractors performing work: review specific hazards in work area (e.g., forklift traffic in DC, electrical hazards, overhead work); (c) visitor/contractor signs safety acknowledgment | Security Guard / Safety Officer | Store/DC Manager | 5-10 min |
| 4 | **Activity monitoring**: during visit/contractor work: (a) host employee or designated escort monitors visitor/contractor compliance with safety rules; (b) for extended contractor work (>1 day): daily check-in with contractor supervisor to confirm safety compliance; (c) Security Guard conducts periodic checks on contractors in restricted areas; (d) any safety violation: Security Guard or Safety Officer issues verbal warning, then written warning, then removal from premises for repeated violations | Host Employee / Security Guard | Store/DC Manager | Ongoing during visit |
| 5 | **Incident handling**: if visitor/contractor is involved in any safety incident: (a) respond per W140 incident management protocol; (b) provide first aid if needed; (c) document incident in visitor/contractor incident log; (d) notify contractor's employer per contractual obligation; (e) for serious incidents: DOLE reporting per W436 may apply | Safety Officer | Store/DC Manager | Per W140 |
| 6 | **Checkout & badge return**: upon departure: (a) visitor/contractor returns badge to Security Guard; (b) Security Guard records exit timestamp; (c) host confirms work completion (for contractors); (d) for contractors: system records work completion for contractor performance tracking; (e) visitor log entry closed | Security Guard | Store/DC Manager | 5 min |
| 7 | **Monthly access compliance reporting**: Security Guard team lead compiles monthly report: total entries by category (contractor, vendor, visitor, inspector), safety briefing completion rate, PPE compliance rate, incident count, average visit duration; report shared with Safety Officer and Store/DC Manager | Security Team Lead | Safety Officer | 2 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints

- Visitor/contractor management module (registration, badge printing, log)
- Contractor safety orientation record per W187
- Certification verification per W655
- Security guard post management system
- Incident management integration per W140
- W658 government inspection response integration
- Data privacy compliance for visitor PII per RA 10173

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Walk-in contractor management**: emergency repairs (plumbing leak, electrical fault) require immediate contractor access without pre-registration; expedited safety briefing and PPE verification needed
- **Badge sharing and tailgating**: contractors or visitors sharing badges or following authorized personnel through access points without check-in
- **Visitor PII data privacy**: visitor logs containing names, ID numbers, and photos are subject to RA 10173; must be stored securely and retained only as long as legally required per W256
- **Language barriers**: foreign contractor specialists (e.g., equipment vendors from Japan, Germany) may not understand safety briefings in English or Filipino; translated materials or interpreters needed
- **Government inspector access**: cannot deny entry to authorized government inspectors; must balance safety compliance with cooperative regulatory relationship per W658

- System auto-record of "record per W187 and required certifications per W655" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "log" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-capture of "entering operational areas" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-314 (ensure controlled execution — Emergency Preparedness (PA-24.2).)
- operational: per W256

### Staffing Implication

- **Security Guards**: ~10–15 min per visitor/contractor for check-in/out. Absorbed within existing security staffing (~2–4 guards per location).
- **Store/DC Manager**: ~15–30 min/day on contractor coordination. Absorbed within existing role.
- **Safety Officer**: ~4–6 hours/month on monthly reporting and incident follow-up. Absorbed within existing role.
- **No incremental headcount**.

### Time Estimate

- Pre-registration: 10-15 min per planned visit
- Check-in: 5-10 min per visitor/contractor
- Safety briefing: 5-10 min per visitor to operational areas
- Activity monitoring: ongoing during work
- Checkout: 5 min per visitor/contractor
- Monthly reporting: 2 hours

## W759. Store-Level Hazardous Material Customer Advisory & Safe Handling Guidance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer purchases or inquires about hazardous products (chemicals, pesticides, solvents, adhesives, paint, sealants, asbestos-containing materials) |
| **Frequency** | Daily; ~20–30 hazmat customer interactions per store per day |
| **Volume** | 200 stores × ~25 hazmat interactions/day = ~5,000 interactions/day chain-wide |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Chemicals/Building Materials) |
| **Participants** | Floor Associate, Department Supervisor, Customer |

### Background
As a hardware and home improvement retailer, BuildRight Depot sells products regulated by the Fertilizer and Pesticide Authority (FPA), DENR, and FDA (W479). Customers purchasing pesticides, industrial solvents, strong adhesives, and other hazardous materials need proper handling, storage, and disposal guidance. Providing this guidance is both a legal obligation and a customer safety imperative that reduces product liability (W185).

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hazmat product identification — When customer inquires about or scans a hazardous product, system identifies hazard classification based on item master GHS (Globally Harmonized System) attributes (W252): flammable, corrosive, toxic, oxidizer, irritant, environmental hazard | System | — | Instant |
| 2 | Customer advisory prompt — Floor associate receives system prompt with key safety information: (a) primary hazards, (b) required PPE, (c) safe handling procedures, (d) first aid measures, (e) proper disposal guidance | System / Floor Associate | — | 1 min |
| 3 | Verbal safety briefing — Floor associate provides verbal safety briefing to customer covering: (a) what the product is designed for, (b) what NOT to do with it, (c) required ventilation and PPE, (d) storage away from children and pets, (e) proper disposal per LGU regulations | Floor Associate | — | 3 min |
| 4 | SDS offer — Floor associate offers customer a printed Safety Data Sheet (SDS) per W698 SDS lifecycle management; for regular trade customers, associate confirms they have current SDS on file | Floor Associate | — | 1 min |
| 5 | Alternative suggestion — For high-hazard products where a safer alternative exists (e.g., water-based vs. solvent-based paint), floor associate suggests the safer alternative | Floor Associate | — | 2 min |
| 6 | Purchase quantity flag — System flags unusually large quantity purchases of regulated products (e.g., > 5L of industrial solvent, > 2kg of pesticide); prompts cashier to verify intended use during checkout (W5B) | System / Cashier | — | 1 min |
| 7 | Customer acknowledgment — For high-hazard purchases, customer signs digital acknowledgment of receiving safety guidance; archived per W256 retention policy for W185 product liability defense | Customer | Floor Associate | 1 min |
| 8 | Incident link — If customer later reports adverse reaction or injury from product, advisory acknowledgment and SDS provided are retrieved from archives for W185 product liability and W140 OHS incident processing | System | — | Automated |

### System Touchpoints
- W252 item master — GHS hazard classification attributes per SKU
- W698 SDS lifecycle management — current SDS availability and distribution
- W185 product liability — advisory documentation for liability defense
- W479 FDA LTO compliance — HHS product registration tracking
- W467 specialized hardware permits — FPA-regulated product tracking
- W236/W237 hazmat storage and handling — store-side hazmat compliance
- Digital acknowledgment capture with 10-year archival per W256

### Pain Points / Risks
- Customer resistance to "lectures" — some customers perceive safety guidance as condescending, especially professional contractors who use the products daily
- Floor associate knowledge gap — associates may not be familiar with all hazardous products in their department; system prompts compensate but cannot replace product knowledge
- SDS availability — maintaining current SDS for thousands of chemical SKUs requires vendor cooperation and systematic refresh; outdated SDS create liability
- Over-flagging — if every cleaning product triggers an advisory, associates and customers will experience alert fatigue; classification thresholds must be calibrated


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-scan of "scans a hazardous product, system identifies hazard classification based on…" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-measure of "Customer advisory prompt — Floor associate receives system prompt" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-generate of "printed Safety Data Sheet" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-314 (ensure controlled execution — Emergency Preparedness (PA-24.2).)
- operational: prompts compensate but cannot replace product knowledge

### Time Estimate
Per interaction: 5–8 minutes total (1 min identification + 3 min briefing + 1 min SDS offer + 1 min acknowledgment + 2 min optional alternative suggestion).

### Staffing Implication
No incremental headcount. Hazardous material advisory is absorbed within existing floor associate duties. Additional training: 4 hours per associate on GHS hazard classification and safety communication, refreshed annually per W655 safety training. 200 stores × 5,000 interactions/day × 6 min = ~500 staff-hours/day, distributed across existing floor staff.

## W806. Annual Fire Safety System Testing, Certification & BFP Compliance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual fire safety system testing schedule; BFP inspection notice per W476; fire safety certificate renewal; post-incident testing after fire event |
| **Frequency** | Annual per location; ad-hoc after fire incidents or BFP orders |
| **Volume** | 200 stores + 4 DCs + HQ = 205 locations; each requires annual fire safety system certification |
| **Owner** | Facilities Manager (per location) |
| **Participants** | BFP-Licensed Fire Safety Inspector, DC/Store Manager, Fire Safety Officer, BFP Regional Office, Insurance Provider |

### Background

The Philippine Fire Code (RA 9514) requires all buildings to maintain functional fire safety systems and obtain annual Fire Safety Inspection Certificate (FSIC) from the Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP). BuildRight's 200 stores and 4 DCs have fire alarm systems, sprinkler systems (for larger stores), fire extinguishers, emergency lighting, fire exit signage, and smoke detectors. Annual testing and certification by BFP-licensed fire safety inspectors is mandatory for FSIC renewal per W476. Failure to maintain current FSIC risks BFP closure order, LGU Business Permit non-renewal per W54, and insurance claim denial per W610 in the event of fire.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Annual Testing Schedule**: Facilities Manager develops annual testing schedule: (a) schedule BFP-licensed fire safety inspector for each location 60 days before FSIC expiry; (b) group proximate stores on same inspector visit for efficiency; (c) ensure all 205 locations tested within 12-month rolling window; (d) schedule tracked in compliance calendar per W506 | Facilities Manager | COO | 1-2 days/year planning |
| 2 | **Pre-Inspection Readiness**: Before inspector visit, Facilities Manager verifies: (a) all fire extinguishers are serviced and within expiry date (monthly inspection per W758 confirms daily visual check); (b) fire alarm panels show no active faults or trouble conditions; (c) sprinkler system pressure gauges within normal range (for equipped locations); (d) emergency lighting functional (tested per W758 monthly); (e) fire exits unobstructed and properly signed; (f) fire exit doors functional and not locked; (g) fire safety signage current and visible | Facilities Manager / Store Safety Officer | Store Manager | 2-4 hours per location |
| 3 | **Fire Safety System Testing**: BFP-licensed inspector conducts annual testing: (a) fire alarm system: test all smoke detectors, heat detectors, manual pull stations, horn/strobe notification devices; verify panel programming and zone mapping; (b) sprinkler system: visual inspection of all heads, flow test, alarm valve test, fire pump test (for equipped locations); (c) fire extinguishers: verify type, placement, pressure gauge, seal integrity, service date tag; (d) emergency lighting: simulate power failure, verify illumination duration (minimum 90 minutes per Fire Code); (e) fire exit routes: walk all exit paths, verify signage, clearance, door hardware functionality; (f) fire suppression kitchen hood system (for stores with food service areas) | BFP-Licensed Inspector | Facilities Manager | 4-8 hours per location |
| 4 | **Deficiency Remediation**: If inspector identifies deficiencies: (a) classify by severity: critical (system non-functional — must fix before FSIC can be issued), major (partial functionality — fix within 30 days), minor (cosmetic/administrative — fix within 90 days); (b) Facilities Manager dispatches maintenance technician or fire safety contractor for repair; (c) critical deficiencies: fire watch protocol activated until repair completed; (d) re-inspection by BFP-licensed inspector after remediation; (e) all costs tracked per location maintenance budget per W47 | Facilities Manager | Store Manager | 1-30 days depending on severity |
| 5 | **FSIC Application**: After successful testing: (a) Facilities Manager submits FSIC application to BFP Regional Office: fire safety inspection report, building occupancy permit, floor plan showing fire exits, fire safety system certificates; (b) BFP issues FSIC valid for 1 year; (c) FSIC displayed prominently in store per BFP requirement; (d) FSIC copy filed in Document Management System per W255; (e) FSIC copy provided to LGU for Business Permit renewal per W54; (f) FSIC copy provided to insurance provider per W610 | Facilities Manager / Fire Safety Officer | COO | 1-2 weeks |
| 6 | **Annual Compliance Report**: COO reviews annual fire safety compliance: (a) % locations with current FSIC (target: 100%); (b) deficiency rate by type and severity; (c) remediation timeliness; (d) locations with recurring deficiencies; (e) fire safety maintenance cost trend; (f) report to CEO and Board per W124 corporate governance | COO | CEO | 1 day/year |

### System Touchpoints

- Compliance calendar per W506 with FSIC expiry tracking per location
- Fire safety inspection checklist mobile app
- Work order system for deficiency remediation per W47
- Document Management System per W255 for FSIC and inspection report filing
- BFP FSIC application tracking
- Insurance policy integration per W610 for certification evidence
- LGU Business Permit integration per W54 for FSIC submission
- Analytics dashboard for annual compliance reporting

### Pain Points / Risks

- **BFP-licensed inspector shortage**: limited pool of BFP-licensed fire safety inspectors, especially in provincial areas; scheduling may require 4-8 week advance booking
- **BFP FSIC processing delays**: BFP regional offices may take 2-4 weeks to issue FSIC after successful inspection; stores cannot operate with expired FSIC in strict LGU jurisdictions
- **Sprinkler system testing disruption**: flow testing sprinkler systems requires temporary system shutdown, which means the building has reduced fire protection during testing; must schedule during lowest-risk hours
- **Fire extinguisher servicing logistics**: 200 stores × 10-15 extinguishers per store = 2,000-3,000 extinguishers requiring annual servicing; logistics of collection, servicing, and return is significant
- **Budget pressure on fire safety maintenance**: fire safety maintenance is a non-revenue cost that store managers may deprioritize; centralized scheduling and budget allocation mitigates

- System auto-validate of "check" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-route of "Fire Safety System Testing" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-314 (ensure controlled execution — Emergency Preparedness (PA-24.2).)
- operational: Facilities Manager (per location) review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication

- **Facilities Manager**: ~4–6 hours/month on fire safety testing coordination; absorbed by existing role (shared across W47, W700, W701)
- **Store Safety Officers**: ~2–4 hours per annual inspection per location; absorbed by existing role

## W1223. Store-Level Emergency Generator Operations & Extended Power Outage Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Commercial power failure at store location; or brownout announced by electric utility; or generator automatic transfer switch (ATS) activates |
| **Frequency** | 4–8 power outages per store per month in provincial areas; 1–2 per month in Metro Manila; extended outages (> 4 hours) ~2–4 times per year per store |
| **Volume** | 200 stores × ~50 outage events/year (including brief flickers) = ~10,000 generator activations/year; extended outages affecting 20–50 stores simultaneously during grid failures |
| **Owner** | Maintenance/Utility Staff |
| **Participants** | Maintenance/Utility Staff, Store Manager, IT Helpdesk, Facilities Maintenance, Electric Utility Customer Service |

### Background

The Philippine power grid experiences frequent outages, particularly in provincial areas where BuildRight Depot has 170 of its 200 stores. Causes include: typhoon damage to transmission lines, overloaded distribution during peak demand (summer aircon season), rotational brownouts in underserved areas, and equipment failure in aging infrastructure. BuildRight stores require continuous power for: POS systems (offline capability covers up to 8 hours per W5G), refrigeration (paint storage temperature control), security systems (CCTV, alarm, EAS), lighting (customer safety), and air conditioning (customer comfort in tropical climate). Each store is equipped with an emergency diesel generator sized to support essential loads (POS, lighting, security, paint refrigeration) but typically NOT full air conditioning (generators sized for 30–40% of total electrical load to manage fuel consumption and capital cost). This workflow governs generator activation, fuel management during extended outages, and coordination with electric utility for restoration.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Power failure**: ATS detects commercial power loss and automatically starts generator; generator reaches operating speed and transfers load within 10–30 seconds; if ATS fails to auto-start: Maintenance Staff manually starts generator per posted emergency procedure | System / Maintenance Staff | Store Manager | Automated (10–30 sec) |
| 2 | Maintenance Staff performs **generator startup check**: (a) verify generator is running and producing power, (b) check generator control panel for warning lights or fault codes, (c) confirm essential loads powered: POS systems, lighting (minimum 50%), CCTV and security, paint area refrigeration; (d) confirm non-essential loads shed: air conditioning (most units), decorative lighting, non-critical equipment | Maintenance Staff | Store Manager | 5 min |
| 3 | Store Manager assesses customer impact: (a) if outage during store hours: announce to customers via PA system that store remains operational on generator power; apologize for reduced air conditioning; (b) reduce POS terminals to 2 (from 3) to manage generator load; (c) if outage during non-store hours: no customer impact; generator maintains security systems and refrigeration | Store Manager | — | 5 min |
| 4 | **Fuel monitoring** (if outage extends beyond 2 hours): Maintenance Staff checks generator fuel gauge: (a) full tank provides ~8–12 hours at essential load, (b) if outage projected to exceed 8 hours: Maintenance Staff contacts Facilities Maintenance for emergency fuel delivery per W913, (c) Store Manager implements fuel conservation: reduce lighting to emergency-only, pause paint refrigeration cycling (short-term acceptable), reduce POS terminals to 1 | Maintenance Staff / Store Manager | Facilities Maintenance | Ongoing |
| 5 | **Extended outage management** (> 4 hours): (a) Store Manager contacts electric utility for estimated restoration time; (b) if restoration > 12 hours: Store Manager considers early store closure (revenue loss vs. fuel cost and customer comfort); (c) if restoration > 24 hours: Regional Manager authorizes store closure; Maintenance Staff configures generator for security-only load (CCTV, alarm, lighting) to conserve fuel; (d) IT Helpdesk monitors POS offline status — if offline approaching 8-hour threshold per W5G, Store Manager executes offline POS procedure | Store Manager / Regional Manager | COO | Event-driven |
| 6 | **Power restoration**: ATS detects commercial power return; (a) ATS automatically transfers load back to commercial power after 5-minute stability verification, (b) generator runs cooldown cycle (5 min unloaded) then shuts down, (c) Maintenance Staff verifies: all systems on commercial power, POS terminals restored to full operation, air conditioning restarts, no equipment damage from power transition | System / Maintenance Staff | Store Manager | 10 min |

| **Volume** | 1 site × full evacuation population (store: ~467 customers/day + 29 staff; DC: ~120–180 staff/shift) × all fire-load zones (paint/solvents, lumber yard, battery-charging stations, rack storage) |
| **Owner** | Store Manager / DC Operations Manager |
| **Participants** | Store/DC Manager, all floor/shift staff (sweep & muster roles), Safety Officer, Security Guard, Facilities Coordinator, BFP (first-alarm response), Regional Manager (multi-site escalation), Insurance/BCP (VS-26 W876 claim chain), VP Legal, HSE Manager, POS/Cash Office (W850 cash securing) |

### Background

W806 owns the annual fire-safety system certification and W1296 the monthly equipment inspection — both are *prevention* surfaces. W330 provides the generic in-store emergency classification, and W140/W436 cover the injury-reporting chain, but no workflow owned the *fire event itself*: suppression activation, evacuation coordination with BFP, post-fire structural assessment, the BFP clearance-to-reopen gate, or the insurance chain. RA 9514 (Fire Code) and its BFP IRR make the occupant responsible for evacuation planning and post-fire clearance before re-occupancy; a retailer trading after a fire without BFP clearance faces citation, insurance-claim denial, and premises-liability exposure (links to VS-147). This workflow is the event canon per the E-19 custody ruling: prevention/testing stays with W806/W1296, the claim trail lands in W876, and the generic protocol W330 defers here for fire-specific execution.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | **Suppression support & utilities isolation**: confirm sprinkler system flowing (never obstruct), close fire doors/shutters, isolate non-essential electrical panels and LPG/cylinder supplies per the site's isolation map; DC: halt all MHE and dock operations, rack aisles cleared for BFP access | Facilities Coordinator / Duty Supervisor | Store/DC Manager | 2–5 min |
| 3 | **Evacuation & muster**: execute the W695 evacuation protocol — zone sweeps (fitting rooms, restrooms, stockrooms, lumber yard, DC rack aisles), PA script, assembly-point roll call against the shift roster and visitor/contractor log per W696; report any unaccounted person to BFP incident commander immediately with last-seen location | All floor/shift staff / Safety Officer | Store/DC Manager | 5–10 min |

| 6 | **Structural assessment & BFP clearance-to-reopen**: licensed structural engineer assesses fire-affected structure (rack anchors, roof trusses, mezzanine) before any restocking; BFP conducts post-fire re-inspection; W806 recertifies impaired suppression/detection systems; trading resumes only with written BFP clearance + engineer's fit-out report + recertified systems — tracked as a formal gate with Regional Manager sign-off | Structural Engineer / Safety Officer / BFP | VP Store Operations (store) / VP Supply Chain (DC) | 3–14 days elapsed |

- Fire-alarm panel event log & sprinkler flow-switch signals (BFP-reportable export; linked to the site emergency plan per W695)
- Facility work-order module: fire-damage corrective work orders, boarding/securing jobs, suppression-system impairment permits (links to W47)

### Time Estimate
Event effort is severity-scaled and event-driven: an incipient suppressed event ≈ 6–10 staff-hours (Steps 1–2, 5, 7); a reportable structural fire ≈ 120–400 staff-hours across evacuation, scene security, claim assembly and the reopen gate. Drill contribution: the semi-annual fire drill per W158 (~1 hour × 29 staff/store × 2/yr × 200 stores ≈ 11,600 hours/yr) rehearses Steps 1–3 and is absorbed in the existing drill budget — no incremental headcount.

- **Premature-reopening risk**: trading resumes before BFP clearance or engineer sign-off → RA 9514 citation, claim denial, premises-liability exposure; mitigated by the formal three-party reopen gate in Step 6 (BFP clearance + engineer report + recertified systems) with VP-level sign-off

- **Evidence risk**: smoke/water-damaged stock disposed before photographic documentation forfeits claim rights; mitigated by the W90-grade damage-case evidence standard in Step 5
- **Battery-event recurrence risk**: charging-station thermal events recurring after a near-miss; mitigated by the W1007 root-cause loop and chain-wide corrective communication

## W5538. Bomb Threat Response & Search-or-Evacuation Decision Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | A bomb threat received against a store, DC, or corporate site by phone call, written note, email, or social-media post — whether credible, vague, or assessed as hoax |
| **Frequency** | Rare; ~1–3 threats chain-wide per year (the vast majority hoaxes) |
| **Volume** | 1 site × full premises population (store: ~467 customers/day + 29 staff; DC: 120–180 staff/shift) × all zones (sales floor, stockrooms, yards, parking) |
| **Owner** | Store Manager / Regional Manager |
| **Participants** | Store/DC Manager, Security Guard, trained staff search teams (zone-based), Safety Officer, PNP (911) & PNP-EOD bomb disposal, BFP (standby), VP Legal, Corporate Communications (W855 tree if event escalates), LP (CCTV preservation per W471) |

### Background

W330 classifies a bomb threat as a Major emergency and PA-24.2's location emergency-plan template lists a bomb-threat procedure, but no workflow owned the event: the call-takedown checklist, the search-vs-evacuate decision, the PNP-EOD coordination, and the re-entry gate. The decision discipline matters because the two failure modes are opposite — evacuating on every threat trains hoaxers and creates crowd/traffic injury risk, while searching without protocol risks an actual device. Philippine practice (PNP EOD guidance adopted as company standard) is: record everything, never touch, search with trained staff only, and evacuate when a device is found or the threat carries specific placement/timing detail. This workflow provides that single canon; the W330 generic protocol defers here for bomb-threat-specific execution, and closure/reopening mechanics ride W850.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Threat intake & recording**: receiving employee uses the bomb-threat data card — exact wording, caller details (accent, background sounds, number display), claimed device location/type/timing, demanded action/deadline; keep the caller talking, do not hang up first; written/electronic threats preserved untouched (bag photos of notes; screenshot emails/posts before any deletion); report to Manager immediately | Receiving Employee / Manager | Store/DC Manager | During call + 5 min |
| 2 | **Notify & escalate**: 911/PNP report (regardless of credibility assessment), Regional Manager notified, silent alert to Security and zone-search team leads; radios and mobile phones minimized in the searched area (RF-trigger precaution) | Manager / Security Guard | Store/DC Manager | 5 min |
| 3 | **Search-or-evacuate decision**: Manager decides per the two-branch rule — (a) threat with specific placement/timing detail, found suspicious package, or written/graphic threat → immediate evacuation branch (Step 4); (b) vague/generic threat → trained-staff covert sweep of their own work zones (staff search only areas they know: look, do not touch — packages that don't belong: out-of-place containers, unattended bags, unusual wiring), 15–20 min sweep; any find at any point → escalate to evacuation + PNP-EOD | Store/DC Manager (decision) / trained staff (sweep) | Store/DC Manager | Decision < 5 min; sweep 15–20 min |
| 4 | **Evacuation execution** (if invoked): W695 mechanics with a threat-specific script — PA announcement avoids the words "bomb"/"threat" (e.g. "managers to the floor, please leave your transactions and proceed to the exits"), customers and staff routed to the upwind assembly point ≥ 300 m from the building and beyond the parking perimeter, parking entrances closed to inbound traffic, cash-left-in-drawers documented for later reconciliation per W850 | All Staff / Safety Officer / Security | Store/DC Manager | 5–10 min |
| 5 | **PNP-EOD coordination & scene control**: security hands the threat recording, data card, and CCTV snapshot of the caller/receiving area to PNP; PNP-EOD sweep leads; building remains closed and the perimeter held until the formal PNP all-clear; no staff re-entry for any reason before all-clear | Security Guard / PNP-EOD | Store/DC Manager (site control) | PNP-led (1–6 hrs) |
| 6 | **Re-entry & recovery**: after all-clear — W850 reopening mechanics (cash reconciliation vs Step-4 documentation, cold-chain/product checks at DCs), delayed-transaction customer handling, resume trading; if evacuation was *not* invoked (sweep negative), log the stand-down decision with rationale | Manager / Cash Office / Duty Supervisors | Regional Manager | 1–3 hrs |
| 7 | **Documentation, hoax tracking & review**: W471 Category-A incident report with the complete threat recording and data card; CCTV preserved per the W471 evidence chain; site threat log updated (recurrence pattern → enhanced mail/screening and PNP liaison); VP Legal assesses harassment/prosecution referral; event debriefed into the W158 drill program | LP / HR / VP Legal | Head of Internal Audit & Risk | 1–2 days |

### System Touchpoints
- Bomb-threat data card & call recording retained to the incident case (Category A per W471; links to VS-88 document control for retention)
- CCTV retrieval & preservation request (evidence chain per W471)
- Store-closure status flag & POS trading-resume control (per W850)
- Visitor/contractor log & muster roster (per W696/W695) for the evacuation roll call
- Site threat log (hoax-recurrence tracking; PNP-liaison contact register)

### Time Estimate
Event effort is threat-path-scaled and event-driven: a vague-threat/covert-sweep/stand-down event ≈ 30–50 staff-hours across intake, sweep, and documentation; a full-evacuation event ≈ 80–200 staff-hours (evacuation execution, PNP-EOD wait, reopening reconciliation). No annualized effort figure is claimed at this frequency; the protocol is exercised through the W158 emergency-drill calendar (bomb-threat module in the rotation) rather than through real events.

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Panic-injury risk**: an unscripted announcement naming the threat triggers a crush at exits; mitigated by the coded PA script and staff-positioned exit management in Step 4
- **RF-trigger risk**: radios/phones used beside a suspected device; mitigated by the Step-2 communications discipline and the trained-staff-only sweep rule
- **Handling risk**: a staff member moving/opening a suspicious package; mitigated by the absolute look-don't-touch rule and PNP-EOD ownership of any find
- **Hoax-escalation risk**: repeated threats against the same site normalizing non-response; mitigated by the site threat log, recurrence escalation to the Regional Manager, and PNP liaison
- **Evidence risk**: the call recording or data card lost before PNP handover; mitigated by the W471 evidence-preservation chain

### Automation Opportunity
- The bomb-threat data card ships as a guided POS/back-office form that timestamps entries and auto-attaches to the incident case on submission (replaces the paper card in Step 1)
- Zone-sweep confirmation checklists (per team lead) tracked on mobile with an auto-timer to the Manager's decision point (replaces verbal sweep reporting in Step 3)

### Controls
- CTL-314 (ensure controlled execution — Emergency Preparedness (PA-24.2).)
- operational: threats are reported to PNP regardless of credibility assessment; credibility is never a reason to withhold notification
- operational: only trained staff sweep, only in their own zones, look-don't-touch; any find transfers control to PNP-EOD

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed by existing store/DC staff, Security, Safety Officers and the W158 drill rotation; PNP/EOD costs are government services. No incremental headcount.

## W5570. Gas-Leak Event Response (LPG/Natural-Gas Odor on Premises)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | A gas odor is reported inside or adjacent to a BuildRight site: LPG smell at or near the cylinder-exchange cage (VS-175), a leaking appliance or regulator connection, a gas line serving the food concession/vending or hot-water service, or a gas odor migrating from a neighboring premises — before any ignition |
| **Frequency** | ~10–25 odor-report events per year chain-wide across ~205 sites; the overwhelming majority are minor (faint smell traced to a cage-adjacent cylinder or appliance) and resolve without evacuation of the full trading floor |
| **Volume** | 1 event × the exposure-zone tier (zone closure, partial closure, or full evacuation for 1–4 hours) × responders (Store Manager, security, cage/Store Services, BFP, LPG supplier) × the clearance-reading set that gates reopening |
| **Owner** | Store Manager (site command); HSE Manager (protocol owner) |
| **Participants** | Security Guard, Store Services Lead (cage operations per VS-175), First Aider (VS-83), Facilities (W650), Regional Manager, LPG supplier emergency-response desk, BFP (fire station), gas utility (where a piped-gas service exists), Corporate Communications (W134/W855 if the event becomes public) |

### Background

'gas leak'/'gas odor'/'smell of gas' appear in zero PA files as event content — VS-175's W5069 owns cylinder leak *inspection, condemnation and quarantine* at the cage (an intake/periodic QC layer, not a trading-hours event), W5537 owns the fire event *after ignition* with its post-fire BFP clearance gate, and W330 names in-store emergencies generically — nobody owned the pre-ignition gas event: the odor-is-real-until-proven-otherwise law (an odor report is never dismissed as a false alarm while the source is unverified), the ignition-source ban (light switches, phone flashlights and anything that arcs stay off in the exposure zone — LPG is heavier than air and pools in low areas where the smell reads weakest), the evacuate-up-and-out discipline for pooled gas, the isolation rule (close the cage master valve or appliance valve only within the trained two-person perimeter; never chase a leak into a confined space), and the documented reopen gate (the W5537 three-party-gate analog: detector readings clear + the source repaired or condemned + the ventilation standard met, evidenced in the incident case). This workflow is the single canon for that event class — the W5537/W5538 in-store emergency family's pre-ignition counterpart.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Odor report & classification**: the report is logged with location, strength, and time; the cage and appliance lines are visually checked from a distance for frost, haze or hiss; the exposure-zone tier is set (zone / partial / full) with the heavier-than-air rule in mind — low areas read the hazard last, not first | Store Manager / Security Guard | Store Manager | 2–5 min |
| 2 | **Evacuate the exposure zone**: zone closure at minimum; full evacuation where the odor is strong, the source is unresolved, or the cage line is implicated; customers and staff move up and out (no basement/low-route egress); PWD assistance per the W4400 protocols; assembly upwind | Store Manager / Security Guard | Regional Manager | 5–15 min |
| 3 | **Ignition-source ban**: in the exposure zone — no light switches, no POS power cycling, no phone flashlights; exterior master kill where the zone is electrical; signage and a physical cordon hold the ban while the event runs | Security Guard / Facilities | Store Manager | Immediate |
| 4 | **Isolate the source (trained-only, two-person)**: cage master valve closed per the VS-175 training standard where the cage line is implicated; appliance/regulator valves closed by Facilities; never pursue a leak into a confined space or behind racking — the isolation boundary is where training ends | Store Services Lead / Facilities | HSE Manager | 5–20 min |
| 5 | **Emergency callout**: BFP notified for any event beyond a faint zone-level trace; the LPG supplier's 24-hour emergency desk dispatched for cage/appliance events; the gas utility called where a piped service exists; the W650 Facilities call-out runs in parallel | Store Manager | Store Manager | Immediate |
| 6 | **Ventilation & clearance**: natural/mechanical ventilation per the supplier/BFP guidance with the zone held empty; gas-detector readings at the floor level taken as the clearance standard; the source is repaired, condemned (W5069 quarantine for cylinders) or isolated pending repair | Facilities / HSE Manager | HSE Manager | 1–4 hours |
| 7 | **Documented reopen gate**: trading resumes only when the clearance set is evidenced in the incident case — detector-clear readings + source repaired/condemned/isolated + ventilation standard met + (where BFP attended) BFP clearance recorded; the three-party-gate analog of W5537 | Store Manager / HSE Manager | Regional Manager | At clearance |
| 8 | **After-action & CAPA**: the source failure mode is classified (cylinder valve, regulator, appliance, third-party migration) and feeds the CAPA register; cage-standards or appliance-maintenance retrofits route to VS-175/W650; the event timeline folds into the W695 drill rotation | HSE Manager / Store Manager | HSE Manager | 1–2 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- Incident case record with a gas-leak type, exposure-zone tier, and the clearance-reading set attached before closure
- VS-175 cage-event flag on the affected cylinders (W5069 quarantine state) and supplier emergency-desk call log
- Facility work orders for the source repair with a gas-line flag (links to W650/W47)
- BFP attendance and clearance record on the incident case (links to W1296/W806 inspection history)
- CAPA register entry and drill-rotation update (links to W695/W4404-class)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Return-too-early risk**: LPG pools low and lingers; a cleared-feeling floor can still hold an ignitable layer; mitigated by Step 6's floor-level detector standard and Step 7's documented gate
- **Ignition-improvisation risk**: a light switch or phone flashlight operates inside the exposure zone; mitigated by Step 3's ban with the exterior master kill
- **Source-misidentification risk**: the smell migrating from a neighbor's premises is treated as ours (or the reverse), leaving the real source running; mitigated by Step 1's perimeter check and Step 5's supplier/utility desk calls
- **Normalcy-bias risk**: repeated faint cage-adjacent smells habituate staff into non-reporting; mitigated by the odor-is-real-until-proven-otherwise law and the Step 8 failure-mode classification
- **Confined-space pursuit risk**: an untrained responder chases the smell behind racking or into a low room; mitigated by Step 4's trained-only two-person boundary

### Automation Opportunity
- Incident-case template whose gas-leak flag auto-creates the clearance-set checklist and blocks case closure without the detector readings and source disposition recorded (replaces manual Step 6/7 verification)
- Cage-event flag that auto-quarantines implicated cylinder serials in the VS-175 exchange inventory (replaces manual Step 6 quarantine)

### Controls
- CTL-314 (ensure controlled execution — Emergency Preparedness (PA-24.2).)
- operational: trading resumes only after the documented clearance gate (detector-clear + source disposition + ventilation standard, plus BFP clearance where attended); the ignition-source ban and two-person isolation boundary briefed at every store with a cage; implicated cylinders carry the W5069 quarantine state

### Time Estimate
Minor event ≈ 4–10 staff-hours (zone closure, isolation, callout, documentation); escalated event ≈ 12–30 staff-hours across the response and clearance window. At ~10–25 events/year chain-wide ≈ 100–250 staff-hours/year, absorbed by store, VS-175 cage and HSE rosters — no incremental headcount.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed by the store management bench, Store Services/cage staffing, HSE and the VS-175 supplier emergency desks; no incremental headcount.
