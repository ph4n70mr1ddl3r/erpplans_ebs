## W140. Occupational Health & Safety (OHS) Incident Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Workplace accident, "near miss," or safety hazard reported (involving employee, customer, or contractor) |
| **Frequency** | Ad-hoc; ~50–100 reportable incidents/year chain-wide |
| **Volume** | Covers all 200 stores, 4 DCs, and HQ |
| **Owner** | Safety Officer (HQ/DC); Store Manager (Store) |
| **Participants** | Affected Individual, First Aider, Store/DC Manager, HR, Legal, Insurance |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Emergency Response**: Immediate first aid or medical evacuation; secure the scene to prevent further injury | First Aider / Manager | Store Manager | Immediate |
| 2 | **Incident Reporting**: Manager creates Incident Report in system within 2 hours: date/time, location, individuals involved, description of event, and immediate actions taken | Store/DC Manager | Safety Officer | 20 min |
| 3 | **Evidence Capture**: Upload photos of the scene, witness statements, and CCTV footage to the incident record | Store/DC Manager | — | 30 min |
| 4 | **Investigation**: Safety Officer conducts root cause analysis (e.g., "5 Whys"); classifies incident (Minor, Medical Treatment, Lost Time Injury, Fatality) | Safety Officer | CHRO | 1–3 days |
| 5 | **Corrective Action (CAPA)**: System generates CAPA tasks (e.g., repair floor, replace PPE, retraining); tracks completion | Safety Officer | Dept Head | Varies |
| 6 | **Regulatory Notification**: If required (e.g., serious injury), DPO/Safety Officer notifies DOLE (Department of Labor and Employment) within prescribed timelines | Safety Officer | VP Legal | 4 hours |
| 7 | **Insurance/Claims**: If involving customer injury or significant property damage, initiate insurance claim per W3.6a | Store Manager / Finance | CFO | Per W3.6a |
| 8 | **Closure**: Review all actions completed; Safety Officer closes the case; system archives for 10 years | Safety Officer | CHRO | 15 min |
| 9 | **Monthly Review**: Monthly Safety Committee meeting reviews incident trends, "near miss" patterns, and CAPA completion rates | Safety Committee | CEO | 2 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Incident Reporting Module with photo/evidence upload and witness statement capture (Step 2–3)
- CAPA Task Tracking System with automated reminders and escalation for overdue corrective actions (Step 5)
- DOLE Notification Workflow Integration — auto-generates DOLE-compliant report forms within prescribed timelines (Step 6)
- Insurance Claims Integration linked to W59/W185 for customer injury and property damage claims (Step 7)
- Safety Compliance Dashboard showing incident trends, near-miss patterns, and CAPA completion rates by location/region (Step 9)
- CCTV Footage Retrieval System — timestamped clip extraction linked to incident record (Step 3)
- 10-year digital archiving with legal-hold capability for regulatory audit (Step 8)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **DOLE Department Order 252-25 (revised IRR of RA 11058, superseding DO 198-18)** — full compliance with the OSH Standards requires documented risk assessments, safety committees, and qualified safety officers per establishment; mitigated by CTL-226 (OHS compliance & incident management)
- **DOLE reporting timelines** — 24 hours for accidents resulting in death or serious injury/disability (OSH Standards Rule 1050); late filing triggers penalties and potential work stoppage orders
- **PhilHealth ECC claims** — work-related injuries require Employees' Compensation Commission processing; incomplete incident documentation delays claims
- **RA 11058 (OSH Standards compliance)** — mandates employer-provided PPE, safety training, and worker participation; non-compliance carries fines per violation
- **Contractor non-compliance risk**: unaccredited or uninsured workers on-site expose the company to joint liability (see W187); mitigated by CTL-226 (OHS compliance & incident management)
- **Permit-to-Work enforcement** — hot work, confined space, and height work permits must be issued per task; lapses create regulatory and safety exposure
- **Fire Safety Inspection Certificate (FSIC)** — required from Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP) per location; expired FSIC blocks business permit renewal


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-create of "Incident Reporting" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-record of "record" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-generate of "generates CAPA tasks" (replaces manual Step 5).

### Controls
- CTL-226 (ensure OHS compliance & incident management.)
- operational: current FSIC from Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP) per location, tracked to expiry

### Staffing Implication
- ~50–100 reportable incidents/year chain-wide across 200 stores, 4 DCs, and HQ
- Each incident: ~20 min for initial reporting (Step 2) + 1–3 days investigation (Step 4) + varies for CAPA completion (Step 5)
- 1 Safety Officer per region (~8 Safety Officers, within the 10-person HSE team that also includes the company nurse and wellness staff); investigation time absorbed into existing responsibilities
- No incremental headcount required; workload peaks are managed through regional rotation and HQ Safety Committee support

### Time Estimate
| Phase | Duration |
|---|---|
| Emergency Response & Reporting | 2–3 hours (includes 2-hour reporting SLA) |
| Evidence Capture & CCTV Retrieval | 30 min – 1 hour |
| Investigation & Root Cause Analysis | 1–3 business days |
| CAPA Implementation | Varies (hours to weeks depending on severity) |
| Regulatory Notification (if applicable) | 4 hours |
| Case Closure & Archiving | 15 min |
| **Total (typical non-fatal incident)** | **3–5 business days** |

- operational: Safety Officer review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication

- 1–2 hours per inspection (walkthrough + findings logging); completed by Store/DC Managers as part of regular operational duties
- Safety Officers review dashboards and flag high-risk locations (~1 hour/month regional review)
- No incremental headcount required; absorbed into existing Store Manager and Safety Officer responsibilities

## W436. Annual OHS Statutory Reporting (WAIR/AMR)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual statutory deadline (typically January/February) |
| **Frequency** | Annual |
| **Volume** | 200 Stores + 4 DCs + 1 HQ |
| **Owner** | Safety Officer |
| **Participants** | HR Manager, Company Physician/Nurse, Store Managers, DOLE |

### Background

The **Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE)** mandates the submission of annual safety reports under the **Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) Standards**. Key reports include the **Work Accident/Illness Report (WAIR)**, the **Annual Medical Report (AMR)**, and the **Report on Safety Organization (RSO)**.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Data Consolidation**: Aggregates all workplace incident records (W140) and safety inspection findings (W141) for the calendar year | Safety Officer | — | 3 days |
| 2 | **Medical Data**: Company Nurse consolidates employee health records, clinic consultations, and annual physical exam (APE) results for the AMR | Company Nurse | HR Manager | 2 days |
| 3 | **Report Drafting**: Safety Officer prepares the WAIR and RSO; Physician prepares the AMR | Safety Officer / Physician | CHRO | 1 day |
| 4 | **Review & Signing**: Store Managers sign the branch-specific reports; Safety Officer and Physician sign the technical sections | Safety Officer / Physician | Store Manager | 1 hour/loc |
| 5 | **Online/Manual Submission**: Submit the consolidated reports to the DOLE Regional Office or via the DOLE Establishment Report System (ERS) | Safety Officer | — | 4 hours |
| 6 | **Acknowledgement**: System archives the "Received" copy of the reports for future DOLE inspections | System | Safety Officer | 15 min |

### System Touchpoints
- Incident Management Module (W140)
- Employee Health Record (HRIS integration)
- DOLE Online Reporting Portal (External)
- Document Management System (W255)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Non-Compliance Fines**: Failure to submit annual OHS reports leads to fines and increased likelihood of a comprehensive DOLE labor inspection.
- **Data Integrity**: Inconsistencies between the monthly incident logs and the annual summary reports.

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "records" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-generate of "prepares the WAIR and RSO" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-review of "Review & Signing" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-226 (ensure OHS compliance & incident management.)
- operational: Safety Officer review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
~6 staff-days of Safety Officer/Nurse/Physician effort per occurrence (3 days data consolidation + 2 days medical-data assembly + 1 day drafting = 6 days), plus unit-priced steps at their own unit (4 hours submission + 15 min acknowledgement + 1 hour/loc branch sign-offs); elapsed timeline 3 days + 2 days + 1 day — at Annual ≈ 1 occurrence/year ≈ 52–60 hours/year plus branch sign-offs.

## W697. Workplace Ergonomics Assessment & Musculoskeletal Injury Prevention

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Employee reports musculoskeletal discomfort; quarterly assessment cycle; new workstation or work process introduction; post-injury return-to-work |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly workstation assessments; continuous for reported discomfort; annual program review |
| **Volume** | ~6,911 employees across 200 stores, 4 DCs, and HQ; high-risk roles: cashiers (prolonged standing, repetitive scanning), DC pickers (lifting, bending), lumber yard staff (heavy lifting), paint mixing staff (repetitive arm motion) |
| **Owner** | Safety Officer |
| **Participants** | HR (employee health records), Department Supervisors, External Ergonomics Consultant, Occupational Health Provider, Store/DC Managers |

### Background

Retail hardware operations involve physically demanding tasks: lifting heavy building materials (cement bags 40kg, lumber, steel bars), repetitive scanning at POS terminals, prolonged standing for cashiers and sales associates, and repetitive motion for paint mixing and lumber cutting operations. The Philippine DOLE Department Order No. 252-25 requires employers to implement occupational safety and health programs including ergonomics. Musculoskeletal disorders (MSDs) are among the most common workplace injuries in retail, leading to lost workdays, workers' compensation claims, and reduced productivity.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Risk identification by role**: Safety Officer classifies all roles by ergonomic risk level: (a) **High risk**: DC pickers/packers (lifting >20kg repeatedly, bending, twisting), lumber yard staff (lifting long/heavy materials), paint mixing operators (repetitive arm motion, chemical exposure); (b) **Medium risk**: cashiers (prolonged standing, repetitive scanning, awkward postures for bagging heavy items), receiving clerks (unloading delivery trucks); (c) **Low risk**: office staff (prolonged sitting, computer use), sales associates (standing, walking); (d) document risk assessment per role with specific hazard identification | Safety Officer | CHRO | 1-2 days initial; updated annually |
| 2 | **Quarterly workstation assessment**: for high and medium risk roles: (a) Department Supervisor conducts quarterly self-assessment using ergonomic checklist: workstation height, reach distances, lifting requirements, posture, break frequency; (b) for cashiers: verify anti-fatigue mat condition, scanner height, bagging area layout; (c) for DC pickers: verify lifting aid availability (hand trucks, lift tables), shelving height optimization (heavy items at waist level); (d) for lumber yard: verify lifting team protocols, mechanical aid availability; (e) submit assessments to Safety Officer for review | Department Supervisor | Safety Officer | 30 min per workstation/quarter |
| 3 | **Employee discomfort reporting**: employees can report musculoskeletal discomfort via HR self-service portal: (a) anonymous option available to encourage reporting without fear of judgment; (b) system prompts for: affected body part (back, shoulder, wrist, knee, etc.), severity (1-5), specific task triggering discomfort, duration of symptoms; (c) reports routed to Safety Officer for assessment and response within 48 hours | Employee | Safety Officer | 5 min per report |
| 4 | **Ergonomic assessment by consultant**: for reported discomfort cases or high-risk areas: (a) External Ergonomics Consultant conducts on-site assessment: observe work tasks, measure workstation dimensions, assess posture and movement patterns, review injury history; (b) consultant provides written recommendations: workstation modification, equipment changes (anti-fatigue mats, adjustable-height platforms, lifting aids), task rotation schedules, stretching exercise programs; (c) recommendations shared with Department Supervisor for implementation | External Ergonomics Consultant | Safety Officer | 2-4 hours per location |
| 5 | **Intervention implementation**: Department Supervisor implements ergonomic improvements: (a) immediate low-cost fixes: adjust shelf heights, reposition equipment, provide anti-fatigue mats, implement task rotation schedule; (b) medium-cost fixes: purchase lifting aids (hand trucks, lift tables), ergonomic floor mats, adjustable cashier platforms; submit request per W188 maintenance workflow; (c) high-cost fixes: shelving system redesign, automated material handling equipment — submit capex request per W184; (d) all fixes documented in system with before/after photos and expected impact | Department Supervisor | Store/DC Manager | Varies by intervention |
| 6 | **Stretching & exercise program**: Safety Officer implements stretching break program for high-risk roles: (a) 5-minute group stretching sessions at start of shift and mid-shift for DC and lumber yard staff; (b) posted stretching exercise guides at cash registers and DC workstations; (c) incorporate ergonomic awareness into W655 safety training curriculum | Safety Officer / Department Supervisor | CHRO | Ongoing (~10 min/day per team) |
| 7 | **Return-to-work ergonomic accommodation**: for employees returning from musculoskeletal injury: (a) Safety Officer coordinates with Occupational Health Provider on work restrictions (weight limits, restricted movements, maximum standing time); (b) Department Supervisor implements temporary modified duty: reduced lifting, alternate task assignment, additional breaks; (c) gradual return-to-full-duty schedule over 2-4 weeks; (d) system tracks modified duty assignments and clearance dates | Safety Officer / Department Supervisor | CHRO | 2-4 weeks per case |
| 8 | **Annual MSD trend analysis**: Safety Officer compiles annual MSD trend report: (a) MSD incident rate by location and role type; (b) common injury types (back strain, shoulder strain, wrist CTS, knee strain); (c) lost workdays due to MSDs; (d) workers' compensation claims related to MSDs; (e) intervention effectiveness: pre/post-intervention MSD rates at assessed locations; (f) feed findings into annual OHS report per W436 and BC plan per W685 | Safety Officer | CHRO | 1 day/year |

### System Touchpoints

- Ergonomic risk assessment module by role type
- Quarterly workstation self-assessment checklist tool
- Employee discomfort reporting portal (HR self-service)
- Ergonomic assessment tracking and recommendation implementation
- Maintenance request integration per W188
- Capex request integration per W184
- Training management integration per W655
- Incident management integration per W140
- Workers' compensation claims tracking
- Annual OHS reporting integration per W436

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Underreporting of discomfort**: employees may not report early symptoms for fear of being seen as weak or complaining, leading to progression from discomfort to injury
- **Lifting culture in hardware retail**: macho workplace culture in hardware stores may discourage use of mechanical aids or requesting help with heavy loads; Safety Officer must reinforce that using aids is standard practice, not a sign of weakness
- **Cost of ergonomic interventions**: comprehensive ergonomic retrofit (anti-fatigue mats, lifting aids, shelving redesign) across 200 stores and 4 DCs represents significant investment; must be prioritized by injury rate and risk level
- **Seasonal workload variation**: during peak seasons (Christmas, back-to-school), increased customer traffic and stocking volumes increase physical demands; stretching programs and task rotation may be skipped under pressure
- **Contractor and agency worker inclusion**: temporary and agency workers may not receive the same ergonomic training and assessment as regular employees, creating risk gaps


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-scan of "scanning, awkward postures for bagging heavy items" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "Quarterly workstation assessment" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-route of "routed to Safety Officer for assessment and response within 48 hours" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-313 (ensure controlled execution — Occupational Health & Safety (PA-24.1).)
- operational: Safety Officer review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication

- **Safety Officer**: ~8–12 hours/quarter on ergonomic assessments and program management. Absorbed within existing role.
- **External Ergonomics Consultant**: ~5–10 days/year for high-risk location assessments.
- **Department Supervisors**: ~30 min/quarter per workstation for self-assessments. Absorbed within existing role.
- **No incremental headcount**.

### Time Estimate

- Risk identification: 1-2 days (initial), updated annually
- Quarterly self-assessment: 30 min per workstation
- Consultant assessment: 2-4 hours per location
- Intervention implementation: varies
- Annual trend analysis: 1 day
- Stretching program: ~10 min/day per team

## W758. Store-Level Fire Safety Equipment Daily Inspection & Compliance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Daily store opening (W5A); scheduled daily inspection |
| **Frequency** | Daily per store |
| **Volume** | 200 stores × daily inspection |
| **Owner** | Maintenance Associate |
| **Participants** | Maintenance Associate, Store Manager, BFP (annual inspection W476) |

### Background
Beyond the annual Fire Safety Inspection Certificate (FSIC) from the Bureau of Fire Protection (W476) and periodic fire drills (W582), daily fire safety equipment inspections ensure that fire extinguishers, fire alarm systems, emergency lighting, sprinkler systems, and fire exits are functional at all times. A single non-functional fire extinguisher during an actual fire event can result in catastrophic liability and loss of life. The monthly self-inspection cycle and the annual BFP FSIC compliance program are owned by W1296 (PA-24.2, Store-Level Fire Safety Equipment Inspection & BFP Compliance); this workflow owns the daily functional checks only.

### Steps

## W5549. Suicide Attempt / Self-Harm Incident Response & Psychosocial-Aftermath Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | A suicide attempt, act of self-harm, or death by suspected suicide occurs on BuildRight premises (store, DC, yard, or parking) involving a customer, employee, contractor, or visitor — including attempts discovered in progress |
| **Frequency** | Rare; the realistic chain-wide expectation is fewer than one event every several years; the aftermath protocol is exercised through the critical-incident drill rotation |
| **Volume** | 1 event × the immediate scene population plus directly-witnessing staff (typically 3–10 first-witness staff per event) × responders (EMS, PNP/SOCO where death is suspected) |
| **Owner** | Store Manager (site command during the event); HSE Manager (aftermath program owner) |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, trained first-aiders per W501, Security Guard (scene control), PNP (incl. SOCO where death is suspected), EMS, HR & Occupational Health (W2865 counseling/EAP), VP Legal, Corporate Communications (single-spokesperson protocol), CHRO, DOLE reporting chain where an employee is involved (W140/W436), barangay crisis responders where engaged |

### Background

W140 owns the OHS incident framework and W1266 the customer-incident/first-aid layer, but this event class needs handling those frameworks do not script: a suspected suicide scene is a potential crime scene that must be preserved even while aid is rendered; witness care and staff re-exposure management determine the long psychological tail; cause speculation in records, comms, or social posts creates legal exposure and family harm; and the trading decision (section-off vs. full closure) is a judgment with liability on both sides. 'Suicide'/'self-harm' appear in zero PA files today. This protocol exists so the first hour is run from a script rather than improvisation, and so the aftermath — the part most often neglected — has an owner. The medical dimension rides W501 (Tier-1 first-aid canon); this workflow owns the scene, witness, communication, and aftermath disciplines around it.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Immediate response**: render aid per W501 immediately (EMS 911); clear onlookers and create privacy screens (fixture covers, aisle closure); assign one staff member to meet EMS at the entrance; do not delay aid for scene-perfection — aid first, preservation second | First Aider / Duty Supervisor | Store Manager | Immediate |
| 2 | **Scene preservation & police handoff**: where death is suspected (or uncertain), treat the area as a potential crime scene — nothing moved or removed beyond what aid required, staff rotated out of the scene, PNP called; SOCO processes the scene; store provides CCTV preservation request per the W471 evidence chain | Security Guard / Store Manager | Store Manager | Until PNP release |
| 3 | **Trading decision**: section-off the affected area vs. full temporary closure — decision by Store Manager with Regional Manager consult, factoring scene extent, police direction, and staff readiness; if trading continues, the scene area stays closed and screened; no re-staffing of directly-witnessing employees at the scene zone same-day | Store Manager | Regional Manager | < 1 h |
| 4 | **Witness & staff immediate care**: directly-witnessing staff removed from duty with pay; psychological first aid by trained peers/managers (listen, do not probe); EAP counseling activated per W2865 with same-day access; a named HR contact assigned to each affected employee; no statements to anyone except police, HR, or Legal | Duty Supervisors / HR | CHRO | Same day |
| 5 | **Communication protocol**: single-spokesperson rule (Corporate Communications) — a no-comment-until-facts script for media and social; no details in any internal channel beyond operational need-to-know; no cause characterization anywhere in writing; if the person involved is an employee, family contact is made by a designated senior HR/Legal pair, in person where possible, never by phone script | Corporate Communications / HR | VP Legal | Ongoing |
| 6 | **Employee-victim branch** (if the person involved is an employee): hospital liaison and family single-point contact; DOLE 24-hour notification where the event is work-related per W140/W436 with facts only (no cause speculation in any report); employment-status, benefits, and medical-leave handling per W805/W2850-class chains; confidentiality maintained — mental-health information is sensitive personal information under the privacy canon | HR / HSE | CHRO | Days–weeks |
| 7 | **Aftermath program**: structured staff debrief within 72 hours by a trained facilitator (external CISM resource where internal capacity is thin); ongoing counseling access monitored; duty-roster changes for scene-zone staff; site walk-through after PNP release to restore and review the area (a standard means-restriction and access review of the site — heights, enclosed or secluded zones — as prudent practice); union/employee-representative coordination where applicable | HSE Manager / HR | CHRO | 2–6 weeks |
| 8 | **Documentation & review**: incident record per W140 with a sensitive-handling flag (access-restricted; no cause speculation); the comms log archived; after-action review of the response (timing, script adherence, care coverage) feeding the critical-incident drill rotation; no disciplinary or policy conclusions drawn except from the verified facts | HSE / HR / VP Legal | Head of Internal Audit & Risk (review sign-off) | 1–2 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- Incident-case record per W140 with sensitive/confidential access flag (restricted-visibility attachment class)
- CCTV preservation request & evidence chain (per W471 rules; police-release clock)
- EAP case tracking & counseling access log (links to W2865)
- DOLE notification record where employee-related (links to W436 WAIR chain)
- Duty-roster adjustment records for affected staff (links to W34-class scheduling)
- Store-closure status flag & trading-resume control (per W850 mechanics if full closure invoked)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Re-traumatization risk**: directly-witnessing staff returned to the scene zone too early; mitigated by the Step-3 no-same-day rule and Step-7 roster changes
- **Speculation/privacy risk**: cause speculation in records, messages, or social posts — legal exposure and family harm; mitigated by the no-characterization rule in Steps 5–6 and the access-restricted record flag
- **Scene-handling risk**: well-meant tidying destroys what police need; mitigated by the aid-first-then-freeze sequencing in Steps 1–2
- **Media-mishandling risk**: unscripted staff comments go viral; mitigated by the single-spokesperson no-comment script
- **Under-treated-witness risk**: silent staff carry the event untreated; mitigated by the named-contact assignment, 72-hour debrief, and monitored counseling access

### Automation Opportunity
- Sensitive-incident case template auto-applies the restricted-visibility flag, the CCTV preservation hold, and the DOLE clock (where employee-related) on open (replaces manual Steps 2/6 bookkeeping)
- EAP linkage auto-creates same-day counseling slots and follow-up tracking for the witness list captured at the event (replaces manual Step-4/7 coordination)

### Controls
- CTL-226 (ensure OHS compliance & incident management.)
- operational: no statement on the event's nature or cause is made or recorded by any function other than the single spokesperson and, for employees, the HR/Legal family pair
- operational: directly-witnessing staff are stood down same-day with pay and offered counseling access within 24 hours — both logged

### Time Estimate
Per event: Steps 1–5 ≈ 20–50 staff-hours across the first 48 hours (response, scene liaison, staff care, comms); Steps 6–8 ≈ 30–70 staff-hours over the aftermath window. Event-driven, no annual figure claimed; the protocol is exercised through the critical-incident drill rotation (tabletop module) rather than real events.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed by existing store leadership, HSE, HR/EAP and Security; external CISM facilitation is engaged per need (counseling vendor roster). No incremental headcount.

## W5552. DOLE Imminent-Danger Work-Stoppage Order Response, Abatement & Reinstatement Gate

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | DOLE issues a work-stoppage or work-suspension order on an imminent-danger finding under RA 11058 and its IRR (DO 198-18 class) — covering a BuildRight store, DC, or a specific operation within a site (e.g., lumber-yard crane, paint-mixing room, racking, mezzanine storage) — halting the affected operations until the danger is abated and DOLE clears resumption |
| **Frequency** | Rare; fewer than one order per several years chain-wide; the abatement discipline is exercised through the W512 safety-committee drill rotation |
| **Volume** | 1 event × the ordered scope (one operation to an entire site) × affected workers (10–40 per store operation; 40–120 at a DC) × the trading-continuity surface (partial closure per W850; full closure rides the E-02 custody chain if site-wide) |
| **Owner** | HSE Manager |
| **Participants** | Safety Officer (site), Store Manager / DC Operations Manager, Maintenance Staff (W240/W47), Regional Manager, HR Business Partner (worker redeployment & pay continuity), Regulatory Compliance Officer (W506 calendar), VP Store Operations, DOLE Regional Office, Labor Relations Director where workers are covered (W2880) |

### Background

RA 11058 appears across the corpus as the duties/penalties context of inspections (W505) — 'work stoppage', 'stoppage order' and 'imminent danger' appear in zero dedicated workflow headers, and PA-24.1's own DOLE pain point names work-stoppage orders only as a late-reporting consequence. The stop-work order is the OSH analog of the BIR closure order owned by W5545 (custody event E-25): an external order that halts trading, with a comply-first-contest-second posture, an abatement program, and a written-lifting reopen gate — but its trigger is life safety, its scope is usually partial (an operation, not a whole store), and the workers' wages continue during the stoppage per the OSH regime's no-wage-loss rule, with retaliation criminalized. Nobody owns the event today: W695 owns evacuation mechanics, W505 inspection response, W850 closure mechanics, W1223 power contingencies — none owns the DOLE-ordered stop, its abatement evidence chain, or the reinstatement gate.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Order receipt & scope confirmation**: obtain the written order (or its written same-day confirmation where issued orally at inspection); confirm the exact scope — operations, areas, equipment — effective time and resumption conditions; register it in the W506 compliance calendar | Safety Officer / Regulatory Compliance Officer | HSE Manager | Same day |
| 2 | **Immediate compliance & area control**: halt the ordered operations with no partial trading of the affected zone; barricade and lockout/tag-out affected equipment; post the order at the site; remove workers from the danger area | Store Manager / Safety Officer / Maintenance Staff | Store Manager (store) / DC Operations Manager (DC) | Immediate |
| 3 | **Worker redeployment & wage continuity**: redeploy affected workers to unaffected duties where feasible; pay continuity for stoppage-affected workers per the OSH no-wage-loss rule; no forced leave and no retaliation — RA 11058 attaches penalties to retaliation | HR Business Partner / Store Manager | CHRO | 1–3 days |
| 4 | **Abatement program**: engineering/administrative fixes per the order's findings (structural, guarding, ventilation, electrical) via the W240/W47 maintenance chains or an external engineer on the W1450-class assessment pattern; interim controls documented; photographic and works evidence retained for the lifting case | Maintenance Staff / Facilities / Safety Officer | HSE Manager | Per order (days–weeks) |
| 5 | **Contest/clarification posture (where warranted)**: request clarification or reconsideration of scope through the Regulatory Compliance Officer with VP Legal — comply first, contest second (the W5545 posture); no unilateral resumption while a contest runs | Regulatory Compliance Officer / VP Legal | COO | Per event |
| 6 | **DOLE re-inspection & written lifting**: request re-inspection on abatement completion; resumption only after DOLE's written lifting/clearance — never on internal judgment alone (the E-25 reopen-gate rule) | HSE Manager / Safety Officer | HSE Manager | 1–2 weeks |
| 7 | **Resumption & verification**: reopening mechanics per W850 (E-02 chain if site-wide); worker re-induction on the corrected hazards through the W512 safety-committee briefing; trading restored with monitoring through any ordered observation period | Store Manager / Safety Officer / HR Business Partner | VP Store Operations | 1–3 days |
| 8 | **Register, drill & chain-wide sweep**: record event, root cause and abatement evidence in the OSH learning register (W140-class); feed the W512 drill rotation; sweep the same defect class across all ~205 sites (the W5545 chain-wide defect-sweep pattern) | HSE Manager / Safety Officer | Head of Internal Audit & Risk (review sign-off) | 1–2 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- Compliance-calendar entry & order document repository (links to W506)
- Equipment lockout/tag-out and maintenance work-order records (links to W240/W47)
- Workforce redeployment & timekeeping exception records (links to W561/W594)
- OSH incident/learning register (links to W140/W436)
- Chain-wide hazard-sweep campaign tooling (~205-site defect-class check)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Partial-compliance risk**: trading continues in a technically-affected zone "because customers are waiting," converting an OSH order into a criminal-penalty event under RA 11058; mitigated by Step 2's no-partial-trading rule and Step 6's written-lifting gate
- **Wage-retaliation risk**: affected workers forced onto leave or docked pay, drawing RA 11058 retaliation penalties and labor cases; mitigated by Step 3's redeployment-and-pay rule with CHRO accountability
- **Reopen-on-judgment risk**: resuming on internal sign-off before DOLE's written lifting repeats the violation at its most visible moment; mitigated by the E-25-pattern reopen gate in Step 6
- **Recurrence risk**: abating the symptom at the ordered site while the same defect class persists at the other ~204 sites; mitigated by the Step-8 chain-wide sweep
- **Evidence risk**: abatement work not photographed or documented forfeits the lifting case and extends the stop; mitigated by Step 4's evidence-retention rule

### Automation Opportunity
- Compliance-calendar auto-escalation on stop-work-order entry with the abatement-evidence checklist attached (replaces manual Steps 1/4 coordination)
- Chain-wide hazard-sweep campaign auto-generated from the order's defect classification (replaces manual Step-8 sweep setup)

### Controls
- CTL-313 (ensure controlled execution — Occupational Health & Safety (PA-24.1).)
- CTL-226 (ensure OHS compliance & incident management.)
- operational: no resumption without the written DOLE lifting order filed in the compliance calendar; wage continuity for stoppage-affected workers verified by payroll exception report through the order's life

### Time Estimate
Per event: Steps 1–3 ≈ 20–40 staff-hours in week 1 (HSE, site management, HR); Steps 4–8 ≈ 40–80 staff-hours over the abatement life plus trade labor. Steady state: register upkeep and drill integration ≈ 4–8 hours/year absorbed in the W512 rotation — no incremental headcount.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed by the HSE posts, site management, Facilities/Maintenance and the HRBP bench; the chain-wide sweep rides existing audit capacity. No incremental headcount.
