## W1668. Store Supplies & Consumables Procurement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly replenishment cycle or low stock alert |
| **Frequency** | Monthly blanket PO releases; weekly ad-hoc requests |
| **Volume** | ~200 stores × ~50 supply SKUs = ~10,000 supply items/month |
| **Owner** | Procurement Specialist — Non-Merchandise |
| **Participants** | Procurement Specialist, Store Managers, AP Clerk |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Procurement Specialist manages store supplies catalog: (a) shelf labels and price tags, (b) receipt paper rolls (thermal), (c) shopping bags (paper and plastic per LGU regulations — some Philippine cities ban plastic bags), (d) cleaning supplies, (e) cash register tape, (f) signage materials, (g) go-bags (security bags for cash transport), (h) first aid kit supplies, (i) packing materials (stretch wrap, boxes for ecommerce) | Procurement Specialist | VP Store Operations | Ongoing |
| 2 | Monthly: system generates store supply replenishment suggestions based on: (a) standard supply allocation per store (e.g., 10 rolls of receipt paper per POS terminal per month), (b) usage rate from prior months, (c) special events (promotional signage materials for sale events) | System | Procurement Specialist | Automated |
| 3 | Procurement Specialist reviews and adjusts replenishment quantities; releases blanket PO to contracted suppliers; suppliers deliver to DC for consolidation with merchandise shipments to stores | Procurement Specialist | VP Store Operations | 4 hours/month |
| 4 | Store Managers submit ad-hoc supply requests through system (e.g., unexpected supply consumption, broken equipment replacement); approved by Regional Manager; Procurement Specialist processes via express order | Store Manager / Regional Manager | Procurement Specialist | 15 min/request |

### System Touchpoints
- Store supplies catalog with standard allocation parameters per store (W1668.1)
- Automated replenishment suggestion engine (W1668.2)
- Blanket PO release against annual supply contracts (W1668.3)
- Ad-hoc supply requisition with approval workflow (W1668.4)


### Pain Points / Risks
- **LGU-compliance risk**: bag supplies (paper vs. plastic) mismatched to each city's plastic-ban ordinances; mitigated by the LGU-aware catalog line items per store (step 1)
- **Checkout-stockout risk**: receipt-paper or price-tag stockouts halt POS lanes; mitigated by allocation-based replenishment suggestions plus the express ad-hoc requisition path (steps 2, 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "Procurement Specialist manages store supplies catalog" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-generate of "generates store supply replenishment suggestions based" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews and adjusts replenishment quantities" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-343 (ensure controlled execution — Non-Merchandise Procurement Operations (PA-34.1).)
- operational: Procurement Specialist — Non-Merchandise review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Monthly blanket-release cycle 4 hours (replenishment review and blanket PO release) ≈ 48 hours/year on ~10,000 supply items/month (replenishment suggestions automated); ad-hoc store supply requests 15 min each at realized volume; supplies catalog management ongoing.

## W1669. IT Equipment & Software Procurement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | IT procurement plan, equipment replacement cycle, or ad-hoc request |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly planned procurement; ad-hoc for replacements |
| **Volume** | ~50–100 IT purchase orders/quarter |
| **Owner** | IT Procurement Manager |
| **Participants** | IT Procurement Manager, CIO, IT team, Finance |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CIO develops annual IT procurement plan: (a) POS hardware refresh schedule (600 terminals; target 5-year lifecycle = ~120 replacements/year), (b) server and network infrastructure upgrades, (c) employee laptop/desktop refresh (525 active HQ + DC office staff — the TO's 532-role design retains the disabled—prepared Trade department), (d) software license renewals (annual), (e) new technology projects (per strategic plan W1645), (f) barcode scanners, RF guns, printers for stores and DCs | CIO | CFO | 1 week |
| 2 | IT Procurement Manager executes procurement: (a) obtain 3 quotes for items > PHP 50K, (b) evaluate: specifications, total cost of ownership, warranty, vendor support capability in Philippines, (c) recommend vendor to CIO, (d) create purchase requisition per W1675 | IT Procurement Manager | CIO | 2 hours/PO |
| 3 | Software procurement specifics: (a) SaaS subscriptions: annual license cost, user count, renewal date, auto-renewal flag, (b) perpetual licenses: maintenance and support fees, upgrade rights, (c) open source: license compliance review by IT, (d) all software procurement requires IT security review before purchase | IT Procurement Manager | CIO | Per item |
| 4 | IT asset tracking: all IT equipment recorded in asset register with: (a) asset tag, (b) assigned user/location, (c) warranty expiry, (d) depreciation schedule, (e) maintenance schedule | IT Procurement Manager / IT Team | CIO | Ongoing |


### System Touchpoints
- IT procurement plan repository — POS refresh (~120 terminals/year on 5-year lifecycle), infrastructure, laptop refresh for 525 active HQ + DC office staff (step 1)
- Three-quote workflow for items > PHP 50K with TCO evaluation (spec, warranty, PH support capability) (step 2)
- SaaS license register — annual cost, user count, renewal dates, auto-renewal flags (step 3)
- IT asset register interface (W35.1) — asset tag, user/location, warranty expiry, depreciation schedule (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Shadow-IT risk**: departments buy software outside IT, bypassing security review (links to W388); mitigated by the mandatory IT-security review before any software purchase (step 3)
- **TCO-blindness risk**: cheapest quote wins while warranty, support, and lifecycle costs lose; mitigated by total-cost-of-ownership as an explicit evaluation criterion (step 2)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-scan of "scanners, RF guns, printers for stores and DCs" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-create of "create purchase requisition per W1675" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "review by IT" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-343 (ensure controlled execution — Non-Merchandise Procurement Operations (PA-34.1).)
- operational: IT Procurement Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Annual IT procurement plan 1 week ≈ 40 hours; PO execution 2 hours/PO × ~50–100 IT POs/quarter ≈ 400–800 hours/year; software subscription renewals per item; IT asset-register tracking ongoing.

## W1670. Uniform, PPE & Safety Equipment Procurement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual uniform refresh, new hire onboarding, or PPE replenishment |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly bulk orders; ad-hoc for new hires |
| **Volume** | ~1,200–1,600 new hires/year × 3 sets uniform + quarterly PPE replenishment |
| **Owner** | HR Operations |
| **Participants** | HR Operations, Procurement Specialist, Store Managers, Safety Officer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | HR Operations manages uniform and PPE catalog: (a) uniforms: polo shirts (3 per employee), slacks/pants (2 per employee), safety shoes (1 per employee), ID lanyards, name badges, (b) PPE per role: hard hats (lumber yard staff), gloves (receiving clerks), safety goggles (cutting services), masks (paint mixing), high-visibility vests (stockroom), harnesses (high-bay access), (c) seasonal PPE: raincoats (monsoon season), cooling towels (summer) | HR Operations | CHRO | Ongoing |
| 2 | Quarterly: HR Operations forecasts uniform and PPE needs: (a) new hire demand (from recruitment pipeline per W292), (b) replacement demand (wear/tear cycle ~6 months for active roles), (c) regulatory PPE replenishment (per DOLE requirements), (d) creates purchase requisition per W1675 | HR Operations | Procurement Specialist | 1 day/quarter |
| 3 | Procurement Specialist sources from contracted uniform supplier and PPE suppliers; manages delivery to DC for distribution to stores and HQ | Procurement Specialist | VP Store Operations | 2 days/quarter |
| 4 | Store Manager distributes uniforms to new hires during onboarding (W292); PPE issued per role and tracked per employee in system (W1670.4 of HR-017); issues draw from an expense subinventory per requisition — never capitalized — under the store's named storeroom custodian, with balances counted quarterly against issues and returns, and separated employees' uniforms and PPE recovered or written off per policy | Store Manager | HR Operations | 15 min/new hire |


### System Touchpoints
- Uniform & PPE catalog — entitlements per employee (3 polo shirts, 2 slacks, safety shoes) and role-based PPE items (step 1)
- Quarterly demand forecast — new-hire demand from the recruitment pipeline (W292) plus wear/tear replacement (step 2)
- Supplier contract and DC distribution workflow — delivery consolidated for stores and HQ (step 3)
- Per-employee PPE issuance tracking (links to HR-017) (step 4)
- Expense subinventory issue-per-requisition with quarterly count under the named store custodian (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **PPE-compliance risk**: missing or ill-fitting PPE in safety-sensitive roles is a DOLE/RA 11058 exposure; mitigated by the role-based PPE catalog with per-employee issuance tracking (step 4)
- **Size-mismatch waste**: wrong size mix ordered and never issued; mitigated by forecasting from the hiring pipeline and replacement-demand analysis (step 2)
- **Stockpiling-and-loss risk**: PPE issued without requisition records evaporates between stores; mitigated by issue-per-requisition from the expense subinventory with named custodian and quarterly count (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "HR Operations manages uniform and PPE catalog" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-create of "creates purchase requisition per W1675" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-track of "tracked per employee in system" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-343 (ensure controlled execution — Non-Merchandise Procurement Operations (PA-34.1).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
Quarterly forecast-and-source cycle 3 days (uniform/PPE forecast 1 day + supplier sourcing and management 2 days) ≈ 96 hours/year across ~1,200–1,600 new-hire uniform sets plus PPE replenishment; uniform distribution 15 min/new hire ≈ 300–400 hours/year within onboarding; catalog management ongoing.

## W1671. Marketing Materials & Print Procurement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Promotional calendar event, campaign launch, or in-store signage refresh |
| **Frequency** | 6 major promotional events/year + monthly marketing activities |
| **Volume** | ~20–30 marketing procurement orders/year |
| **Owner** | Marketing Operations Manager |
| **Participants** | Marketing Operations, CMO, Procurement Specialist |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Marketing Operations Manager manages marketing materials procurement: (a) promotional flyers and catalogs (6 major sale events × 200 stores = 1,200 print runs), (b) in-store signage: banners, shelf talkers, danglers, window displays, (c) outdoor signage: tarpaulins, billboards, (d) digital assets: not physical procurement but vendor management for creative agency, photography, video production, (e) loyalty program materials: membership cards, welcome kits, (f) brand materials: store exterior signage (for new stores), uniforms (coordinated with HR) | Marketing Operations | CMO | Ongoing |
| 2 | Per campaign: Marketing Operations creates print specification: (a) quantity per store (varies by store size and traffic), (b) total print run (200 stores × allocation + 10% buffer), (c) paper quality, dimensions, finish, (d) delivery deadline (must arrive at DC 5 days before campaign launch for distribution to stores), (e) creates purchase requisition per W1675 | Marketing Operations | CMO | 2 hours/campaign |
| 3 | Procurement Specialist obtains quotes from contracted print vendors; evaluates quality, price, and delivery timeline; places order | Procurement Specialist | Marketing Operations | 1 day/campaign |
| 4 | Print materials delivered to DC; consolidated with store replenishment shipments; Store Manager receives and deploys per marketing instructions | DC Operations / Store Manager | Marketing Operations | Per campaign |


### System Touchpoints
- Campaign print-spec generator — quantity per store by size/traffic, total run across 200 stores (step 2)
- Contracted print-vendor quote workflow with quality/price/timeline evaluation (step 3)
- DC consolidation interface — print materials ride store replenishment shipments (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Timing-miss risk**: materials arrive after the campaign launches; mitigated by delivery-timeline evaluation in vendor selection and campaign-linked ordering (steps 2–3)
- **Overprint waste**: run sizes misjudged against real store need; mitigated by the store-size and traffic-based quantity model (step 2)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-generate of "Marketing Operations Manager manages marketing materials procurement" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-create of "creates print specification" (replaces manual Step 2).

### Controls
- CTL-343 (ensure controlled execution — Non-Merchandise Procurement Operations (PA-34.1).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
~10 hours per campaign (print specification 2 hours + quote evaluation and award 1 day), with print delivery consolidated into store replenishment shipments; × ~20–30 marketing procurement orders/year ≈ 200–300 hours/year; marketing materials catalog ongoing.

## W1672. Facility Maintenance Supplies & Services Procurement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Maintenance schedule, equipment failure, or seasonal maintenance needs |
| **Frequency** | Monthly recurring services; ad-hoc for repairs |
| **Volume** | ~500–700 maintenance POs/month across 205 locations |
| **Owner** | Facilities Manager |
| **Participants** | Facilities Manager, Store Managers, DC Supervisor, Procurement Specialist |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Facilities Manager manages contracted maintenance services: (a) aircon/HVAC cleaning and maintenance (quarterly per store — 200 stores × 4 = 800 service visits/year), (b) elevator/escalator maintenance (stores with multi-level format), (c) fire suppression system inspection (annual per BFP requirement), (d) pest control (monthly per store/DC — 205 locations × 12 = 2,460 service visits/year), (e) generator maintenance (per W808), (f) plumbing and electrical repairs, (g) floor maintenance (polishing, repair) | Facilities Manager | VP Store Operations | Ongoing |
| 2 | Store Managers submit maintenance service requests through system: (a) describe issue with photo, (b) urgency level: routine (7-day SLA), urgent (48-hour SLA), emergency (4-hour SLA), (c) system routes to Facilities Manager for approval and vendor dispatch | Store Manager | Facilities Manager | 10 min/request |
| 3 | Facilities Manager dispatches contracted vendor; tracks service completion; vendor submits service report and invoice; Facilities Manager confirms completion and approves invoice for payment | Facilities Manager | VP Store Operations | 15 min/request |
| 4 | Maintenance supplies procurement: (a) light bulbs (LED replacements), (b) cleaning chemicals, (c) paint for touch-ups, (d) plumbing fittings, (e) electrical components (switches, breakers), (f) ordered monthly through blanket PO from contracted suppliers | Facilities Manager | Procurement Specialist | 4 hours/month |


### System Touchpoints
- Contracted maintenance schedule — quarterly HVAC cleaning across 200 stores (~800 services/year) plus recurring services (step 1)
- Service request workflow with urgency SLAs — routine 7-day, urgent 48-hour, emergency response (step 2)
- Vendor dispatch and completion confirmation with invoice tie (step 3)
- Maintenance supplies catalog — LED bulbs, chemicals, paint, fittings (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Vendor-self-grading risk**: vendor invoices paid without confirmed completion; mitigated by Facilities completion confirmation before invoice approval (step 3)
- **Deferred-maintenance spiral**: small issues deferred become major failures; mitigated by SLA-tiered routing that forces urgent/emergency classification at intake (step 2)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-inspect of "Facilities Manager manages contracted maintenance services" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-submit of "submit maintenance service requests through system" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-dispatch of "dispatches contracted vendor" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-343 (ensure controlled execution — Non-Merchandise Procurement Operations (PA-34.1).)
- operational: Facilities Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Maintenance request cycle 25 min/request (store submission 10 min + dispatch and completion tracking 15 min) × ~500–700 maintenance POs/month ≈ 2,500–3,500 hours/year; monthly maintenance-supplies procurement 4 hours ≈ 48 hours/year; contracted-services administration ongoing.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | HQ departments submit monthly office supply requests to Admin Manager: (a) paper, pens, folders, binders, (b) printer toner and cartridges, (c) pantry supplies (coffee, water, utensils), (d) meeting room supplies (whiteboard markers, projector bulbs) | HQ Staff | Admin Manager | Monthly |

- **Hoarding risk**: departments over-order each cycle to build private stockpiles; mitigated by Admin Manager consolidation review with usage tracking (steps 2–3)

## W1674. Emergency & Urgent Non-Merchandise Purchase Processing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Urgent operational need (equipment breakdown, storm damage, safety issue) |
| **Frequency** | ~50–100 emergency purchases/month across all locations |
| **Volume** | ~0.25–0.5 per store/month (50–100 chain-wide); higher during typhoon season |
| **Owner** | Store Manager (store-level); DC Supervisor (DC-level); Department Head (HQ) |
| **Participants** | Requesting manager, Finance, Procurement Specialist |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Requesting manager creates emergency purchase request in system: (a) description and justification, (b) estimated cost, (c) urgency level: (i) **Emergency** — safety or operational continuity threat (e.g., roof leak during typhoon, POS terminal failure, security system failure), (ii) **Urgent** — significant operational impact if not resolved within 48 hours (e.g., aircon failure in peak summer, forklift breakdown at DC), (d) emergency requests bypass standard 3-quote requirement for items < PHP 50K | Requesting Manager | — | 10 min |
| 2 | Approval workflow: (a) < PHP 10K: Store Manager or DC Supervisor self-approve, (b) PHP 10–50K: Regional Manager or VP approval, (c) > PHP 50K: CFO approval, (d) emergency flag expedites approval — SLA: < 2 hours for emergency, < 4 hours for urgent | Approver | — | Per SLA |
| 3 | Requesting manager procures from available vendor (often local supplier for speed); submits receipt and documentation for reimbursement or direct payment | Requesting Manager | Finance | Variable |
| 4 | Post-emergency: Finance reviews emergency purchases monthly for: (a) total spend by location, (b) pattern analysis (same location recurring emergencies → indicates need for planned maintenance or capital investment), (c) policy compliance (were emergency procedures followed?) | Finance | CFO | 2 hours/month |


### System Touchpoints
- Emergency purchase request workflow with urgency tiers and tiered self-approval (< PHP 10K Store Manager/DC Supervisor; PHP 10–50K RM/VP; > PHP 50K CFO) (steps 1–2)
- Receipt and documentation submission for reimbursement/direct payment (step 3)
- Monthly emergency-spend pattern review by location (step 4)
- **Custody & precedence** (per the [Event Custody & Precedence Register](../event-custody-and-precedence-register.md), E-12): store walk-in cash emergencies under Store-Manager authority (≤ PHP 5,000/purchase, ≤ PHP 20,000/month, Regional Manager phone approval above) execute the VS-07 W1272 fast path; anything above store authority, DC/HQ emergencies, and all system-routed urgent POs execute here (quote bypass < PHP 50K for Emergency class)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Emergency-abuse risk**: routine buys routed as emergencies to bypass approvals; mitigated by monthly pattern analysis flagging recurring same-location emergencies (step 4)
- **Unliquidated-advance risk**: receipts lost in the rush leave advances undocumented; mitigated by reimbursement gated on receipt submission (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-create of "creates emergency purchase request in system" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System rule-based auto-approval of "approve" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "documentation for reimbursement or direct payment" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-343 (ensure controlled execution — Non-Merchandise Procurement Operations (PA-34.1).)
- operational: Store Manager (store-level); DC Supervisor (DC-level); Department Head review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
~10 min per emergency purchase request plus expedited procurement effort that varies with local sourcing; × ~50–100 emergency purchases/month ≈ 100–600 hours/year; monthly post-emergency spend review 2 hours ≈ 24 hours/year; approval routing per SLA.

## W1675. Non-Merchandise Purchase Requisition to Payment Workflow

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Any non-merchandise purchase need identified |
| **Frequency** | ~1,500–2,000 non-merchandise purchase requisitions/month |
| **Volume** | ~8–10 per location/month + HQ departmental purchases |
| **Owner** | Procurement Specialist |
| **Participants** | Requesting employee, Approving manager, Procurement Specialist, AP Clerk |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Requesting employee creates purchase requisition in system: (a) item/service description, (b) quantity and estimated cost, (c) preferred vendor (if known), (d) delivery location, (e) required-by date, (f) budget category and cost center | Requesting Employee | — | 10 min |
| 2 | Approval workflow (tiered by amount): (a) < PHP 5K: direct supervisor, (b) PHP 5–50K: department head, (c) PHP 50–500K: VP + Finance Manager, (d) > PHP 500K: CFO + CEO, (e) budget check: system verifies spend against departmental budget; over-budget requisitions require VP + Finance approval | System / Approver | — | Per SLA (24 hours standard) |
| 3 | Procurement Specialist processes approved requisition: (a) < PHP 50K: order from contracted or preferred vendor (no competitive quote required), (b) PHP 50–500K: obtain minimum 3 quotes, (c) > PHP 500K: formal bidding process with bid evaluation committee, (d) create PO and send to vendor | Procurement Specialist | Finance Manager | 1–5 days |
| 4 | Goods/service received: (a) requesting employee confirms receipt and quality in system, (b) for services: confirm service completion and satisfaction, (c) system performs 2-way match (PO → Invoice) for non-merchandise purchases (no GR for services) | Requesting Employee | — | 5 min |
| 5 | AP Clerk processes payment per standard P2P workflow (W89/W90) — 2-way match for non-PO items; 3-way match for PO-based items with goods receipt | AP Clerk | Controller | Per W89 |


### System Touchpoints
- Purchase requisition workflow with tiered approvals (< PHP 5K supervisor; 5–50K dept head; 50–500K VP + Finance; > 500K CEO/Board) (steps 1–2)
- Quote rules — ≥ PHP 50K requires competitive quotes (step 3)
- Receipt and quality confirmation with 3-way match handoff to AP (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Split-purchase risk**: requests carved below thresholds to dodge quotes/approvals; mitigated by vendor-level aggregation and split-detection in compliance monitoring (W1689) (steps 2–3)
- **Receipt-fraud risk**: requester confirms goods never delivered; mitigated by receipt confirmation paired with 3-way match before payment release (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-capture of "Requesting employee creates purchase requisition in system" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-validate of "check" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-create of "create PO and send to vendor" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-207 (prevent unauthorized non-merchandise spend.)
- operational: Procurement Specialist review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Requisition-to-receipt handling ~15 min of requester effort (creation 10 min + receipt confirmation 5 min) plus Procurement Specialist processing on the 1–5-day cycle ≈ 30–45 min total per requisition × ~1,500–2,000/month ≈ 9,000–18,000 hours/year chain-wide; approval routing per 24-hour SLA; payment per W89.
