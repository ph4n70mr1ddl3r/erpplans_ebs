| Field | Detail |

### Staffing Implication
15–20 evaluations/month, 10–15 approvals/year. ~2 weeks per evaluation. 1 Property Acquisition Manager + Director, Facilities & Real Estate oversight. Absorbed.

## W430. LGU Business Permit & "Amillaramiento" (RPT) On-Site Inspection

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled or surprise inspection by LGU (Bureau of Fire Protection, Sanitary, Building Official) |
| **Frequency** | Annual (for renewal) or ad-hoc |
| **Volume** | 200 stores × ~3–4 inspection types/year = ~600–800 inspections |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Admin, Facilities Coordinator, LGU Inspector, Regional Manager |

### Background

In the Philippines, operating a big-box retail store requires multiple local government permits (Mayor's Permit, Fire Safety Inspection Certificate, Sanitary Permit, etc.). LGU inspectors from the Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP) and the City/Municipal Engineering Office conduct on-site inspections to verify compliance with safety, health, and building standards. This workflow manages the interaction with inspectors and the resolution of "Notice of Violations."

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Inspector Arrival**: LGU inspector arrives at the store; Store Manager verifies inspector credentials (Mission Order) and registers them in the Compliance Log | Store Manager | — | 15 min |
| 2 | **On-Site Walkthrough**: Store Manager and Store Admin accompany the inspector through the premises: (a) fire exits, (b) extinguisher pressure/maintenance, (c) sanitary conditions in restrooms/canteen, (d) structural safety of lumber yard racks | Store Manager | Regional Mgr | 1–2 hours |
| 3 | **Inspection Report**: Inspector issues an "After Mission Report" or "Notice of Inspection"; Store Manager scans and uploads the document to the Compliance Portal | Store Manager | — | 15 min |
| 4 | **Notice of Violation (NOV) Intake**: If violations are found (e.g., blocked fire exit, expired extinguisher), the inspector issues an NOV with a 5-to-15 day correction period | Store Manager | Regional Mgr | 30 min |
| 5 | **Correction Action**: Store Manager triggers an "Emergency Work Order" (W47) to fix the violation; Facilities Coordinator manages the contractor/maintenance team | Store Manager / Facilities | VP Store Operations | 1–3 days |
| 6 | **Re-Inspection & Clearing**: Store Manager notifies LGU of correction; inspector returns for re-inspection; Store Manager receives the "Cleared" status and pays any required fines | Store Manager | Finance Mgr | 2 hours |
| 7 | **Permit Renewal Linkage**: Cleared inspection reports are attached to the "LGU Permit Renewal" workflow (W54) to ensure the Mayor's Permit can be renewed the following year | Store Admin | — | 15 min |

### System Touchpoints

- Compliance Portal: tracks inspection history, pending NOVs, and correction deadlines for 200 sites (W430.3)
- Integration with W47 (Store Maintenance — for fixing violations) and W54 (Permit Renewal)
- LGU Violation Log: dashboard for Regional Managers to track systemic issues (e.g., recurring fire safety violations)

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Discretionary Enforcement**: Different LGUs may interpret fire or sanitary codes inconsistently, leading to unexpected NOVs for previously compliant stores
- **Closure risk**: Severe fire safety violations (e.g., non-functional sprinkler system) can lead to immediate "Cease and Desist" or store closure orders by the BFP; mitigated by standard operating procedures and system-enforced validation rules
- **Record Inaccessibility**: If the store cannot produce the previous year's permit or inspection receipt during a surprise visit, it faces immediate fines


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "log" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-inspect of "inspector through the premises" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-scan of "scans and uploads the document to the Compliance Portal" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-218 (ensure facility compliance & lease-cost integrity.)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication

Managed by the Store Manager as part of store administration duties. Effort is ~4–6 hours per year per store.

### Time Estimate

**Total**: Arrival & Walkthrough — 2 hours; NOV Resolution — 1–3 days elapsed; Re-inspection — 2 hours; **Total cycle: ~1 week for NOV resolution**

## W807. Store Closure, Lease Termination & Asset Recovery Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Store performance review per W67 identifies chronically underperforming location; lease expiry without renewal decision; strategic market exit; force majeure (typhoon damage, LGU closure order) |
| **Frequency** | Ad-hoc; estimated 2-5 store closures/year as BuildRight optimizes its 200-store network |
| **Volume** | Each closure involves inventory valued at PHP 15-30M, fixed assets of PHP 5-10M, and 25-35 employees |
| **Owner** | VP for Store Operations |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Finance, HR, Legal, Real Estate, Merchandising, IT, Loss Prevention, VP Supply Chain |

### Background

BuildRight operates 200 stores and regularly evaluates store performance per W67. Underperforming locations — those with negative EBITDA for 2+ consecutive quarters, or where lease renewal terms are economically unfavorable — are candidates for closure. Store closure is the reverse of W16 new store opening: it involves inventory liquidation, employee transfer or separation, lease termination, asset recovery, regulatory de-registration, and community relations. This workflow ensures store closures are executed in compliance with Philippine labor law (DOLE due process for separations), lease agreements (proper notice per contract terms), and regulatory requirements (BIR, LGU, BFP de-registration).

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Closure Decision & Approval**: VP Store Operations presents closure recommendation to CEO and Board: (a) store P&L analysis per W67 showing sustained underperformance; (b) lease terms review per W117: remaining lease term, early termination penalty, renewal option; (c) market analysis: is underperformance location-specific or market-wide; (d) employee impact: number of employees, transfer opportunities at nearby stores, separation cost estimate per W43; (e) inventory and asset recovery estimate; (f) total closure cost vs. continued operation loss projection; (g) CEO approves closure; Board approves if closure cost exceeds DOA threshold | VP Store Operations | CEO / Board | 2-4 weeks |
| 2 | **Lease Termination**: Legal handles lease termination per W117: (a) review lease agreement for termination notice requirements (typically 60-90 days); (b) if early termination: negotiate termination fee with landlord per lease terms; (c) if lease expiry: serve non-renewal notice per contractual deadline; (d) negotiate security deposit return; (e) document condition of premises (photos, video) to protect against damage claims; (f) coordinate with VP Legal on any lease disputes | VP Legal / Real Estate | VP Store Operations | 2-3 months |
| 3 | **Employee Communication & Transition**: HR manages employee impact per DOLE requirements: (a) 30-day advance notice to affected employees per DOLE guidelines; (b) individual meetings with each employee: present options (transfer to nearby store, voluntary separation with enhanced package, or retrenchment with statutory separation pay per W43); (c) for transfers: coordinate with receiving Store Manager per W511 cross-location transfer; (d) for separations: compute final pay per W643 including separation pay (1/2 month per year of service for retrenchment under LC Art. 298); (e) assist with DOLE reporting if retrenchment exceeds threshold; (f) employee communication timeline must not precede public announcement to prevent premature store disruption | HR Manager / CHRO | VP Store Operations | 30-60 days |
| 4 | **Inventory Liquidation**: Merchandising and Supply Chain execute inventory removal: (a) W93 markdown execution for slow-moving items: progressive discounts (25%, 50%, 75%) over 4-6 weeks before closure; (b) transferable inventory: ship to nearby stores or DC per W22 at cost; (c) consignment inventory: return to vendor per W23; (d) unsold remaining inventory: bulk liquidation to discount retailers or jobbers; (e) inventory write-off for unsalvageable items per W587; (f) target: recover 70-85% of inventory value | Merchandising / Supply Chain | VP Store Operations | 4-8 weeks |
| 5 | **Asset Recovery**: Facilities and IT execute asset removal: (a) IT equipment (POS terminals per W265, servers, networking) decommissioned and shipped to IT warehouse for redeployment per W131; (b) store fixtures and furniture: assess reuse potential; ship reusable items to warehouse for new store deployment; (c) shelving and racking: sell to used fixture dealers or scrap per W443; (d) signage and branding: removed per lease requirements; (e) fixed assets: update register per W39 with disposal accounting | IT / Facilities / Finance | VP Store Operations | 1-2 weeks |
| 6 | **Regulatory De-Registration**: Legal and Finance handle regulatory closure per W437: (a) BIR: notify RDO of branch closure, cancel CAS registration per W54A, file final tax return; (b) LGU: surrender Business Permit, cancel LBT registration per W802; (c) BFP: FSIC cancellation per W476; (d) DENR: cancel Permit to Operate if applicable per W477; (e) SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG: update employer location records; (f) update Location Master per W254 to "Closed" status | Legal / Finance | VP Legal | 1-2 months |
| 7 | **Community & Customer Communication**: Marketing/Communications Manager (CMO org) manages stakeholder communication: (a) customer notification: post signage in-store 30 days before closure, redirect to nearest BuildRight store; (b) loyalty members per W17: notify of nearest alternative location, maintain points balance; (c) community: coordinate with Barangay per W209 for respectful departure; (d) media: prepare press statement if closure attracts media attention; (e) update store locator on website and app per W615 | Marketing Comms Manager / VP Store Operations | CEO | 30 days before closure |
| 8 | **Premises Handover & Close-Out**: Final handover to landlord: (a) remove all BuildRight fixtures, signage, and materials per lease "make-good" requirements; (b) restore premises to contractually agreed condition; (c) final walkthrough with landlord representative; (d) document condition with photos; (e) settle any outstanding rent, CAM charges, or utility bills per W118; (f) receive security deposit return; (g) close all utility accounts (electricity, water, telecom); (h) Finance closes store cost center in GL per W9 | Facilities / Finance / Legal | VP Store Operations | 1-2 weeks |

### System Touchpoints

- Store performance analytics per W67 for closure decision support
- Oracle Property Manager per W117 (fit-gap F3) for termination terms
- HR separation processing per W43, W643 for employee transition
- Inventory management per W93, W22, W23, W587 for liquidation
- IT asset management per W131, W265 for equipment recovery
- Fixed asset disposal per W39
- Location Master per W254 for deactivation
- Compliance calendar per W506 for regulatory de-registration deadlines
- Customer notification module per W615 for digital channel updates
- Finance GL per W9 for cost center closure

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Lease termination penalty**: early lease termination penalties can reach 6-12 months of rent; for a PHP 500K/month lease, this is PHP 3M-6M in sunk cost; negotiating favorable exit terms is critical
- **DOLE retrenchment compliance**: if 10+ employees are retrenched at a single location, DOLE requires 30-day advance notice to both employees and DOLE Regional Office; non-compliance risks illegal dismissal case
- **Landlord "make-good" disputes**: landlords may claim the premises require restoration beyond what BuildRight considers fair wear-and-tear; disputes can delay security deposit return by months
- **Inventory liquidation value erosion**: markdown prices attract opportunistic buyers but erode margin; transferring inventory to nearby stores is better for value recovery but requires logistics coordination
- **Customer migration leakage**: not all customers from a closed store will migrate to the nearest alternative BuildRight; some will defect to competitors, permanently reducing market share
- **Employee morale impact**: store closures at one location create anxiety at nearby stores; clear communication about closure criteria and employee transfer opportunities is essential


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "review per W117" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-document of "document condition of premises" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-report of "reporting if retrenchment exceeds threshold" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-301 (ensure controlled execution — Site Selection & Lease Management (PA-20.1).)
- operational: VP Store Operations review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication

- **VP Store Operations**: 20-30 hours per closure on coordination and decision-making; absorbed by existing role
- **HR Manager**: 20-40 hours per closure on employee transition; absorbed by existing role with peak support from HR team
- **Legal**: 10-20 hours per closure on lease termination; absorbed by existing role
- **Finance**: 10-15 hours per closure on financial close-out; absorbed by existing role
- **No incremental headcount**; closure team assembled from existing functional roles per closure project

### Time Estimate

- Closure decision: 2-4 weeks
- Lease termination: 2-3 months
- Employee transition: 30-60 days
- Inventory liquidation: 4-8 weeks
- Asset recovery: 1-2 weeks
- Regulatory de-registration: 1-2 months
- Premises handover: 1-2 weeks
- **Total closure timeline**: 3-6 months from decision to final handover

## W1183. Store Lease CAM Reconciliation, Rent Escalation & Landlord Relationship Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual CAM reconciliation from landlord; scheduled rent escalation per lease; lease renewal negotiation; or landlord dispute |
| **Frequency** | Annual CAM reconciliation per lease; rent escalation per lease schedule (typically annual 3–5% increase); lease renewals every 3–5 years |
| **Volume** | 200 store leases + 4 DC leases + HQ office lease = ~205 active leases; ~50–70 CAM reconciliations/year; ~150–180 rent escalations/year (most leases escalate annually, per W1863) |
| **Owner** | Real Estate Manager |
| **Participants** | Real Estate Manager, Finance Controller, Legal, Store Manager, COO, Landlord Representative |

### Background

BuildRight leases all 200 store locations from external landlords (or from BuildRight Property Management, Inc. for company-owned sites). Philippine commercial retail leases typically include: (a) base rent (fixed or percentage of sales), (b) Common Area Maintenance (CAM) charges covering building maintenance, parking, security, and utilities for shared spaces, (c) annual rent escalation (3–5% compound increase), (d) Real Property Tax (RPT) pass-through from landlord, (e) tenant improvement allowances for store build-out. CAM charges are often estimated during the year and reconciled annually — landlords may overcharge or include expenses that are the landlord's responsibility per the lease. With 200+ leases, CAM reconciliation represents significant potential savings (or overcharges if not audited). Rent escalation clauses must be tracked and validated against lease terms to avoid overpayment.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lease database maintenance**: Real Estate Manager maintains master lease database in ERP: (a) lease term, renewal options, termination clauses; (b) base rent schedule with escalation dates and amounts; (c) CAM charge methodology (fixed, pro-rata share, actual pass-through); (d) RPT allocation; (e) tenant improvement allowance balance; (f) key dates: escalation, renewal notice, lease expiry; (g) system generates alerts 90 days before each key date | Real Estate Manager | COO | 2 hours/week |
| 2 | **Annual CAM reconciliation review**: When landlord submits annual CAM reconciliation statement: (a) Finance Controller reviews: (i) verify CAM calculation methodology matches lease terms; (ii) audit landlord's supporting invoices for shared expenses — flag expenses that are landlord's responsibility (structural repairs, roof replacement) vs. tenant responsibility; (iii) verify pro-rata share calculation (BuildRight's leasable area ÷ total building area); (iv) compare to prior year — flag line items with > 15% increase; (v) identify duplicate charges or expenses not authorized per lease; (b) for overcharges: Real Estate Manager files dispute with landlord within lease-specified dispute period (typically 30 days); (c) for undercharges: advise landlord of discrepancy to avoid future retroactive billing; (d) document agreed CAM amount and process adjustment payment or credit | Finance Controller / Real Estate Manager | COO | 4–8 hours/lease |
| 3 | **Rent escalation validation**: System calculates expected rent per escalation clause; Finance validates against landlord's invoice: (a) verify escalation percentage matches lease (e.g., 3% annual compound increase); (b) recalculate base rent for new period; (c) if landlord's invoiced amount exceeds calculated amount: dispute and request correction; (d) update lease database with new rent amount; (e) update store P&L budget per W489 with escalated rent | System / Finance Controller | Real Estate Manager | 30 min/lease |
| 4 | **Lease renewal negotiation**: 90–180 days before lease expiry (per system alert): (a) Real Estate Manager assesses: store performance (revenue, margin, foot traffic), local market rental rates, alternative locations nearby, landlord relationship history; (b) negotiate renewal terms: (i) base rent — benchmark against market rate (PHP/sqm/month for comparable retail space in area); (ii) CAM cap — negotiate maximum annual CAM increase (e.g., capped at 5%); (iii) renewal term — seek 3–5 year renewal for stability; (iv) tenant improvement allowance — negotiate refresh allowance for store upgrades; (v) exclusive use clause — ensure landlord cannot lease adjacent space to direct competitor; (c) Legal reviews renewal agreement per W62; (d) COO approves renewal or directs closure per W807 | Real Estate Manager / Legal | COO | 2–4 weeks/renewal |
| 5 | **Landlord relationship management**: (a) quarterly: Real Estate Manager conducts landlord satisfaction check-in: address maintenance issues, parking concerns, signage disputes, shared facility problems; (b) escalate unresolved issues to COO for executive-level landlord engagement; (c) maintain positive relationships for lease negotiations, CAM disputes, and emergency situations (e.g., building damage from typhoon requiring landlord structural repair); (d) annual landlord meeting for top-20 leases by value | Real Estate Manager | COO | 4 hours/month |
| 6 | **Monthly rent & CAM payment**: (a) system generates monthly rent and CAM payment schedule per lease; (b) AP processes payment per W7 with automatic GL coding to store cost center; (c) for BuildRight Property Management, Inc. leases: intercompany settlement per W87; (d) track rent expense per store for P&L reporting per W102 | System / AP Clerk | Finance Controller | Automated |

### System Touchpoints

- AP integration per W7 for automated rent payments
- Finance GL per W86 for rent expense tracking
- Intercompany module per W87 for Property Management entity leases
- Store P&L reporting per W102

### Pain Points / Risks
- **CAM overcharging**: landlords routinely overestimate CAM charges; without annual reconciliation review, BuildRight overpays; with 200 leases, even PHP 50,000/year overcharge per lease = PHP 10M/year
- **Rent escalation errors**: manual rent calculation errors by landlords are common; automated validation per Step 3 catches overcharges
- **Landlord maintenance disputes**: landlords responsible for structural repairs (roof, foundation, electrical main panel) may delay or refuse; store operations impacted during rainy season (roof leaks) or power outages
- **Lease renewal urgency**: missing a renewal notice deadline (typically 90–180 days before expiry) may result in holdover tenancy at higher month-to-month rates or landlord non-renewal forcing store closure
- **Philippine commercial lease law**: Civil Code provisions on lease contracts provide limited tenant protections compared to residential leases; lease terms heavily favor landlords in prime locations

## W1445. New Store Site Feasibility Assessment, Traffic Analysis, Competitive Mapping & Real Estate Due Diligence

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Expansion pipeline milestone (10–15 new stores/year target); or broker/landlord inbound proposal for new location |
| **Frequency** | 10–15 site assessments per year; each assessment takes 4–6 weeks from initial screening to go/no-go recommendation |
| **Volume** | Each assessment covers 1 candidate site; typically 2–3 candidate sites evaluated per approved new store location; ~20–45 site assessments/year total |
| **Owner** | COO & Construction |
| **Participants** | COO, Real Estate Manager, VP Store Operations, Finance Controller, Category Manager (local demand assessment), Legal Counsel, External Real Estate Broker, LGU Planning Office |

### Background

BuildRight Depot targets 10–15 new store openings annually across the Philippines. Each new store represents a PHP 30–50M investment (lease commitments, fit-out, initial inventory, pre-opening expenses). Selecting the right location is the single most important decision for store success — a poorly located store underperforms for the entire lease term (typically 5–10 years). The Philippines' archipelagic geography, varying municipal zoning regulations, and rapidly changing urban landscapes make site selection particularly complex. This workflow governs the end-to-end site feasibility assessment: market analysis, traffic and accessibility evaluation, competitive mapping, financial modeling, and real estate due diligence before lease commitment.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Market opportunity screening**: Real Estate Manager conducts initial market screening: (a) identify high-growth municipalities/cities using: (i) Philippine Statistics Authority population and household growth data, (ii) DTI registered construction permits (proxy for construction activity), (iii) residential development pipeline (new subdivisions, condominiums = future customers), (iv) existing BuildRight store coverage map — identify gaps in the network (> 30-minute drive to nearest BuildRight = underserved), (b) prioritize regions aligned with BuildRight expansion strategy: North/Central Luzon (20 stores → 40+ target) and Visayas (40 stores → 60+ target), (c) produce shortlist of 5–10 candidate municipalities for deeper analysis | Real Estate Manager | COO | 2 weeks |
| 2 | **Candidate site identification and traffic analysis**: For shortlisted municipalities: (a) Real Estate Manager identifies 2–3 candidate sites per municipality via: (i) broker network, (ii) property listings, (iii) direct landlord/developer outreach, (iv) LGU economic zone inquiries, (b) conduct traffic and accessibility analysis for each candidate site: (i) vehicle traffic count (DTI/LGU traffic data + manual count during peak hours), (ii) pedestrian foot traffic (proxy for walk-in potential), (iii) road network accessibility: major road frontage, highway proximity, truck accessibility for DC deliveries, (iv) public transportation access (jeepney/bus routes), (c) minimum site requirements: 8,000–15,000 sqm selling area + parking + loading dock; standalone or end-cap in retail park preferred | Real Estate Manager / External Broker | COO | 2 weeks |
| 3 | **Competitive mapping**: For each candidate site: (a) map all competing hardware/home improvement retailers within 10 km radius: (i) other hardware chains (Wilcon, CitiHardware, Truworths), (ii) independent hardware stores, (iii) building material dealers, (iv) online competitors (Lazada, Shopee delivery coverage), (b) assess competitive intensity: (i) number of competitors, (ii) estimated competitor revenue per store, (iii) BuildRight's differentiation opportunity (wider assortment, lower prices, better service, BOPIS), (c) estimate BuildRight's addressable market share based on competitive landscape | Real Estate Manager / Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1 week |
| 4 | **Financial feasibility modeling**: Finance Controller builds store-level financial model: (a) revenue projection: (i) estimated monthly sales based on catchment population, household income, construction activity, and competitive share, (ii) Year 1–5 revenue ramp (typical: 60% of mature store revenue in Year 1, 80% in Year 2, 95% in Year 3, 100% in Year 4), (b) cost projection: (i) lease cost (base rent + CAM + escalation), (ii) fit-out and capex amortization, (iii) staffing cost (29 employees), (iv) inventory carrying cost, (v) DC delivery cost, (c) profitability analysis: (i) gross margin estimate (28–32%), (ii) store-level EBITDA breakeven timeline, (iii) NPV and IRR over lease term, (d) minimum investment criteria: NPV > 0 at 15% discount rate; EBITDA positive by Year 3; payback period < 5 years | Finance Controller | CFO | 1 week |
| 5 | **Real estate due diligence and LGU navigation**: For sites passing financial feasibility: (a) Legal Counsel conducts due diligence: (i) title verification (Registry of Deeds), (ii) zoning compliance (residential/commercial/mixed-use), (iii) tax declaration and real property tax status, (iv) environmental compliance (DENR clearance if applicable), (v) access rights and easements, (b) LGU engagement: (i) secure locational clearance from municipal/city planning office, (ii) confirm building permit requirements and timeline, (iii) identify local business tax rates and incentives (some LGUs offer tax holidays for new investors), (iv) assess political support (meeting with mayor/economic development office), (c) Real Estate Manager negotiates Letter of Intent (LOI) with landlord: (i) lease rate, (ii) lease term (5–10 years + renewal options), (iii) tenant improvement allowance, (iv) escalation terms, (v) exclusivity clause (prevent competing hardware tenant in same development) | Legal Counsel / Real Estate Manager | COO | 2 weeks |
| 6 | **Go/no-go decision and lease execution**: (a) COO presents complete feasibility package to CEO/CFO: market analysis, site specifics, competitive assessment, financial model, due diligence findings, lease terms, (b) CEO approves or rejects; if approved, Legal Counsel executes lease agreement, (c) VP Store Operations activates new store opening project per W702 (target: 4–6 months from lease signing to store opening) | COO | CEO | 1 week |

### System Touchpoints
- Geographic Information System (GIS) — market mapping, competitor locations, drive-time analysis (W1445.2)
- Store financial model template — standardized NPV/IRR model for new store locations (W1445.4)
- Real estate pipeline tracker — site assessment status, scoring, and decision tracking (W1445.6)
- Integration with W116 (site selection), W702 (new store opening), W16 (new store setup in ERP)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Data availability in provincial markets**: population, income, and construction activity data for Philippine municipalities is often outdated or incomplete; reliance on local broker intelligence and field surveys is necessary
- **Landlord leverage imbalance**: in high-demand retail locations (Metro Manila, Cebu), landlords have multiple tenants competing for the same space and may demand above-market rents that fail BuildRight's financial feasibility threshold
- **LGU regulatory uncertainty**: zoning reclassifications, pending LGU development plan changes, or political transitions can alter site viability after lease commitment; include lease termination clauses for regulatory changes
- **Construction timeline risk**: Philippine construction permits may take 3–6 months in some LGUs, delaying store opening and extending the pre-revenue investment period; mitigated by the COO & Construction review and approval gate

- operational: COO & Construction review and approval gate

- Competitive mapping: 1 week per municipality
- Financial modeling: 1 week per site
- Due diligence: 2 weeks per site
- Decision: 1 week

## W1446. Store Lease Renewal Negotiation, Market Rent Benchmarking & Strategic Exit or Relocation Decision

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Lease expiration within 12 months; or landlord-initiated renegotiation; or store performance review triggering location reassessment |
| **Frequency** | ~20–30 lease renewals/year (assuming 200 stores with average 7–10 year lease terms; some leases may be shorter or negotiated mid-term) |
| **Volume** | Each renewal involves 1 store location; ~20–30 renewal decisions/year; ~5–10 of these may involve strategic exit or relocation evaluation |
| **Owner** | COO & Construction |
| **Participants** | COO, Real Estate Manager, Finance Controller, VP Store Operations, Store Manager (performance input), Legal Counsel, External Real Estate Broker (market data) |

### Background

BuildRight Depot operates 200 stores, most of which are leased from third-party landlords (BuildRight Property Management, Inc. owns a limited number of sites). Lease terms typically run 5–10 years with renewal options. As leases approach expiration, BuildRight faces a strategic decision: (a) renew at current terms (often with rent escalation), (b) renegotiate for better terms (lower rent, longer term, improvement allowance), (c) relocate to a better site nearby (if store is underperforming due to location), or (d) exit the market entirely (if the location is no longer viable due to market changes). This workflow manages the lease renewal process: market rent benchmarking, store performance assessment, renewal negotiation, and the strategic exit/relocation decision.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lease expiration inventory and prioritization**: Real Estate Manager maintains rolling lease expiration calendar: (a) identify all leases expiring in the next 12–18 months, (b) prioritize by: (i) annual rent value (higher rent = higher financial impact), (ii) store performance (underperforming stores need deeper assessment), (iii) landlord relationship (difficult landlords require earlier engagement), (c) assign each renewal to a negotiation track: (i) standard renewal (performing store, fair market rent), (ii) renegotiation (performing store but above-market rent), (iii) relocation evaluation (underperforming store), (iv) exit evaluation (market no longer viable) | Real Estate Manager | COO | 1 week |
| 2 | **Store performance assessment**: VP Store Operations and Finance Controller assess each store's performance: (a) sales performance: (i) actual vs. plan (Year-over-Year), (ii) sales per sqm vs. chain average, (iii) same-store sales growth trend (3-year), (b) profitability: (i) store-level EBITDA margin, (ii) rent-to-revenue ratio (target: < 5% of revenue), (c) strategic value: (i) market coverage (does closing this store create a coverage gap?), (ii) competitive presence (does this store prevent competitor entry into the market?), (iii) brand visibility in the community | VP Store Operations / Finance Controller | COO | 1 week per batch |
| 3 | **Market rent benchmarking**: Real Estate Manager benchmarks current lease terms against market: (a) comparable retail space rental rates in the same area (from broker data, property listings, industry reports), (b) commercial real estate market trends in the region (rising/falling/stable), (c) assess if current rent is: (i) below market (landlord likely to push for increase), (ii) at market (fair renewal), (iii) above market (BuildRight should negotiate reduction), (d) calculate rent impact on store P&L under different scenarios (market rate, 10% increase, 10% decrease) | Real Estate Manager / External Broker | COO | 1 week per batch |
| 4 | **Renewal negotiation**: For stores where renewal is recommended: (a) Real Estate Manager initiates negotiation with landlord: (i) present market rent data supporting BuildRight's position, (ii) negotiate: base rent, escalation formula (fixed % vs. CPI-linked), CAM caps, tenant improvement allowance, exclusivity renewal, lease term (longer term in exchange for lower rent), (b) Legal Counsel reviews lease renewal terms: (i) confirm renewal option language matches original lease, (ii) identify any unfavorable terms to renegotiate, (iii) ensure assignment/sublease rights are preserved, (c) target outcomes: (i) rent at or below market, (ii) annual escalation capped at 3–5%, (iii) 5–10 year renewal term, (iv) tenant improvement allowance for store refresh | Real Estate Manager / Legal Counsel | COO | 2–4 weeks |
| 5 | **Relocation evaluation** (for underperforming stores): If store performance assessment (Step 2) indicates the location is the primary cause of underperformance: (a) Real Estate Manager identifies 2–3 alternative sites within the same market area, (b) compare: (i) new site lease cost vs. current site (including estimated moving cost and downtime), (ii) new site traffic and accessibility advantages, (iii) financial model: projected performance at new site vs. current site, (c) if relocation is more profitable than renewal: execute new site lease per W1445 and plan store closure per W703 with transition to new location, (d) if renewal is more profitable despite underperformance: negotiate rent reduction as condition for renewal | Real Estate Manager / Finance Controller | COO | 2 weeks |
| 6 | **Decision and execution**: (a) COO presents recommendation to CEO: (i) renew at negotiated terms, (ii) relocate to alternative site, or (iii) exit market, (b) CEO approves; Legal Counsel executes renewal or new lease, (c) if relocating or exiting: VP Store Operations activates W703 (store closure/transition) timeline, (d) update lease tracker and financial projections for the location | COO | CEO | 1 week |

### System Touchpoints
- Lease management system — lease expiration tracker, terms database, renewal status (W1446.1)
- Store financial performance dashboard — sales per sqm, EBITDA margin, rent-to-revenue ratio (W1446.2)
- Commercial real estate benchmarking tool — market rent data by location (W1446.3)
- Integration with W1183 (CAM reconciliation), W702 (new store opening), W703 (store closure), W67 (store performance review)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Landlord negotiation leverage**: if the location is in a high-demand retail area, the landlord may demand above-market rent knowing BuildRight has invested in fit-out and built a customer base; the cost of relocating may be higher than accepting a rent increase
- **Relocation disruption**: moving a store to a new location causes 2–4 months of disruption (closure, moving, reopening, customer re-acquisition); existing customers may defect to competitors during the transition
- **Market timing risk**: commercial real estate market cycles may make renewal unattractive during peak rental periods and favorable during downturns; BuildRight should time negotiations accordingly; mitigated by the COO & Construction review and approval gate

- System auto-create of "create a coverage gap?" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-calculate of "calculate rent impact on store P&L under different scenarios" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls

- Per-batch rent benchmarking: 1 week per 10 stores

- No incremental headcount.
