## W232. Letter of Credit (LC) & Bank Guarantee Lifecycle

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Approved Import PO (W2B) requiring LC; or contract requirement for Bank Guarantee |
| **Frequency** | 40–60 LCs/month (imports, consistent with W1199); 5–10 Bank Guarantees/year |
| **Volume** | High-value import shipments (Machinery, bulk construction materials) |
| **Owner** | Treasury Manager |
| **Participants** | Import Coordinator, Buyer, Bank, Finance |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Application**: Import Coordinator requests LC issuance based on Pro-forma Invoice and PO | Import Coord | Treasury Mgr | 1 day |
| 2 | **Bank Submission**: Treasury submits LC application to partner bank via electronic portal | Treasury Mgr | CFO | 1 day |
| 3 | **LC Issuance**: Bank issues LC; notifies Vendor's bank (Advising Bank) | Bank | — | 2–3 days |
| 4 | **Document Negotiation**: Vendor ships goods; submits shipping documents (Bill of Lading, Invoice, Packing List) to bank | Vendor | — | 1 week |
| 5 | **Discrepancy Check**: Treasury and Bank review documents for discrepancies vs. LC terms | Treasury Mgr | — | 2 days |
| 6 | **Payment/Acceptance**: Treasury authorizes payment or acceptance of draft; Bank releases documents to BuildRight for customs clearance (W233) | Treasury Mgr | CFO | 1 day |
| 7 | **Closure**: LC closed in system once goods received (W3) and payment settled | Treasury Mgr | — | 1 day |

### System Touchpoints

- Treasury Management Module (LC tracking, bank limits, LC aging report)
- Import Management module linked to W2B (Import PO)
- Accounts Payable module for payment settlement linked to W7 (Payments)
- Bank portal integration for electronic LC submission and status tracking

### Pain Points / Risks

- **BSP foreign exchange regulations**: FX purchases for import payments above USD 500,000 require prior BSP approval under the FX Manual; below that, banks process with supporting documents — non-compliance risks penalties and delayed shipments
- **Document discrepancy delays**: 30–40% of first-time LC presentations contain discrepancies, adding 2–5 days per occurrence and demurrage risk at Philippine ports
- **Bank credit line saturation**: High LC volumes during construction season may exhaust available credit lines across the 5 banking relationships
- **Currency exposure**: PHP volatility against USD/CNY can materially increase landed cost between LC issuance and payment settlement
- **Regulatory risk**: BIR requires withholding tax documentation on LC-related import service fees; missed WTD deductions trigger audit flags; mitigated by the Treasury Manager review and approval gate


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-import of "Import Coordinator requests LC issuance based on Pro-forma Invoice and PO" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-submit of "submits LC application to partner bank via electronic portal" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-issue of "issues LC" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-63 (ensure FX exposure reporting accuracy.)
- operational: Treasury Manager review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication

40–60 LCs/month (per the Frequency) plus 5–10 bank guarantees/year. Each LC requires 2–3 days of processing (application, bank liaison, discrepancy review, payment authorization). This workload is absorbed by the Treasury Manager (1 FTE) with support from the Import Coordinator for documentation preparation. Peak periods (major infrastructure project orders) may require temporary overtime approval.

### Time Estimate

**Total**: 2–3 days per LC (end to end, including bank processing time); ~30 minutes per bank guarantee renewal

## W317. Bank Account Lifecycle & Signatory Management

| Field | Detail |

| **Volume** | ~215–230 bank accounts across 5 entities and 4 banks (BDO, BPI, Metrobank, Chinabank): ~200 store deposit accounts, 4 DC operating accounts, 5 entity main operating accounts, 5 USD import accounts, and additional payroll, savings, and investment accounts per entity |
| **Owner** | Treasury Manager |

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |

### System Touchpoints

- Bank account register: centralized register across all entities and banks with account number, type, entity, purpose, signatories, opening/closure dates, and status (W317.4)
- Signatory tracking with specimen signature document storage, last-update date, and staleness alerting at 12 months (W317.5–6)
- HR integration: automated alert to Treasury when signatory employee separates (W43) or transfers; system cross-references active signatories against active employee roster (W317.5)
- Account lifecycle status: Pending → Active → Pending Closure → Closed; linked to store/DC location in system (W317.4, W317.7)
- Bank fee tracking: fee schedule per bank per account type; actual fees charged from bank statement import (W89) compared against schedule; variance dashboard (W317.9)
- Integration with W16 (new store opening — triggers account opening), W30 (daily cash position — all active accounts feed cash position), W43 (employee separation — triggers signatory update), W45 (store closure — triggers account closure), W89 (bank reconciliation — validates account activity)

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Signatory update lag after employee separation**: with ~15–20% annual turnover across 6,911 employees, signatory-authorized staff separate frequently; if HR does not promptly alert Treasury, separated employees may retain system and bank portal access, creating a fraud and unauthorized transaction risk
- **Board Resolution delays blocking account opening**: new store openings (W16) follow a tight timeline; if Legal cannot produce Board Resolutions and Secretary's Certificates within the project schedule, bank account opening is delayed, preventing store deposit functionality for the first days of operations
- **Inconsistent bank documentation requirements**: each of the 4 banks (BDO, BPI, Metrobank, Chinabank) has different documentation requirements for account opening and signatory changes; maintaining a current requirements matrix per bank is manual and error-prone
- **Provincial store banking access challenges**: new stores in remote areas (e.g., Bicol, Mindanao provincial towns) may not have branches of BuildRight's preferred banks nearby; Treasury must identify alternative banks or arrange cash-in-transit (W174) for deposit logistics
- **Dormant account risk**: store closures (W45) that do not complete the account closure process leave dormant accounts exposed to unauthorized access and incurring ongoing maintenance fees; mitigated by CTL-59 (prevent unauthorized bank account changes)

- System auto-document of "Account opening request" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-count of "account opening and designating authorized signatories" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-submit of "submits complete documentation package to bank" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-59 (prevent unauthorized bank account changes.)
- operational: and bank portal access, creating a fraud and unauthorized transaction risk

### Staffing Implication

~10–15 new accounts/year + ~60–90 signatory updates/year (~5–8/month) + ~2–3 closures/year. Each account opening requires 2–3 hours of Treasury Analyst time for documentation preparation and bank liaison; each signatory update requires 1–2 hours. Total: ~100–150 hours/year (~8–12 hours/month), absorbed by the Treasury Analyst within the existing 2–3 analyst team. The Treasury Manager adds ~4–6 hours/year for banking relationship review and quarterly signatory staleness review.

### Time Estimate

**Total**: Account opening — 2–3 hours preparation + 1–2 weeks bank processing per account; Signatory update — 1–2 hours per change; Quarterly signatory staleness review — 2 hours; Annual banking relationship review — 4–6 hours; Monthly bank fee review — 1 hour; Account closure — 1–2 hours per account

## W320. Electronic Banking Security & Payment Control

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Daily payment operations; new user onboarding; periodic access review; or security incident |
| **Frequency** | Daily (payment operations); quarterly (access review); annual (policy review); ad-hoc (incident response) |
| **Volume** | ~210 bank accounts across 4 banks; ~25–35 payment runs/month (W7 twice-weekly + payroll + tax + capex + intercompany); ~30–40 authorized payment users across Finance and Treasury |
| **Owner** | Treasury Manager |
| **Participants** | Treasury Manager, Treasury Analyst, AP Supervisor, CFO, IT Security, Banks |

### Background

Electronic banking security is the control framework that prevents unauthorized, fraudulent, or erroneous payments across BuildRight's banking relationships. With ~210 bank accounts, PHP 62B annual revenue flowing through electronic channels, and 4 banking portals, the risk of payment fraud (external cyber-attack, internal unauthorized payment, or social engineering) is material. This workflow establishes maker-checker controls, user access governance, payment approval workflows, and incident response procedures.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Payment initiation (Maker)**: authorized AP Clerk or Treasury Analyst creates payment batch in ERP — system generates payment file (vendor payments, payroll, tax, intercompany transfers); payment file includes payee name, bank account, amount, payment reference, and cost center | AP Clerk / Treasury Analyst | AP Supervisor / Treasury Mgr | Per W7, W10, W90 |
| 2 | **First-level approval (Checker)**: AP Supervisor or Treasury Manager reviews payment batch — verifies payee details, amount vs. approved invoice/purpose, bank account vs. vendor master; approves batch in ERP; system locks payment details from further modification after first approval | AP Supervisor / Treasury Mgr | — | 15–30 min/batch |
| 3 | **Second-level approval (Authorizer)**: for payments above threshold — (a) ≤ PHP 5M: Treasury Manager or AP Supervisor, (b) PHP 5M–20M: CFO, (c) > PHP 20M: CEO + CFO; authorizer reviews and releases payment batch in bank portal (or ERP-to-bank integration); system records approver ID, timestamp, and authorization level | Treasury Mgr / CFO / CEO | CFO | 10–15 min/batch |
| 4 | **Bank portal access control**: Treasury Manager maintains bank portal user access matrix — (a) user roles: inquiry-only (view balances), payment initiator (create payment), payment approver (release payment), admin (manage users); (b) segregation of duties: no single user may both create and approve payments for the same account; (c) user access reviewed quarterly (step 8) | Treasury Mgr | CFO | Ongoing |
| 5 | **New user onboarding**: when a new Treasury Analyst or AP Clerk joins (W15), Treasury Manager requests bank portal access based on role; user completes bank's identity verification; initial access set to inquiry-only for first 30 days; elevated to payment initiator after training and competency confirmation | Treasury Mgr | CFO | 1–2 hours/user |
| 6 | **User deactivation**: when a payment user separates (W43) or transfers out of Treasury/AP, Treasury Manager immediately (same day) deactivates bank portal access; HR separation notification triggers automated alert to Treasury Manager; system cross-references bank portal user list against active employee roster weekly | Treasury Mgr | CFO | 30 min/user |
| 7 | **Token and credential management**: bank security tokens (hardware or software OTP) are issued per user; tokens are stored securely and never shared; lost or compromised tokens reported to bank immediately and replaced; token inventory tracked per user with issuance and expiry dates | Treasury Analyst | Treasury Mgr | Ongoing |
| 8 | **Quarterly access review**: Treasury Manager reviews all bank portal users — (a) confirms each user's access level is appropriate for current role, (b) identifies any users with access who have changed roles or should no longer have access, (c) reviews payment approval logs for any unusual patterns (payments approved outside business hours, rapid sequential approvals, payments to new vendors without recent PO), (d) documents review results with sign-off | Treasury Mgr | CFO | 2–3 hours/quarter |
| 9 | **Payment anomaly detection**: system flags payment exceptions for Treasury Manager review — (a) payment to vendor bank account changed within last 7 days, (b) payment amount > 200% of average payment to same vendor, (c) payment to vendor not in active vendor master, (d) multiple payments to same vendor on same day exceeding PHP 5M cumulative, (e) payment initiated outside business hours (before 7 AM or after 8 PM) | System / Treasury Mgr | CFO | 30 min/week |
| 10 | **Security incident response**: if suspected unauthorized payment or bank portal breach is detected — (a) Treasury Manager immediately contacts bank to freeze affected account(s), (b) CFO and IT Security notified within 1 hour, (c) bank conducts investigation with BuildRight cooperation, (d) if confirmed unauthorized payment: Legal assesses recovery options and insurance claim (W59 cyber liability), (e) post-incident: Treasury Manager conducts root cause analysis and implements additional controls, (f) incident reported to Internal Audit for control assessment (W120) | Treasury Mgr / CFO / IT Security | CEO | As needed |
| 11 | **Annual policy review**: CFO reviews and updates electronic banking security policy — maker-checker thresholds, approval tiers, user access standards, token management procedures, and incident response protocol; policy changes approved by CEO; updated policy communicated to all payment users; annual training completion required for continued bank portal access | CFO | CEO | 4 hours/year |

### System Touchpoints

- Payment approval workflow: configurable maker-checker-approver tiers per bank account per entity; payment details locked after first approval; full audit trail of initiator, approver, and authorizer per payment batch (W320.1–3)
- Bank portal user access matrix: centralized tracking of all bank portal users with role, access level, accounts accessible, and last review date (W320.4)
- HR integration: automated alerts when payment users separate or transfer; weekly cross-reference of bank portal users vs. active employee roster (W320.6)
- Payment anomaly detection rules: configurable rules engine flagging unusual payment patterns for review (W320.9)
- Token inventory tracking per user with issuance and expiry dates (W320.7)
- Quarterly access review documentation with sign-off (W320.8)
- Security incident log with root cause analysis and control improvement tracking (W320.10)
- Integration with W7 (AP payment runs), W10 (payroll bank files), W30 (treasury transfers), W43 (employee separation — triggers user deactivation), W89 (bank reconciliation — validates authorized payments), W120 (internal audit — annual controls assessment)

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Business email compromise (BEC) targeting vendor bank account changes**: attackers impersonate vendors and request bank account details be updated in BuildRight's vendor master; if the change is processed without verification (callback to confirmed vendor contact number), subsequent payments are diverted to the attacker's account — this is the most common payment fraud vector in Philippine corporate treasury
- **Same-day deactivation enforcement**: with payment users across HQ and DCs, ensuring same-day bank portal deactivation for every separation is operationally challenging; any gap creates a window for unauthorized payments
- **Approval bottleneck for high-value payments**: payments > PHP 20M requiring CEO + CFO dual approval may be delayed if either is unavailable (travel, sick leave, meetings); delay may result in missed vendor payment deadlines and late payment penalties
- **Social engineering of Treasury staff**: attackers may call or message Treasury staff impersonating senior management (CEO, CFO) and request urgent payment transfers; the approval tier structure mitigates this but cannot eliminate it entirely without callback verification procedures
- **Bank portal downtime during critical payment windows**: if a bank portal is unavailable during payroll or tax payment deadlines, Treasury must have contingency procedures (alternative payment method, manual check issuance, or bank branch over-the-counter payment) to avoid regulatory penalties


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-capture of "center" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "reviews payment batch — verifies payee details, amount vs." (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-record of "records approver ID, timestamp, and authorization level" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-59 (prevent unauthorized bank account changes.); CTL-62 (ensure electronic banking security.)
- operational: Treasury Manager review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication

Daily payment operations add ~15–30 min/batch for review and approval (absorbed within W7 and W30 staffing). Quarterly access review adds 2–3 hours/quarter. Annual policy review adds 4 hours/year. New user onboarding adds 1–2 hours/user (~5–10 users/year). Total: ~4–6 hours/month incremental Treasury Manager time, absorbed within existing role.

### Time Estimate

**Total**: Payment review and approval — 15–30 min/batch (~25–35 batches/month); Quarterly access review — 2–3 hours/quarter; New user onboarding — 1–2 hours/user; Annual policy review — 4 hours/year; Payment anomaly review — 30 min/week; Incident response — as needed

## W1342. Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) & High-Value Payment Processing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vendor payment > PHP 5M per W7; payroll disbursement per W10; intercompany fund transfer > PHP 10M; tax payment > PHP 5M; or emergency payment requiring same-day settlement |
| **Frequency** | ~15–25 RTGS transactions per month; ~5–10 high-value intercompany transfers per month |
| **Volume** | ~20–35 RTGS transactions/month totaling ~PHP 800M–1.5B; average transaction PHP 30M–50M |
| **Owner** | Treasury Manager |
| **Participants** | Treasury Analyst, CFO, CEO (for payments > PHP 20M), AP Supervisor, Bank Relationship Manager |

### Background

BuildRight's high-value payments — large vendor settlements (cement, steel, appliance imports), intercompany transfers between 5 entities, payroll disbursements for 6,911 employees, and tax payments — require Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) through PhilPaSS (Philippine Payment and Settlement System) operated by the BSP (Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas). Unlike standard electronic fund transfers that settle in batches, RTGS provides immediate, irrevocable settlement, which is essential for: (a) time-critical payments (import LC settlements per W232 with bank deadlines, BIR tax payments with statutory deadlines), (b) high-value payments where counterparty requires confirmed settlement before releasing goods or services, (c) intercompany transfers requiring same-day settlement for entity-level cash management. RTGS transactions are irreversible once processed — errors cannot be recalled — requiring enhanced controls beyond standard payment processing per W320.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Payment initiation**: AP Supervisor or Treasury Analyst initiates high-value payment in ERP: (a) select payment type: vendor payment, intercompany transfer, payroll, tax payment, (b) enter payment details: beneficiary name, bank account number, bank name (BSP-member bank), amount, payment reference, purpose, (c) attach supporting documentation: approved invoice (vendor), Board Resolution (intercompany), BIR tax return (tax), payroll register (payroll), (d) system validates: vendor master match, bank account format (Philippine bank account validation), payment purpose per entity policy, (e) system flags payment as "RTGS required" if amount > PHP 5M or payment type requires same-day settlement | AP Supervisor / Treasury Analyst | Treasury Manager | 15–30 min |
| 2 | **First-level verification (Maker)**: Treasury Manager reviews payment in ERP: (a) verify beneficiary details match supporting documentation, (b) verify amount matches approved invoice/transfer request, (c) verify bank account matches vendor master per W320 payment anomaly detection, (d) confirm RTGS is appropriate (vs. standard PESONet batch transfer for non-urgent payments), (e) approve payment — system locks details from further modification | Treasury Manager | — | 10–15 min |
| 3 | **Second-level authorization (Authorizer)**: CFO authorizes high-value payment: (a) for payments PHP 5M–20M: CFO reviews and authorizes via bank portal or ERP-to-bank integration, (b) for payments > PHP 20M: CEO and CFO dual authorization per W320.3 — both must authorize independently in bank portal, (c) authorizer reviews: payment summary, beneficiary, amount, supporting documentation reference, (d) system records authorizer ID, timestamp, and authorization level for audit trail | CFO / CEO | — | 10–20 min |
| 4 | **RTGS submission**: Treasury Analyst submits payment to bank via electronic banking portal: (a) log into bank portal (dual-login for security per W320), (b) enter or confirm payment details in bank RTGS module, (c) bank validates: beneficiary bank is BSP-member, sufficient account balance, cut-off time compliance (BSP PhilPaSS cut-off: 3:30 PM for same-day settlement), (d) bank submits to PhilPaSS for real-time settlement, (e) bank confirms settlement with PhilPaSS reference number | Treasury Analyst | Treasury Manager | 15–20 min |
| 5 | **Settlement confirmation & reconciliation**: (a) Treasury Analyst obtains settlement confirmation from bank portal: PhilPaSS reference number, settlement timestamp, amount, beneficiary, (b) record settlement in treasury management system: PhilPaSS reference, payment reference, settlement status = "Settled", (c) update cash position per W30 — high-value payment immediately reflected in daily cash position, (d) for vendor payments: update AP invoice status to "Paid" per W7, (e) for intercompany transfers: update intercompany account per W17, (f) file settlement confirmation for audit trail per W120 | Treasury Analyst | Treasury Manager | 10 min |
| 6 | **Failed/rejected payment handling**: If RTGS payment is rejected: (a) bank returns payment with rejection reason: insufficient funds, invalid beneficiary account, bank system error, past cut-off time, (b) Treasury Analyst investigates and resolves: (i) insufficient funds: arrange intraday liquidity via credit facility or fund transfer from another account, (ii) invalid account: contact beneficiary to verify bank details, (iii) past cut-off: resubmit next business day or arrange PESONet batch as alternative, (c) re-initiate payment after resolution, (d) if payment deadline is at risk (e.g., BIR tax deadline): escalate to CFO for emergency resolution | Treasury Analyst | Treasury Manager | 30–60 min per rejection |

### System Touchpoints

- ERP payment module with RTGS payment type and enhanced validation rules
- Bank portal integration for RTGS submission and confirmation retrieval
- Treasury management system for settlement recording and cash position update
- Dual authorization workflow per W320 with amount-based tiering
- Integration with W7 (AP payment), W10 (payroll), W17 (intercompany), W30 (cash position), W320 (electronic banking security), W232 (LC settlement)

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Irreversibility risk**: RTGS payments are final and irrevocable — incorrect beneficiary or amount cannot be recalled; mitigated by dual authorization, vendor master validation, and pre-submission verification checklist
- **Cut-off time pressure**: BSP PhilPaSS cut-off at 3:30 PM requires Treasury to process high-value payments early in the day; late-afternoon payment requests may miss same-day settlement
- **Liquidity management**: high-value payments can deplete account balances unexpectedly if not coordinated with cash positioning per W30; mitigated by morning cash position review and pre-funding arrangement
- **Bank portal downtime**: bank portal maintenance or outages during critical payment windows (payroll date, tax deadline); mitigated by early processing and maintaining alternative banking relationships for contingency


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-capture of "enter payment details" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-document of "documentation" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-record of "records authorizer ID, timestamp, and authorization level for audit trail" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-294 (ensure controlled execution — Banking & Payments (PA-18.2).)
- operational: dual authorization, vendor master validation, and pre-submission verification checklist; operational: morning cash position review and pre-funding arrangement; operational: early processing and maintaining alternative banking relationships for contingency

### Staffing Implication

- **Treasury Manager**: ~2–3 hours/week on RTGS review and authorization. Absorbed.
- **Treasury Analyst**: ~3–4 hours/week on RTGS processing and confirmation. Absorbed.
- **CFO**: ~30–60 min/week on high-value authorization. Absorbed.
- **No incremental headcount.**

### Time Estimate

- Per RTGS payment: 30–60 min (initiation + verification + authorization + submission + confirmation)
- ~30 RTGS payments/month × 45 min average = ~22 hours/month
- **Annual estimate: ~270 hours (Treasury team)**

## W1343. Petty Cash Fund Lifecycle, Custodian Accountability & Periodic Replenishment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Small, urgent business expense requiring cash payment; or petty cash fund replenishment request (fund depleted to ~20% of authorized level); or annual petty cash policy review |
| **Frequency** | ~50–80 petty cash disbursements per store per month; replenishment every 2–4 weeks per store; annual policy review |
| **Volume** | 200 stores × the authorized petty cash fund of PHP 20,000 per store = ~PHP 4.2M total petty cash float (incl. DC floats, per profile §10.4); ~10,000–16,000 disbursements/month chain-wide |
| **Owner** | Store Manager (custodian) |
| **Participants** | Cash Office Clerk, Store Manager, Regional Manager (approval > PHP 5,000), AP Clerk (replenishment processing), Finance Controller |

### Background

Despite the prevalence of electronic payments, BuildRight's 200 stores require petty cash funds for small, urgent expenses that cannot wait for standard procurement or electronic payment processing: (a) emergency store maintenance supplies (light bulbs, cleaning materials, small repair parts not in inventory), (b) customer refund for small amounts where electronic refund is impractical, (c) employee transportation for urgent store errands, (d) small community engagement expenses (barangay event sponsorship, emergency relief contribution), (e) cash advance for store events or workshops per W53. Each store maintains an authorized petty cash fund (imprest system) managed by the Store Manager as custodian. The imprest system keeps the fund at a fixed authorized level; replenishment restores the fund to the authorized amount after expenses are documented and approved. This workflow manages the petty cash lifecycle from authorization through disbursement, documentation, replenishment, and audit.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Petty cash fund establishment**: For new stores per W16 or when existing fund amount is adjusted: (a) Store Manager requests petty cash fund authorization from Regional Manager: proposed amount, justification, (b) standard authorized levels: small stores (PHP 20,000), medium stores (PHP 30,000), large/flagship stores (PHP 50,000), (c) Finance Controller approves fund authorization and sets up petty cash GL account per store, (d) initial funding: Treasury transfers authorized amount to store bank account; Store Manager withdraws cash and secures in store safe per W541, (e) system records petty cash fund: store, authorized amount, custodian (Store Manager), establishment date | Store Manager / Finance Controller | Regional Manager | 1–2 days |
| 2 | **Petty cash disbursement**: For each expense requiring petty cash: (a) requester (Department Supervisor, stock associate) submits Petty Cash Voucher: date, amount, purpose, payee, supporting documentation (receipt, quotation), (b) Store Manager reviews and approves: (i) is the expense legitimate business purpose?, (ii) is petty cash the appropriate payment method (vs. procurement card or standard PO)?, (iii) is the amount within petty cash policy limit (max PHP 5,000 per transaction; > PHP 5,000 requires Regional Manager approval), (iv) is supporting documentation adequate (official receipt from BIR-registered supplier preferred)?, (c) Cash Office Clerk disburses cash from petty cash fund, (d) requester signs Petty Cash Voucher acknowledging receipt, (e) voucher filed in petty cash binder with supporting documentation, (f) system logs disbursement: date, amount, purpose, payee, voucher number, custodian approval | Requester / Cash Office Clerk / Store Manager | Store Manager | 5–10 min per disbursement |

## W1361. Multi-Bank Cash Position Daily Aggregation & Automated Zero-Balance Sweep

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Daily treasury operations cycle (every banking day morning) |
| **Frequency** | Daily (260 banking days/year) |
| **Volume** | 4 banking relationships (BDO, BPI, Metrobank, Chinabank) × multiple accounts per entity (5 entities) = ~20–30 bank and e-wallet settlement accounts aggregated daily (15–20 entity treasury accounts per W309 plus settlement accounts); total daily cash position ~PHP 1–3B across all accounts |
| **Owner** | Treasury Analyst |

### Background

BuildRight operates 5 legal entities across 4 banking relationships, maintaining ~215–230 bank accounts (of which ~20–30 entity treasury/settlement accounts are aggregated daily) for operating collections, disbursements, payroll, import LCs, and petty cash. Without daily cash position aggregation, treasury has fragmented visibility: each bank's balance is visible only through that bank's portal, and intra-day fund transfers between accounts require manual initiation. | 5 | Treasury Analyst reviews daily sweep confirmation: verifies sweep amounts match expected balances; investigates any partial or failed sweeps | Treasury Analyst | Treasury Manager | 15 min/day |

### System Touchpoints

- Bank API connectivity failures: if one bank's statement import fails, the consolidated cash position is incomplete for that day, potentially causing overdraft decisions on accounts whose actual balance was not visible

### Pain Points / Risks
- Bank file format changes: Philippine banks periodically update PESONet/InstaPay file specifications; if BuildRight's payment file format is not updated in time, the bank rejects the entire payment file, delaying all vendor payments in that batch
- Duplicate payment risk: if an invoice appears in both the merchandise AP queue and the non-PO AP queue (e.g., a vendor providing both goods and services), the payment proposal may select it twice; duplicate detection rules must cross-reference across AP sub-ledgers
- Check payment logistics: checks require physical printing, signing (dual signatories per bank mandate), and mailing/delivery; for provincial vendors without bank accounts, check processing adds 3–5 days to the payment cycle vs. electronic transfer


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "reviews aged payables" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-count of "discounts where eligible" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "checks for duplicate invoices or unusual amounts" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-294 (ensure controlled execution — Banking & Payments (PA-18.2).)
- operational: Treasury Analyst review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
- AP payment proposal: 1–2 hours/run × 8 runs/month = 8–16 hours/month (AP Manager)
- Treasury review and file generation: 45–75 min/run × 8 runs/month = 6–10 hours/month (Treasury Analyst)
- Authorization: 15–30 min/run × 8 runs/month = 2–4 hours/month (Treasury Manager + CFO for large payments)
- Bank upload: 15–20 min/run × 8 runs/month = 2–3 hours/month (Treasury Analyst)
- Reconciliation: 15–30 min/run × 8 runs/month = 2–4 hours/month (Treasury Analyst)
- Total: ~20–37 hours/month across all roles

### Staffing Implication
- Treasury Analyst: ~10–17 hours/month on payment run execution; absorbed within existing treasury analyst duties
- AP Manager: 8–16 hours/month on payment proposal; absorbed within AP management duties
- Treasury Manager: 2–4 hours/month on authorization; absorbed within treasury management
- No incremental headcount required

## W5559. Vendor Payment-Diversion & Business Email Compromise (BEC) Fraud Event Response & Recovery Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Discovery that a completed BuildRight disbursement was diverted to a fraudster-controlled account — the vendor reports payments unpaid after a bank-detail change, a bank flags a beneficiary anomaly, or review finds a vendor-master bank change immediately followed by payment runs — or the near-miss variant: an executive-impersonation urgent-transfer request intercepted before execution |
| **Frequency** | Low-single-digit attempted events per year — BEC is named in this PA's own risk register as the most common payment-fraud vector in Philippine corporate treasury; successful diversion is rarer wherever CTL-59's callback discipline held |
| **Volume** | 1 event × the diverted tranche (typically 1–6 payments inside one W1362 run; a PHP 0.3M–8M exposure band) × the recovery surface (bank recall window, beneficiary bank, police case, crime-policy claim) |
| **Owner** | Treasury Manager |
| **Participants** | CFO, AP Manager (VS-15.1 invoice-match chain W556/W770), Vendor Master Data steward (VS-29 change audit trail), Legal Counsel (VS-100), Fraud Management Lead (VS-125), IT Security (W383; mailbox audit per W5518), Insurance Coordinator (VS-26 crime/fidelity policy), Internal Audit (VS-21), the affected vendor's pre-change verified contacts, BuildRight's bank fraud desk and the beneficiary bank, PNP Anti-Cybercrime Group |

### Background

BEC appears in the corpus only as pain points — this PA's own risk register carries "attackers impersonate vendors and request bank account details be updated … subsequent payments are diverted to the attacker's account" (mitigated by callback verification) and "attackers may call Treasury staff impersonating senior management" — while VS-125.2's monitoring step watches only the customer-side (trade-account receivables) diversion class. 'payment diversion'/'wire fraud'/'account change fraud' appear in zero dedicated headers: nobody owns the event after prevention fails — the same-day recall race, the honest-ledger treatment (a diverted payment does not discharge the payable; the vendor remains owed), the master-data forensics that separate insider collusion from mailbox compromise, the police and crime-policy files, or the verification retrofit that turns one incident into a standing cooling-off control.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Verify & freeze**: confirm the fraud with the vendor through the pre-change verified contact held in the W317 account records — never any contact detail supplied on the change request or in the fraudulent correspondence; freeze all pending payments to the vendor; suspend the vendor-master record from further runs | Treasury Manager / AP Manager | CFO | Same day |
| 2 | **Bank recall & trace race**: fraud-desk engagement on both sides (remitting bank + beneficiary bank) within hours — PESONet/InstaPay recall odds collapse after the first day; document the recall trail; request a beneficiary-bank freeze while funds remain | Treasury Manager / Bank Fraud Desk | CFO | First 24–72 hours |
| 3 | **Classify & forensically contain**: internal (collusion/insider — the VS-29 vendor-master change audit trail shows who changed what, when, from where) vs external (mailbox compromise — export mail rules and M365 audit logs per W5518; W383 handles the host/incident core); executive-impersonation variant → preserve the correspondence and call logs | IT Security / Internal Audit | CFO | 2–5 days |
| 4 | **Police & regulatory referral**: sworn affidavit and evidence pack (change requests, before/after bank-detail snapshots, payment confirmations, recall trail) to PNP ACG under RA 10175; AML officer consulted where a covered-entity channel (VS-86 estate) touched the flow | Legal / Treasury | VP Legal | 1–2 weeks |
| 5 | **Honest-ledger treatment**: reverse the misdirected application — the diverted payment does not discharge the payable; keep the vendor's aging truthful; park the loss in a fraud-recovery suspense account; written notice to the vendor and re-payment of the still-owed invoices over verified rails | Treasury / AP / Corporate Accounting | CFO | 1–2 weeks |
| 6 | **Recovery & insurance**: fidelity/crime-policy notice filed within the policy window (VS-26); restitution pursued through the criminal case; no double-claim against the bank absent its own control failure | Insurance Coordinator / Legal | CFO | 1–4 months |
| 7 | **Verification retrofit & sweep**: dual-channel callback audit of every vendor bank-detail change in the trailing 90 days; cooling-off window before the first payment to any changed account (the CTL-59 extension); callback evidence attached to the master-data change record; red-card briefing to Treasury and AP on the urgent-transfer impersonation script | Treasury / Vendor Master Data / Internal Audit | CFO | 2–3 weeks |
| 8 | **Case closure & intelligence**: full case file to the VS-21/VS-125 fraud-intelligence registers; loss and recovery reconciled; the control-failure note documented for the CTL-59 control owner | Internal Audit / Fraud Management | CFO | 2–4 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- Vendor-master change audit trail (VS-29) with before/after bank-detail snapshots and requester identity
- Payment-run objects (W1362/W556) with recall-status flags and payment-hold markers
- Bank-portal recall/freeze correspondence records tied to the payment references
- Fraud case management (VS-125) and internal-audit case files (VS-21)
- Crime-policy claim records (VS-26); fraud-recovery suspense account in the GL
- M365 audit-log export and mail-rule history (links to W5518)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Verification-capture risk**: calling back the number printed on the fraudster's change request "verifies" the fraud; mitigated by Step 1's pre-change verified contacts held since onboarding, never numbers supplied on change documents
- **Recall-window loss**: waiting for statement reconciliation to discover the diversion forfeits the recovery odds; mitigated by Step 2's same-day fraud-desk race and the aging-alert pattern (bank change → immediate payment)
- **Double-victimization risk**: dunning a still-owed vendor for invoices whose payment was diverted destroys the relationship and invites disputes; mitigated by Step 5's honest-ledger treatment with written re-payment
- **Control-atrophy risk**: fixing one vendor's record and leaving the class unchanged; mitigated by Step 7's 90-day sweep and standing cooling-off window
- **Insider blind spot**: treating a master-data insider change as purely external mailbox compromise; mitigated by Step 3's internal/external classification over the change audit trail

### Automation Opportunity
- Bank-detail change → automatic dual-channel callback task plus payment-hold flag until verified (moves CTL-59 from discipline to system enforcement)
- Anomaly alert on the change-then-pay pattern (bank-detail change followed within N days by a first payment above threshold)
- Recall-status tracker on the payment objects with bank-correspondence attachments
- Auto-assembled affidavit evidence pack (change request, before/after details, payment confirmations) — replaces manual Step 4 assembly

### Controls
- CTL-59 (prevent unauthorized bank account changes)
- CTL-62 (ensure electronic banking security)
- operational: dual-channel callback on every bank-detail change before the next payment run; diverted funds never netted against the vendor payable; bank recall initiated within 24 hours of discovery

### Time Estimate
First 72 hours ≈ 12–20 staff-hours (Treasury, AP, bank coordination); case and recovery lifecycle ≈ 20–40 further staff-hours over 1–4 months; at the low-single-digit cadence ≈ 100–300 staff-hours/year worst case, absorbed within the Treasury desk's capacity.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed by the Treasury desk with AP, Legal, Fraud Management, and Internal Audit support; no incremental headcount.
