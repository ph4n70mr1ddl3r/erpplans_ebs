## W2993. Product Safety Issue Intake, Triage & Hazard Classification

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Safety complaint, regulator alert, vendor notice, or field incident |
| **Frequency** | Event-driven (~30–60 candidate issues/year) |
| **Volume** | ~30–60 issues triaged; ~3–8 escalate to formal recall |
| **Owner** | Product Safety & Compliance Manager |
| **Participants** | Product Safety Mgr, Quality, Customer Service, Vendor Mgmt, Legal, Store Ops |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customer Service / Store Ops / Quality logs safety issue in recall case system (source, SKU, lot, incident description, photos, injury/property damage flag) | Customer Service / Store Ops / Quality | Product Safety & Compliance Manager | Per issue (<24 hr) |
| 2 | Product Safety Mgr triages and classifies hazard (electrical/fire, chemical/toxic, mechanical/laceration, structural/failure, labeling/allergen) and assigns preliminary severity (Class I–III per Consumer Act / DTI-BPS) | Product Safety & Compliance Manager | Quality Manager | Per issue |
| 3 | Quality/Vendor Mgmt confirms affected SKU, lot, vendor, and whether the issue is isolated or systemic; duplicates against existing open recalls | Quality / Vendor Mgmt | Product Safety & Compliance Manager | Per issue |

### System Touchpoints
- Recall case management; quality non-conformance module; item/lot/serial master; vendor master linkage

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Detection risk**: Safety issues lost in generic complaint queues; mitigated by mandatory hazard-classification field and dedicated triage queue


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "logs safety issue in recall case system" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-assign of "assigns preliminary severity" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-issue of "issue is isolated or systemic" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-496 (ensure controlled execution — Recall Initiation, Risk Assessment & Regulatory Notification (PA-89.1).)
- operational: mandatory hazard-classification field and dedicated triage queue

### Time Estimate
- 2–4 hours per issue

## W2994. Recall Risk Assessment & Corrective Action Decision

## W5556. Product-Tampering Threat, Extortion Demand & Merchandise-Integrity Sweep Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | A threat, extortion demand, or malicious claim that BuildRight merchandise on shelves has been or will be contaminated, tampered with, or booby-trapped — by call, letter, email, social post, or in-person note — with or without a money demand |
| **Frequency** | The rarest integrity-event class in the product-safety estate: years can pass chain-wide without a credible episode; the sweep-decision discipline is exercised through the W627 mock-recall rotation |
| **Volume** | 1 event × the threat's claimed scope (a single SKU, a category, one store, or the whole chain) × the sweep surface (targeted SKU pulls at ~200 stores, hours to a full-day campaign if ordered) × the notification set (PNP assessment, DTI-BPS/FDA per W2995 where consumers are at risk, the W2997 committee) |
| **Owner** | Product Safety & Compliance Manager |
| **Participants** | Recall Committee chair (W2997), VP Legal (extortion/no-ransom posture), Store Operations leadership (sweep task campaigns), Loss Prevention (evidence and W1337 law-enforcement coordination), Corporate Communications (W3001-class customer-communication discipline), HSE Manager (VS-24), Category Manager (affected SKUs), PNP anti-cybercrime / economic-crime units |

### Background

VS-89 owns product-safety intake (W2993), the recall decision (W2994), regulatory notification (W2995), committee command (W2997), and stop-sale execution (W3000) — but every one of those triggers on a defect found or a vendor/regulator notice. 'Tampering threat'/'contamination threat'/'extortion' appear in zero dedicated headers anywhere: nobody owns the *threat* — the caller who claims needles in the cement bags unless paid, the letter claiming bleach was injected into a paint line — where the distinctive decisions are the never-pay rule, the specificity-gated sweep-or-monitor decision (the W5538 bomb-threat decision law transposed to merchandise: sweeping the whole chain on every vague threat trains extortionists and burns a full trading day across ~200 stores, while dismissing a specific one is the consumer-harm catastrophe), and the W3000 stop-sale canon executing only on the credible path. This workflow is that event owner; if altered product is actually found, W2994's recall machinery takes over and this protocol becomes its evidence and communications spine.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Threat capture & preservation**: verbatim record of every contact attempt; caller-line capture, envelope, email headers, and screenshots preserved in the case file; if the caller is live, keep them talking without negotiating | Store Manager / Contact-center Agent / LP Duty | Product Safety & Compliance Manager | Immediate |
| 2 | **Posture decision — never pay, never improvise public statements**: VP Legal with Product Safety confirm the no-payment rule and route all public communication through the W3001 discipline (no confirmation, no denial, no speculation outside scripted channels) | VP Legal / Product Safety & Compliance Manager | VP Legal & Compliance | Same day |
| 3 | **Credibility assessment with law enforcement**: specificity of SKU/store/timing claimed, the claimant's knowledge of real product detail, prior-contact history, and platform metadata assessed jointly with PNP; the assessment feeds and is filed with the sweep decision | Product Safety & Compliance Manager / LP Investigator | Product Safety & Compliance Manager | 4–24 hours |
| 4 | **Sweep-or-monitor decision (documented either way)**: specific and credible → targeted sweep/quarantine of the named SKUs via W3000 stop-sale and quarantine at affected sites; vague or not credible → heightened monitoring and sample pulls only — the decision and its basis recorded on the case before execution | Product Safety & Compliance Manager / Recall Committee chair | COO (chain-wide sweep only) | 4–24 hours |
| 5 | **Physical verification & chain of custody**: sample units pulled and inspected for seal/pack integrity; laboratory testing where contamination is chemically plausible; any altered unit found is preserved as evidence, never re-shelved or destroyed | Store Operations / Quality / LP | Product Safety & Compliance Manager | 1–3 days |
| 6 | **Regulatory posture**: W2995 DTI-BPS/FDA notification where a credible contamination risk to consumers exists; if altered product is verified, the W2994 recall decision runs on this evidence | Product Safety & Compliance Manager | VP Legal & Compliance | Per W2995 |
| 7 | **Customer & store communication**: scripted, no-speculation customer guidance per W3001 if a sweep is public-visible; store briefing pack so counter staff never improvise answers | Corporate Communications / Store Operations | Corporate Communications Director | Per event |
| 8 | **Case file, hardening & recurrence watch**: packaging/sealing review on tamper-prone SKUs, camera coverage review on affected aisles, threat intelligence filed to W1337, and the episode folded into the W627 mock rotation | Product Safety & Compliance Manager / LP | Product Safety & Compliance Manager | 2–6 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- Threat/case log with evidence attachments and restricted visibility
- Stop-sale, quarantine, and POS block objects on the targeted-SKU path (links to W3000)
- Sample-pull task campaigns with chain-of-custody records (links to W63-class store task tooling)
- Regulatory notification objects (links to W2995) and recall-decision records (links to W2994)
- Customer-notice channels and script version control (links to W3001)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Panic-sweep risk**: a chain-wide stop-sale ordered on every vague threat trains extortionists and costs a full trading day across ~200 stores; mitigated by Step 4's specificity-gated decision documented before execution
- **Under-reaction risk**: dismissing a specific threat that proves real is the consumer-harm and RA 7394/DTI-enforcement catastrophe; mitigated by Step 3's mandatory law-enforcement assessment on every case
- **Evidence-destruction risk**: the letter discarded, the email deleted, or the inspected unit re-shelved destroys both the criminal case and the recall basis; mitigated by Step 1/5 custody rules
- **Admission-in-communication risk**: improvised statements implying prior knowledge of contamination; mitigated by Step 2/7 scripted-channel discipline
- **Copycat risk**: paying once, or visibly over-reacting once, invites the next demand; mitigated by the never-pay rule with VP Legal sign-off recorded on every case

### Automation Opportunity
- Threat-case template with a built-in sweep-decision gate (assessment fields, decision, basis, approver) that must be completed before any W3000 stop-sale object can be raised from the case
- Auto-generated targeted store sweep task campaign on the credible path, with sample-pull and chain-of-custody forms attached (replaces manual store-by-store calls)

### Controls
- CTL-496 (ensure controlled execution — Recall Initiation, Risk Assessment & Regulatory Notification (PA-89.1).)
- operational: never-pay rule with VP Legal sign-off recorded on the case; sweep/stop-sale decision and its documented basis precede execution; altered units preserved under chain of custody, never re-shelved

### Time Estimate
Per event: ≈ 40–80 staff-hours over the first week (committee, legal, LP, store task campaigns) plus store sweep labor scaled to the targeted-SKU surface. Readiness rides the W627 mock rotation — no incremental headcount.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed by Product Safety & Compliance, the W2997 committee bench, and store task-campaign capacity; no incremental headcount.
