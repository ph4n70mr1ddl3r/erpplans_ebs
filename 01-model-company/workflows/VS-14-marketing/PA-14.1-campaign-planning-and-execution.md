## W83. Marketing Campaign Planning, Execution & Performance Measurement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Campaign calendar milestone; or ad-hoc campaign request from C-suite / Category Manager |
| **Frequency** | 6 major bi-monthly campaigns/year + 12 monthly hot-deal cycles + ad-hoc campaigns (store openings, seasonal pushes, competitive response) |
| **Volume** | ~18–24 planned campaigns/year + ~5–10 ad-hoc campaigns; each campaign involves 2–8 channels and 200–500+ SKUs |
| **Owner** | Marketing Campaign Manager |
| **Participants** | Campaign Manager, Content Manager, CMO, VP Merchandising, Category Managers, Pricing Analysts, VP Store Operations, Ecom Team, external creative / media agencies |

### Background

BuildRight's ~25-person Marketing team (profile §3.3) operates across brand, promotions, loyalty, ecommerce, digital, content, and customer service. While W13 covers promotional pricing execution and W50 covers product content management, there is no end-to-end operational workflow for marketing campaign management — from strategic brief through creative production, channel execution, budget tracking, and post-campaign ROI measurement. This workflow bridges the gap between merchandising strategy (W1, W13, W32) and customer-facing execution across all channels.

### Campaign Types

| Campaign Type | Frequency | Channels | Typical Budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Major bi-monthly sale event** | 6/year | In-store signage, POS materials, email, SMS, social media, website banners, digital ads | PHP 2–5M/event |
| **Monthly hot deals** | 12/year | Email, SMS, website, in-store shelf talkers | PHP 200–500K/cycle |
| **New store opening** | ~10–15/year | Local radio, flyers, social media geo-targeted ads, SMS blast to nearby loyalty members, PR | PHP 500K–1M/opening |
| **Seasonal push** | 4/year (aligned with W32) | Full multi-channel: TV, digital, print, in-store events, email, SMS, social | PHP 3–8M/season |
| **Loyalty member engagement** | Monthly | Email, app push notification, in-store POS prompts | PHP 100–300K/month |
| **Competitive response** | Ad-hoc | Social media, email, SMS, local radio | PHP 200–500K |
| **Community / CSR event** | 2–4/year | PR, social media, in-store | PHP 100–300K |

### Phase 1: Campaign Planning & Brief (T-8 to T-6 weeks before campaign launch)

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Category Manager or VP Merchandising submits campaign brief request to Campaign Manager: campaign objective (traffic, revenue, margin, clearance, brand awareness), target customer segment, proposed SKU list, desired timing, and rough budget expectation | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1 hour/brief |
| 2 | Campaign Manager reviews brief with CMO; assesses feasibility against campaign calendar (avoid overlapping campaigns), budget availability, and resource capacity (creative team workload, external agency availability) | Campaign Manager | CMO | 30 min/brief |
| 3 | Campaign Manager develops campaign brief document: (a) campaign name and code (for system tracking and cost allocation), (b) objectives with measurable KPIs (target revenue lift, foot traffic increase, ecommerce conversion rate, new loyalty sign-ups), (c) target audience (all customers, loyalty tiers, trade accounts, geographic segments), (d) channel plan — which channels (in-store, email, SMS, social media, digital ads, print, radio, PR), (e) promotional mechanics (percentage off, bundle deals, gift-with-purchase, loyalty points multiplier, clearance pricing), (f) SKU list and pricing per W13, (g) timeline — key dates for creative review, material production, distribution, and go-live, (h) budget allocation by channel with estimated cost per channel, (i) success metrics and measurement plan | Campaign Manager | CMO | 4–8 hours/campaign |
| 4 | CMO reviews and approves campaign brief; VP Merchandising confirms SKU list and pricing strategy; CFO approves budget for campaigns exceeding PHP 1M | CMO / VP Merchandising / CFO | CEO (if budget > PHP 5M) | 1 hour/campaign |
| 5 | Campaign Manager creates campaign record in system: campaign code, objectives, KPIs, budget, timeline, assigned team members, linked promo pricing records (W13), and linked SKU list; system generates campaign cost center for expense tracking | Campaign Manager | CMO | 30 min/campaign |

| 8 | Campaign Manager reviews all creative assets against brand guidelines: consistent visual identity, correct pricing (match W13/W40 system prices), correct SKU descriptions, legal disclaimers (BIR-compliant pricing disclosures, promo terms and conditions), and inclusive language | Campaign Manager | CMO | 2–4 hours/campaign |

- Creative asset storage linked to campaign record with version control (W83.9)
- Campaign performance dashboard: real-time sell-through, ecommerce metrics, digital engagement, loyalty activity (W83.16)
- Channel execution tracking: material distribution status to stores, digital asset publication status, email/SMS delivery status (W83.10–15)
- Post-campaign analytics: revenue attribution, ROI per channel, customer acquisition cost, store-level performance (W83.19–21)
- Campaign archive with full documentation for future reference (W83.22)
- Integration with W13 (promotional pricing), W17 (loyalty mechanics), W35 (management reporting), W40 (regular pricing), W50 (product content), W58 (corporate account campaigns), W62 (agency contracts), W65 (CSAT measurement)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Pricing discrepancy across channels**: If promotional pricing in POS (W13) does not exactly match ecommerce, social media, email, and in-store signage, customer complaints and DTI pricing compliance issues follow; coordinating pricing updates across 6+ channels for 200–500 SKUs per campaign is error-prone
- **Creative production bottleneck**: The Content Manager and 2–3 Content Specialists must produce assets for ~18–24 campaigns/year plus ad-hoc requests; during peak seasons (Q4 holiday, back-to-school), the creative queue overflows, leading to rushed work or delayed campaign launches
- **ROI attribution across channels**: Accurately attributing revenue to specific campaign channels (e.g., did the customer buy because of the email, the social media post, or the in-store signage?) is methodologically challenging; without reliable attribution, CMO cannot optimize channel spend with confidence
- **Store-level execution variability**: With 200 stores, in-store POS material display compliance varies widely; some stores display materials late, incorrectly, or not at all, undermining campaign consistency and making store-level performance comparisons unreliable

## W104. Loyalty Program Financial Governance & Periodic Review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly loyalty program review calendar; or ad-hoc triggered by significant points liability change, competitive loyalty program launch, or program design change request |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly financial review; annual program design review |
| **Volume** | 600,000 members; ~PHP 150–600M cumulative points liability canon (W1316; W570's PHP 150–250M is the per-cycle expiring subset); ~29M points earned/month (~343M/year per the earnings-base canon) |
| **Owner** | Loyalty Manager (operations); Cost Accountant (financial); CMO (strategic) |
| **Participants** | Loyalty Manager, Cost Accountant, Controller, CMO, CFO, Marketing Analytics |

### Background

W17 (Customer Loyalty Program Operations) covers day-to-day loyalty operations — enrollment, earning, redemption, tier management, and fraud detection. However, there is no periodic governance workflow reviewing the financial health of the loyalty program. With a cumulative points liability of ~PHP 300–600M on the balance sheet (deferred revenue, per the Volume), 600,000 members, and a program cost of ~1% of revenue, the loyalty program is a material financial obligation requiring regular oversight. This workflow covers financial reporting, liability management, breakage analysis, tier economics, program ROI, and strategic design decisions.

### Steps

### Quarterly Financial Review

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Liability reporting**: Cost Accountant prepares quarterly loyalty liability report: (a) total outstanding points balance and estimated liability (outstanding points × PHP 1.00 redemption value × expected redemption rate ~75–85%), (b) quarterly points earned vs. redeemed vs. expired (waterfall), (c) deferred revenue balance vs. prior quarter — trend analysis, (d) liability as % of trailing 12-month revenue (benchmark: should remain stable, not growing as % of revenue), (e) breakage rate (points expired ÷ points earned) — compare to PFRS 15 assumption used in W17.4 allocation, (f) deferred revenue adequacy — is the recorded deferred revenue sufficient to cover expected redemptions? | Cost Accountant | Controller | 2 hours/quarter |
| 2 | **Program engagement metrics**: Loyalty Manager prepares quarterly engagement report: (a) active member rate (members with ≥ 1 transaction in trailing 90 days), (b) enrollment trend (new sign-ups per month, channel breakdown: in-store vs. online), (c) redemption rate (points redeemed ÷ points available for redemption), (d) tier distribution (Bronze/Silver/Gold/Platinum member count and revenue contribution per tier), (e) average points balance per member by tier, (f) earn-to-burn ratio (points earned ÷ points redeemed — ratio > 1.0 means liability is growing), (g) member spend lift — do loyalty members spend more per transaction than non-members? (target: ≥ 15% higher ATV) | Loyalty Manager | CMO | 2 hours/quarter |
| 3 | **Program cost analysis**: Cost Accountant calculates quarterly program cost: (a) points redemption cost (value of discounts given at POS), (b) deferred revenue allocation impact on gross revenue recognition, (c) program administration cost ( Loyalty Manager time, system licensing, card printing, communication), (d) promotional loyalty costs (double-points weekends, bonus points per W17.10), (e) total program cost as % of qualifying revenue; benchmark against industry norms (1.0–1.5% for Philippine retail) | Cost Accountant | Controller | 1 hour/quarter |
| 4 | **Tier economics review**: Loyalty Manager and Cost Accountant analyze tier-level economics: (a) revenue per member by tier, (b) margin per member by tier (after loyalty discount, promotional costs, and tier-specific benefits), (c) upgrade/downgrade rates between tiers, (d) tier retention rate (% of members maintaining or upgrading tier year-over-year), (e) Platinum/Gold tier cost-benefit analysis — do high-tier benefits (exclusive pricing, priority service) drive sufficient incremental margin to justify the cost? | Loyalty Manager / Cost Accountant | CMO / Controller | 1 hour/quarter |
| 5 | **CMO and CFO joint review**: CMO (program strategy) and CFO (financial stewardship) review quarterly loyalty program performance: (a) is the liability growing sustainably?, (b) is engagement improving or declining?, (c) is the program cost within budget?, (d) are tier economics viable?, (e) competitive loyalty benchmarking — how does BuildRight's program compare to competitors (earn rate, redemption value, tier benefits)?; agree on action items (e.g., adjust earn rate, change tier thresholds, introduce new redemption options, run re-engagement campaign for dormant members) | CMO / CFO | CEO | 1 hour/quarter |

### Annual Program Design Review

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Loyalty Manager prepares annual program review document: (a) full-year financial summary (liability, cost, breakage, ROI), (b) program engagement trend over 3 years, (c) member segmentation analysis — heavy redeemers, points hoarders, dormant members, new members, (d) competitive program analysis — what are competitors offering?, (e) member satisfaction data from W65 CSAT surveys specific to loyalty, (f) proposed program changes for next year (earn rate, tier thresholds, new benefits, redemption catalog expansion, partnership opportunities) | Loyalty Manager | CMO | 8 hours/year |
| 7 | CMO, CFO, and CEO review annual program design; approve or modify proposed changes; changes to earn rate or redemption value require Board awareness due to balance sheet impact | CMO / CFO | CEO / Board | 2 hours/year |
| 8 | If program changes approved: Loyalty Manager coordinates implementation timeline with IT and Marketing; system configuration changes deployed; member communication plan executed (30-day advance notice for changes affecting earn rate or tier thresholds); updated program terms published on website and in-store | Loyalty Manager / IT | CMO | Per change scope |

### System Touchpoints
- Quarterly loyalty liability report: outstanding points, deferred revenue, breakage waterfall, liability trend (W104.1)
- Program engagement dashboard: active rate, enrollment, redemption, tier distribution, earn-to-burn, member spend lift (W104.2)
- Program cost reporting: redemption cost, deferred revenue impact, admin cost, promotional cost (W104.3)
- Tier economics analysis: revenue, margin, upgrade/downgrade, retention per tier (W104.4)
- Annual program review with competitive benchmarking and member segmentation (W104.6–7)
- Integration with W17 (day-to-day loyalty operations), W65 (CSAT measurement), W83 (marketing campaigns — loyalty mechanics), W35 (management reporting — program KPIs in executive dashboard)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Points liability growth on balance sheet**: With ~PHP 300–600M in deferred revenue and an earn-to-burn ratio potentially > 1.0, the liability grows faster than redemptions; if the CFO and CMO do not actively manage this, the liability can become material enough to concern external auditors and lenders
- **Breakage rate assumption sensitivity**: PFRS 15 revenue recognition for loyalty points depends on an estimated breakage rate (typically assumed at 15–25%); if actual breakage diverges significantly from the assumption, the deferred revenue balance is misstated, requiring retrospective adjustment
- **Tier economics erosion**: Platinum and Gold tier benefits (exclusive pricing, priority service, bonus points) cost more to deliver as the program matures and members accumulate status; if tier upgrade rates exceed projections, the program's cost-to-revenue ratio can deteriorate silently
- **Competitive loyalty escalation**: If a major competitor launches an aggressive loyalty program (e.g., 2× earn rate, lower redemption thresholds), BuildRight faces a dilemma: match the competitive offer (increasing program cost and liability) or risk losing high-value loyalty members to the competitor

- System auto-generate of "Program cost analysis" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-187 (ensure marketing spend & loyalty-fraud control.)
- operational: Loyalty Manager (operations); Cost Accountant (financial); CMO (strategic) review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
- Quarterly liability reporting: 2 hours/quarter for Cost Accountant (step 1)
- Quarterly engagement metrics: 2 hours/quarter for Loyalty Manager (step 2)
- Quarterly cost analysis: 1 hour/quarter for Cost Accountant (step 3)
- Quarterly tier economics review: 1 hour/quarter (step 4)
- CMO/CFO joint quarterly review: 1 hour/quarter (step 5)
- Annual program review preparation: 8 hours/year for Loyalty Manager (step 6)
- Annual program design review meeting: 2 hours/year (step 7)
- Program change implementation: varies per scope (step 8)

- **Loyalty Manager**: adds ~2 hours/quarter for engagement analysis + ~8 hours/year for annual review = ~16 hours/year. Absorbed.
- **CMO / CFO**: 1 hour/quarter joint review + 2 hours/year annual review = ~6 hours/year. Absorbed.
- **No incremental headcount.**

## W149. Bank & Credit Card Partnership Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Seasonal promo planning; or bank-initiated partnership proposal |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly campaigns |
| **Volume** | Covers top 3–5 credit card issuers in the Philippines |
| **Owner** | Partnerships Manager |
| **Participants** | Marketing, Finance, Bank Partner, POS/IT |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Negotiation**: Define promo mechanics (e.g., 0% interest for 12 months, 5% rebate, or points multiplier) | Partnerships Mgr | CMO | 1–2 weeks |
| 2 | **Financial Modeling**: Calculate cost-sharing (Bank vs. BuildRight) and impact on margin | Finance Mgr | CFO | 1 day |
| 3 | **POS Configuration**: Setup Bank BIN (Bank Identification Number) ranges and promo codes in POS | IT / POS Team | Partnerships Mgr | 2 days |
| 4 | **Communication**: Brief store staff on offer details and settlement process (e.g., specific card terminals) | Marketing | Store Mgr | 1 day |
| 5 | **Execution**: Customer uses partner card at POS; system auto-applies benefit (rebate/points) | Cashier / System | — | Continuous |
| 6 | **Settlement**: Reconcile partner bank statements vs. POS records; invoice bank for their cost share | Finance (AR) | Controller | Monthly |
| 7 | **ROI Review**: Measure incremental sales and new customer acquisition from the partnership | Partnerships Mgr | CMO | 2 hours/campaign |

### System Touchpoints
- POS terminal configuration module for Bank BIN ranges and promo codes
- Payment gateway integration for partner card transaction identification and auto-discounting
- AR module for partner bank settlement reconciliation and cost-share invoicing
- Campaign performance analytics for incremental sales and customer acquisition measurement
- Partnership contract management (W62) for bank partnership agreements
- Integration with W83 (campaigns — bank promo campaign coordination), W13 (promotional pricing — POS discount configuration), W30 (treasury — bank settlement processing), W48 (IT — POS configuration support)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Bank-dominant negotiation dynamics**: Major Philippine credit card issuers (BDO, BPI, Metrobank) have significantly more negotiating power than BuildRight; promo cost-sharing terms (who absorbs the 0% interest subsidy or rebate cost) often favor the bank, compressing BuildRight's margin
- **POS configuration errors**: Incorrect BIN ranges or promo codes in POS terminals can result in customers not receiving the promised benefit (causing complaints and DTI issues) or BuildRight absorbing the full cost of a benefit intended to be shared with the bank
- **Settlement reconciliation complexity**: Bank partner statements must be reconciled against POS transaction records; discrepancies (timing differences, disputed transactions, returned items with promo benefits already applied) are common and time-consuming to resolve
- **Promo fatigue and cannibalization**: With 3–5 bank partners each running quarterly campaigns, customers may delay purchases to wait for the next bank promo, creating a "promotion-dependent" purchasing pattern that erodes regular-price sales


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-calculate of "Calculate cost-sharing" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-record of "records" (replaces manual Step 6).
- System auto-review of "review" (replaces manual Step 7).

### Controls
- CTL-281 (ensure controlled execution — Campaign Planning & Execution (PA-14.1).)
- operational: Partnerships Manager review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
- Negotiation and terms definition: 1–2 weeks per partnership (step 1)
- Financial modeling: 1 day/partnership (step 2)
- POS BIN range and promo code configuration: 2 days/partnership (step 3)

## W496. Customer Loyalty Fraud Detection & Prevention

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Fraud alert from loyalty system analytics; anomaly detection threshold breach; customer complaint investigation; manual tip-off from store staff |
| **Frequency** | Weekly (automated scanning); event-driven (investigation activation); monthly (reporting) |
| **Volume** | ~50–100 flagged cases/month for review; ~5–15 confirmed fraud cases/month |
| **Owner** | Marketing — Loyalty Program Manager |
| **Participants** | LP Analyst, IT Security, Customer Service, Store Manager, Finance — Payment Settlement |

### Background

With 600,000 loyalty members, a points-based system (1 point per PHP 100 spent, PHP 1.00 per point redemption value), and tiered benefits (Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum), BuildRight's loyalty program represents a significant financial liability (deferred revenue per PFRS 15 — W487) and an attractive target for fraud. Common loyalty fraud schemes include: (a) **Account takeover**: unauthorized access to member accounts to redeem points; (b) **Points farming**: creating multiple accounts to accumulate promotional bonus points; (c) **Return fraud for points**: purchasing items, earning points, then returning items — points are not always reversed, creating arbitrage; (d) **Employee-assisted points manipulation**: cashiers manually awarding extra points to their own or friends' accounts; (e) **Promotional abuse**: exploiting system loopholes during double-points events or sign-up bonuses; (f) **Fake account creation for signup bonuses**: automated or manual creation of fake accounts to harvest enrollment bonuses. W104 covers loyalty program financial governance (financial liability, tier economics) but does not address fraud detection and prevention. W466 covers general LP but is focused on merchandise theft, not loyalty program fraud.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Automated Fraud Scanning**: Loyalty system runs automated fraud detection rules weekly: (a) **Velocity rules**: accounts earning or redeeming points at abnormal rates (e.g., >10x average member activity in 24 hours); (b) **Pattern rules**: multiple accounts sharing same device, IP address, or phone number; (c) **Return-points mismatch**: accounts with high return rates but retained points; (d) **Geographic anomaly**: points earned at a store the member has never visited, or at multiple distant stores on the same day; (e) **Employee-linked accounts**: accounts linked to employee IDs (W205) showing unusual point accumulation; (f) flagged cases are queued for manual review | System / Loyalty Program Manager | IT Security | Automated |
| 2 | **Case Triage**: Loyalty Program Manager reviews flagged cases: (a) classify case severity: **High** (employee involvement, organized ring, financial impact >PHP 10,000), **Medium** (individual fraud, PHP 1,000–10,000 impact), **Low** (suspicious but possibly legitimate activity); (b) assign investigation priority; (c) for High severity: escalate to LP Analyst (W466) for cross-functional investigation; (d) for Medium/Low: Loyalty Program Manager investigates directly | Loyalty Program Manager | Marketing Director | 4 hours/week |
| 3 | **Investigation**: Loyalty Program Manager investigates flagged cases: (a) review account activity log: all point earn/redeem transactions, linked devices, store visits, purchase history; (b) cross-reference with POS transaction data (W5B) — verify that purchases generating points actually occurred; (c) cross-reference with returns data (W12A) — verify points reversal on returned items; (d) for employee-linked accounts: cross-reference with employee work schedule (W34) — are they at the store when points are earned?; (e) for account takeover: review login logs (device, IP, location, timestamp); (f) document findings in fraud case file | Loyalty Program Manager / LP Analyst | Marketing Director | 1–3 hours/case |

| 6 | **Monthly Fraud Report**: Loyalty Program Manager prepares monthly loyalty fraud report: (a) total flagged cases, confirmed fraud cases, and false positive rate; (b) fraud loss quantification (points fraudulently earned/redeemed × PHP 1.00/point); (c) fraud type breakdown (account takeover, points farming, return arbitrage, employee-assisted, promotional abuse); (d) recovery actions taken; (e) rule enhancements implemented; (f) fraud trend analysis (increasing/decreasing by type); (g) update loyalty program financial liability estimate (W104) to reflect fraud adjustments | Loyalty Program Manager | Marketing Director | 4 hours/month |

| **Owner** | Marketing Communications Manager |
| **Participants** | Marketing Comms Manager, Store Manager (designate), VP Store Operations, LGU officials, Category Managers, local media |

### Background

A new BuildRight Depot grand opening is a major local event in Philippine provincial cities. For many communities, BuildRight is the first organized big-box home improvement retailer in the area, and the opening creates significant local employment (29 positions per store) and economic activity. The grand opening marketing campaign serves three purposes: (1) drive immediate foot traffic and sales during the critical first 30 days, (2) establish BuildRight's brand presence in the local community for long-term loyalty, and (3) build relationships with local government units (LGU), barangay officials, and local contractors/trade professionals who become key customer segments. Philippine retail culture places high importance on personal relationships and community presence — a well-executed grand opening that includes the local mayor, barangay captain, and community leaders establishes goodwill that translates into sustained business.

| 5 | **Grand opening day execution**: (a) ceremonial ribbon-cutting by Mayor/Barangay Captain with BuildRight executives; (b) store opens to public; (c) opening day activities: (i) first 500 customers receive free gift (tote bag, tape measure, or BuildRight voucher), (ii) hourly raffle prizes (power tools, paint sets, gift certificates), (iii) live product demonstrations (paint mixing, lumber cutting, tile estimation — per PA-09.1/09.2), (iv) children's activity area (to keep families in-store longer), (v) food stalls from local vendors (community partnership); (d) Store Manager and team manage customer flow, ensure checkout queues are managed (all 3 POS terminals active); (e) Marketing Communications Manager coordinates media coverage: photo opportunities, customer testimonials, social media live posts | Store Manager / Marketing Comms Manager / All Store Staff | VP Store Operations | Full-day event |

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Construction delays impacting marketing timeline**: if store construction runs 2–4 weeks late (common in Philippine construction due to weather, permit delays, material delivery issues), all pre-planned marketing (radio spots, flyer distribution, LGU invitations) may need to be rescheduled — some costs (printed flyers with wrong date, radio ad time slots) are non-recoverable
- **LGU political dynamics**: Philippine local politics can complicate grand openings; if the Mayor and Barangay Captain are from rival political parties, seating arrangements, speaking order, and photo opportunities require diplomatic handling to avoid offending either party
- **Opening day operational chaos**: first-day customer volume can exceed projections by 2–3x in underserved provincial markets where residents have never had a big-box home improvement store; all 29 staff may be overwhelmed, leading to long queues, stockouts on promo items, and negative first impressions
- **Budget overrun**: community expectations for sponsorship, giveaways, and entertainment can expand scope beyond budget; Store Manager and Marketing Communications Manager must enforce budget discipline while maintaining community goodwill

## W1551. Local Store Marketing Campaign Execution, Barangay-Level Outreach & Community Event Partnership

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly local marketing calendar cycle; or new store pre-opening; or barangay/community event opportunity |
| **Frequency** | ~200–300 local campaigns/month across all stores (each store executes 1–2 local initiatives/month) |
| **Volume** | Barangay fiestas (1,000+ events/year nationwide), school-building drives, local sports tournament sponsorships |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Sales Associates, Marketing Coordinator (HQ), Barangay officials |

### Background

Big-box retail in the Philippines is deeply embedded in local communities. Success depends not only on national branding but on hyper-local presence — participating in barangay (village/neighborhood) fiestas, sponsoring local sports teams, partnering with schools for building projects, and maintaining relationships with local government officials. Each of BuildRight's 200 stores operates in a distinct community context requiring tailored local marketing. This workflow governs store-level local marketing execution within brand guidelines, funded through a monthly local marketing budget per store.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monthly local marketing plan**: Store Manager submits monthly local marketing plan via mobile app — proposed activities (barangay event sponsorship, school partnership, local sports team support, community workshop), budget request (up to PHP 15,000/month per store), expected foot traffic impact; Marketing Coordinator (HQ) reviews and approves within brand guidelines | Store Manager | Marketing Coordinator | 1 hour/month |
| 2 | **Barangay relationship management**: Store Manager maintains ongoing relationship with barangay captain and council — attends barangay assembly when invited, participates in disaster preparedness planning (W49), offers BuildRight as community resource for building materials during emergencies; Store Manager logs all barangay interactions in CRM (W253) | Store Manager | VP Store Operations | 2–3 hours/month |
| 3 | **Community event execution**: For approved events — Store Manager coordinates logistics (booth setup, product display, promotional flyers, staff assignment); Sales Associates staff the BuildRight presence at the event; distribute promotional materials with store-specific discount codes (trackable); collect customer leads for loyalty enrollment (W263) | Store Manager / Sales Associates | Marketing Coordinator | 4–8 hours/event |
| 4 | **Local partnership development**: Store Manager identifies and develops partnerships with local contractors' associations, technical/vocational schools (TESDA), and homeowner associations — offers exclusive store tours, contractor loyalty enrollment drives (W897), workshop invitations (W147); logs partnership details in CRM | Store Manager | VP Store Operations | 2 hours/month |
