## W1350. Email Marketing Campaign Operations, Segmentation & Engagement Analytics

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Marketing campaign calendar milestone; or triggered event (abandoned cart, post-purchase, loyalty tier upgrade) |
| **Frequency** | 2–3 broadcast campaigns/week; daily triggered/automated emails |
| **Volume** | ~600,000 loyalty members + ~5,400 trade and corporate accounts ≈ ~605,400 reachable contacts; average open rate 15–20% for Philippine retail |
| **Owner** | Email Marketing Specialist |
| **Participants** | Content Specialist, Category Manager (for product selection), Marketing Data Analyst, CMO |

### Background

Email remains a high-ROI channel for BuildRight Depot, complementing the social media and SMS channels. With ~600,000 loyalty members, segmented email campaigns drive store traffic during bi-monthly sale events, promote category-specific offers to trade professionals, and support the seasonal calendar (e.g., rainy season waterproofing promos in Jun–Aug, ber-months home improvement in Sep–Oct). Automated lifecycle emails (welcome, post-purchase, re-engagement) provide always-on engagement beyond manual campaigns.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Email Marketing Specialist builds audience segment using CRM data: by loyalty tier, purchase history (category-level), trade account status, geographic region, or last-active date | Email Marketing Specialist | CMO | 30 min/campaign |
| 2 | Content Specialist creates email creative: hero image, product grid (6–12 items), promotional copy in Filipino/English, call-to-action buttons linking to buildright.com.ph or store locator | Content Specialist | Email Marketing Specialist | 2–3 hours/campaign |
| 3 | Email Marketing Specialist configures email in marketing automation platform: personalization tokens (first name, loyalty points balance, nearest store), dynamic product blocks based on segment, A/B test subject lines | Email Marketing Specialist | — | 1 hour/campaign |
| 4 | CMO or designated approver reviews and approves email content, offer validity, and audience size | CMO | CMO | 15 min/campaign |
| 5 | System sends email at optimal send time (historically 10 AM Tuesday/Thursday for B2C; 8 AM Monday for trade); triggered emails sent in real-time | System | — | Automated |
| 6 | Email Marketing Specialist monitors real-time dashboard: delivery rate, open rate, click-through rate (CTR), unsubscribe rate, spam complaints | Email Marketing Specialist | CMO | 15 min/day |
| 7 | Marketing Data Analyst produces weekly email performance report: segment-level engagement, revenue attribution (via UTM-tagged links), and list growth/attrition metrics | Marketing Data Analyst | CMO | 2 hours/week |

### System Touchpoints
- Marketing automation platform integrated with CRM/loyalty database for real-time segmentation
- Dynamic content blocks pulling from product catalog (active SKUs, current prices, promotional pricing)
- UTM parameter tagging for Google Analytics attribution
- Ecommerce platform integration for product click-through and conversion tracking
- Unsubscribe/compliance management (opt-out processing within 24 hours per Data Privacy Act)

### Pain Points / Risks
- List fatigue from over-emailing: with 2–3 broadcasts/week plus triggered emails, active members may receive 5+ emails/week, driving unsubscribe rates above the 0.3% industry benchmark
- Product pricing accuracy in dynamic email blocks: if promotional pricing changes between email build and send time, emails display stale prices, creating customer expectations that cannot be honored at POS
- Low open rates for trade/B2B segment (~10–12% vs. 18–20% for B2C) suggest trade professionals prefer SMS or direct sales rep communication over email


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "account status, geographic region, or last-active date" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-create of "creates email creative" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-test of "test subject lines" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-282 (ensure controlled execution — Digital Marketing & Social Media (PA-14.2).)
- operational: Email Marketing Specialist review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
- Broadcast campaign: ~4–5 hours from segmentation to send (30 min segmentation + 2–3 hours creative + 1 hour configuration + 15 min approval)
- Triggered/automated emails: one-time setup ~2–3 hours per automation; ongoing monitoring ~15 min/day
- Performance monitoring: 15 min/day + 2 hours/week reporting = ~5 hours/week
- Total: ~20–25 hours/week for 2–3 broadcast campaigns + daily triggered email management

### Staffing Implication
- 1 Email Marketing Specialist (dedicated role within 25-person Marketing team) manages all email operations; ~20–25 hours/week dedicated to email, balance on other digital channels
- Content Specialist support: ~2–3 hours/week for email creative, shared with social media and campaign duties
- Marketing Data Analyst: ~2 hours/week for email reporting, absorbed from existing analytics capacity

## W1351. Customer Referral Program Operations, Reward Fulfillment & Fraud Prevention

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Existing loyalty member shares referral link/code with a new customer |
| **Frequency** | Continuous (always-on program) |
| **Volume** | ~2,000–3,000 referral attempts/month; ~800–1,200 successful conversions (40% conversion rate) |
| **Owner** | Loyalty Program Manager |
| **Participants** | Customer (Referrer), Customer (Referee), Customer Service, Finance (points liability), Marketing Data Analyst |

### Background

Word-of-mouth is particularly powerful in the Philippine home improvement market, where contractors and homeowners rely heavily on peer recommendations for building material suppliers. BuildRight's referral program incentivizes existing loyalty members to recruit new customers by awarding loyalty points to both the referrer and the referee upon the referee's first qualifying purchase. The program must balance growth objectives with fraud prevention — self-referrals, duplicate accounts, and manufactured purchases are common abuses in points-based referral programs.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Referrer generates unique referral code/link via buildright.com.ph account or mobile app; system creates referral record linked to referrer's loyalty ID | System | — | Automated |
| 2 | Referee uses referral link/code during online registration or presents code at POS during first in-store purchase | Customer (Referee) | — | 1 min |
| 3 | System validates referral: referee must be a new loyalty member (no prior purchase history), with a unique name, phone, and address; flags duplicates for manual review | System | Loyalty Program Manager | Automated |
| 4 | Referee makes first qualifying purchase (minimum PHP 2,000 single receipt within 30 days of registration) | Customer (Referee) | — | — |
| 5 | System auto-awards referral bonus: Referrer receives 500 points (PHP 500 value), Referee receives 200 points (PHP 200 value); points posted within 24 hours | System | — | Automated |
| 6 | Loyalty Program Manager reviews weekly fraud detection report: flags clusters from same IP address, same household address, rapid sequential referrals, or referee purchase-then-return patterns | Loyalty Program Manager | CMO | 1 hour/week |
| 7 | Customer Service processes referral inquiries and disputes (referee did not receive points, referral code not recognized at POS) | Customer Service | Loyalty Program Manager | 10 min/inquiry |
| 8 | Marketing Data Analyst produces monthly referral program performance report: new member acquisition, conversion rate, points liability, cost-per-acquisition, and referral chain analysis (referees who become referrers) | Marketing Data Analyst | CMO | 3 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Unique referral code generation tied to loyalty member ID
- POS and ecommerce referral code validation at checkout
- Loyalty points engine for automated reward posting
- Fraud detection rules engine (IP clustering, address matching, velocity checks)
- CRM integration for referee profile creation and linkage
- Finance integration for points liability accounting (cross-reference W1316)

### Pain Points / Risks
- Referral fraud via "family farms": multiple household members creating accounts using the same address and IP to farm referral bonuses; address-matching alone is insufficient because extended family households are common in the Philippines
- Referee qualification window (30 days) may be too short for project-based B2B customers who plan purchases months in advance after initial store visit
- Points liability accumulates rapidly: at 800–1,200 successful referrals/month × 700 total points per referral = 560,000–840,000 points/month (PHP 560K–840K/month in points liability); requires accurate forecasting for financial reserves


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "record linked to referrer's loyalty ID" (Step 1, already system-executed).
- System auto-review of "System validates referral" (Step 3, already system-executed).
- System auto-post of "posted within 24 hours" (Step 5, already system-executed).

### Controls
- CTL-282 (ensure controlled execution — Digital Marketing & Social Media (PA-14.2).)
- operational: Loyalty Program Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
- Automated steps (code generation, validation, points award): zero manual effort
- Fraud review: 1 hour/week × Loyalty Program Manager
- Customer service inquiries: ~50 inquiries/month × 10 min = ~8 hours/month, distributed across existing CSR team
- Monthly reporting: 3 hours/month × Marketing Data Analyst
- Total incremental effort: ~15–20 hours/month across all roles

### Staffing Implication
- Loyalty Program Manager dedicates ~1 hour/week to fraud review; absorbed within existing loyalty management duties
- Customer Service: ~8 hours/month for referral inquiries across 200 stores + call center; negligible per-store impact
- Marketing Data Analyst: 3 hours/month for referral reporting; absorbed from existing analytics capacity

- **Conversion attribution difficulty**: Tracking social media engagement (likes, shares, views) to actual in-store or online purchases is technically challenging; without reliable conversion data, the CMO cannot justify social media spend to the CFO with concrete ROI

## W736. Marketing Data Platform Daily Operations & Campaign Analytics

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Start of marketing operations day; or campaign performance alert |
| **Frequency** | Daily review; real-time campaign monitoring |
| **Volume** | ~5–10 active marketing campaigns at any time; ~1M+ customer touchpoints/day (email, SMS, push, social) |
| **Owner** | Marketing Analytics Manager |
| **Participants** | Campaign Manager, Digital Marketing Specialist, Customer Experience Manager, CMO |

### Background

W83 covers campaign planning and execution. W565 covers campaign ROI and attribution analysis. This workflow addresses the daily operational management of the marketing data platform (MDP) — the analytics infrastructure that powers campaign measurement, audience segmentation, and channel optimization. For a company with 600,000+ loyalty members processing 2.8M monthly POS transactions, daily marketing analytics operations ensure that campaigns are performing as expected and data-driven optimizations are applied in real-time.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Morning campaign performance dashboard review**: Marketing Analytics Manager reviews: (a) active campaign performance: (i) email campaigns: open rate, click-through rate, conversion rate, unsubscribe rate; (ii) SMS campaigns: delivery rate, click rate, opt-out rate; (iii) push notifications: delivery rate, open rate, app engagement; (iv) social media ads per W142: impressions, CTR, cost-per-click, ROAS; (v) in-store promotional lift per W13: promoted SKU sales vs. baseline; (b) campaign vs. target: each campaign's KPI vs. its planned target per W83; (c) alert on underperforming campaigns: any campaign below 70% of target KPI flagged for review | Marketing Analytics Manager | CMO | 20-30 min/day |
| 2 | **Real-time optimization**: for underperforming campaigns: (a) Marketing Analytics Manager diagnoses: (i) creative issue: low engagement despite good reach → test new creative per W190; (ii) audience issue: good engagement but low conversion → refine targeting per W673 segmentation; (iii) offer issue: good engagement but no purchase → adjust offer value or terms; (iv) channel issue: campaign performing well on one channel but not others → reallocate budget; (b) implement optimizations: (i) A/B test new creative or copy; (ii) adjust audience targeting rules; (iii) modify offer parameters; (iv) reallocate budget from underperforming to overperforming channels | Marketing Analytics Manager | Campaign Manager | 30-60 min/day |
| 3 | **Data pipeline health check**: verify marketing data platform pipelines: (a) POS transaction data feed: last successful sync, data freshness (<24 hours); (b) ecommerce behavioral data: website clicks, cart activity, search queries flowing into MDP; (c) loyalty transaction feed: points earning/redemption events updating customer profiles; (d) third-party data feeds: social media platform metrics, email platform metrics, ad platform metrics; (e) any pipeline failures: escalate to IT per W733 API monitoring | Marketing Analytics Manager / IT | IT Manager | 10-15 min/day |
| 4 | **Weekly campaign analytics report**: Marketing Analytics Manager compiles weekly report: (a) campaign-level performance summary with KPI tracking; (b) channel performance comparison: email vs. SMS vs. push vs. social vs. in-store; (c) audience segment performance: which segments responded best; (d) attribution analysis: last-click, first-click, and multi-touch attribution for major campaigns; (e) cost analysis: cost per acquisition, cost per conversion, ROAS by campaign and channel; (f) recommendations for next week's campaign adjustments | Marketing Analytics Manager | CMO | 2-3 hours/week |

### System Touchpoints

- Marketing data platform (CDP/DMP) for audience data and segmentation per W156
- Campaign management platform for multi-channel execution per W676
- POS transaction data per W533 for promotional lift measurement
- Ecommerce analytics for online campaign attribution
- Loyalty engine per CRM-001 for customer engagement tracking
- Social media management per W142 for social campaign metrics
- W677 marketing budget management for spend tracking
- W565 ROI and attribution analysis for cross-campaign comparison

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Attribution complexity**: customers interact with multiple campaigns and channels before purchasing; accurately attributing the sale to the most influential touchpoint is analytically challenging
- **Data pipeline latency**: marketing analytics depend on timely data from POS, ecommerce, and loyalty systems; pipeline delays reduce the ability to optimize campaigns in real-time
- **Privacy constraints**: RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act) limits the types of customer data that can be used for marketing targeting, requiring careful consent management per W405
- **Channel fragmentation**: tracking customer journeys across POS, ecommerce, mobile app, email, SMS, and social media requires a unified customer identifier that is not always available


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "Morning campaign performance dashboard review" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-allocate of "reallocate budget" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "Data pipeline health check" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-282 (ensure controlled execution — Digital Marketing & Social Media (PA-14.2).)
- operational: Marketing Analytics Manager review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication

Marketing Analytics Manager: ~1–2 hours/day on monitoring and optimization + 2-3 hours/week on reporting. Campaign Manager: collaborates on optimization. No incremental headcount.

### Time Estimate

**Daily operations**: 60-90 min. **Weekly reporting**: 2-3 hours.

|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Marketing identifies influencer for partnership; or influencer reaches out; or campaign planning cycle |
| **Frequency** | ~20–30 active influencer partnerships at any time; ~5–10 new partnerships onboarded per quarter |
| **Volume** | ~50–80 sponsored content pieces/month across all partnerships; mix of YouTube, TikTok, Facebook, and Instagram |
| **Owner** | Digital Marketing Manager |
| **Participants** | Digital Marketing Manager, Content Creator/Influencer, Category Manager, Social Media Specialist, Legal, Finance |

### Background

The Philippines has one of the highest social media engagement rates globally, with Filipinos spending an average of 3–4 hours/day on social media. Home improvement and DIY content is growing rapidly on YouTube ("Tadhana ng Bahay" renovation vlogs), TikTok (#DIYPH, #HomeImprovement tutorials), and Facebook (community groups). Filipino content creators specializing in home improvement — contractors sharing tips, homeowners documenting renovations, interior designers showcasing makeovers — have highly engaged audiences that align with BuildRight's customer base. Influencer partnerships provide authentic, relatable content that traditional advertising cannot replicate. However, Philippine FTC-equivalent guidelines (DTI's Consumer Act, ADBoard standards) and RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act) require proper disclosure of sponsored content and consent for personal data use.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Influencer identification & vetting**: (a) Social Media Specialist monitors home improvement content on YouTube, TikTok, Facebook, and Instagram for potential partners; (b) evaluate candidates: (i) audience size (YouTube: > 10,000 subscribers; TikTok: > 50,000 followers; Facebook: > 20,000 page likes), (ii) audience demographics (homeowners, DIYers, contractors within BuildRight's geographic coverage), (iii) engagement rate (comments, shares, saves — not just likes), (iv) content quality and brand alignment, (v) audience authenticity (no purchased followers), (vi) previous brand partnerships; (c) create shortlist of 5–10 candidates per quarter for outreach | Social Media Specialist | Digital Marketing Manager | 8 hours/month |
| 2 | **Partnership negotiation & onboarding**: (a) Digital Marketing Manager contacts selected influencers; discusses partnership scope: content deliverables, timeline, exclusivity (no competing home improvement retailers), compensation; (b) compensation models: (i) flat fee per content piece (YouTube: PHP 15,000–100,000; TikTok: PHP 5,000–30,000; Facebook: PHP 5,000–20,000 depending on following), (ii) affiliate commission (2–5% of sales via unique promo code), (iii) product seeding (free materials/tools for content creation), (iv) hybrid (reduced fee + commission); (c) Legal prepares Influencer Agreement per W62: deliverables, IP rights, disclosure requirements, exclusivity, termination, RA 10173 consent; (d) onboard influencer: provide brand guidelines, product catalog, store locations, unique promo code, and affiliate tracking link | Digital Marketing Manager / Legal | CMO | 1–2 weeks/partnership |

| 4 | **Content review and approval**: (a) influencer submits draft content for review; (b) Digital Marketing Manager reviews for: (i) factual accuracy of product information, (ii) brand safety (no controversial content), (iii) disclosure compliance, (iv) visual quality standards; (c) provide feedback within 48 hours; (d) approved content published per agreed schedule; (e) for unapproved modifications after review: contract remedy per W62 agreement | Digital Marketing Manager / Influencer | CMO | 30–60 min/content piece |

## W1560. Customer Review & User-Generated Content Management, Online Reputation Monitoring & Response

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New customer review posted on Google Business, Facebook, TripAdvisor, or buildright.com.ph; or weekly reputation monitoring cycle; or negative review alert trigger |
| **Frequency** | ~200–400 new reviews/month across all platforms for 200 store locations + national brand pages; weekly monitoring cycle; real-time negative review alerts |
| **Volume** | ~2,400–4,800 reviews/year; average 3.8–4.2 star rating target; ~5–10% negative reviews (1–2 stars) requiring response |
| **Owner** | Digital Marketing Manager |
| **Participants** | Digital Marketing Manager, Customer Service Manager, Store Manager (for store-specific reviews), VP Marketing, Category Manager |

### Background

Online reviews are the digital equivalent of word-of-mouth for retail. BuildRight Depot's 200 store locations each have a Google Business Profile and Facebook Page that accumulate customer reviews. A single 1-star review describing a negative experience (rude staff, out-of-stock item, wrong pricing) can deter dozens of potential customers in the local community. Conversely, positive reviews build trust and drive foot traffic. Philippine consumers are particularly influenced by Facebook reviews and recommendations (Facebook is the dominant social platform). This workflow manages BuildRight's online reputation: monitoring reviews across all platforms, responding to negative reviews with service recovery, encouraging satisfied customers to leave reviews, and leveraging user-generated content (customer project photos, testimonials) for marketing.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Real-time review monitoring**: System monitors all 200 store Google Business Profiles, BuildRight Facebook Page, and buildright.com.ph product reviews: (a) new review notifications pushed to Digital Marketing Manager dashboard; (b) sentiment classification: positive (4–5 stars), neutral (3 stars), negative (1–2 stars); (c) negative review alert: real-time SMS/email to Digital Marketing Manager and relevant Store Manager for reviews < 3 stars; (d) weekly: compile review summary by store, region, and chain-wide | System / Digital Marketing Manager | — | Automated + 2 hours/week |
| 2 | **Negative review response & service recovery**: For negative reviews (1–2 stars): (a) Digital Marketing Manager drafts public response within 24 hours: (i) acknowledge the customer's experience, (ii) apologize sincerely, (iii) invite the customer to contact Customer Service Manager directly (provide email/phone), (iv) assure the customer that the issue will be addressed; (b) simultaneously: Store Manager investigates the incident — review internal records (POS transaction, staffing, incident reports per W140) and speaks with involved staff; (c) Customer Service Manager contacts the reviewer privately (if contact information available): (i) understand the full situation, (ii) offer resolution (refund, replacement, discount on next visit, loyalty points credit), (iii) request review update if resolved to customer's satisfaction; (d) resolution logged in CRM per W41 for pattern tracking | Digital Marketing Manager / Store Manager / Customer Service Manager | VP Marketing | 30–60 min per negative review |
| 3 | **Positive review engagement**: For positive reviews (4–5 stars): (a) Digital Marketing Manager or system auto-responds with: (i) thank the customer by name, (ii) highlight the specific positive aspect mentioned, (iii) invite the customer to the next workshop or promotion; (b) monthly: identify the top 5 most enthusiastic reviewers per store — consider for loyalty program upgrade or brand ambassador per W1561 | System / Digital Marketing Manager | — | Automated + 1 hour/week |

Leave a review and earn 50 bonus loyalty points!"; (b) in-store: Customer Service Counter displays "Review us on Google/Facebook" signage with QR code; (c) post-workshop per W147: "Enjoyed the workshop? ## W5568. Official-Channel Account-Takeover Response (Brand-Account Hijack)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | BuildRight's own official social-media pages, messaging channels (Viber/WhatsApp/Messenger), marketplace seller accounts or app-store listings are compromised — scam promotions, fake giveaways or phishing links posted from the genuine verified account, admin access lost to a hijacker, anomalous admin logins flagged, or followers reporting fraud content that carries BuildRight's real handle |
| **Frequency** | Rare; ~0–2 confirmed takeover attempts per year across the official channel estate (the verified accounts are high-value targets precisely because their scam posts inherit platform trust) |
| **Volume** | 1 takeover × the hijacked account's follower base (the largest page reaches ~2.1M followers) × every follower who sees the fraud post before takedown — the blast radius is trust, not inventory |
| **Owner** | Social Media Manager (channel recovery); VP Legal & Compliance (fraud-content posture) |
| **Participants** | Digital Marketing Manager (channel program), IT Security (credential forensics, W383/W5518), VS-27.3 (access & infrastructure containment), platform trust-&-safety channels (recovery appeals), VS-13/VS-07 (inbound customer-inquiry scripts), VS-14.3 (crisis comms per W134/W855 ring), VS-91 (data exposure where follower DMs or records are accessible), W5563 (the fake-page sibling canon for any copycat waves riding the confusion) |

### Background

The brand-integrity canon covers the accounts that only *pretend* to be BuildRight — W5563's fake sellers and W5557's fake recruiters — and W3871 covers customer-account takeover; but nobody owned the takeover of BuildRight's *own* verified accounts, where the trust inversion is total: a scam post from the genuine page inherits the platform's verification checkmarks, so even media and partners cannot self-verify. The event is distinct from W5550 (the platform enforcing against us — here the platform is the recovery channel, not the accuser) and from W5558 (enterprise intrusion — the takeover may ride one phished admin credential with no wider estate impact). The response needs an owner for the credential kill-chain, the platform-recovery appeal, the fraud-post remediation record, the follower advisory that re-points trust to unaffected channels, and the privacy assessment where the hijacker could read follower messages.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Contain the credential**: force password/reset and revoke sessions and API tokens for the affected account and every admin sharing the credential; suspend scheduled-post queues so stale promo content cannot fire mid-incident | Social Media Manager / IT Security | CIO | First hour |
| 2 | **Evidence & scope**: capture the fraud posts, timestamps and reach before takedown; IT Security establishes the entry vector (phished admin, reused password, third-party tool token) and whether any other channel or mailbox shares the compromise (W383/W5518) | IT Security / Social Media Manager | CIO | 2–8 hours |
| 3 | **Platform recovery appeal**: engage the platform's verified-account/compromised-business channels with corporate proof (SEC records, domain control, ad-account history); request fraud-post removal and admin restoration — the appeal rides the platform relationship, never paid "recovery services" | Social Media Manager / VP Legal | VP Legal & Compliance | 1–3 days |
| 4 | **Follower advisory**: same-day advisory from unaffected official channels — the hijacked account is compromised, BuildRight never runs pay-to-claim giveaways or e-wallet-only promos, and the website/app are the transaction surface of record; service desks get the inquiry script | VS-14.3 / Brand | VP Legal & Compliance | Same day |
| 5 | **Victim & fraud watch**: followers who interacted with the fraud post get the W5563-class guidance (platform report channel, e-wallet/bank recall request, PNP Anti-Cybercrime referral); VS-125/VS-13 watch for presentment abuse and complaint spikes | VS-13 / VS-125 | VP Store Operations | Event-long |
| 6 | **Privacy assessment**: where the hijacker could access follower messages or audience data, route the VS-91 assessment — the W53 NPC clock applies from confirmation of unauthorized access | VS-91 / DPO | Data Protection Officer | Per W53 |
| 7 | **Restore & harden**: admin restoration verified against the role roster; hardware-token/2FA mandatory on the channel estate; third-party posting-tool tokens rotated; the W5563 fake-page watch tuned for copycats riding the incident | Social Media Manager / IT Security | CIO | 1–2 weeks |

### System Touchpoints

- Social-media management platform (admin roster, session/token revocation, scheduled-queue freeze)
- IT-Security incident case with credential-forensics evidence (links to W383/W5518)
- Platform trust-&-safety appeal case file with corporate-proof attachments
- Advisory publishing via unaffected official channels and the careers-site banner (links to W3762)
- Service-desk inquiry scripts pushed through the store-communications cascade (links to VS-63)
- VS-91 breach-assessment intake where follower-data access is confirmed (links to W53)

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Trust-inversion risk**: fraud content carries the real verification badge, defeating follower self-verification; mitigated by Step 4's same-day advisory from unaffected channels naming the compromise explicitly
- **Serialization risk**: recovering the account before freezing shared credentials lets the hijacker hop channels; mitigated by Step 1's estate-wide credential containment before any recovery appeal
- **Recovery-scam risk**: fake "account recovery services" approach the team during the incident; mitigated by the platform-channel-only rule in Step 3
- **Privacy exposure risk**: hijacker access to follower DMs triggers RA 10173 duties teams forget outside a classic breach; mitigated by Step 6's explicit VS-91/W53 routing
- **Copycat risk**: fake pages multiply while the real page is untrusted; mitigated by Step 7's W5563 watch tuning and Step 5's victim guidance

### Automation Opportunity

- Channel-estate admin roster with token-age and 2FA-state dashboard, auto-flagging shared credentials (replaces manual Step 7 audit)
- Social-listening alerts keyed to fraud-keyword patterns on official handles (replaces manual follower-report discovery)
- Advisory-template publishing workflow to unaffected channels with one-click activation (replaces manual Step 4 drafting under pressure)
- Service-desk macro pack auto-deployed on the incident flag (replaces per-store improvised answers)

### Controls

- CTL-282 (ensure controlled execution — Digital Marketing & Social Media (PA-14.2).)
- operational: official-channel admin access is 2FA-protected with a named-role roster; takeover containment (credential revocation and queue freeze) executes within the first hour of confirmation; follower advisories run only from unaffected verified channels

### Time Estimate

Per confirmed takeover ≈ 4–10 staff-hours for containment, evidence and the advisory plus 8–24 hours of IT-Security forensics and platform-appeal handling across the recovery window; at the 0–2-attempts-per-year cadence this stays absorbed within the social-media team, IT Security and Legal — no incremental headcount.

### Staffing Implication

- Absorbed by the social-media/digital-marketing team, IT Security and Legal; no incremental headcount.
