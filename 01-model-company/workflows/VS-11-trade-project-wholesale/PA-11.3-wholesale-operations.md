## W145. Wholesale Reseller Onboarding & Credit Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | 3rd-party hardware store or construction supply company requests reseller partnership |
| **Frequency** | 2–5 new resellers/month |
| **Volume** | ~100–150 active resellers |
| **Owner** | B2B Sales Manager |
| **Participants** | Sales Rep, Credit Analyst, Legal, Finance |

### Background

While BuildRight is primarily retail, it acts as a regional distributor for certain brands and high-volume commodities (cement, steel) for smaller hardware stores. Resellers require different tax handling (withholding tax on sales) and volume-based pricing.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Application**: Reseller submits business permits, SEC/DTI registration, and BIR 2303 (Certificate of Registration) | Reseller / Sales Rep | B2B Sales Mgr | 1 hour |
| 2 | **Credit Review**: Credit Analyst performs deep-dive on financial statements and trade references per W24; sets "Wholesale Credit Limit" | Credit Analyst | Finance Mgr | 1–3 days |
| 3 | **Tax Configuration**: System flags account as "Reseller"; configures automatic handling of Expanded Withholding Tax (EWT) certificates (BIR 2307) | Finance (AR) | Controller | 30 min |
| 4 | **Price List Assignment**: Assign "Wholesale Tier" price list (W40.15–19) based on projected annual volume | B2B Sales Mgr | VP Merch | 15 min |
| 5 | **Onboarding**: Create account in ERP with "Reseller" category; provide login to B2B ordering portal | Sales Rep | — | 30 min |

### System Touchpoints
- B2B Portal / Customer Master (wholesale account setup)
- Credit Management module (W24 integration)
- Tax configuration engine (EWT handling per BIR 2307)
- Price list management (wholesale tier assignment)
- ERP onboarding workflow

### Pain Points / Risks
- BIR Expanded Withholding Tax (EWT) complexity for wholesale transactions
- Reseller credit risk in Philippine construction market
- Pricing sync between wholesale portal and ERP


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-submit of "submits business permits, SEC/DTI registration, and BIR 2303" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "performs deep-dive on financial statements and trade references" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-count of "account" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-274 (ensure controlled execution — Wholesale Operations (PA-11.3).)
- operational: B2B Sales Manager review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
2-5 new resellers/month. Approximately 4-5 hours per onboarding (credit review + tax config + price list). Absorbed by B2B Sales Manager + Credit Analyst.

### Time Estimate
- Credit review: 1-3 days (externally driven)
- Tax configuration + price list assignment: ~45 min
- ERP onboarding: ~30 min
- Total internal effort per reseller: 4-5 hours

## W598. Wholesale Pricing & Quotation Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Reseller requests quotation; new customer tier assessment; price list update cycle; government procurement RFQ; project-based pricing request |
| **Frequency** | ~80–120 quotations/month; tier assessments quarterly; price list updates monthly or upon cost change |
| **Volume** | ~100–150 active resellers across 4 wholesale tiers (Bronze/Silver/Gold/Platinum); trade accounts represent ~30% of revenue (PHP ~18.7B annually); ~15–20 government procurement quotations/year; ~30–40 project-based quotations/year |
| **Owner** | B2B Sales Manager |
| **Participants** | B2B Sales Manager, Sales Representative, Category Manager (A for below-threshold pricing), VP Merchandising (A for government/project pricing), Finance Analyst (R for margin validation), Credit Analyst (R for customer tier assessment), B2B Support Representative |

### Background

W145 covers wholesale reseller onboarding and credit management. W58 covers corporate/project account management. W163 covers contract pricing. W78 covers government procurement. W85 covers product costing and margin analysis. However, no workflow covers the day-to-day management of wholesale pricing tiers, B2B quotation generation, and price negotiation — the operational process of translating pricing strategy into customer-facing quotations and converting quotations into orders.

BuildRight's wholesale business operates on a tiered pricing model: Bronze (annual purchases <PHP 5M, ~5% discount from SRP), Silver (PHP 5–15M, ~10% discount), Gold (PHP 15–30M, ~15% discount), Platinum (>PHP 30M, ~18–20% discount). These tiers are based on projected annual volume and reviewed quarterly. In addition, project-based pricing (one-time quotations for specific construction projects) and government procurement pricing (per RA 9184 Government Procurement Reform Act requirements) require custom quotation generation with margin validation and multi-level approval.

Philippine wholesale pricing complexity includes: (1) Expanded Withholding Tax (EWT) on sales to resellers — 1% for goods, requiring BIR 2307 certificate issuance; (2) VAT computation (12%) and its interaction with wholesale discounts; (3) government procurement pricing which must comply with RA 9184 bidding requirements and may require different margin structures; (4) credit terms negotiation linked to W145 credit limits.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Customer tier assessment**: for each active reseller, quarterly tier review — (a) system pulls trailing 12-month purchase volume per reseller; (b) compare actual volume to tier threshold: Bronze <PHP 5M, Silver PHP 5–15M, Gold PHP 15–30M, Platinum >PHP 30M; (c) if reseller has moved to a higher tier: notify B2B Sales Manager for upgrade — apply new tier pricing to all future orders; (d) if reseller has dropped below tier threshold: notify for downgrade review — Sales Manager assesses whether drop is temporary (e.g., customer between projects) or permanent; temporary drops get 1-quarter grace period before downgrade; (e) tier change documented in customer master per W508; (f) for new resellers: assign initial tier based on projected annual volume declared during onboarding per W145; re-assess after first 90 days | Credit Analyst / B2B Sales Manager | B2B Sales Manager | 15 min/reseller/quarter (~25 hours/quarter for ~100 resellers) |
| 2 | **Price list generation/confirmation**: monthly, B2B Sales Manager reviews wholesale price lists — (a) system generates updated wholesale price list by tier: SRP × (1 − tier discount %) for each active SKU in wholesale-eligible categories; (b) highlight SKUs with cost changes since last month (from W85 product costing) — verify that wholesale margin remains above minimum threshold per tier: Bronze minimum margin 8%, Silver 7%, Gold 6%, Platinum 5%; (c) highlight SKUs where wholesale price crosses a psychological price point (e.g., PHP 1,000, PHP 5,000) — minor adjustment may improve competitiveness; (d) flag SKUs where current promotional pricing (W13) is lower than wholesale tier pricing — apply "whichever is lower" rule or exclude from promo per category policy; (e) publish updated price list to B2B portal and punchout catalog per W283 | B2B Sales Manager / Finance Analyst | Category Manager | 2–3 hours/month |
| 3 | **Quotation creation**: Sales Representative creates B2B quotation in response to customer request — (a) customer requests quotation for specific items, quantities, and delivery requirements; (b) Sales Rep enters quotation in ERP: item lines with quantities, delivery address, requested delivery date; (c) system auto-prices items based on customer's assigned tier; (d) Sales Rep adds delivery terms: (i) ex-DC pickup (customer arranges transport — no delivery charge), (ii) BuildRight delivery to customer site (delivery charge based on distance and weight per W52/W62B rates), (iii) cross-dock to customer's designated location; (e) quotation validity: standard 30 days; for volatile commodities (cement, steel): 7 days; for government procurement: per bid validity requirement (typically 60–90 days); (f) Sales Rep reviews quotation totals and sends to customer via B2B portal or email | Sales Representative | B2B Sales Manager | 20–30 min per quotation |
| 4 | **Margin validation against threshold**: system performs real-time margin check as quotation is created — (a) for each line item: calculate margin = (quotation price − landed cost per W85) / quotation price; (b) compare to minimum margin threshold per tier; (c) for individual items below threshold: system flags line with warning — "Margin below minimum threshold for this tier"; (d) for total quotation margin: if overall margin is below 4% (floor margin), system blocks quotation submission and requires override approval; (e) margin report attached to quotation for approver visibility | System | — | Automated |
| 5 | **Approval for below-threshold pricing**: if quotation contains items below minimum margin threshold — (a) Sales Rep documents reason for below-threshold pricing: (i) competitive match (competitor offered lower price — attach evidence), (ii) strategic account retention (risk of losing customer to competitor), (iii) volume commitment (customer commits to higher volume in exchange for lower price), (iv) new customer acquisition (introductory pricing for first 3 orders), (v) government procurement (mandated pricing per bid requirements); (b) approval routing: below Bronze/Silver threshold → B2B Sales Manager approves; below Gold/Platinum threshold → Category Manager approves; any quotation with overall margin <4% → Category Manager + Finance Manager joint approval; (c) government procurement quotations: VP Merchandising approval required for all bids — pricing may be below normal thresholds per government contract economics (volume certainty, payment terms) | Sales Representative / B2B Sales Manager | Category Manager / VP Merchandising | 10–15 min per below-threshold quotation (plus approval wait time) |
| 6 | **Quotation validity management**: system tracks quotation lifecycle — (a) active quotations: not yet expired, not yet converted to order; (b) quotation expiry alerts: 5 days before expiry, system alerts Sales Rep to follow up with customer; (c) expired quotations: auto-close with "Lost — No Response" status if not converted; (d) quotation revision: if customer requests changes (quantity, items, delivery terms), Sales Rep creates revised quotation (versioned — V1, V2, V3); revised quotation resets validity period; (e) for volatile commodity quotations (7-day validity): system sends daily reminder to Sales Rep starting day 3 | System / Sales Representative | — | 5 min per follow-up |
| 7 | **Order conversion**: customer accepts quotation — (a) Sales Rep converts quotation to Sales Order in ERP; (b) system validates: quotation still within validity period, items still in stock at DC, customer credit limit not exceeded per W145; (c) if all validations pass: Sales Order created with quotation pricing locked (price protection for validity period); (d) if stock issue: system checks alternative DCs or suggests delivery split (partial now, balance on replenishment); (e) if credit limit exceeded: Sales Rep coordinates with Credit Analyst per W145 for temporary credit limit increase or split delivery; (f) converted quotation triggers fulfillment per W146 (bulk) or standard order fulfillment per W11/W19 | Sales Representative / System | B2B Sales Manager | 10–15 min per conversion |
| 8 | **Post-sale price variance analysis**: monthly, Finance Analyst reviews wholesale pricing performance — (a) average margin achieved by tier vs. target margin; (b) below-threshold quotation frequency and total margin impact; (c) quotation-to-order conversion rate by tier and by Sales Rep; (d) quotation win/loss analysis: reasons for lost quotations (price too high, competitor won, project cancelled, customer went direct to vendor); (e) price realization analysis: quoted price vs. invoiced price variance (identifies manual overrides at order stage); (f) report shared with B2B Sales Manager, Category Managers, and VP Merchandising | Finance Analyst | B2B Sales Manager / VP Merchandising | 3–4 hours/month |

**Total time per quotation**: ~30–45 min for standard quotation (creation + validation + delivery terms); ~45–60 min for below-threshold quotation (add approval process); ~90–120 min for government/project quotation (add pricing analysis + VP approval)

### System Touchpoints (W598 — Wholesale Pricing & Quotation)

- B2B quotation management module with tier-based auto-pricing and margin validation (WSL-003)
- Customer master integration for tier assignment and credit limit validation (W508, W145)
- Product costing integration for landed cost and margin calculation (W85)
- Wholesale price list management with tier discount tables (WSL-003)
- B2B portal and punchout catalog integration for price list publication (W283)
- Quotation-to-order conversion workflow with stock and credit validation (WSL-003)
- Promotional pricing integration for "whichever is lower" rule application (W13)
- Approval workflow with tiered routing based on margin threshold (GOV-008)
- Quotation analytics: conversion rate, win/loss, price realization (WSL-003)
- EWT computation integration for BIR 2307 certificate generation (FIN-010)
- Government procurement compliance per RA 9184 (W78)

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Commoditized pricing erodes wholesale margins**: cement, steel bars, plywood, and tiles are commodity products where resellers price-compete aggressively; BuildRight's wholesale margins on these categories are thin (5–8%) and subject to cost volatility (import costs, exchange rates per W144); mitigated by W85 landed cost tracking, monthly price list review, and clear minimum margin floors with approval gates
- **Government procurement pricing complexity**: RA 9184 bidding requirements mandate specific documentation (PhilGEPS registration, bid security, omnibus sworn statement); pricing must comply with Approved Budget for the Contract (ABC) ceiling; if BuildRight's bid is above ABC, it is automatically disqualified; mitigated by dedicated government procurement specialist per W78 and pre-bid pricing analysis
- **Quotation-to-order conversion rate is low**: estimated ~30–40% of quotations convert to orders — resellers multi-source and use quotations for price benchmarking rather than purchase commitment; high quotation volume with low conversion wastes Sales Rep time; mitigated by requiring reseller commitment indicators (e.g., deposit, LOI) for large quotations and tracking conversion rate by Sales Rep
- **Tier downgrade creates customer relationship friction**: resellers who are downgraded from Gold to Silver lose 5% discount and may threaten to switch to competitors; mitigated by grace period policy, proactive Sales Manager outreach before downgrade, and retention offers (project-based pricing to bridge volume gap)
- **Price list synchronization across channels**: wholesale prices in ERP, B2B portal, punchout catalog (W283), and Sales Rep mobile app can drift if updates are not synchronized; a reseller seeing different prices on the portal vs. the quotation vs. the invoice creates trust erosion; mitigated by single-source price master in ERP and automated push to all channels


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-document of "documented in customer master per W508" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "psychological price point" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-capture of "enters quotation in ERP" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-274 (ensure controlled execution — Wholesale Operations (PA-11.3).)
- operational: W85 landed cost tracking, monthly price list review, and clear minimum margin floors with approval gates; operational: dedicated government procurement specialist per W78 and pre-bid pricing analysis; operational: requiring reseller commitment indicators (e.g., deposit, LOI) for large quotations and conversion-rate tracking by Sales Rep

### Staffing Implication

- **B2B Sales Manager**: ~15–20 hours/month on pricing and quotation management (tier review, price list validation, below-threshold approvals, monthly review). Absorbed within existing role.
- **Sales Representatives**: ~20–30 min per quotation × ~100 quotations/month = ~40–50 hours/month across all Sales Reps. Distributed across ~3–4 Sales Reps = ~12 hours/Sales Rep/month.
- **Finance Analyst**: ~3–4 hours/month for post-sale price variance analysis. Absorbed within existing Finance team.
- **Category Manager**: ~2–3 hours/month for below-threshold quotation approvals. Absorbed within existing Merchandising team.
- **No incremental headcount** at current wholesale volumes (~100–150 active resellers).

### Time Estimate
Quotation stream 20–30 min per quotation × ~80–120/month ≈ 320–720 hours/year (plus below-threshold approvals 10–15 min each, validity follow-ups 5 min each, and conversions 10–15 min each at realized mix); quarterly tier assessments 15 min/reseller × ~100–150 resellers ≈ 100–150 hours/year; monthly price-list confirmation 2–3 hours ≈ 24–36 hours/year and price-variance analysis 3–4 hours ≈ 36–48 hours/year — total ≈ 480–960 hours/year (margin validation automated).

## W599. Wholesale Returns, Credit & Adjustment Processing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Reseller submits return request; quality issue identified post-delivery; pricing/quantity discrepancy on invoice; vendor-caused defect requiring chargeback |
| **Frequency** | ~40–60 wholesale returns/month; ~80–100 credit memos/month (including adjustments for pricing errors, quantity variances, and damaged goods) |
| **Volume** | B2B return rate ~1.5–2% of wholesale revenue (vs. ~3–5% for consumer retail); average wholesale return value PHP 15,000–50,000 per transaction (significantly higher than consumer returns due to bulk quantities); return reasons: quality defect (30%), wrong item delivered (20%), quantity discrepancy (15%), overstock/excess (15%), pricing error (10%), damaged in transit (10%) |
| **Owner** | B2B Sales Manager |
| **Participants** | Sales Representative, B2B Sales Manager, DC Receiving Supervisor (R for goods receipt), Quality Inspector, AR Clerk (R for credit memo), Tax Accountant (R for EWT adjustment), AP Clerk (R for vendor chargeback), Credit Analyst (I for account impact) |

### Background

W12 covers consumer retail returns and exchanges. W146 covers bulk fulfillment for wholesale. W70 covers credit/debit note aging management. W88 covers return-to-vendor (RTV) processing. W145 covers wholesale onboarding. However, no workflow covers wholesale returns, credit memos, and adjustments — which differ fundamentally from consumer returns in scale, process, and regulatory requirements.

Wholesale returns differ from consumer returns in several critical ways: (1) **Volume and value** — a single wholesale return may involve 500 bags of cement or 200 boxes of tiles, requiring DC receiving capacity and quality inspection resources far beyond a consumer return; (2) **Restocking feasibility** — bulk materials (cement, steel, lumber) may not be restockable if packaging is damaged or if the return occurs after extended storage at the reseller's warehouse; (3) **BIR credit note requirements** — Philippine BIR requires specific credit note documentation (BIR-registered credit note with serial number, reference to original invoice, reason for credit) for any reduction in previously invoiced sales; (4) **EWT implications** — if the original wholesale sale had 1% Expanded Withholding Tax deducted by the reseller (who issued BIR Form 2307), the credit memo must adjust the EWT amount, and the reseller must account for the reduced withholding; (5) **Vendor chargeback** — if the return is caused by a vendor quality defect, BuildRight must simultaneously issue a customer credit memo and initiate a vendor chargeback per W88/RTV, creating a three-way reconciliation.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Return request intake**: reseller submits return request via B2B portal, email, or Sales Rep — (a) reseller provides: original Sales Order/Invoice number, item codes, quantities, reason for return (quality defect, wrong item, quantity discrepancy, overstock, pricing error, damaged in transit), photos of defect/damage if applicable; (b) system validates: original invoice exists, return is within return policy window (wholesale return window: 30 days from delivery, except quality defects which have 90-day window), items match original invoice; (c) system creates Return Authorization (RA) request with unique RA number, linked to original invoice and Sales Order | Sales Representative / Reseller | B2B Sales Manager | 15–20 min per return request |
| 2 | **Return authorization**: B2B Sales Manager reviews and approves/denies RA request — (a) verify return reason and supporting evidence; (b) for quality defects: verify complaint is consistent with known quality issues for the SKU (cross-reference W110 supplier quality records); (c) for overstock returns: check if return is allowed under reseller agreement (some wholesale contracts limit overstock returns to 5% of purchases per quarter); (d) for pricing errors: verify original quotation vs. invoiced price — if pricing error confirmed, may process credit memo without physical return; (e) if approved: RA issued with return shipping instructions (reseller delivers to nearest DC) and expected credit memo amount (subject to inspection); (f) if denied: Sales Rep communicates denial reason to reseller with escalation option to the B2B Head of Trade / Account Management | B2B Sales Manager | — | 10–15 min per RA |
| 3 | **Goods receipt at DC**: reseller delivers returned goods to designated DC — (a) DC Receiving Supervisor checks returned goods against RA: item codes, quantities, packaging condition; (b) for quality defect returns: DC Supervisor does not accept goods into regular inventory — goods placed in quarantine/returns area per W219 pending quality inspection; (c) for overstock/wrong item returns (goods in resalable condition): DC Supervisor accepts into returns staging area for restocking assessment; (d) DC Supervisor records goods receipt in system: actual quantities received vs. RA quantities; (e) any quantity discrepancy (received less than authorized): documented, credit memo adjusted accordingly | DC Receiving Supervisor | DC Manager | 30–60 min per return receipt (bulk quantities require forklift and staging) |
| 4 | **Quality inspection**: for quality defect and damaged-in-transit returns — (a) Quality Inspector examines returned goods: verify defect matches described reason, assess if defect is manufacturing quality (vendor-caused), handling damage (BuildRight-caused), or customer-caused (reseller mishandling); (b) for vendor-caused defects: initiate vendor chargeback process (step 8) in parallel with customer credit memo; (c) for BuildRight-caused damage (occurred during DC picking or delivery): full credit to reseller, internal root cause investigation per W507; (d) for customer-caused damage: deny credit memo or apply restocking fee, Sales Rep negotiates with reseller; (e) Quality Inspector records findings in system with photos, classification, and recommendation (restock, refurbish, scrap, or RTV per W88) | Quality Inspector | Quality Manager | 15–30 min per return (depends on item complexity) |
| 5 | **Credit memo computation**: AR Clerk computes credit memo based on inspection results — (a) full credit: goods returned in resalable condition or BuildRight-caused defect — credit = original invoiced price × quantity returned; (b) restocking fee: for overstock returns in resalable condition — credit = original invoiced price × quantity × (1 − restocking fee %); restocking fee schedule: 5% for standard items, 10% for bulky items (cement, lumber, tiles), 15% for custom/special-order items; (c) partial credit: goods returned with minor damage but usable — credit = original price × quantity × condition discount (negotiated per inspection); (d) no credit: goods returned with customer-caused damage beyond resalability — reseller notified, goods disposed per W91; (e) all credits computed exclusive of VAT — credit memo will add VAT credit separately; (f) system auto-calculates EWT adjustment: if original sale had 1% EWT withheld by reseller, credit memo reduces the taxable base, and reseller must adjust their EWT records accordingly | AR Clerk | B2B Sales Manager | 15–20 min per credit memo |
| 6 | **BIR-compliant credit note issuance**: AR Clerk generates BIR-registered credit note — (a) system generates credit note with: BIR-registered serial number, BuildRight TIN, reseller TIN, original Sales Invoice number and date, original Sales Invoice amount, credit amount, VAT adjustment, reason for credit (BIR requires specific reason codes), credit note date; (b) credit note must be issued within the same month as the return or the following month to comply with BIR invoicing rules; (c) credit note printed on BIR-registered form or issued as BIR-registered electronic credit note (if BuildRight is authorized for e-invoicing per BIR Revenue Regulations); (d) credit note transmitted to reseller via B2B portal or email; (e) system updates AR subledger: credit memo applied against reseller's outstanding balance per W8 | AR Clerk / System | Controller | 10–15 min per credit note |

- **Three-way reconciliation for vendor-caused defects**: when a reseller return is caused by a vendor defect, the system must track three parallel transactions: (1) customer credit memo (selling price), (2) vendor debit note (purchase cost), and (3) margin loss accounting; if any of these are processed at different times or by different teams, reconciliation breaks down; mitigated by linking all three transactions to a single RA number in the system and weekly reconciliation by Finance Analyst

## W809. Wholesale Consignment Inventory Management & Settlement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Strategic reseller agreement includes consignment terms; new product launch requiring market seeding; seasonal inventory push to key reseller locations |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 consignment agreements active at any time; settlement cycles monthly or per agreement terms |
| **Volume** | ~5% of wholesale revenue (~PHP 935M annually) on consignment; primarily seasonal items (paint during summer, cement during construction peak), new product launches, and slow-moving inventory pushed to reseller shelves |
| **Owner** | B2B Sales Manager |
| **Participants** | Sales Representative, B2B Sales Manager, Reseller (R for sell-through reporting), Finance Analyst (R for settlement), AR Clerk (R for invoicing), DC Inventory Controller (R for consignment stock tracking) |

### Background

W145 covers reseller onboarding and W598 covers pricing and quotation management. W146 covers bulk fulfillment. However, no workflow addresses consignment inventory — a sales model where BuildRight places inventory at a reseller's location but retains ownership until the reseller sells the goods to their end customer. Consignment is a strategic tool for BuildRight to: (1) seed new products into reseller shelves without requiring the reseller to commit capital upfront, (2) push seasonal inventory to reseller locations closer to end customers (reducing BuildRight's DC carrying cost), and (3) maintain shelf presence for strategic brands against competitor encroachment.

Philippine wholesale consignment complexity includes: (1) BIR consignment rules — goods on consignment are not considered "sold" until the consignee sells them; VAT is triggered at the point of sale to the end customer, not when goods are transferred to the consignee; (2) BIR Form 2307 EWT implications — withholding tax applies when the consignee remits payment for goods sold, not when goods are transferred; (3) inventory ownership and insurance — BuildRight owns the consignment stock and bears the risk of loss, theft, or damage at the reseller's location; (4) physical count reconciliation — consignment stock must be counted periodically to verify sell-through reporting accuracy.

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |

## W810. Wholesale Backorder Management, Allocation & Customer Communication

### Background

W146 covers bulk fulfillment when stock is available. W598 covers quotation management. W31 covers general backorder processing for retail. ## W811. Wholesale Delivery Proof, Discrepancy Resolution & POD Reconciliation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Wholesale delivery completed; reseller reports quantity or quality discrepancy on delivery; POD document not returned; invoice-to-delivery mismatch identified during AR reconciliation |
| **Frequency** | ~50–100 wholesale deliveries/week across 4 DCs; ~10–15% of deliveries have some form of discrepancy (quantity, damage, wrong item) |
| **Volume** | ~2,500–5,000 wholesale deliveries/year; ~300–750 delivery discrepancies/year requiring resolution |
| **Owner** | B2B Sales Manager |
| **Participants** | Driver (R for POD), DC Dispatcher, Reseller Receiving Clerk, Sales Representative, B2B Sales Manager, AR Clerk, Quality Inspector (R for quality discrepancies), Finance Analyst (R for reconciliation) |

### Background

W146 covers bulk fulfillment delivery. W599 covers wholesale returns and credit memo processing. W62B covers 3PL transportation management. However, no workflow covers the critical middle ground — the delivery proof-of-delivery (POD) capture, quantity/quality discrepancy identification at point of delivery, and the reconciliation of delivery records with invoicing. This is especially important for wholesale because: (1) **High value per delivery** — a single wholesale delivery may be worth PHP 200,000–1,000,000, making even a small percentage discrepancy material; (2) **Bulk quantity verification difficulty** — counting 500 bags of cement or 200 steel bars at delivery is impractical; resellers rely on weight checks or tally sheet verification which have inherent error rates; (3) **POD as legal document** — the signed POD is the primary evidence for AR collection per W8 and insurance claims per W610; missing or disputed PODs delay invoicing and payment; (4) **Three-way match requirement** — wholesale invoicing requires match between Sales Order, Delivery Receipt/POD, and AR Invoice for BIR compliance.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Delivery and POD capture**: At point of delivery per W146: (a) driver presents Delivery Receipt (DR) to reseller receiving clerk; (b) reseller and driver jointly verify delivery: (i) check seal integrity on sealed shipments; (ii) verify pallet count and visual condition; (iii) for bulk commodities (cement, steel): weight check at reseller's scale or driver's scale ticket; (iv) for counted items: tally count against DR line items; (c) reseller notes any discrepancies on DR: quantity shortage, visible damage, wrong item, missing items; (d) if discrepancy found: driver and reseller both note discrepancy on DR with quantities, item codes, and description of issue; both sign the annotated DR; (e) if no discrepancy: reseller signs DR as "Received in good order and correct quantity"; (f) driver captures POD via mobile app: photo of signed DR, GPS coordinates, timestamp; (g) for resellers requiring BIR 2307 (EWT): driver collects signed BIR 2307 from reseller or notes that 2307 will be mailed | Driver / Reseller Receiving Clerk | DC Dispatcher | 20–45 min per delivery |
| 2 | **POD return and system recording**: (a) driver returns signed POD/DR to DC Dispatcher at end of delivery run (or POD transmitted in real-time via mobile app); (b) DC Dispatcher records POD in system: delivery confirmed, any discrepancies noted, signed DR scanned and attached to Sales Order; (c) system matches POD to Sales Order and triggers: if POD matches SO (no discrepancy): auto-trigger billing per step 5; if POD has discrepancy: route to discrepancy resolution per step 3; if POD not returned within 48 hours of delivery: system alerts DC Dispatcher and Sales Representative for follow-up | DC Dispatcher / System | DC Manager | 10–15 min per POD |
| 3 | **Discrepancy triage and classification**: Sales Representative and DC Dispatcher review delivery discrepancy: (a) quantity shortage: driver delivered less than DR quantity — (i) check DC pick records: was correct quantity picked? (ii) check loading tally: was correct quantity loaded? (iii) check driver route: any intermediate stops or evidence of pilferage? (iv) if DC error: acknowledge shortage, issue credit memo per W599 for undelivered quantity; (v) if transit loss/pilferage: file insurance claim per W610 and issue credit memo; (b) quantity excess: driver delivered more than DR quantity — (i) verify against Sales Order: if excess matches SO, update DR and bill for full SO quantity; (ii) if excess exceeds SO: arrange return pickup or treat as complimentary sample per W69; (c) damage during transit: (i) driver and reseller document damage with photos at delivery; (ii) if minor damage (packaging only, product intact): accept delivery, note condition, no credit needed; (iii) if significant damage (product unusable): reject damaged items at delivery, return to DC, credit memo per W599, insurance claim per W610; (d) wrong item delivered: (i) reseller rejects wrong items at delivery; (ii) wrong items returned to DC on same truck or next delivery; (iii) correct items shipped on priority basis; (iv) root cause analysis at DC picking per W262 | Sales Representative / DC Dispatcher | B2B Sales Manager | 30–60 min per discrepancy |
| 4 | **Unresolved POD follow-up**: For deliveries where POD is not returned or is disputed: (a) POD not returned within 48 hours: (i) DC Dispatcher contacts driver for POD; (ii) if driver cannot provide: Sales Representative contacts reseller to confirm delivery verbally; (iii) if reseller confirms delivery: system records verbal confirmation (weaker than signed POD but sufficient for billing); (iv) if reseller does not confirm: escalate to B2B Sales Manager for investigation; (b) POD disputed by reseller (reseller claims they did not receive, or received less than DR): (i) system pulls GPS record from driver mobile app and in-cab dashcam footage if available; (ii) system checks weight bridge records if applicable; (iii) DC pick and loading records reviewed; (iv) B2B Sales Manager mediates between driver, DC, and reseller; (v) if unresolved: insurance claim for loss, credit memo to reseller, driver disciplinary action if negligence determined | DC Dispatcher / Sales Representative / B2B Sales Manager | DC Manager | 1–3 hours per unresolved POD |
| 5 | **Three-way match and billing**: System performs three-way match before AR invoice generation: (a) Sales Order (what was ordered); (b) Delivery Receipt/POD (what was delivered and accepted); (c) AR Invoice (what will be billed); (d) if SO = POD: invoice generated for full SO amount — standard process; (e) if POD < SO (shortage): invoice generated for POD quantity only; remaining SO quantity either backordered per W810 or cancelled; (f) if POD > SO (excess): invoice adjusted per step 3b resolution; (g) if POD has quality discrepancy: invoice adjusted per step 3c resolution; (h) invoice transmitted to reseller via B2B portal with POD attachment for reseller's records; (i) system updates AR subledger per W8 with invoice and payment terms | System / AR Clerk | Controller | Automated + 10 min per discrepancy adjustment |
| 6 | **Monthly POD reconciliation and analytics**: Finance Analyst conducts monthly reconciliation: (a) POD return rate: % of deliveries with signed POD returned within 48 hours — target >95%; (b) discrepancy rate by type: quantity shortage, quantity excess, damage, wrong item; (c) discrepancy rate by DC, driver, and product category; (d) average time from delivery to invoice (billing lag) — target <3 business days for clean deliveries; (e) unresolved POD aging: deliveries without confirmed POD >30 days — escalate to B2B Sales Manager; (f) insurance claims filed per W610 for transit losses; (g) driver performance scorecard feed for repeat discrepancy drivers; (h) findings shared with B2B Sales Manager, DC Managers, and COO | Finance Analyst / B2B Sales Manager | COO | 3–4 hours/month |

**Total time per clean delivery**: ~30 min (POD capture 30 min + system recording 10 min + auto-billing automated). **Total time per discrepancy delivery**: ~2–3 hours (POD capture 45 min + triage 45 min + resolution 30–60 min + billing adjustment 10 min).

### System Touchpoints (W811 — Wholesale POD & Reconciliation)

- Driver mobile app with POD capture, photo, GPS, and timestamp (WSL-008)
- Delivery Receipt management module linked to Sales Order (WSL-008)
- Three-way match engine: SO vs. DR/POD vs. AR Invoice (FIN-004)
- DC pick and loading record integration for discrepancy root cause (WMS-003)
- Weight bridge integration for bulk commodity verification (WMS-005)
- Insurance claim module per W610 for transit loss and damage
- B2B portal for invoice and POD delivery to reseller (WSL-008)
- AR subledger per W8 for invoice posting and collection
- W599 credit memo integration for discrepancy-related credits
- W810 backorder integration for shortage-related backorder creation
- Driver performance scorecard feed
- Dashcam/GPS integration for disputed delivery evidence
- POD reconciliation analytics dashboard (FIN-004)

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Bulk quantity verification is inherently imprecise**: counting 500 bags of cement at delivery is not feasible; resellers rely on weight checks or visual pallet counts, both of which have error margins; a 2% error on 500 bags = 10 bags = ~PHP 2,500–4,000 discrepancy per delivery; mitigated by weight bridge records and pallet-level barcoding where feasible
- **POD not returned delays billing**: ~5–10% of PODs are not returned within 48 hours, delaying invoicing and creating unbilled AR; unbilled AR over 7 days requires manual follow-up and increases collection risk; mitigated by real-time mobile POD capture (step 1f) and 48-hour escalation (step 4a)
- **Driver-reseller collusion risk**: in rare cases, a driver may collude with a reseller to short-deliver and split the difference; mitigated by GPS tracking, sealed shipments, random DC-to-reseller physical audits, and driver rotation
- **Discrepancy resolution delays revenue recognition**: every unresolved discrepancy delays invoicing and revenue recognition; if 10–15% of deliveries have discrepancies and resolution takes 3–5 days, this creates a persistent unbilled receivable balance; mitigated by resolution SLA: standard discrepancies resolved within 2 business days, complex within 5 business days
- **Reseller disputes as payment delay tactic**: some resellers use delivery discrepancies (real or fabricated) to delay payment; a reseller disputing PHP 5,000 on a PHP 500,000 invoice may withhold the entire payment pending resolution; mitigated by partial payment agreement (reseller pays undisputed amount, disputed amount held in suspense per W8)

- System auto-validate of "check seal integrity on sealed shipments" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-scan of "scanned and attached to Sales Order" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-record of "Discrepancy triage and classification" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-274 (ensure controlled execution — Wholesale Operations (PA-11.3).)
- operational: weight bridge records and pallet-level barcoding where feasible; operational: real-time mobile POD capture (step 1f) and 48-hour escalation (step 4a); operational: GPS tracking, sealed shipments, random DC-to-reseller physical audits, and driver rotation

### Staffing Implication

- **DC Dispatcher**: ~10–15 min per POD recording × ~75 deliveries/week = ~15–20 hours/week. Absorbed within existing DC Dispatch role (may require dedicated dispatcher at high-volume DCs).
- **Sales Representatives**: ~30–60 min per discrepancy × ~50 discrepancies/month = ~25–50 hours/month across all Sales Reps. Absorbed within existing roles.
- **Finance Analyst**: ~3–4 hours/month for monthly reconciliation. Absorbed within existing Finance team.
- **B2B Sales Manager**: ~3–5 hours/month on dispute mediation and escalation. Absorbed within existing role.
- **No incremental headcount**.

### Time Estimate

- POD capture at delivery: 20–45 min per delivery
- POD return and system recording: 10–15 min per POD
- Discrepancy triage: 30–60 min per discrepancy
- Unresolved POD follow-up: 1–3 hours per case
- Three-way match and billing: automated + 10 min per adjustment
- Monthly reconciliation: 3–4 hours

## W972. Customer Franchise & Dealer Mini-Store Program Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Prospective franchisee or dealer applies to open a BuildRight-branded mini-store in an underserved location |
| **Frequency** | ~30–50 new applications/year; ~150 active mini-stores under management |
| **Volume** | 1 application per evaluation; ongoing management of active franchise/dealer network |
| **Owner** | VP Store Operations |
| **Participants** | Prospective Franchisee/Dealer, VP Store Operations, Legal, Finance, Merchandising, Supply Chain, IT, HR |

### Background

The Philippine retail landscape includes thousands of small, independent hardware stores (sari-sari hardware) in rural municipalities and urban neighborhoods where big-box retail is not economically viable. BuildRight's franchise and dealer mini-store program extends the brand's reach into these underserved markets through independently owned and operated small-format stores (100–300 sqm) that carry a curated assortment of 2,000–5,000 fast-moving SKUs sourced from BuildRight's supply chain. The mini-store model benefits both parties: (a) the franchisee/dealer gains access to BuildRight's brand, merchandising expertise, centralized procurement pricing, and supply chain; (b) BuildRight extends its geographic reach without capital investment in new big-box stores, captures incremental wholesale revenue, and builds brand awareness in growth markets that may eventually support full-format stores. The program targets municipalities with populations of 20,000–100,000 that are outside the trade area of existing BuildRight big-box stores. This workflow covers the full lifecycle: application, evaluation, agreement, setup, operations support, and periodic review.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Application Intake & Initial Screening**: Prospective franchisee/dealer submits application via: (a) BuildRight website franchise inquiry form; (b) referral from existing BuildRight store manager; (c) walk-in inquiry at any BuildRight store; application includes: applicant background and business experience, proposed location (address, municipality, population, distance to nearest BuildRight store), available capital/investment range, proposed store size, and market knowledge; VP Store Operations reviews application for initial eligibility: (a) proposed location is outside 15km radius of existing BuildRight store; (b) municipality population meets minimum threshold (20,000); (c) applicant has relevant business experience or willingness to undergo training; (d) applicant has minimum investment capacity (PHP 1–3 million for dealer; PHP 3–5 million for franchise) | VP Store Operations / Applicant | VP Store Operations | 30–60 min (review) |

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Credit risk**: Dealer model involves BuildRight extending wholesale credit; dealer default on payments creates bad debt per W81; credit evaluation at agreement execution, credit limits per W24, weekly payment monitoring, and credit insurance mitigate; dealer deposit requirement (10–20% of credit line) as security

- **Channel conflict**: Mini-stores located near BuildRight big-box stores may cannibalize big-box sales; strict 15km minimum distance rule and differentiated assortment (mini-stores carry only fast-moving SKUs; special orders routed to big-box) prevent cannibalization
