## W1645. Annual Strategic Planning Workshop & Strategy Refresh

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual strategic planning cycle (Q4 for following year) |
| **Frequency** | Annual (2-day offsite workshop) + quarterly strategy check-ins |
| **Volume** | 1 comprehensive annual cycle |
| **Owner** | CEO |
| **Participants** | CEO, CFO, COO, CIO, CMO, CHRO, VP Legal, VP Supply Chain, Strategy Analyst |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CEO initiates annual strategic planning cycle (October): (a) distributes strategy discussion paper covering: (i) current year performance vs. plan, (ii) market trends (Philippine retail, construction industry, consumer behavior), (iii) competitive landscape update, (iv) SWOT refresh, (v) strategic questions for discussion | CEO | — | 1 week prep |
| 2 | C-Suite prepares departmental strategy inputs: (a) COO — store expansion pipeline, operational efficiency initiatives, supply chain strategy, (b) CFO — financial performance analysis, capital allocation priorities, profitability improvement opportunities, (c) CMO — market positioning, ecommerce growth strategy, customer insights, (d) CIO — technology roadmap, digital transformation priorities, cybersecurity posture, (e) CHRO — organizational development, talent strategy, culture initiatives, (f) VP Supply Chain — supply chain resilience, vendor diversification, logistics optimization | C-Suite | CEO | 2 weeks prep |
| 3 | 2-day strategic planning workshop (offsite): (a) Day 1 — review current year performance; discuss market outlook; identify strategic themes for next 3 years; competitive analysis, (b) Day 2 — define next year strategic priorities (3–5 themes); set top-level financial targets (revenue, EBITDA margin, store count, ecommerce penetration); identify major initiatives and owners; agree on resource allocation principles | CEO / C-Suite | CEO | 2 days |
| 4 | CEO synthesizes workshop outcomes into Strategic Priorities Document: (a) strategic themes with measurable objectives, (b) top-level financial targets, (c) major initiatives with owners and timelines, (d) resource allocation framework (new stores vs. existing store improvement vs. technology vs. people) | CEO | Board | 1 week |
| 5 | Strategic Priorities Document presented to Board for alignment; Board provides feedback and approval; approved priorities cascade to annual budget process (W1646) | CEO | Board | 2 hours (Board meeting) |


### System Touchpoints
- Strategic planning collaboration workspace — strategy discussion paper distribution, C-Suite departmental input consolidation, and workshop output capture (steps 1–3)
- Strategic Priorities Document repository — themes, measurable objectives, top-level financial targets, initiatives with owners, and the resource-allocation framework cascading into W1646 (step 4)
- Board portal (links to VS-36) — priorities presentation, feedback, and approval (step 5)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Anchoring risk**: the workshop is dominated by the loudest voice rather than evidence; mitigated by the pre-read discussion paper and Day-1 grounding in performance, market, and SWOT data (steps 1, 3)
- **Cascade-decay risk**: Q4 priorities never reach next year's operating plans; mitigated by the explicit handoff — priorities cascade into the W1646 budget process with named initiative owners and the resource-allocation framework (steps 4–5)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-distribute of "distributes strategy discussion paper covering" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "technology roadmap, digital transformation priorities, cybersecurity posture" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "review current year performance" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-340 (ensure controlled execution — Annual Business Planning & Budgeting (PA-33.1).)
- operational: CEO review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
~4.5 weeks per annual cycle (CEO framing and distribution 1 week + C-Suite departmental inputs 2 weeks + 2-day offsite workshop + synthesis into the Strategic Priorities Document 1 week + Board presentation 2 hours) ≈ 180 hours/year, plus quarterly strategy check-ins.

## W1646. Annual Budget Preparation & Bottoms-Up Forecasting

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Board-approved strategic priorities from W1645 |
| **Frequency** | Annual (October–December for following year) |
| **Volume** | 1 consolidated budget covering 5 entities, 200 stores, 4 DCs, HQ |
| **Owner** | CFO |
| **Participants** | CFO, FP&A, all department heads, Store Operations, VP Supply Chain |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CFO distributes budget guidelines and templates: (a) top-level targets from strategic plan (revenue growth, EBITDA margin, store count), (b) budget assumptions: inflation rate (Philippine CPI ~4–6%), wage increase guidance (~5–8%), peso-dollar exchange rate assumption, interest rate assumption, (c) budget templates: revenue by store/category/channel, COGS by category, OpEx by department, capex by project, headcount by department, working capital assumptions | CFO | CEO | 1 week |
| 2 | Bottoms-up revenue forecasting: (a) existing stores: Category Managers forecast category-level revenue growth per store cluster based on historical trends, planned promotions, competitive activity, (b) new stores: Store Operations provides planned opening schedule and ramp-up revenue curve (Month 1: 50% of target; Month 3: 75%; Month 6: 90%; Month 12: 100%), (c) ecommerce: CMO forecasts ecommerce growth from ~3% to target penetration (~5%), (d) FP&A consolidates into total revenue forecast by entity, store, channel, and category | Category Managers / Store Operations / CMO / FP&A | CFO | 3 weeks |
| 3 | Cost budgeting: (a) COGS: Category Managers forecast category margin assumptions (commodity prices, vendor negotiations, private label expansion), (b) Store OpEx: Store Operations forecasts per-store operating costs (rent, utilities, maintenance), (c) DC/logistics cost: VP Supply Chain forecasts distribution cost per unit, (d) HQ OpEx: department heads forecast operating expenses, (e) Payroll: CHRO forecasts total compensation cost including planned wage increases and headcount changes, (f) IT: CIO forecasts technology spend (infrastructure, licenses, projects), (g) Marketing: CMO forecasts marketing spend by channel | All department heads | CFO | 3 weeks |
| 4 | FP&A consolidates all departmental budgets into enterprise model: (a) projected P&L by entity and consolidated, (b) projected balance sheet (working capital, capex, debt), (c) projected cash flow, (d) sensitivity analysis (best case, base case, worst case for key assumptions: revenue growth, inflation, exchange rate, interest rate) | FP&A | CFO | 2 weeks |
| 5 | CFO reviews consolidated budget; iterates with department heads on gaps: (a) budget vs. strategic target alignment, (b) margin improvement opportunities, (c) cost reduction options, (d) capex prioritization (if total capex request exceeds affordability) | CFO | CEO | 1 week |
| 6 | Final budget package prepared for Board approval per W1650 | CFO | CEO | 1 week |


### System Touchpoints
- Budgeting/planning module — guideline and template distribution with the centrally issued assumption set (inflation, wage, FX, interest) (step 1)
- Bottoms-up revenue forecast model — cluster/category growth, new-store ramp curves (Month 1 50% → Month 12 100%), ecommerce penetration (step 2)
- Departmental budget templates with enterprise consolidation — projected P&L/balance sheet/cash flow plus best-base-worst sensitivity (steps 3–4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Sandbagging risk**: departmental budgets pad costs and lowball revenue; mitigated by CFO iteration reviews against strategic targets, margin opportunities, and capex affordability (step 5)
- **Assumption-drift risk**: stale inflation/FX/wage assumptions bake error through the whole enterprise model; mitigated by the single centrally issued assumption set in the guidelines (step 1)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "CFO distributes budget guidelines and templates" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-schedule of "schedule and ramp-up revenue curve" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "logistics cost" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-340 (ensure controlled execution — Annual Business Planning & Budgeting (PA-33.1).)
- operational: CFO review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
11 weeks elapsed per occurrence (1 week + 3 weeks + 3 weeks + 2 weeks + 1 week + 1 week) — at Annual (October–December for following year) ≈ 1 occurrence/year.

## W1647. Store-Level Annual Target Setting & P&L Budget Allocation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual budget process (W1646) |
| **Frequency** | Annual (November–December) |
| **Volume** | 200 stores + 4 DCs |
| **Owner** | VP Store Operations |
| **Participants** | VP Store Operations, Regional Managers, Store Managers, FP&A |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | VP Store Operations and FP&A develop store-level P&L budget template: (a) revenue target by category (based on store size, location, maturity, competitive landscape), (b) gross margin target by category, (c) controllable expenses: payroll, utilities, maintenance, supplies, shrinkage, (d) non-controllable expenses: rent, allocated HQ costs | VP Store Operations / FP&A | CFO | 2 weeks |
| 2 | Revenue target allocation to stores: (a) base: current year run-rate with adjustments for: (i) new competitive openings/closures in trade area, (ii) category mix shifts, (iii) planned promotional calendar impact, (iv) maturity curve for stores < 3 years old, (b) stretch target: +5–10% above base for incentive compensation calculation | FP&A / VP Store Operations | CFO | 1 week |
| 3 | Regional Managers review store-level targets with Store Managers: (a) Store Manager provides local market intelligence (construction activity in area, competitor pricing, barangay events), (b) adjustments made for store-specific factors, (c) Store Manager commits to revenue and controllable expense targets | Regional Manager / Store Manager | VP Store Operations | 30 min/store |
| 4 | Store-level budgets consolidated into Store Operations P&L; VP Store Operations and CFO review and approve | VP Store Operations / CFO | CEO | 1 week |


### System Touchpoints
- Store P&L budget template — category revenue and margin targets with controllable vs. non-controllable expense split (step 1)
- Target allocation model — run-rate base with trade-area, mix, promo, and maturity-curve adjustments plus the +5–10% stretch overlay (step 2)
- Store commitment workflow — Regional Manager reviews, local-market adjustments, and Store Manager commitment capture (step 3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Target-gaming risk**: managers lobby for soft targets that distort incentive pools; mitigated by the model-driven base and stretch calibration with RM-chaired commitments (steps 2–3)
- **Local-context blindness risk**: cookie-cutter targets ignore construction activity and competitor realities in the trade area; mitigated by Store Manager local-intelligence input with documented adjustments (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-allocate of "allocated HQ costs" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "review store-level targets with Store Managers" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System rule-based auto-approval of "Store-level budgets consolidated into Store Operations P&L" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-230 (ensure strategy-to-budget alignment.)
- operational: VP Store Operations review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Annual cycle ~4 weeks of central effort (store P&L template development 2 weeks + revenue target allocation 1 week + consolidation and CFO review 1 week) ≈ 160 hours, plus Regional-Manager store-target reviews 30 min/store × 200 stores = 100 hours — total ≈ 260 hours/year.

## W1648. Capital Expenditure Budget Planning & Prioritization

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual budget process (W1646) |
| **Frequency** | Annual with quarterly reviews |
| **Volume** | ~10–15 new store builds + ~20–30 existing store renovation projects + IT capex + DC expansion |
| **Owner** | CFO |
| **Participants** | CFO, COO, CIO, VP Supply Chain, VP Store Operations, Board |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Department heads submit capex requests: (a) Store Operations — new store construction (~10–15/year × ~PHP 40–60M per store = ~PHP 400–900M), store renovations and refresh, (b) VP Supply Chain — DC expansion, automation equipment, fleet additions, (c) CIO — IT infrastructure, software licenses, development projects, (d) CHRO — office renovation, training facilities, (e) CFO — financial systems, office equipment | All department heads | CFO | 2 weeks |
| 2 | CFO and FP&A evaluate capex requests: (a) financial evaluation: IRR, NPV, payback period for each project, (b) strategic alignment: maps to approved strategic priorities per W1645, (c) affordability: total capex vs. available capital (retained earnings + debt capacity), (d) prioritization matrix: strategic importance vs. financial return | CFO / FP&A | CEO | 1 week |
| 3 | Capex prioritization review with CEO and COO: (a) mandatory capex (store openings committed in lease agreements, regulatory compliance, safety), (b) growth capex (new stores, DC expansion, technology), (c) maintenance capex (renovations, replacements), (d) discretionary capex (nice-to-have projects) | CFO / CEO / COO | Board | 1 day |
| 4 | Board approves annual capex budget with project-level authorization thresholds: (a) < PHP 1M: CFO approval, (b) PHP 1–10M: CEO approval, (c) PHP 10–50M: Board committee approval, (d) > PHP 50M: full Board approval | Board | — | Board meeting |


### System Touchpoints
- Capex request workflow — departmental submissions with justification and estimates (new stores ~PHP 40–60M each, renovations, IT, DC) (step 1)
- Investment appraisal model — IRR, NPV, payback per project with strategic-priority mapping (step 2)
- Prioritization matrix and authorization thresholds — mandatory/growth/efficiency tiers; CFO < PHP 1M, CEO PHP 1–10M, Board PHP 10–50M+ (steps 3–4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Pet-project bias risk**: capital flows by influence rather than returns; mitigated by financial evaluation of every request and tiered Board-level authorization thresholds (steps 2, 4)
- **Mandatory-capex squeeze risk**: compliance and safety capex deferred behind growth projects; mitigated by ring-fencing the mandatory tier (committed openings, regulatory, safety) ahead of growth capex (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-submit of "submit capex requests" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System rule-based auto-approval of "approved strategic priorities per W1645" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "Capex prioritization review with CEO and COO" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-230 (ensure strategy-to-budget alignment.)
- operational: CFO review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
~3.5 weeks per annual cycle (departmental capex submissions 2 weeks + CFO/FP&A evaluation 1 week + prioritization review with CEO/COO 1 day + Board approval) ≈ 140 hours/year across ~10–15 new stores, ~20–30 renovations, IT capex, and DC expansion, with quarterly reviews.

## W1649. Department Annual Operating Plan Development

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Budget approval and strategic priorities cascade |
| **Frequency** | Annual (December–January) |
| **Volume** | ~15 department operating plans |
| **Owner** | Department Heads |
| **Participants** | All department heads, C-Suite |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Each department head develops annual operating plan: (a) key initiatives (aligned to strategic priorities), (b) quarterly milestones and deliverables, (c) resource requirements (headcount, budget, technology), (d) risks and mitigation plans, (e) KPIs and targets, (f) cross-functional dependencies | Department Head | C-Suite sponsor | 1 week/department |
| 2 | Department heads present operating plan to C-Suite in 30-minute sessions; C-Suite reviews: (a) alignment to strategy, (b) resource feasibility, (c) cross-functional coordination requirements, (d) risk mitigation adequacy | Department Head | C-Suite | 30 min/department |
| 3 | Cross-functional dependencies identified and resolved: (a) new store opening timeline requires HR (staffing), IT (systems), Supply Chain (stocking), Marketing (grand opening), (b) ecommerce feature launch requires IT (development), Merchandising (product content), Marketing (promotion) | Department Heads / C-Suite | CEO | 2 days |
| 4 | Final operating plans documented and distributed; KPIs entered into performance management system | Department Heads | C-Suite | 1 week |


### System Touchpoints
- Operating plan template — initiatives aligned to strategic priorities, quarterly milestones, resource requirements (step 1)
- Plan review calendar — 30-minute C-Suite sessions checking alignment, feasibility, cross-functional dependencies (steps 2–3)
- KPI entry into the performance management system (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Silo-plan risk**: department plans optimize locally and collide cross-functionally (new-store timelines needing HR/IT/Supply Chain/Marketing); mitigated by dependency identification and resolution before finalization (step 3)
- **Shelfware-plan risk**: plans documented, distributed, never referenced again; mitigated by KPIs loaded into the performance management system feeding W1653 monthly reviews (step 4)

- System auto-log of "Each department head develops annual operating plan" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "Department heads present operating plan to C-Suite in 30-minute" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-capture of "entered into performance management system" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-340 (ensure controlled execution — Annual Business Planning & Budgeting (PA-33.1).)
- operational: Department Heads review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
9 days elapsed per occurrence (2 days + 1 week); cadence-carried steps as authored (1 week/department) — at Annual (December–January) ≈ 1 occurrence/year.

## W1650. Budget Approval, Board Presentation & Communication

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Final budget package completed (W1646) |
| **Frequency** | Annual (December Board meeting) |
| **Volume** | 1 consolidated budget |
| **Owner** | CFO |
| **Participants** | CFO, CEO, Board of Directors |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CFO prepares Board budget presentation: (a) executive summary: key targets, strategic initiatives, major changes from current year, (b) P&L summary: revenue, gross margin, OpEx, EBITDA, net income by entity and consolidated, (c) balance sheet and cash flow summary, (d) capex plan with project-level detail and ROI analysis, (e) sensitivity analysis (best/base/worst case), (f) key risks and mitigations, (g) headcount plan and payroll cost | CFO | CEO | 1 week |
| 2 | CEO and CFO present budget to Board; Board reviews and provides feedback; typical Board questions: (a) revenue growth assumptions vs. macroeconomic outlook, (b) margin improvement credibility (what specific initiatives drive margin improvement?), (c) capex return assumptions, (d) working capital management, (e) competitive risk, (f) contingency planning | CEO / CFO | Board | 2–3 hours |
| 3 | Board approves budget (with or without modifications); CFO distributes approved budget to department heads; department heads align operating plans to approved budget | Board / CFO | CEO | 1 week |
| 4 | Communication to organization: (a) CEO communicates strategic priorities and high-level targets to all employees (town hall or video message), (b) C-Suite communicates department-specific targets to their teams, (c) Store Managers receive store-level P&L targets per W1647 | CEO / C-Suite | — | 1 week |


### System Touchpoints
- Board presentation pack — executive summary, P&L, capex, cash flow, and risk sections (step 1)
- Board portal distribution and resolution tracking (links to VS-36) (steps 2–3)
- Budget distribution workflow — approved budget to department heads with operating-plan realignment (step 3)
- Organization-wide cascade channel — CEO town hall / video and C-Suite cascades (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Surprise-question risk**: the Board is blindsided by known risks and approval stalls; mitigated by anticipated-question preparation in the CEO/CFO presentation rehearsal (step 2)
- **Version-confusion risk**: departments execute against a superseded budget draft; mitigated by single-source distribution of the approved budget before operating-plan alignment (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "headcount plan and payroll cost" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "reviews and provides feedback" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System rule-based auto-approval of "Board approves budget" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-230 (ensure strategy-to-budget alignment.)
- operational: CFO review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
2–3 hours per occurrence (per-step effort 2–3 hours); elapsed timeline 1 week + 1 week + 1 week — at Annual (December Board meeting) ≈ 1 occurrence/year ≈ 2–3 hours/year.

## W1651. Mid-Year Budget Review & Recalibration

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Mid-year (June/July) or significant variance from budget |
| **Frequency** | Semi-annual (mid-year mandatory; ad-hoc as needed) |
| **Volume** | 1 comprehensive review |
| **Owner** | CFO |
| **Participants** | CFO, FP&A, C-Suite, department heads |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | FP&A prepares mid-year variance analysis: (a) actual vs. budget for H1 by entity, department, store cluster, (b) forecast for H2 based on H1 actuals and known changes, (c) full-year revised forecast vs. original budget, (d) variance drivers (volume, price, mix, timing, one-time items) | FP&A | CFO | 1 week |
| 2 | CFO reviews with each C-Suite member: (a) identify areas requiring corrective action (cost overruns, revenue shortfalls), (b) identify opportunities to reallocate budget from underperforming to overperforming areas, (c) assess if strategic priorities need recalibration based on H1 results | CFO | CEO | 2 days |
| 3 | If recalibration needed: (a) FP&A prepares revised full-year forecast, (b) specific budget reallocations documented and approved, (c) revised forecast communicated to department heads and Store Managers | CFO / FP&A | CEO | 1 week |
| 4 | Board communication: CFO presents mid-year results and revised forecast to Board at next scheduled meeting | CFO | Board | 1 hour |


### System Touchpoints
- Variance analysis engine — H1 actual vs. budget by entity/department/store cluster with H2 forecast from actuals plus known changes (step 1)
- Reallocation workflow — revised full-year forecast with documented, approved budget reallocations (step 3)
- Board reporting pack for the mid-year update (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Ratchet risk**: overruns are re-forecast instead of corrected; mitigated by the CFO/C-Suite review identifying corrective actions before any recalibration is prepared (step 2)
- **Forecast-padding risk**: the H2 forecast is inflated to protect incentive outcomes; mitigated by forecast construction from H1 actuals and known changes with CFO challenge (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-generate of "prepares mid-year variance analysis" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "reviews with each C-Suite member" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "documented and approved" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-230 (ensure strategy-to-budget alignment.)
- operational: CFO review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
FP&A ~1–2 weeks of build + CFO ~2 days of review per cycle (elapsed timeline 1 week + 2 days + 1 week per the step durations) — at Semi-annual (mid-year mandatory; ad-hoc as needed) ≈ 2 occurrences/year ≈ 2 hours/year.

## W1652. New Store Financial Model & Pre-Opening Budget Planning

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New store site approved per W20.1 (Site Selection & Lease Management) |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 new stores/year |
| **Volume** | 1 financial model per new store |
| **Owner** | FP&A |
| **Participants** | FP&A, VP Store Operations, COO, CFO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | FP&A builds new store financial model: (a) revenue projection by category (based on trade area demographics, competitive analysis, comparable store performance), (b) 5-year revenue ramp: Year 1 (70% of mature), Year 2 (85%), Year 3 (95%), Year 4+ (100%), (c) gross margin projection by category, (d) operating cost projection (rent, utilities, payroll for 29 staff, maintenance, supplies), (e) capex: construction, fixtures, equipment, POS, initial inventory, (f) working capital: initial stock investment, cash float | FP&A | CFO | 4 hours/store |
| 2 | Financial returns: (a) store-level IRR and NPV over 10-year lease period, (b) payback period (target: < 3 years), (c) EBITDA contribution at maturity, (d) sensitivity analysis: revenue -10%/+10%, rent escalation, wage increase | FP&A | CFO | 2 hours/store |
| 3 | CFO reviews and approves financial model; if returns meet threshold (IRR > 15%, payback < 3 years): proceed to construction per W20.2; if returns below threshold: COO renegotiates lease terms or site is rejected | CFO | CEO | 1 hour/store |
| 4 | Pre-opening budget: FP&A tracks cumulative pre-opening costs (construction, initial inventory, staff hiring/training, marketing/grand opening); pre-opening costs capitalized per PFRS and amortized over lease term | FP&A | CFO | Ongoing through opening |


### System Touchpoints
- New-store financial model — category revenue from trade-area demographics, competitive analysis, and comparable stores (step 1)
- Returns engine — store-level 10-year IRR/NPV, payback (target < 3 years), maturity EBITDA, sensitivity (step 2)
- Threshold approval gate — IRR > 15% / payback < 3 years routing to construction per W20.2 (step 3)
- Pre-opening cost tracker — construction, initial inventory, hiring/training, grand-opening marketing vs. cap (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Rosy-scenario risk**: optimistic ramp curves justify weak sites; mitigated by sensitivity analysis, comparable-store grounding, and the hard returns threshold (steps 2–3)
- **Pre-opening overrun risk**: cumulative pre-opening costs exceed the cap and erode first-year P&L; mitigated by FP&A tracking of cumulative spend against the cap (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "reviews and approves financial model" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-track of "tracks cumulative pre-opening costs" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-340 (ensure controlled execution — Annual Business Planning & Budgeting (PA-33.1).)
- operational: FP&A review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
~7 hours per new-store financial model (model build 4 hours + IRR/NPV returns over the 10-year lease 2 hours + CFO review 1 hour) × ~10–15 new stores/year ≈ 70–105 hours/year; pre-opening budget tracking ongoing through opening.
