## W1661. Competitor Store Visit Program & Systematic Data Collection

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly competitor visit calendar; new competitor store opening |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly structured visits + ad-hoc visits for new openings |
| **Volume** | ~20–30 competitor visits/quarter across key trade areas |
| **Owner** | Category Manager (merchandising focus); VP Store Operations (operations focus) |
| **Participants** | Category Managers, Regional Managers, Pricing Analysts, Store Managers, Strategy Analyst |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | VP Store Operations and VP Merchandising jointly plan quarterly competitor visit schedule: (a) prioritize competitors: (i) direct competitors (other hardware/home improvement chains), (ii) indirect competitors (general merchandise retailers with hardware sections, building supply dealers), (iii) emerging competitors (online-first building supply retailers), (b) assign visits to Category Managers, Regional Managers, and Store Managers, (c) provide standardized competitor visit form | VP Store Operations / VP Merchandising | — | 2 hours/quarter |
| 2 | Visitor conducts competitor store visit using standardized form: (a) **Merchandising assessment**: category breadth/depth, private label presence, new product introductions, display quality, pricing (select 20–30 benchmark SKUs across categories), promotional activity (sale events, bundles), (b) **Operations assessment**: store layout and flow, staffing levels, checkout speed, customer service quality, store cleanliness, technology (self-checkout, mobile POS), (c) **Customer assessment**: customer traffic (count 15-min sample), customer demographics observed, customer-employee interactions | Assigned visitor | VP Merchandising / VP Store Operations | 2 hours/visit |
| 3 | Visitor submits completed form to FP&A for compilation; data entered into competitive intelligence database | Visitor / FP&A | VP Merchandising | 30 min |
| 4 | FP&A consolidates visit data into quarterly competitive intelligence report: (a) pricing comparison (benchmark SKU basket price index vs. BuildRight), (b) assortment comparison (categories where competitors have deeper/broader range), (c) operational benchmarking (staffing, technology adoption), (d) competitive threat assessment by region | FP&A | VP Merchandising | 1 day |


### System Touchpoints
- Standardized competitor visit form — merchandising breadth/depth, private label, service, pricing assessment fields (step 2)
- Competitive intelligence database — visit submissions and the benchmark-SKU basket price index vs. BuildRight (steps 3–4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Snapshot-bias risk**: a single visit impression misrepresents the competitor's normal state; mitigated by the quarterly structured cadence and standardized form (steps 1–2)
- **Data-graveyard risk**: visits logged but never synthesized into decisions; mitigated by FP&A consolidation into the quarterly competitive intelligence report (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-schedule of "VP Store Operations and VP Merchandising jointly plan quarterly" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "Visitor conducts competitor store visit using standardized form" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-capture of "entered into competitive intelligence database" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-342 (ensure controlled execution — Competitive Intelligence & Market Analysis (PA-33.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
Quarterly cycle: visit planning 2 hours + visits 2 hours/visit × ~20–30 + form submission and compilation 30 min/visit + quarterly intelligence report 1 day ≈ 60–85 hours/quarter ≈ 240–340 hours/year.

## W1662. Market & Industry Trend Monitoring

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Ongoing monitoring cycle |
| **Frequency** | Weekly scan; quarterly deep-dive analysis |
| **Volume** | Continuous |
| **Owner** | CMO |
| **Participants** | CMO, Category Managers, FP&A, CEO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CMO monitors Philippine market trends weekly: (a) construction industry data (Philippine Statistics Authority building permits, construction spending), (b) real estate development pipeline (property portals, developer announcements), (c) retail industry reports (Philippine Retailers Association, Euromonitor, Nielsen), (d) consumer spending trends (BSP consumer expectations survey), (e) government infrastructure projects (DPWH, Build Build Build program updates), (f) remittance trends (overseas Filipino workers — drives home construction spending in provinces) | CMO | — | 2 hours/week |
| 2 | Category Managers monitor product-specific trends: (a) commodity price trends (steel, cement, copper — drives pricing strategy), (b) product innovation trends (smart home, sustainable materials, energy-efficient products), (c) import trade data (Philippine customs import statistics for key categories), (d) vendor market intelligence (new vendor products, supply disruptions) | Category Managers | VP Merchandising | 1 hour/week |
| 3 | Quarterly: CMO prepares Market Intelligence Briefing: (a) Philippine macro outlook (GDP growth, inflation, interest rates, construction sector growth), (b) retail market outlook (consumer confidence, retail sales growth, channel shift), (c) competitive landscape changes (new entrants, market exits, M&A activity), (d) emerging opportunities (product categories, geographic markets, customer segments), (e) emerging threats (economic slowdown, supply chain disruption, regulatory changes), (f) strategic implications and recommendations for BuildRight | CMO | CEO | 4 hours/quarter |


### System Touchpoints
- Market data feeds — PSA building permits, construction spending, real estate indicators scanned weekly (step 1)
- Commodity price trackers — steel, cement, copper trends feeding pricing strategy (step 2)
- Quarterly Market Intelligence Briefing pack — macro outlook, retail market, category implications (step 3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Signal-delay risk**: macro shifts are noticed only after strategy is locked; mitigated by the weekly scan cadence with escalation of material changes (step 1)
- **Data-without-implication risk**: trends reported with no connection to action; mitigated by the briefing format forcing category and pricing implications (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-monitor of "monitors Philippine market trends weekly" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-import of "import trade data" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-generate of "prepares Market Intelligence Briefing" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-342 (ensure controlled execution — Competitive Intelligence & Market Analysis (PA-33.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
Weekly scanning 3 hours (CMO market-trend monitoring 2 hours + category commodity-trend monitoring 1 hour) ≈ 156 hours/year; quarterly Market Intelligence Briefing 4 hours ≈ 16 hours/year.

## W1664. Competitive Pricing Intelligence & Benchmarking

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly pricing intelligence cycle |
| **Frequency** | Monthly competitive price collection; weekly for key promotional periods |
| **Volume** | ~500 benchmark SKUs priced across 5–10 competitor stores |
| **Owner** | Pricing Analyst |
| **Participants** | Pricing Analysts, Category Managers, FP&A Analyst, VP Merchandising |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pricing Analysts consolidate the weekly W2214 competitor-price feed (web-scraped, mystery-shop, and third-party prices) and run the monthly benchmark deep-dive: (a) in-store visits (5–10 competitor store locations of the 4–5 tracked chains, per W2214), (b) competitor websites and marketplaces (Lazada, Shopee for overlapping products), (c) mystery shopping calls for trade/B2B pricing, (d) record prices for ~500 benchmark SKUs (top items by revenue across categories) | Pricing Analysts | VP Merchandising | 2 days/month |
| 2 | FP&A calculates competitive price index: (a) BuildRight price vs. competitor average price per SKU (BuildRight price ÷ competitor average × 100), (b) index by category, (c) index by store cluster (prices may vary by region for some competitors), (d) price position: BuildRight target = index 95–105 (competitive parity) | FP&A | VP Merchandising | 1 day/month |
| 3 | Pricing Analyst flags price outliers: (a) SKUs where BuildRight is > 10% above competitor average → Category Manager review for price adjustment or value justification, (b) SKUs where BuildRight is > 10% below competitor average → potential margin improvement opportunity, (c) new competitor promotional pricing → match-or-beat assessment per W40 (Pricing & Promotions) | Pricing Analyst | Category Manager | 2 hours/month |


### System Touchpoints
- Competitive price collection workflow — in-store visits and competitor websites/marketplaces covering ~500 benchmark SKUs (step 1)
- Price-index calculator — BuildRight price ÷ competitor average by SKU and category (step 2)
- Outlier flagging — SKUs > 10% above or below competitor average (step 3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Basket-bias risk**: an unrepresentative benchmark basket skews the index and misleads pricing; mitigated by maintaining the benchmark SKU set across all key categories (steps 1–2)
- **Reactionary-pricing risk**: knee-jerk matching to every competitor price destroys margin; mitigated by Category Manager review choosing adjustment or value justification (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "record prices for ~500 benchmark SKUs" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-calculate of "calculates competitive price index" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "review for price adjustment or value justification" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-342 (ensure controlled execution — Competitive Intelligence & Market Analysis (PA-33.3).)
- operational: Pricing Analyst review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Monthly collection cycle ~26 hours (competitive price collection 2 days + price-index calculation 1 day + outlier flagging 2 hours) ≈ 312 hours/year across ~500 benchmark SKUs at 5–10 competitor stores, moving to weekly collection during key promotional periods.

## W1665. New Market Entry & Store Location Feasibility Analysis

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Expansion pipeline review (quarterly) or specific site opportunity |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 analyses/year |
| **Volume** | 1 per prospective store location |
| **Owner** | VP Store Operations |
| **Participants** | VP Store Operations, COO, FP&A, CMO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | COO identifies prospective site; VP Store Operations initiates feasibility analysis: (a) trade area definition (15 km radius for provincial; 5 km for Metro Manila), (b) population and household count within trade area, (c) income demographics (PSA census data, LGU data), (d) competitive landscape (existing competitors within trade area, planned competitor openings), (e) construction activity indicators (building permits, new subdivisions, commercial developments), (f) accessibility (main road frontage, public transport, parking), (g) proximity to existing BuildRight stores (cannibalization risk if < 15 km from existing store) | VP Store Operations / COO | COO | 1 week |
| 2 | FP&A builds financial model per W1652 (New Store Financial Model) with location-specific assumptions | FP&A | CFO | 4 hours |
| 3 | VP Store Operations and FP&A present feasibility analysis to COO and CFO: (a) recommendation: proceed, hold, or reject, (b) financial returns, (c) strategic fit, (d) risks (competitive, demographic, infrastructure) | VP Store Operations / FP&A | COO / CFO | 1 hour |
| 4 | COO and CFO present to CEO for go/no-go; CEO approves sites meeting financial thresholds | COO / CFO / CEO | Board (if capex > PHP 50M) | 30 min |


### System Touchpoints
- Trade-area analysis dataset — radius definition (15 km provincial / 5 km Metro), demographics, competitor mapping (step 1)
- Feasibility model per W1652 with location-specific assumptions (step 2)
- Go/no-go decision pack — recommendation, returns, strategic fit, risks (steps 3–4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Cannibalization risk**: the new store draws sales from existing BuildRight stores; mitigated by trade-area overlap analysis in the feasibility stage (step 1)
- **Threshold-exception risk**: emotionally favored sites approved despite sub-threshold returns; mitigated by CEO go/no-go gated on the financial thresholds (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "count within trade area" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-reject outside tolerance of "VP Store Operations and FP&A present feasibility analysis to" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System rule-based auto-approval of "approves sites meeting financial thresholds" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-342 (ensure controlled execution — Competitive Intelligence & Market Analysis (PA-33.3).)
- operational: VP Store Operations review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
5.5 hours per occurrence (per-step effort 4 hours + 1 hour + 30 min); elapsed timeline 1 week — at ~10–15 analyses/year ≈ 10–15 occurrences/year ≈ 55–82.5 hours/year.

## W1666. Philippine Retail Regulatory & Policy Impact Assessment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New legislation, regulatory change, or government policy announcement |
| **Frequency** | Ad-hoc (as regulatory changes occur) |
| **Volume** | ~5–10 significant regulatory assessments/year |
| **Owner** | VP Legal & Compliance |
| **Participants** | VP Legal, Compliance Officer, CFO, CIO, CHRO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | VP Legal monitors regulatory landscape: (a) pending legislation affecting retail (e.g., Retail Trade Liberalization Act amendments, foreign ownership rules), (b) BIR revenue regulations affecting tax compliance (e.g., e-invoicing mandates), (c) DOLE labor regulations (wage orders, employment classification rules), (d) LGU zoning and business permit changes, (e) DTI consumer protection regulations, (f) NPC data privacy regulations and enforcement trends | VP Legal | — | 2 hours/week |
| 2 | For significant regulatory changes: VP Legal prepares impact assessment: (a) description of change and effective date, (b) affected business areas (which departments, processes, systems), (c) compliance requirements (what needs to change), (d) financial impact (cost of compliance, potential penalties for non-compliance), (e) implementation timeline and resource needs, (f) risk rating (high/medium/low) | VP Legal | CEO | 4 hours/assessment |
| 3 | VP Legal and affected C-Suite member present impact assessment to CEO; agree on response: (a) comply — allocate budget and resources for implementation, (b) influence — engage industry association (Philippine Retailers Association) or government consultation to advocate for BuildRight interests, (c) monitor — track regulatory development without immediate action | VP Legal / C-Suite | CEO | 1 hour/assessment |
| 4 | Implementation: affected department head executes compliance plan with Legal oversight; progress tracked per W1660 | Department Head | C-Suite sponsor | Per assessment |


### System Touchpoints
- Regulatory monitoring feed — pending legislation, BIR/DENR/DTI/LGU rule changes (step 1)
- Impact assessment template — change description, effective date, affected areas, cost, response options (step 2)
- Compliance-plan tracker per W1660 with Legal oversight (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Late-awareness risk**: rules take effect before BuildRight adapts, forcing rushed retrofits; mitigated by continuous monitoring with assessment triggered on announcement (step 1)
- **Comply-vs-influence misjudgment**: defaulting to costly compliance where industry influence was viable; mitigated by the CEO decision explicitly weighing influence/association engagement (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-monitor of "monitors regulatory landscape" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-generate of "prepares impact assessment" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-track of "track regulatory development without immediate action" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-342 (ensure controlled execution — Competitive Intelligence & Market Analysis (PA-33.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
recurring effort 2 hours/week ≈ 104 hours/year; cadence-carried steps as authored (Per assessment).

## W1667. Competitive Intelligence Quarterly Briefing & Strategy Input

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly competitive intelligence cycle |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly |
| **Volume** | 1 comprehensive briefing |
| **Owner** | CMO |
| **Participants** | CMO, VP Merchandising, VP Store Operations, CEO, C-Suite |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CMO compiles quarterly competitive intelligence briefing from: (a) competitor store visit data (W1661), (b) market trend analysis (W1662), (c) customer research insights (W1663), (d) pricing intelligence (W1664) | CMO | CEO | 2 days |
| 2 | Briefing content: (a) competitive landscape summary: new entrants, exits, expansions, format changes, (b) competitor strategy analysis: pricing strategy, assortment strategy, channel strategy, customer acquisition strategy, (c) BuildRight competitive position: market share estimate (where data available), price index trend, customer perception vs. competitors, (d) emerging opportunities and threats, (e) strategic recommendations for BuildRight response | CMO | CEO | — |
| 3 | CMO presents briefing to CEO and C-Suite: (a) discuss competitive implications for BuildRight strategy, (b) identify 3–5 strategic actions (e.g., launch private label in category where competitor is strong, increase promotional frequency in region where competitor is expanding, accelerate ecommerce features where competitor is weak), (c) actions integrated into initiative tracker per W1660 | CMO / CEO | CEO | 2 hours |


### System Touchpoints
- CI synthesis pack — competitor visits (W1661), market trends (W1662), customer research (W1663), pricing intelligence (W1664) (step 1)
- Strategic-action tracking — 3–5 actions per briefing routed into W1660 (step 3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Competitor-blindside risk**: a competitor format change or price war is noticed late; mitigated by the quarterly cadence with immediate escalation between cycles (step 1)
- **Briefing-without-action risk**: intelligence is consumed but never acted on; mitigated by the mandatory 3–5 strategic actions with owners and timeline (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-compile of "compiles quarterly competitive intelligence briefing" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-track of "tracker per W1660" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-342 (ensure controlled execution — Competitive Intelligence & Market Analysis (PA-33.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
2 hours per occurrence (per-step effort 2 hours); elapsed timeline 2 days — at Quarterly ≈ 4 occurrences/year ≈ 8 hours/year.
