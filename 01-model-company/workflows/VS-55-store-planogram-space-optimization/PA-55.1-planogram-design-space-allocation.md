## W2166. Seasonal Planogram Refresh

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Seasonal calendar trigger (6 rotations/year) |
| **Frequency** | Bi-monthly; 200 stores × 8 zones |
| **Volume** | ~1,600 planogram updates per rotation |
| **Owner** | Merchandising Planner |
| **Participants** | Merch Planner, Category Mgr, VP Store Operations |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Planner reviews category performance (sales/sqm, margin/sqm, turns — from W2169) and designs the seasonal planogram with space-planning software assigning linear meterage per category | Merchandising Planner | Category Manager | 2–3 days |
| 2 | Category Manager validates the assortment alignment and cross-merchandising (links to W2168); VP Store Operations reviews the format-specific adaptations for large/standard/compact stores (the planogram must flex to the 8,000–15,000 sqm range) | Category Mgr / VP Store Operations | VP Merchandising | 4–6 hours |
| 3 | System generates the store-specific planogram packages (one per store-cluster format); distributes to stores via the VS-63 communication system with the compliance-photo requirement | System | Merchandising Planner | Automated |

### System Touchpoints
- Space-planning / planogram module (links to VS-29 / W314 planogram template master) — the design tool and the versioned planogram
- Analytics (links to VS-27 / W113, W2169) — the performance basis for the space allocation
- Store communication (links to VS-07 / W571 HQ→store comms) — the planogram-package distribution and compliance-photo return

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Format-mismatch risk**: a single planogram doesn't fit the format range (a planogram designed for a 15,000 sqm store overcrowds an 8,000 sqm store); mitigated by the store-cluster format variants in step 2 (3–4 format tiers, not one-size)
- **Late-arrival risk**: the planogram lands at the store after the seasonal merchandise, so the merchandise sits in the backroom unbuilt; mitigated by the planogram-package distributed 2 weeks ahead of the merchandise reset wave

### Automation Opportunity
- Store-cluster planogram variants auto-generated from the master planogram + cluster dimensions; compliance-photo diff (store's build vs. the plan) auto-scored

### Controls
- CTL-394 (ensure controlled execution — Planogram Design & Category Space Allocation (PA-55.1).)
- operational: Category Manager sign-off on each rotation's planogram before distribution

### Time Estimate
~2.5 days Planner + ~5 hours Category Mgr/Store Ops × 6 rotations + ~3 hours/store reset execution × 200 stores = **~4,000–5,000 hours/year** distributed across Planner, Category Mgrs, and Store teams (Store reset labor is the bulk, absorbed into store-ops).

## W2167. New Product Shelf Placement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New SKU introduction (~200–300/month) |
| **Frequency** | ~200–300 placement decisions/month |
| **Volume** | ~200–300/month |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Category Mgr, Merch Planner, Buyer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Buyer submits the new SKU with dimensions, category, expected velocity (A/B/C classification per the ABC analysis); Planner evaluates the available shelf space and the optimal position (eye-level for A-items, etc.) | Buyer / Merch Planner | Category Manager | 45 min |
| 2 | Planner assigns the position, facing count, minimum shelf quantity; determines the displaced SKU (the C-item that loses facing to make room); system pushes the update to stores with the shelf labels (links to W181 price-tag printing) | Merchandising Planner | Category Manager | 30 min |

### System Touchpoints
- Planogram module (links to VS-29 / W314) — the shelf-position assignment
- Item master (links to VS-29 / W252, W1 SKU lifecycle) — the new SKU's dimensions and classification
- Shelf-label/price-tag (links to VS-08 / W181) — the label generation triggered by the placement

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Velocity-misestimate risk**: a new SKU is placed as an A-item (prime eye-level space) but sells as a C-item, wasting prime space; mitigated by the W2460 new-vendor/new-SKU baseline monitoring that re-tiers the placement after 90 days of actual velocity
- **Displacement-blindness risk**: the displaced C-item is silently delisted from the shelf (still in assortment but no facing), creating an unfulfillable assortment promise; mitigated by the displaced-SKU report and the delist-or-relocate decision recorded

### Automation Opportunity
- Placement auto-suggested from the SKU dimensions + velocity class + available facing; displaced-SKU auto-flagged for the delist-or-relocate decision

### Controls
- CTL-394 (ensure controlled execution — Planogram Design & Category Space Allocation (PA-55.1).)
- operational: Category Manager sign-off on the placement

### Time Estimate
~75 min/decision × ~250 placements/month = **~300 hours/month ≈ ~3,600 hours/year** Planner + Buyers (absorbed into the buying/planning role).

## W2168. Cross-Merchandising Display Planning

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Seasonal promotion requiring cross-merch display |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 displays per rotation |
| **Volume** | ~2,000 implementations per rotation |
| **Owner** | Merchandising Planner |
| **Participants** | Merch Planner, Category Mgr, Marketing |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Planner identifies the cross-merch opportunities from the POS basket-analysis (a bathroom set, an electrical-starter corner, a garden-starter endcap — the complementary categories that co-purchase); designs the display layout with fixtures and signage | Merchandising Planner | Category Manager | 6–8 hours |
| 2 | Marketing designs the POP materials; coordinates the print for 200 stores; Store Ops communicates the display requirements with the compliance-photo return | Marketing / Store Ops | VP Marketing | 3–5 days |

### System Touchpoints
- Basket analytics (links to VS-27 / W113, VS-13 CRM) — the co-purchase analysis driving cross-merch selection
- Planogram/space (links to VS-29 / W314) — the cross-merch display as a planogram element
- Marketing/POP (links to VS-14 / W262 promotional setup, W190 in-house design) — the POP materials

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Forced-cross-merch risk**: a cross-merch display is built on a weak co-purchase signal (the categories don't actually basket together), wasting the space; mitigated by the basket-analysis evidence requirement in step 1
- **POP-store-mismatch risk**: the POP materials arrive at a store that hasn't built the display (or vice versa), creating an incoherent customer experience; mitigated by the synchronized distribution (POP and planogram-package in the same wave)

### Automation Opportunity
- Basket-analysis auto-surfaces the top cross-merch opportunities per season; compliance-photo auto-matched to the display spec

### Controls
- CTL-394 (ensure controlled execution — Planogram Design & Category Space Allocation (PA-55.1).)
- operational: Marketing + Planner joint sign-off on each cross-merch display

### Time Estimate
~7 hours Planner + ~4 days Marketing × ~12 displays/rotation × 6 rotations = **~400–500 hours/year** Planner + Marketing.

## W2169. Space Productivity Analysis

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly analytics cycle |
| **Frequency** | Monthly; 200 stores × 8 zones |
| **Volume** | ~1,600 zone-level analyses/month |
| **Owner** | Analytics Manager |
| **Participants** | Analytics Manager, Category Mgr, Merch Planner |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System calculates sales/sqm, margin/sqm, turns/sqm per zone per store (the productivity metrics that drive the W2166 space allocation); identifies the top-performing and underperforming zones and stores | System / Analytics Manager | Category Manager | 2–3 hours |
| 2 | Category Manager reviews the underperformers; Planner adjusts the planogram for the next rotation (expand high-productivity zones, reduce or restructure low-productivity zones) | Category Mgr / Merch Planner | VP Merchandising | 4–6 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- Analytics/BI (links to VS-27 / W113, W208 retail analytics) — the productivity dataset
- Planogram/space (links to VS-29 / W314) — the zone and sqm definitions
- Store-P&L (links to VS-17 / W26) — the margin input

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Productivity-without-context risk**: a zone shows low sales/sqm but serves a strategic purpose (a loss-leader destination category that draws traffic to adjacent high-margin zones); mitigated by the Category Manager judgment in step 2, not a pure productivity-driven cut
- **New-store-noise risk**: a new store's zones show artificially low productivity (ramp-up) and get cut prematurely; mitigated by the ramp-up filter (exclude stores <12 months from the cut decision)

### Automation Opportunity
- Productivity dashboard auto-refreshed monthly with rank-order; underperformer auto-flagged with the ramp-up filter applied

### Controls
- CTL-394 (ensure controlled execution — Planogram Design & Category Space Allocation (PA-55.1).)
- Operational: Analytics Manager methodology sign-off; Category Manager review of any zone-cut decision (strategic-purpose check)

### Time Estimate
~2.5 hours/month Analyst + ~5 hours/month Category Mgr/Planner = **~7.5 hours/month ≈ ~90 hours/year** distributed across the analytics and merch teams.

## W2170. Planogram Version Control

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Every planogram change |
| **Frequency** | Continuous; ~500–800 changes/month |
| **Volume** | ~500–800 version updates/month |
| **Owner** | Merchandising Planner |
| **Participants** | Merch Planner, IT, Store Ops |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System auto-versions every planogram change (date, user, zones affected, SKUs added/removed, reason); Planner reviews the change log weekly; a 24-month history is maintained per store for audit and rollback | System / Merch Planner | IT System Admin | 1–2 hours/week |

### System Touchpoints
- Planogram module (links to VS-29 / W314) — the version-control engine
- Document/audit (links to VS-88 / W255 document storage) — the 24-month retained history
- IT change management (links to VS-27 / W132) — bulk planogram changes released via the change pipeline

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Unversioned-change risk**: an off-process change (a Store Manager locally rearranges a zone) isn't versioned, so the master planogram and the store diverge; mitigated by the W86 planogram-compliance audit that detects the divergence and the no-local-change policy
- **History-loss risk**: the 24-month history is purged prematurely, losing the audit trail for a later-disputed decision; mitigated by the retention setting enforced at the system level (not a manual purge)

### Automation Opportunity
- Change-auto-versioned at the planogram-module level (no manual versioning); change-diff auto-generated for the weekly review

### Controls
- CTL-394 (ensure controlled execution — Planogram Design & Category Space Allocation (PA-55.1).)
- operational: Planner weekly change-log review sign-off

### Time Estimate
~1.5 hours/week Planner = **~75 hours/year**, absorbed into the planning routine.

## W2171. Store Layout Zone Optimization

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual review or new store design |
| **Frequency** | Annual per store; ~10–15 new stores/year |
| **Volume** | ~10–15 new + 20–30 optimizations/year |
| **Owner** | Store Design Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Design Mgr, VP Store Operations, VP Merch |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Store Design Manager analyzes the traffic flow, dwell time, and conversion-by-zone (where sensor or POS-heatmap data exists); designs the optimized layout with expanded high-conversion zones and contracted low-conversion zones | Store Design Manager | COO | 3–5 days |
| 2 | VP Merchandising and COO review and approve with the expected ROI (links to W2173); scheduled for a low-sales period to minimize disruption | VP Merch / COO | CEO | 2–3 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- Store-design / CAD (links to VS-109 store remodel, VS-20 real-estate) — the layout design tool
- Analytics (links to VS-27 / W113, W2169) — the traffic/dwell/conversion data
- Capex (links to VS-40 / W21 capex) — major layout changes are capex, not opex

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Disruption-during-execution risk**: a layout optimization during peak season disrupts sales more than the optimization recovers; mitigated by the low-sales-period scheduling in step 2
- **Sensor-blindness risk**: the optimization relies on traffic/dwell data that only exists for sensor-equipped stores, so non-sensor stores get a less-informed design; mitigated by the sensor-store results applied as a template to comparable non-sensor stores

### Automation Opportunity
- Traffic-heatmap auto-generated from sensor data; layout-variant auto-proposed from the productivity analysis

### Controls
- CTL-394 (ensure controlled execution — Planogram Design & Category Space Allocation (PA-55.1).)
- CTL-394 (capex-approval — links to VS-40 / W21 for major layout changes); operational: COO + VP Merch sign-off on the layout and the execution timing

### Time Estimate
~4-day elapsed window per optimization (analysis + leadership review ≈ 4.5–6 hours active) × ~30 optimizations/year ≈ **~140–180 hours/year** Store Design Manager + leadership.

## W2172. Category Adjacency Planning

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly review cycle — Category Adjacency Planning |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly; 13 categories |
| **Volume** | ~13 adjacency reviews/quarter |
| **Owner** | Merchandising Planner |
| **Participants** | Merch Planner, Category Mgr, Store Design Mgr |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Planner reviews the adjacency map and the cross-category purchase patterns from POS basket data (which categories co-purchase and should be adjacent); proposes the changes for logical complementary placement (e.g., tiles adjacent to thin-set/grout; paint adjacent to brushes/rollers) | Merchandising Planner | Category Manager | 6–8 hours |
| 2 | Category Manager approves; incorporates into the next seasonal planogram refresh (W2166) so the adjacency change lands in a coordinated reset, not piecemeal | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1 hour |

### System Touchpoints
- Basket analytics (links to VS-27 / W113, VS-13) — the co-purchase signal
- Planogram module (links to VS-29 / W314) — the adjacency map
- Space-planning (links to W2166) — the adjacency change implemented in the refresh

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Physical-constraint risk**: the co-purchase-optimal adjacency is physically impossible (the two categories are on opposite walls due to the store footprint); mitigated by the Store Design Manager's input on feasibility before approval
- **Adjacency-churn risk**: changing adjacencies every quarter destabilizes the customer's mental model of the store; mitigated by limiting adjacency changes to material co-purchase shifts, not minor ones

### Automation Opportunity
- Co-purchase heatmap auto-generated from basket data; adjacency-change-impact auto-estimated from the prior cycle

### Controls
- CTL-394 (ensure controlled execution — Planogram Design & Category Space Allocation (PA-55.1).)
- Operational: Category Manager + Store Design Manager joint sign-off on adjacency changes

### Time Estimate
~7 hours/quarter Planner + ~1 hour/quarter Category Mgr × 13 categories = **~80 hours/year**.

## W2173. Planogram Cost-Benefit Analysis

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Bi-monthly (seasonal rotations) |
| **Frequency** | 6 analyses/year |
| **Volume** | 6 analyses/year |
| **Owner** | FP&A Analyst |
| **Participants** | FP&A, Merch Planner, Category Mgr |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | FP&A calculates the change cost (reset labor, labels, fixtures, signage) vs. the projected benefit (sales uplift, margin improvement, inventory-cost reduction from better space allocation) | FP&A Analyst | Finance Manager | 6–8 hours |
| 2 | Post-rotation: measures the actual vs. projected uplift; incorporates the learning into the next projection model so the cost-benefit estimates improve cycle-over-cycle | FP&A Analyst | VP Merchandising | 2–3 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- FP&A / cost accounting (links to VS-17 / W26 budget, VS-158 / W85) — the cost and benefit inputs
- Analytics (links to VS-27 / W113, W2169) — the actual-uplift measurement
- Planogram history (links to VS-29 / W314, W2170) — the version that's being evaluated

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Attribution-noise risk**: the post-rotation uplift is confounded by other changes (a promo ran during the same period, a competitor closed nearby); mitigated by the controlled comparison where possible (stores that got the change vs. a holdout cluster)
- **Projection-optimism risk**: the projected benefit is consistently overstated, eroding trust in the cost-benefit model; mitigated by the post-actual reconciliation in step 2 and the model recalibration

### Automation Opportunity
- Cost-benefit model auto-assembled from the planogram change + labor standard; post-actual auto-reconciled with variance flagging

### Controls
- CTL-394 (ensure controlled execution — Planogram Design & Category Space Allocation (PA-55.1).)
- CTL-394 (budget/forecast accuracy — links to VS-17 / W26); operational: Finance Manager sign-off on the methodology

### Time Estimate
~9 hours/rotation × 6 rotations = **~50–60 hours/year** FP&A Analyst.
