## W959. Vendor Rebate Volume Tier Compliance Reconciliation & Shortfall Processing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly/quarterly vendor rebate period closes; system reconciles actual purchase volume against rebate agreement tier thresholds |
| **Frequency** | ~200–300 reconciliations/quarter (for vendors with tiered rebate agreements) |
| **Volume** | 1 vendor per reconciliation; multi-tier agreements (e.g., 3% at PHP 1M, 5% at PHP 3M, 7% at PHP 5M quarterly) |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Buyer, Finance Analyst, Vendor Account Manager, VP Merchandising, AP Manager |

### Background

Vendor rebate agreements are a significant margin contributor for BuildRight — estimated PHP 200–300M/year in rebate income. Many agreements use volume-based tiered structures where the rebate percentage increases as BuildRight's purchase volume from the vendor increases. Existing workflows cover rebate accrual and settlement (W27), vendor contract lifecycle (W62), and vendor rebate claim filing (W767). W959 addresses the gap between these workflows: the periodic reconciliation of actual purchase volume against tier thresholds, the financial impact of missing or exceeding tiers, and the proactive management of purchase timing to optimize tier achievement. This is especially important at quarter-end and year-end when purchasing decisions (accelerate or defer orders) can determine which tier applies, potentially shifting hundreds of thousands of pesos in rebate income.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Period-End Volume Calculation**: At rebate period close (monthly or quarterly per agreement): (a) system extracts all purchase volume (PO value at cost) per vendor from AP module per W7; (b) matches against vendor rebate agreement terms: (i) agreement period start/end; (ii) tier thresholds (e.g., PHP 1M/3M/5M); (iii) rebate rate per tier (e.g., 3%/5%/7%); (iv) eligible product categories (some agreements exclude clearance or consignment); (v) exclusion rules (returns, credits, sample items); (c) system calculates: actual qualifying volume, applicable tier, earned rebate amount, and variance to next tier (up or down) | System / Finance Analyst | Category Manager | 10–15 min/vendor (automated calculation + manual review) |
| 2 | **Tier Achievement Assessment**: Finance Analyst reviews each vendor's tier status: (a) tier maintained (e.g., qualified for 5% tier, same as last period) → routine processing; (b) tier upgraded (e.g., qualified for 7% tier, up from 5%) → calculate incremental rebate income; (c) tier downgraded (e.g., qualified for 3% tier, down from 5%) → calculate lost rebate income and investigate volume decline; (d) near-miss analysis: vendor fell short of next tier by ≤ 10% — flag for Buyer review: could accelerated purchasing have pushed volume into next tier?; (e) system generates tier achievement report per vendor with variance analysis | Finance Analyst | Category Manager | 5–10 min/vendor |
| 3 | **Shortfall Investigation & Vendor Negotiation**: For tier downgrades and significant near-misses: (a) Buyer investigates volume decline: (i) SKU discontinuations per W68 reducing purchases; (ii) competitive vendor gains (purchases shifted to alternative vendor); (iii) store-level stock balancing reducing replenishment orders; (iv) seasonal demand decline; (b) for tier downgrades: Buyer contacts vendor to negotiate: (i) retroactive tier maintenance with purchase commitment for next period; (ii) extended transition period (vendor grants old tier for one more period); (iii) revised tier thresholds reflecting current market conditions; (c) negotiation outcome documented in vendor rebate agreement with updated terms per W62; (d) for near-misses: Buyer reviews upcoming purchase requirements — if sufficient volume expected early next period, may negotiate "rollover" credit | Buyer / Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1–2 hours/vendor (for downgrade/near-miss) |
| 4 | **Rebate Accrual Adjustment & Financial Processing**: (a) Finance adjusts rebate accrual per W27: (i) for tier upgrade — increase accrual rate to new tier for current and forward periods; (ii) for tier downgrade — decrease accrual rate; true-up prior period accrual at old rate to actual earned rebate; (iii) true-up amount posted to P&L as rebate income adjustment; (b) AP Manager processes vendor rebate settlement per W767: system generates rebate claim with calculated amount based on actual tier achievement; vendor verifies and pays rebate per agreed timeline (30–60 days); (c) Finance reconciles: rebate claimed vs. rebate received vs. rebate accrued; variance investigated per W100 vendor statement reconciliation | Finance Analyst / AP Manager | CFO | 15–30 min/vendor |
| 5 | **Quarterly Rebate Portfolio Optimization**: (a) Category Manager reviews rebate portfolio: (i) total rebate income vs. target; (ii) tier achievement rate across all vendors (target: ≥ 80% at committed tier or above); (iii) top 10 vendors by rebate income; (iv) vendors with declining purchase volumes trending toward downgrade; (v) vendors where BuildRight is purchasing below tier potential; (b) VP Merchandising reviews: (i) opportunities to consolidate purchases with fewer vendors to achieve higher tiers (volume concentration vs. vendor diversification trade-off per W763); (ii) renegotiation schedule for agreements approaching expiry; (iii) proactive purchase acceleration at period-end for near-miss vendors (weighing carrying cost per W220 vs. incremental rebate income); (c) annual: Finance includes vendor rebate income in category P&L analysis per W102 | Category Manager / VP Merchandising / Finance | VP Merchandising / CFO | 4–6 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- AP module (W7) for purchase volume extraction
- Vendor rebate agreement module (W62) with tier threshold definitions
- Automated volume calculation engine with exclusion rules
- Rebate accrual module (W27) for rate adjustments
- AP settlement module (W767) for rebate claim generation
- Vendor statement reconciliation (W100) for rebate receipt verification
- BI dashboard for rebate portfolio analytics and tier tracking
- Purchase planning module (W31) for proactive volume management
- Category P&L reporting (W102) for rebate income allocation

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Purchase timing distortion**: Aggressively accelerating purchases to hit tier thresholds can inflate inventory and carrying costs; Buyer must calculate incremental rebate income vs. incremental carrying cost before period-end purchase acceleration; simple rule: accelerate only if incremental rebate exceeds 2× incremental carrying cost for the acceleration period
- **Vendor data discrepancies**: Vendor's recorded sales to BuildRight may not match BuildRight's recorded purchases from vendor (different recognition timing, credit memos, returns); reconciliation per W100 resolves; allow 5–10% volume discrepancy tolerance before escalation
- **Rebate income volatility**: Tier changes create income volatility that complicates financial forecasting per W639; Finance models rebate income scenarios (maintain/upgrade/downgrade) quarterly for budget accuracy
- **Vendor resistance to downgrade negotiation**: Vendors may resist granting old tier rates after volume decline; leverage: BuildRight's large purchase volume (200 stores) and potential to shift volume to competitors; documented escalation path to VP Merchandising for negotiation deadlock


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-calculate of "Period-End Volume Calculation" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "reviews each vendor's tier" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "documented in vendor rebate agreement with updated terms per W62" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-248 (ensure controlled execution — Vendor Performance & Contracts (PA-03.3).)
- operational: Category Manager review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Staffing Implication
- **Finance Analyst**: ~8–12 hours/quarter on tier reconciliation across 200–300 vendors; absorbed by existing Merchandising Finance team
- **Buyers**: ~2–4 hours/quarter on shortfall investigation and vendor negotiation for affected vendors; absorbed by existing buying team
- **No incremental headcount**

### Time Estimate
- Volume calculation: 10–15 min/vendor (mostly automated)
- Tier assessment: 5–10 min/vendor
- Shortfall investigation: 1–2 hours/affected vendor
- Accrual adjustment: 15–30 min/vendor
- Portfolio optimization: 4–6 hours/quarter
- **Total quarterly effort**: ~40–60 hours across team

## W962. Vendor-Sponsored In-Store Display Compliance Audit & Chargeback

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly display compliance audit cycle; or vendor reports display not meeting contractual requirements |
| **Frequency** | ~400–600 display audits/month (covering ~200 vendor-sponsored displays across 200 stores) |
| **Volume** | 1–5 displays audited per store per month |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Department Supervisor (store), Category Manager, Visual Merchandising Coordinator, Vendor Account Manager, Finance (chargeback processing) |

### Background

Many BuildRight vendors invest in sponsored in-store displays, endcaps, promotional islands, and branded sections under co-op advertising agreements per W513. These agreements typically include contractual requirements for: display location (specified aisle/zone), display dimensions, product assortment (minimum X facings of specified SKUs), signage and branding compliance, stock replenishment (vendor-sponsored displays must be kept stocked), and display condition (clean, organized, undamaged). In exchange, vendors pay BuildRight monthly display fees (PHP 5,000–50,000/display/month) or promotional funding. Existing W262 covers store promotional setup, and W86 covers planogram compliance. W962 addresses the gap of verifying that vendor-sponsored displays meet contractual requirements and processing chargebacks for non-compliance — either BuildRight charging the vendor for failing to maintain their display (e.g., expired promotional materials, damaged signage) or the vendor disputing display fees because BuildRight failed to provide agreed display location/conditions. Proper display compliance management ensures BuildRight receives contracted display revenue and maintains vendor relationships.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monthly Display Audit Schedule**: Category Manager generates monthly audit schedule: (a) all active vendor-sponsored displays across 200 stores listed by vendor, store, and display type; (b) audit priority: (i) new displays (installed within last 60 days — highest priority); (ii) displays with previous non-compliance findings; (iii) high-value display agreements (≥ PHP 20,000/month); (iv) random sample of remaining displays (30%); (c) audit assignments distributed to Department Supervisors via mobile task management; (d) target: all high-priority displays audited within first 2 weeks of month; random sample completed by month-end | Category Manager / System | VP Merchandising | 2–3 hours/month |
| 2 | **In-Store Display Audit Execution**: Department Supervisor conducts physical audit per display: (a) photographs display from 3 angles (front, side, product close-up) using mobile audit app; (b) checks compliance criteria per vendor agreement: (i) location — display in contracted zone per store layout plan; (ii) product assortment — correct SKUs, minimum facing quantities, no competitor products in display area; (iii) signage — current promotional signage, no expired materials, vendor branding intact; (iv) stock level — display adequately stocked (no empty facings); (v) condition — clean, organized, no damaged products or fixtures; (vi) pricing — correct prices displayed per W69 price compliance; (c) records findings in audit app: compliant (pass), minor non-compliance (advisory), major non-compliance (requires corrective action), critical non-compliance (display fee at risk) | Department Supervisor | Store Manager | 10–15 min/display |

| 4 | **Chargeback Processing**: For persistent non-compliance: (a) Category Manager authorizes chargeback per vendor agreement: (i) partial display fee reduction (e.g., 25% for minor compliance gaps); (ii) full display fee forfeiture for critical non-compliance (e.g., display removed, wrong location, competitor products); (iii) damage assessment for vendor-provided fixtures damaged by BuildRight staff; (b) Finance processes chargeback per W245 vendor chargebacks: (i) debit memo issued to vendor; (ii) display fee reduced by chargeback amount; (iii) vendor notified via W705 vendor portal with audit evidence (photos, compliance checklist); (c) vendor dispute resolution per W869 vendor dispute ticketing — vendor can contest chargeback with evidence; (d) Category Manager adjudicates disputes; VP Merchandising escalates unresolved disputes | Category Manager / Finance | VP Merchandising | 30–60 min/chargeback |
| 5 | **Quarterly Vendor Display Performance Review**: (a) Category Manager reviews display compliance portfolio: (i) compliance rate by vendor (target: ≥ 90% compliant); (ii) repeat offenders — vendors with < 80% compliance for 2+ consecutive months; (iii) BuildRight-side non-compliance — stores failing to maintain agreed display conditions (impacts vendor relationship and renewal); (b) VP Merchandising shares compliance data with vendor at quarterly business review per W155 Joint Business Planning; (c) annual: display agreement renewals incorporate compliance history — vendors with strong compliance may negotiate better display locations; vendors with poor compliance face higher scrutiny or non-renewal | Category Manager / VP Merchandising | VP Merchandising | 4–6 hours/quarter |

- Vendor display agreement module with contractual requirements per display

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Display location conflicts**: Multiple vendors competing for prime display locations (endcaps, entrance displays); Category Manager resolves conflicts based on contractual rights, revenue contribution, and seasonal relevance

## W978. Vendor Seasonal Product Post-Season Performance Review & Assortment Rationalization

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | End of seasonal selling period (post-Christmas, post-summer, post-rainy season) |
| **Frequency** | 3–4 reviews/year (post each major Philippine season) |
| **Volume** | ~500–800 seasonal SKUs reviewed per season |
| **Owner** | VP Merchandising |
| **Participants** | Category Managers, Buyers, Pricing Analysts, Merchandise Planners, Vendors, Supply Chain Planning, Finance |

### Background

Seasonal merchandise — Christmas decorations, garden/outdoor products, rainy season items (tarpaulins, waterproofing, flood control), summer items (aircons, fans, water tanks) — follows a predictable sales curve: buildup, peak, and sharp decline. The key challenge in seasonal retail is maximizing sales during the peak while minimizing residual inventory that must be cleared at significant markdowns (often 50–70% off) or written off entirely. Philippine seasonal patterns are particularly pronounced: Christmas spending (September–December "ber months") accounts for a disproportionate share of annual retail sales, while typhoon season (June–November) creates unpredictable demand surges for emergency supplies. Post-season performance review is a structured analytical process that evaluates every seasonal SKU's actual performance against plan, identifies root causes of over/underperformance, captures learnings for future seasons, and makes assortment decisions (continue, discontinue, modify) for the next season's buy. This workflow closes the loop between seasonal buy planning (W32) and actual results, driving continuous improvement in seasonal merchandising effectiveness.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Season Close-Out Data Compilation**: Within 2 weeks of season end: (a) Merchandise Planner compiles post-season data pack per category: (i) **Sales performance**: actual vs. plan sales units, revenue, and margin per SKU; (ii) **Sell-through rate**: percentage of purchased quantity sold at full price vs. markdown; (iii) **Inventory position**: remaining units, value, and aging; (iv) **Pricing effectiveness**: number and depth of markdowns taken per W93; (v) **Timeline adherence**: first receipt date vs. plan, in-store date vs. plan, markdown initiation date vs. plan; (vi) **Vendor performance**: fill rate, on-time delivery, quality complaints per W44; (vii) **Store-level performance**: sales by store cluster (high-performing vs. underperforming stores); (viii) **Channel performance**: in-store vs. ecommerce seasonal sales mix | Merchandise Planner | VP Merchandising | 8–12 hours/season |
| 2 | **SKU-Level Performance Classification**: Category Managers classify each seasonal SKU into performance tiers: (a) **Tier 1 — Winners** (top 20%): sold ≥90% of plan at full price or minimal markdown; high margin; customer demand exceeded supply; action: increase buy quantity 15–25% for next season, explore line extensions; (b) **Tier 2 — Solid Performers** (next 30%): sold 70–89% of plan; acceptable markdown depth (≤20%); action: maintain or slightly adjust buy quantity; review pricing and timing; (c) **Tier 3 — Underperformers** (next 30%): sold 50–69% of plan; significant markdown (20–40%); action: reduce or eliminate from next season's assortment; review root cause (wrong product, wrong price, wrong timing, poor display); (d) **Tier 4 — Losers** (bottom 20%): sold <50% of plan; deep markdown (40–70%+); or total dead stock; action: discontinue; return to vendor per W88 if agreement allows; donate per W444 if unsellable; root cause analysis mandatory | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 4–6 hours/category |
| 3 | **Vendor Performance Review & Negotiation**: For each seasonal vendor: (a) Category Manager evaluates: (i) fill rate — did vendor deliver full ordered quantity on time?; (ii) quality — were there customer returns or complaints due to product quality?; (iii) markdown support — did vendor contribute to markdown costs per W161 (vendor price protection)?; (iv) marketing support — did vendor fund promotional activities per W513?; (b) underperforming vendors: Category Manager initiates discussion — performance improvement plan, markdown cost recovery per W245, or vendor replacement; (c) top-performing vendors: Category Manager negotiates improved terms for next season — volume discounts, earlier delivery, markdown protection, exclusive products; (d) new vendor scouting: Category Manager identifies gaps in seasonal assortment and sources new vendors per W36 (vendor onboarding) to fill gaps | Category Manager / Buyer | VP Merchandising | 2–3 hours/vendor |
| 4 | **Residual Inventory Disposition**: For remaining seasonal inventory: (a) **Markdown acceleration** (within 30 days of season end): progressive markdown schedule — Week 1: 30% off, Week 2: 50% off, Week 3: 70% off; per W93 markdown pricing execution; (b) **Vendor return**: negotiate seasonal buy-back per W901 (vendor seasonal buy-back) for vendors with return agreements; (c) **Store-to-store redistribution**: transfer remaining stock from low-demand stores to stores with continued demand per W204; (d) **Ecommerce clearance**: list remaining inventory on BuildRight website/app with clearance pricing and on marketplace channels (Lazada/Shopee) per W180; (e) **Donation**: unsold items after all disposition channels exhausted → donated to community organizations per W444 (community donation) for tax deduction and ESG reporting per W194; (f) **Write-off**: items with no disposition channel → write-off per W220 (SLOB provisioning) with accounting entry | Category Manager / Finance / Store Operations | VP Merchandising | 1–2 weeks post-season |
| 5 | **Season Retrospective & Next Season Planning Input**: VP Merchandising conducts seasonal retrospective meeting: (a) cross-functional attendees: Category Managers, Buyers, Supply Chain Planning, Store Operations, Marketing, Finance; (b) review: (i) total seasonal revenue vs. plan; (ii) gross margin performance; (iii) inventory turns; (iv) markdown rate as % of seasonal revenue; (v) top 10 winners and losers with root cause; (vi) vendor performance summary; (vii) store execution — seasonal display setup compliance per W262, promotional execution per W13; (c) capture learnings in seasonal knowledge base — documented insights for next season's planning per W32 (seasonal buy planning); (d) initiate next season's buy planning with adjusted parameters; (e) Finance incorporates seasonal margin impact into annual budget and forecast per W639 (rolling forecast) | VP Merchandising / Cross-functional | VP Merchandising | Half-day meeting + documentation |

- Markdown pricing execution module (W93)
- Vendor performance scorecard (W44)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Data timeliness**: Post-season analysis requires complete data (all markdown sales, returns, and final inventory counts); delays in store-level data submission (especially from remote stores with connectivity issues per W535) slow analysis; system enforces data submission deadline within 5 business days of season end

- **Assortment inertia**: There is a tendency to repeat last season's assortment with minor adjustments rather than making bold changes; underperforming SKUs may persist due to buyer familiarity or vendor influence; objective data-driven tier classification (step 2) forces evidence-based decisions; Merchandise Planner provides independent analysis to counter buyer bias
- **Seasonal calendar shift**: Philippine seasonal patterns are shifting — typhoon season is extending, summer heat is intensifying, and Christmas buying patterns are changing; historical performance data may not predict future demand; Merchandise Planner incorporates climate trend data and macroeconomic indicators into next season's planning

- System auto-review of "review pricing and timing" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-count of "discounts, earlier delivery, markdown protection, exclusive products" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls

- operational: enforces data submission deadline within 5 business days of season end

### Staffing Implication
- **No incremental headcount** — absorbed by existing Merchandising team (Category Managers, Buyers, Merchandise Planners, Pricing Analysts); post-season review consumes ~40–60 person-hours per season across the team

### Time Estimate

- Vendor review: 2–3 hours/vendor (10–15 vendors = 20–45 hours)
- Inventory disposition: 1–2 weeks
- Retrospective meeting: half-day
- **Total per season**: 80–120 person-hours across the Merchandising team

## W1033. Vendor Lead Time Accuracy Monitoring & Supply Planning Impact Assessment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Weekly scheduled analysis; also triggered by significant lead time deviation detected during PO monitoring |
| **Frequency** | Weekly analysis; monthly vendor review; quarterly trend reporting |
| **Volume** | ~800–1,000 active vendors with purchase orders; ~1,200 merchandise POs/month |
| **Owner** | Supply Chain Planner |
| **Participants** | Buyer, Category Manager, DC Receiving Supervisor, Procurement Specialist |

### Background

BuildRight Depot's 35,000 active SKUs are sourced from ~800–1,000 vendors with lead times ranging from 7 days (local Manila vendors) to 90 days (import vendors from China, Taiwan, Indonesia). The auto-replenishment system (W2A) calculates reorder points and order quantities based on vendor-stated lead times. If a vendor's actual lead time consistently deviates from the stated lead time, the replenishment system either: (a) orders too early (if actual lead time is shorter than stated), resulting in excess inventory and carrying cost; or (b) orders too late (if actual lead time is longer than stated), resulting in stockouts and lost sales. Philippine supply chain realities exacerbate this: port congestion in Manila (averaging 5–10 days delay), customs processing variability (1–14 days), typhoon-related shipping disruptions, and local vendor production delays. Analysis of historical PO data shows that ~25% of vendors have lead time deviation > 20% from their stated lead time, and ~10% have deviation > 50%. This workflow systematically monitors vendor lead time accuracy, adjusts planning parameters, and feeds back into vendor performance management to ensure the replenishment engine operates on accurate data.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Weekly Lead Time Deviation Calculation**: System automatically calculates for each PO closed in the past week: (a) **Stated lead time** (from vendor master per W287): days between PO date and expected delivery date; (b) **Actual lead time**: days between PO date and goods receipt date at DC/store per W3/W109; (c) **Deviation** = (Actual − Stated) ÷ Stated × 100; (d) system aggregates by vendor: average deviation, standard deviation, on-time delivery rate (% of POs received within stated lead time + 3 day tolerance); (e) weekly vendor lead time scorecard generated: **Tier 1 (Accurate)**: deviation ≤ 10%, on-time ≥ 90%; **Tier 2 (Variable)**: deviation 10–25%, on-time 70–90%; **Tier 3 (Unreliable)**: deviation > 25%, on-time < 70% | System | Supply Chain Planner | Automated |
| 2 | **Planning Parameter Adjustment**: For vendors consistently deviating from stated lead time: (a) if vendor has been Tier 2 (Variable) for 2+ consecutive weeks: Supply Chain Planner adjusts the vendor's lead time parameter in the replenishment system (W312) to reflect actual average lead time + safety buffer; this immediately adjusts reorder points for all SKUs sourced from this vendor; (b) if vendor has been Tier 3 (Unreliable) for 2+ consecutive weeks: (i) increase safety stock for all affected SKUs by 1–2 weeks of demand to buffer against uncertainty; (ii) evaluate alternative sourcing per W921 (Emergency Alternative Vendor) for critical SKUs; (iii) Buyer escalates to vendor per W44 (Vendor Performance Review); (c) parameter changes logged in system with justification; system generates impact analysis: estimated inventory value change from parameter adjustment (increase in safety stock = additional carrying cost) | Supply Chain Planner | VP Supply Chain | 30–60 min/week |
| 3 | **Stockout Root Cause Analysis (Lead Time Related)**: When a stockout occurs: (a) system checks if the stockout was caused by a late vendor delivery (PO past due with no GR); (b) if yes: flag the stockout as "lead time related" and attribute to the specific vendor and PO; (c) calculate lost sales impact: stockout duration × average daily sales velocity × average margin; (d) weekly stockout report includes lead-time-related stockout attribution by vendor; (e) vendors responsible for > 5% of total chain-wide stockouts in a month are flagged for Buyer review per W44 | Supply Chain Planner / System | Category Manager | 30 min/week |

## W1115. Vendor Performance-Based Shelf Space Allocation Review

| Field | Detail |

| **Frequency** | Semi-annual (major review); quarterly (minor adjustments) |

| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Category Manager, Planogram Analyst, Merchandise Planner, VP Merchandising |

### Background

BuildRight Depot operates big-box stores with 8,000–15,000 sqm of selling area and 35,000 active SKUs. Shelf space is the store's most valuable asset — every linear meter of shelving, every endcap, every promotional display area must generate maximum revenue per sqm. Currently, shelf space allocation decisions are made during annual planogram reviews (W86 — planogram compliance) and promotional planning (W13), but there is no systematic mechanism to reallocate shelf space from underperforming vendors to high-performing ones. In a vendor performance-based shelf space allocation model: (a) vendors are scored on a composite index including: sales velocity (units/week per facing), gross margin contribution, fill rate / in-stock rate, promotional support (co-op funding, demo participation), and consumer demand (market share trend); (b) high-scoring vendors earn premium shelf positions (eye-level, endcaps, near entrance) and additional facings; (c) low-scoring vendors face shelf space reduction, repositioning to less desirable locations, or potential delisting; (d) vendor scorecards are published on the vendor portal (W871), creating transparency and motivating vendor performance improvement. This approach is standard practice in global retail but requires systematic execution to avoid subjectivity and vendor relationship damage.

| 5 | **Post-Reset Performance Tracking**: (a) system monitors category performance for 8 weeks post-reset: (i) sales per sqm vs. pre-reset baseline; (ii) gross margin per sqm; (iii) customer traffic in affected sections; (iv) vendor fill rate changes (did Review vendors improve?); (b) results shared with VP Merchandising for quarterly review; (c) lessons learned inform next semi-annual allocation cycle | System / Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 2–3 hours/cycle for analysis |

Product recalls — while infrequent — impose significant costs on BuildRight Depot: (a) direct costs include inventory loss (quarantined and unsalable stock), return logistics (customer returns, DC-to-vendor shipping), and replacement procurement (urgent purchase from alternative vendor); (b) indirect costs include labor for recall execution (W874 — customer notification, W875 — inventory quarantine, W876 — regulatory reporting), store shelf space lost during recall period, and potential brand damage; (c) margin impact from selling replacement stock at original price but at higher procurement cost. This workflow tracks recall costs comprehensively, calculates margin impact, and manages vendor cost recovery claims. ## W1230. Vendor Consignment Inventory Monthly Settlement, Revenue Share & Reconciliation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Month-end close; consignment sell-through report generated |
| **Frequency** | Monthly; per consignment vendor (currently ~12 key vendors, ~300 SKUs) |
| **Volume** | ~12 consignment vendor settlements/month; ~300 SKUs tracked |
| **Owner** | Merchandise Analyst |
| **Participants** | Merchandise Analyst, Category Manager, Finance – AP, Vendor Account Manager |

### Background

BuildRight Depot carries consignment inventory for major appliances and select tiles from ~12 key vendors (~300 SKUs per model company profile Section 6.4). Under consignment terms, BuildRight does not own the inventory — the vendor retains ownership until the item is sold to a customer. At that point, ownership transfers from vendor to BuildRight (momentarily) and then to the customer. BuildRight pays the vendor only for items sold, typically at an agreed revenue-share percentage. This reduces BuildRight's inventory carrying cost and risk (especially for high-value appliances and slow-moving tile variants) but creates a complex reconciliation process: BuildRight must accurately track which consignment items were sold during the period, at what price, calculate the vendor's share, and settle payment — while maintaining inventory records that distinguish owned vs. vendor-owned stock. The ERP must support dual inventory ownership tracking per W23.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monthly consignment sell-through extraction**: System generates consignment sell-through report: (a) all consignment SKU sales for the month by vendor; (b) quantities sold per SKU; (c) selling price per unit; (d) total revenue per SKU; (e) unsold consignment inventory on-hand at month-end by location | System / Merchandise Analyst | Category Manager | 30 min |
| 2 | **Revenue share calculation**: Merchandise Analyst calculates vendor settlement: (a) apply agreed revenue-share percentage per vendor contract (e.g., BuildRight keeps 15–25%, vendor receives 75–85% of selling price); (b) calculate vendor's share per SKU per unit; (c) sum total vendor settlement amount; (d) deduct any applicable chargebacks (damaged units, returns per W12A) | Merchandise Analyst | Category Manager | 1–2 hours/vendor |
| 3 | **Inventory reconciliation**: Merchandise Analyst reconciles: (a) beginning consignment inventory + units received during month – units sold = ending consignment inventory; (b) verify ending inventory matches physical count (perpetual system + cycle count per W6); (c) investigate discrepancies; (d) verify unsold items are still in saleable condition | Merchandise Analyst | Category Manager | 1 hour/vendor |
| 4 | **Vendor settlement statement preparation**: (a) generate vendor settlement statement detailing: SKUs sold, quantities, unit price, revenue share split, deductions, net payable; (b) include aging of unsold consignment inventory (flag items >90 days for potential return-to-vendor per W88); (c) prepare AP invoice for vendor settlement | Merchandise Analyst | Category Manager | 1 hour/vendor |
| 5 | **Category Manager approval**: Category Manager reviews and approves settlement: (a) verify revenue-share calculation matches contract terms; (b) approve unsold inventory aging action (return request, markdown request per W93, or retention); (c) approve AP invoice for payment | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 30 min/vendor |
| 6 | **AP processing & payment**: Finance – AP processes vendor settlement invoice: (a) create AP voucher against consignment vendor account; (b) schedule payment per agreed terms; (c) post settlement to GL (consignment payable cleared, COGS recognized per W23); (d) issue payment via bank transfer per W7 | Finance – AP | Finance Manager | 15 min/vendor |
| 7 | **Quarterly business review**: Include consignment performance in vendor QBR per W1224: (a) sell-through rate by SKU; (b) revenue share profitability vs. owned inventory; (c) inventory aging trends; (d) recommend assortment changes (add/remove consignment SKUs) | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | Quarterly |

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Sell-through data accuracy** — if POS correctly flags consignment SKUs per W543 but system configuration is wrong for new items, sales may not be tracked as consignment, causing underpayment to vendor

- **Dual-stock physical counting** — during cycle counts, stock associates must identify and count consignment items separately; misclassification distorts both owned inventory and consignment reconciliation

## W1303. Vendor Rebate, Co-Op Advertising Fund & Promotional Incentive Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly rebate calculation cycle; or co-op advertising campaign execution; or promotional incentive claim from vendor |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly rebate settlements; monthly co-op advertising reconciliation; promotional incentives per sale event |
| **Volume** | ~200 vendors with rebate agreements; ~50 vendors with co-op advertising arrangements; ~PHP 800M–1.2B in annual vendor rebates and co-op income |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Category Manager, Finance Analyst, Marketing Manager, Buyer, Vendor Account Manager, AP Clerk |

### Background

Vendor rebates and co-op advertising funds are a significant revenue stream for BuildRight Depot, representing ~1.5–2.0% of COGS (~PHP 800M–1.2B annually). These arrangements fall into three categories: (a) **Volume Rebates** — vendors pay BuildRight a percentage of purchases when annual purchase volume exceeds agreed thresholds (e.g., "3% rebate on all purchases exceeding PHP 50M annually"); these are typically settled quarterly or annually; (b) **Co-Op Advertising Funds** — vendors contribute to BuildRight's advertising costs (catalog placement, social media ads, in-store displays) in exchange for prominent product placement; typically calculated as a percentage of BuildRight's purchases from that vendor (2–5%); (c) **Promotional Incentives** — vendors offer one-time payments or discounts to secure BuildRight's participation in promotional events (bi-monthly sales, seasonal promotions); these may include: markdown money (vendor subsidizes the discount on their products during sales), display allowances (payment for end-cap or promotional display placement), and new product launch incentives. Without systematic tracking, BuildRight may: fail to claim earned rebates (leaving money on the table), over-claim rebates (creating vendor disputes), or fail to fulfill co-op advertising obligations (resulting in vendor clawback). This workflow manages the full lifecycle of vendor rebates, co-op funds, and promotional incentives.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rebate agreement setup & tracking**: (a) during annual vendor negotiations (W44), Category Manager negotiates rebate terms: (i) volume rebate tiers (e.g., PHP 0–50M = 1%, PHP 50–100M = 2%, > PHP 100M = 3%), (ii) co-op advertising fund percentage (e.g., 3% of annual purchases), (iii) promotional incentive schedule (e.g., PHP 200K per bi-monthly catalog placement); (b) Finance Analyst enters rebate agreement parameters into Oracle Trade Management (fit-gap B10): tier thresholds, percentage rates, settlement frequency, eligible product categories, exclusions; (c) system tracks cumulative purchases per vendor against rebate tiers in real-time; (d) system alerts Category Manager when vendor approaches next rebate tier threshold | Category Manager / Finance Analyst | VP Merchandising | 2–4 hours/vendor during annual negotiation |
| 2 | **Quarterly rebate calculation & claim**: (a) at quarter-end, system calculates earned rebate per vendor: (i) cumulative purchases for the quarter × applicable rebate rate, (ii) if annual rebate: system calculates projected annual rebate based on YTD purchases × annualized rate; (b) Finance Analyst reviews calculated rebates: (i) verify purchase volume against AP records per W7, (ii) verify eligible products (some agreements exclude consignment, clearance, or imported items), (iii) verify no double-counting across entities (BuildRight Depot purchases vs. Logistics purchases); (c) system generates rebate claim report per vendor with supporting purchase detail; (d) Category Manager reviews and approves claim; (e) claim transmitted to vendor via supplier portal; (f) vendor reviews and confirms or disputes; (g) upon vendor confirmation: system generates AP credit memo or vendor payment per agreement (credit against future invoices or direct payment) | System / Finance Analyst / Category Manager | Category Manager | 4–6 hours/quarter |
| 3 | **Co-op advertising fund reconciliation**: Monthly, Marketing Manager and Category Manager reconcile co-op advertising fund: (a) system calculates co-op fund earned: monthly purchases from vendor × co-op fund %; (b) system tracks co-op advertising expenditures by vendor: (i) catalog placement costs (per page, per edition), (ii) social media advertising spend featuring vendor's products, (iii) in-store display costs (signage, fixtures, point-of-purchase materials), (iv) digital advertising costs (website banners, search ads); (c) reconcile earned fund vs. spent amount: (i) if earned > spent: BuildRight has surplus co-op fund to spend on vendor's advertising, (ii) if spent > earned: BuildRight may owe vendor reimbursement or excess counts against future fund; (d) Marketing Manager plans next month's co-op advertising allocation per vendor based on fund balance and promotional calendar | Marketing Manager / Category Manager | VP Marketing | 2–3 hours/month |
| 4 | **Promotional incentive claim processing**: Per promotional event: (a) before sale event: Category Manager negotiates promotional incentives with vendor: markdown money (vendor subsidizes discount on their products), display allowance (payment for featured placement), bonus stock (vendor provides free product for every X units purchased); (b) promotional incentive terms entered in ERP with promotional event ID; (c) during promotion: system tracks promotional sales by vendor — items sold at promotional price, discount amount, display placement verification; (d) post-promotion: system calculates total promotional incentive earned per vendor: (i) markdown money: total units sold at discount × vendor subsidy per unit, (ii) display allowance: per-placement fee × number of stores displaying, (iii) bonus stock: units earned × cost per unit; (e) Category Manager submits promotional incentive claim to vendor with supporting sales data; (f) vendor confirms and issues credit or payment | Category Manager / System | VP Merchandising | 2–4 hours per promotional event |
| 5 | **Annual vendor program ROI analysis**: Annual, Category Manager and Finance Analyst evaluate vendor program ROI: (a) total rebates, co-op income, and promotional incentives earned per vendor, (b) as % of purchases from that vendor (benchmark: 2–5% of purchases), (c) vendor compliance rate (claims disputed, payments delayed), (d) comparison: vendors with rebate programs vs. vendors without (are rebate vendors giving BuildRight better net pricing or just inflating gross price to fund rebates?); (e) recommend to VP Merchandising: negotiate higher rebate rates with top vendors, replace underperforming rebate programs with flat net pricing, or expand co-op advertising programs | Category Manager / Finance Analyst | VP Merchandising | 1–2 days/year |
