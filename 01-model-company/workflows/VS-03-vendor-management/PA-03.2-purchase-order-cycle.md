## W2. Procurement — Purchase Order Cycle

### W2A. Auto-Replenishment (Stocking Items)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | SKU hits reorder point (ROP) in system |
| **Frequency** | Daily review; POs generated daily |
| **Volume** | ~1,200 merchandise POs/month (auto-replenishment + ad-hoc; ~8,400 lines at the production-measured ~7 lines/PO); ~12,000 PO lines/month all types (~144,000/year, per profile §6.6); excludes ~80–240 blanket/contract release orders/month (W2C), ~20–30 import POs/month (W2B), and ~30–50 non-merchandise POs/month (capex, IT, supplies); total all types: ~1,600–1,900 POs/month (the production-calibrated 55–65/day canon per data-volumes §1.1 — the merchandise + enumerated-class sum ~1,330–1,520 plus the further release/service PO classes inside the measured total) |

> **PO-to-GR ratio**: ~1,200 merchandise POs generate ~6,000 DC goods receipts/month (W3) — an average of ~5 GRs per PO. This ratio reflects partial shipments (vendors delivering across multiple drops), scheduled phased deliveries for large POs, and blanket PO releases each generating a separate GR. Import POs (W2B) typically generate 1 GR per container. DSD POs delivered to stores generate store-level GRs (W18) outside the DC receiving workflow.
| **Owner** | Buyer |
| **Participants** | System (auto-suggest), Buyer, Category Manager (approval if > PHP 50K) |

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System calculates ROP breaches based on: avg daily demand × lead time + safety stock | System | — | Automated |

### PO Lifecycle: Amendment, Cancellation & Close

| Activity | Trigger | Approval | System Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PO Amendment** | Buyer needs to change quantity, delivery date, price, or add/remove lines on an approved PO | Re-approval per tiered matrix (same thresholds as original PO: > PHP 50K → Category Manager, > PHP 500K → VP Merchandising, > PHP 2M → CFO for imports) if change exceeds materiality threshold (quantity change > 20%, price change > 5%, or any delivery date change); minor changes (within threshold) auto-approved with Buyer note | System transmits amendment to vendor (email, EDI, or portal); tracks amendment history with full audit trail (original values, amended values, reason code, approver ID, timestamp); for import POs (W2B), Finance notifies bank if LC amendment is required for value changes |
| **PO Cancellation** | Vendor cannot deliver, demand drops, item discontinued, or PO no longer needed | Buyer initiates with reason code; Category Manager approval required for cancellations > PHP 50K; VP Merchandising for > PHP 500K | System checks for existing GR against PO: if no GR, auto-cancels with vendor notification; if partial GR, Buyer confirms close of remaining quantity; system releases budget commitment; cancelled PO retained in audit trail |
| **PO Auto-Close** | PO fully received (GR quantity matches ordered quantity within tolerance) or PO open beyond configured age limit | Automated | System auto-closes fully received POs; system flags POs open > 90 days for Buyer review (dashboard alert); Buyer reviews aged POs weekly and decides: close remaining quantity, extend delivery date (amendment), or escalate to Category Manager |

### System Touchpoints (PO Lifecycle)
- PO amendment workflow with materiality thresholds triggering re-approval (W2A)
- Amendment history with full audit trail: original vs. amended values, reason, approver (W2A)
- Vendor notification on amendment (email/EDI/portal) (W2A)
- Import PO LC amendment trigger (cross-reference W2B) (W2A)
- PO cancellation with reason code and budget release (W2A)
- Partial-receipt PO close workflow (W2A)
- Auto-close rules: tolerance-based for fully received POs; aging threshold for open POs (W2A)

### Pain Points / Risks
- Suggested PO quantities based on ROP may not reflect actual store-level demand patterns, especially during typhoon season or regional construction booms, leading to overstock in some DCs and stockouts in others
- Tiered approval bottleneck: POs above PHP 50K require Category Manager sign-off, and above PHP 500K need VP Merchandising; during peak seasonal buying (Q4), approval queues can delay PO transmission by 1–2 days, risking vendor lead-time slippage
- Aged open POs beyond 90 days indicate vendor delivery failures or buyer neglect; without disciplined weekly review, ghost commitments inflate budget reports and mask real procurement liabilities


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-calculate of "calculates ROP breaches based" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-generate of "generates suggested POs grouped by vendor, consolidated across DCs" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews suggested PO list each morning" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-247 (ensure controlled execution — Purchase Order Cycle (PA-03.2).)
- operational: Buyer review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
- System ROP breach calculation and PO suggestion generation: automated (steps 1–2)
- Daily buyer PO review and confirmation: ~2–4 hours/day across all buyers (steps 3–4)
- Approval routing (Category Manager / VP Merch): ~15 min/PO for above-threshold orders (steps 5–6)
- Open PO tracking and overdue follow-up: ~1 hour/day across all buyers (step 8)
- PO amendment, cancellation, and auto-close management: ~2–3 hours/week across all buyers
- Total buyer effort: ~3–5 hours/day for daily PO review and follow-up; ~1,200 merchandise POs/month generated

### Staffing Implication
- **Buyers**: ~1,200 POs/month ÷ ~10 buyers = ~120 POs/buyer/month. Daily review of ~3–5 hours/buyer/day is the core buying workload. Absorbed within existing team.
- **Category Managers**: Approval for POs > PHP 50K — estimated ~30% of POs trigger CM approval = ~360 approvals/month ÷ 5 CMs (per §13.1) = ~72/CM/month × ~15 min = ~18 hours/CM/month. Absorbed but significant.
- **VP Merchandising**: POs > PHP 500K — estimated ~5% of POs = ~60 approvals/month × ~15 min = ~15 hours/month. Absorbed within executive role.

### System Touchpoints
- ROP/EOQ auto-calculation per SKU per location (W2A.1–2)
- PO creation with multi-tier approval workflow (W2A.5–6)
- Vendor PO transmission (W2A.7)
- Open PO tracking / overdue alerts (W2A.8)
- Import PO tracking with LC/BL/container fields (W2B.1–9)
- Landed cost calculation engine (W2B.12)
- 3-way match: PO → Goods Receipt → Vendor Invoice (W2B.13)
- FX rate capture at PO creation (budget rate), goods receipt (spot rate or BSR), and invoice (actual rate); automatic FX gain/loss posting (W2B.12–13)
- Month-end FX revaluation of open foreign-currency balances at BIR exchange rate with unrealized FX gain/loss posting and auto-reversal (W9A.5a)

### Pain Points / Risks
- 45–90 day lead time exposes BuildRight to significant PHP/USD or PHP/CNY exchange rate volatility; a 5% adverse FX move on a PHP 2M import PO can erase category margin
- Port congestion in Manila (South Harbor / North Harbor) adds 3–7 days of uncontrolled delay; typhoon-season port closures can push receipt past the selling window for seasonal items
- Landed cost estimation at PO creation relies on estimated freight and duty rates; actual costs at receipt may differ materially, causing margin erosion that is only visible after goods are sold

### Time Estimate
- PO creation and approval (steps 1–2): ~45–60 min/PO
- LC/TT arrangement with Finance (step 3): ~1–2 hours/PO
- Freight booking and shipping coordination (steps 4–7): ~2–3 hours/PO spread over initial weeks
- Shipment tracking and customs clearance (steps 8–10): ~15 min/day/PO for tracking; 1–3 days customs clearance
- Receiving and landed cost calculation (steps 11–12): ~2–4 hours/container
- Payment reconciliation (step 13): ~30 min/PO
- Total active internal time per import PO: ~8–12 hours over 45–90 day cycle
- Aggregate: ~20–30 import POs/month × ~10 hours = ~200–300 hours/month across Buyer, Import Coordinator, and Finance

### Staffing Implication
- **Import Coordinator**: Dedicated role managing ~20–30 import POs/month — shipment tracking, customs broker coordination, document management. ~200–300 hours/month workload confirms this as a full-time position.
- **Buyers**: Import PO creation and vendor communication adds ~1 hour/PO = ~20–30 hours/month. Spread across buyers handling import categories.
- **Treasury Analyst**: LC/TT processing adds ~1–2 hours/PO = ~20–60 hours/month. Absorbed within existing Treasury team.
- **Customs Broker**: External partner; ~1–3 days clearance per shipment. Cost is per-entry fee.

## W38. Special Order / Non-Stock Item Fulfillment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests an item not carried in regular store assortment |
| **Frequency** | ~3,000–5,000 special orders/month chain-wide; ~15–25 per store per month (one register with W1506) |
| **Volume** | Primarily professional trade customers (contractors, builders) and project-specific items; avg order value ~PHP 5,000–15,000 |
| **Owner** | Customer Service Rep (order intake); Buyer (fulfillment) |
| **Participants** | CSR, Sales Rep (trade accounts), Buyer, Receiving Clerk, Customer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customer requests non-stock item at store (CSR counter) or via Sales Rep; provides product description, specification, quantity needed, and desired delivery date | Customer | — | — |

| 4 | Buyer identifies vendor, obtains quotation (price, lead time, minimum quantity); enters quote in system linked to the non-stock SKU | Buyer | Category Manager | 30–60 min |

| 7 | System creates Sales Order linked to non-stock SKU with customer deposit recorded; reservation created against incoming PO | System | — | Automated |
| 8 | Buyer creates Special Order PO (links PO to Sales Order); routes for approval per standard tiered matrix (W2) | Buyer | Category Manager | Per W2 |
| 9 | Buyer tracks PO; follows up with vendor on delivery schedule | Buyer | Buyer | Per W2 |
| 10 | Goods received at store or DC (per W3 or W18); system matches GR to both PO and linked Sales Order | Receiving Clerk | Dept. Supervisor | Per W3/W18 |
| 11 | System alerts CSR that special order has arrived; CSR contacts customer for pickup or arranges delivery | System / CSR | Store Manager | Automated + 5 min |

| 13 | If customer cancels before PO is placed: CSR cancels Sales Order; deposit refunded; no PO created | CSR | Store Manager | 5 min |
| 14 | If customer cancels after PO is placed but before shipment: Buyer negotiates with vendor (restocking fee, return); Finance processes partial refund less any costs | Buyer + Finance | Category Manager | 30 min |
| 15 | Unclaimed deposit management: system tracks age of customer deposits linked to completed special orders (goods received but not picked up); after 30 days with no customer response post-delivery notification, system sends reminder notification (SMS/email); after 90 days with no response, system flags deposit for review; Store Manager or Finance approves recognition of abandoned deposit as other income (Dr. Customer Deposits Payable / Cr. | 16 | **Customer deposit refund (before goods receipt)**: if customer requests cancellation and refund before goods are received from vendor — (a) CSR processes deposit refund in system with reason code (customer cancellation, vendor cannot deliver, project cancelled); (b) system cancels linked Sales Order and triggers Buyer to cancel or reduce the linked PO per W2A PO cancellation process (if PO not yet shipped by vendor); (c) refund issued to original payment method: if deposit paid by cash, CSR disburses from cash drawer with Store Manager authorization; if paid by card/e-wallet, system processes electronic refund (Dr. Customer Deposits Payable / Cr. Cash); if paid by trade account credit, system applies deposit back to customer's AR account; (d) system logs refund with customer ID, original deposit transaction, refund amount, refund method, CSR ID, authorizing manager ID, and reason code; (e) BIR-compliant documentation: system generates official receipt for the refund transaction with all required BIR fields (TIN, registered invoice number, description "Refund of customer deposit — Sales Order #[number]"); refund receipts retained per 10-year BIR retention policy | CSR / Store Manager | Controller | 10 min/refund |

**Total cycle time**: 7–30 days from order to delivery (depends on vendor lead time and whether domestic or import)

### System Touchpoints
- Non-stock item type in item master with no auto-replenishment (W38.3a)
- Item creation request workflow (W38.3)
- Sales Order creation with customer deposit and PO linkage (W38.7–8)
- PO-to-Sales-Order reservation and matching (W38.8, W38.10)
- Customer deposit liability tracking (Cr. Customer Deposits Payable) with revenue recognition trigger at delivery (W38.6, W38.12)
- Customer notification upon receipt (W38.11)
- Sales Order lifecycle: open → PO linked → goods received → customer notified → closed (W38.7–12)
- Cancellation handling with deposit refund workflow (W38.13–14)
- Non-stock SKU archival and cleanup: over time, the item master accumulates non-stock/special-order SKUs (type = "Special Order / Non-Stock" per W38.3a) from one-time customer orders; to prevent item master bloat — (a) monthly: Merchandise Planner generates non-stock SKU aging report listing all non-stock SKUs with no sales activity in the past 12 months; (b) Merchandise Planner reviews each stale non-stock SKU and sets status to "Non-Stock — Archived" — item remains searchable in system for transaction history but is hidden from new Sales Order creation and Sales Associate lookup; (c) archived non-stock SKUs excluded from item count in reporting and system performance metrics; (d) if a customer subsequently requests the same item, Merchandise Planner reactivates the archived SKU (updates status to active) rather than creating a duplicate; system checks for archived SKUs matching the item description before allowing new non-stock SKU creation; (e) annually: Merchandise Planner reviews all archived non-stock SKUs older than 3 years with zero transaction history and proposes permanent deletion per BIR 10-year retention compliance (items with any transaction history retained for full 10 years regardless of archival status)
- Unclaimed deposit aging: system tracks deposit age from goods-receipt date; automated reminder at 30 days; escalation flag at 90 days; abandonment recognition with approval workflow; goods disposition tracking (W38.15)

### Pain Points / Risks
- Non-stock item master bloat: every special order creates a new SKU, and without disciplined archival (step in System Touchpoints), the item master grows by ~1,500–2,500 SKUs/year absent archival (within the new-SKU canon), degrading system search performance and complicating reporting
- Customer deposits held for unclaimed special orders (step 15) represent a BIR compliance risk if not recognized as income within the prescribed period; premature recognition also risks customer disputes if goods are eventually claimed
- Special orders tie up Buyer time at ~150–250 hours/buyer/month (per the Staffing itemization) — diverting attention from core replenishment buying and potentially causing missed ROP signals on stocking items


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-capture of "provides product description, specification, quantity needed" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-validate of "checks if item exists in item master as non-stock/Special Order type" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-route of "routes non-stock item creation request to Merchandise Planner for item master setup" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-247 (ensure controlled execution — Purchase Order Cycle (PA-03.2).)
- operational: Customer Service Rep (order intake); Buyer (fulfillment) review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **CSR**: ~15–25 special orders/store/month (per the Frequency) × ~20 min each (intake + communication + handoff) = ~5–8.5 hours/store/month. Absorbed.
- **Buyer**: ~3,000–5,000 special orders/month (per the Frequency) ÷ 10 buyers = ~300–500/buyer/month × ~30 min each = ~150–250 hours/buyer/month. This is significant — split across buyer pods with CSR intake support. Special orders need a dedicated special-order buyer pod sized from the workload (3,000–5,000 orders/month × ~30 min ≈ 1,500–2,500 buyer-hours/month ≈ 9–16 FTE equivalent across the buying team), with remaining buyers focused on replenishment.
- **Merchandise Planner**: ~1,500–2,500 non-stock SKU creations/year (per the item-master growth canon) = ~5–8 hours/week. Absorbed within existing team.

### Time Estimate

## W1006. Vendor Purchase Order ASN Reconciliation & Discrepancy Resolution

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vendor transmits Advance Shipping Notice (ASN) for a confirmed Purchase Order |
| **Frequency** | ~700–750 ASN receipts/month (~60% of the ~1,200 merchandise POs have ASN capability) |
| **Volume** | 1 ASN per PO; ~7 lines per ASN (the ASN mirrors its PO at the production-measured 6.9–7.4 lines/PO canon, per data-volumes §1.1) |
| **Owner** | Procurement Specialist |
| **Participants** | Vendor, DC Receiving Supervisor, AP Clerk, Buyer |

### Background

BuildRight Depot processes ~1,200 merchandise POs/month (Section 6.5) with ~480–600 vendors capable of transmitting ASNs through the vendor portal (W866; ~60% of the active base per the pain points). An ASN is the vendor's pre-shipment notification detailing what will actually be shipped, including item quantities, lot/batch numbers, expected ship date, and carrier/tracking information. Comparing the ASN against the original PO before physical receipt allows BuildRight to: (a) detect short-shipments (vendor shipping fewer units than ordered), over-shipments (vendor shipping more than ordered), wrong items, and substitutions before goods arrive at the DC; (b) pre-allocate receiving dock labor and staging space (W585); (c) update expected inventory positions for more accurate ATP calculations across all channels; (d) pre-generate goods receipt templates for faster DC receiving (W3); and (e) identify discrepancies early for vendor performance tracking (W44). Currently, ASN-vs-PO discrepancies affect ~15–20% of shipments, causing receiving delays, inventory inaccuracies, and AP matching failures. Proactive reconciliation before physical receipt reduces 3-way match exceptions by ~40% and DC receiving cycle time by ~25%.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **ASN Receipt & Auto-Match to PO**: Vendor transmits ASN via vendor portal (W866) or EDI (cXML/JSON API); system auto-matches ASN to open PO by PO number: (a) match on PO number, vendor code, and expected ship date window (±7 days); (b) system performs line-by-line comparison of ASN quantities vs. PO quantities; (c) auto-match outcomes: **Full Match** (ASN qty = PO qty for all lines, no discrepancies), **Partial Match** (some lines have qty differences within tolerance ±5%), **Mismatch** (significant qty difference, wrong items, or extra items not on PO) | System | Procurement Specialist | 1–2 min (automated) |
| 2 | **Discrepancy Classification & Auto-Action**: System classifies discrepancies and takes automated action: (a) **Minor short-shipment** (ASN qty ≤ PO qty, within 5% tolerance): system auto-accepts, updates PO expected quantity, and adjusts ATP; Buyer notified for information; (b) **Major short-shipment** (ASN qty < 90% of PO qty): system flags for Buyer review; auto-generates vendor notification requesting confirmation; if confirmed, PO qty reduced and DC receiving updated; (c) **Over-shipment** (ASN qty > PO qty): system blocks excess quantity; Buyer must approve or reject over-shipment; excess units received but held in quarantine until Buyer decision; (d) **Substitution** (vendor ships different item than ordered): system flags substitution; Buyer must approve alternative item or reject; if rejected, vendor notified to recall shipment; (e) **Extra items not on PO**: system blocks completely; vendor notified to arrange return pickup at vendor's cost | System / Buyer | Procurement Specialist | 2–5 min per exception |
| 3 | **Buyer Review & Resolution**: Buyer reviews flagged discrepancies: (a) short-shipments: assess impact on store replenishment plan; if critical items shorted, trigger emergency procurement per W60 or alternative supplier activation per W921; confirm partial receipt is acceptable; (b) over-shipments: evaluate if excess inventory is needed (seasonal demand, promotional stock); if accepted, PO quantity amended with appropriate approval; if rejected, vendor notified to arrange return at their cost; (c) substitutions: compare substitute item specifications against original order requirements; if quality-equivalent, accept with same PO price; if inferior, reject; (d) resolution logged in system with buyer comments and vendor notification sent | Buyer | Procurement Specialist | 5–15 min per discrepancy |
| 4 | **Pre-Receipt DC Notification**: System sends pre-receipt notification to DC Receiving Supervisor: (a) expected delivery date/time (from ASN carrier data); (b) confirmed receipt quantities (post-reconciliation); (c) receiving dock assignment (W585); (d) special handling notes (hazmat, temperature-sensitive, oversized); (e) pre-generated goods receipt template for DC scan-receiving; (f) vendor performance scorecard updated with ASN accuracy metrics (ASN qty match rate, on-time ASN submission rate) | System | DC Receiving Supervisor | Automated |
| 5 | **Post-Receipt Variance Analysis**: After physical goods receipt at DC (W3): (a) system compares GR quantities against both ASN quantities and PO quantities; (b) three-way comparison: PO → ASN → GR; (c) if GR ≠ ASN (i.e., vendor shipped differently than ASN stated): system generates carrier damage claim (if transit damage) or vendor discrepancy notification; (d) if GR = ASN ≠ PO (vendor partial shipment as notified): normal partial receipt processing; (e) monthly ASN accuracy report generated for vendor scorecard (W44, W706): metrics include ASN submission rate, ASN-to-PO match rate, ASN-to-GR accuracy rate; (f) vendors with ASN accuracy < 85% for 3 consecutive months placed on ASN compliance improvement plan per W110 | Procurement Specialist / System | Procurement Specialist | 10–15 min/week (batch analysis) |

## W1026. Vendor Product Discontinuation Notification & Last-Time Buy Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vendor notifies BuildRight of product discontinuation; or Category Manager identifies vendor intent to discontinue during quarterly business review |
| **Frequency** | ~30–50 discontinuation events/month (~360–600/year) across the 35,000 active SKU base |
| **Volume** | 1 SKU or SKU range per event; average 3–8 SKUs per discontinuation event |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Vendor, Buyer, Merchandise Planner, Supply Chain Planner, Store Operations Coordinator, Marketing Coordinator |

### Background

In the home improvement retail industry, vendors regularly discontinue products due to model updates, component obsolescence, regulatory changes, or strategic portfolio decisions. At BuildRight's scale (~35,000 active SKUs from ~800–1,000 vendors), approximately 5–10% of SKUs face discontinuation annually. Each discontinuation event triggers a cascade of operational decisions: (a) Is there a direct replacement product? If so, how do we transition? (b) How much "last-time buy" (LTB) inventory should we procure to cover the sell-through period? (c) What happens to existing store inventory and committed purchase orders? (d) How do we communicate the change to customers, especially trade accounts who depend on product continuity? (e) What happens to product listings on ecommerce, loyalty program-linked content, and project price books? Poorly managed discontinuations lead to stockouts (if transition is too fast), excess obsolete inventory (if LTB is too large), customer dissatisfaction (trade accounts find their regular product gone without notice), and margin erosion (clearance markdowns on orphaned inventory). This workflow is distinct from W68 (Product Lifecycle & Discontinuation), which covers BuildRight-initiated discontinuation; this workflow addresses vendor-initiated discontinuation where BuildRight must react.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Discontinuation Notification Receipt & Validation**: Vendor notifies BuildRight of product discontinuation via: (a) vendor portal (W868) — vendor uploads discontinuation notice with effective date and last-order date; (b) email/letter from vendor sales representative; (c) discovery during vendor QBR (W706) or JBP session (W155); (d) Buyer enters discontinuation notice into system: SKU(s) affected, vendor-stated reason, vendor last-order date (deadline for BuildRight to place final order), vendor last-ship date (final delivery date), and warranty/support commitment period for already-sold units; (e) system validates: (i) are there open POs for affected SKUs? (ii) current on-hand inventory at stores and DCs? (iii) is the SKU part of any active promotional plan, bundle (W910), or project price book (W163)? (iv) are there active B2B blanket purchase agreements (W979) referencing the SKU? | Buyer | Category Manager | 15–30 min |
| 2 | **Impact Assessment & Replacement Analysis**: Category Manager and Buyer assess impact: (a) **Sales impact**: system retrieves trailing 12-month sales velocity by store cluster, channel (in-store, ecommerce, B2B), and customer segment; calculate annualized revenue and margin impact; (b) **Customer impact**: identify trade accounts with recurring purchase history for the SKU; flag for proactive outreach; (c) **Replacement product**: (i) does vendor offer a direct successor model? if yes, evaluate specifications, pricing, and compatibility with existing customer applications; (ii) if no successor, can alternative vendor supply equivalent product? evaluate per W631 (Strategic Sourcing); (iii) if no equivalent exists, assess whether BuildRight should source private label replacement per W129; (d) **Inventory disposition plan**: (i) current on-hand + in-transit + on-order quantities vs. expected sell-through rate; (ii) if current inventory exceeds expected sell-through before discontinuation date: reduce/cancel open POs; (iii) if current inventory is insufficient: calculate last-time buy quantity | Category Manager / Buyer | VP Merchandising | 1–3 hours |
| 3 | **Last-Time Buy (LTB) Quantity Calculation & Order**: Merchandise Planner calculates LTB: (a) LTB quantity = (average monthly demand × months of coverage needed) − current on-hand − in-transit − confirmed open PO quantities; (b) months of coverage = min(12 months, remaining product lifecycle per replacement timeline); (c) adjust for seasonal demand patterns — cement demand peaks in dry season (Mar–Apr), paint peaks in Jan–Feb; (d) LTB must be placed before vendor's last-order date; (e) Buyer places LTB PO with vendor per W2; (f) for imported items with 45–90 day lead times, LTB must account for extended lead time; (g) system marks affected SKU(s) as "discontinued — sell-through" status: (i) no new replenishment orders will be generated by auto-replenishment (W2A); (ii) existing inventory available for sale until depleted; (iii) SKU removed from active assortment plan per W679 (Assortment Optimization) | Merchandise Planner / Buyer | Category Manager | 30–60 min |
| 4 | **Stakeholder Communication & Transition Execution**: (a) **Store Operations**: Store Operations Coordinator distributes discontinuation notice to all stores: SKU details, expected sell-through period, replacement product (if any), clearance timeline; stores begin positioning discontinued items for clearance per W93 (Markdown & Clearance Pricing); (b) **Ecommerce**: Marketing Coordinator updates buildright.com.ph: remove product listing once inventory is depleted; if replacement product exists, set up URL redirect from old product page to new product page for SEO preservation per W563; update product comparison tool per W940; (c) **B2B/Trade Accounts**: Account Manager proactively contacts trade accounts with purchase history for the SKU: notify of discontinuation, offer LTB opportunity, introduce replacement product with sample or demo per W1007; update project price books (W163) and blanket purchase agreements (W979) with replacement SKU; (d) **Loyalty/CRM**: update customer wishlist alerts per W934 if discontinued SKU was on wishlists; (e) **Service & Warranty**: update service center (W440) on parts and service availability for discontinued power tools per vendor warranty commitment period | Store Operations Coordinator / Marketing Coordinator / Account Manager | Category Manager | 2–4 hours |
| 5 | **Sell-Through Monitoring & Final Clearance**: Monthly monitoring: (a) system tracks sell-through rate of discontinued inventory vs. plan; (b) if sell-through is slower than expected: (i) progressive markdown schedule: 15% at month 3, 25% at month 4, 40% at month 5, 50%+ at month 6; (ii) bundle with complementary products per W910 to accelerate movement; (iii) offer to wholesale channel per W598 at bulk discount; (c) if any inventory remains after 6 months of sell-through period: system generates write-off request per W220 (SLOB Provisioning) with approval by Category Manager and Finance Controller; (d) upon final unit sold or written off: system updates SKU lifecycle status to "inactive," removes from store replenishment parameters (W312), and archives product data per W316 (Digital Asset Master) | Category Manager / Merchandise Planner | VP Merchandising | 30 min/month per event |

### System Touchpoints

## W1137. Vendor Purchase Order Line-Level Partial Shipment Acceptance & Backorder Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vendor delivers partial shipment against a multi-line purchase order, or vendor notifies of partial shipment capability |
| **Frequency** | ~360–480 partial shipments/month (~30–40% of the ~1,200 merchandise POs) |
| **Volume** | 2–5 PO lines affected per partial shipment |
| **Owner** | Buyer |
| **Participants** | Buyer, DC Receiving Team, Vendor, Supply Chain Planner |

### Background

BuildRight Depot issues ~1,200 merchandise POs/month with ~7 lines per PO (production-measured 6.9–7.4; ~8,400 merchandise lines, ~12,000 PO lines/month all types per profile §6.6). In practice, vendors frequently ship partial quantities: (a) some items are out-of-stock at vendor's warehouse; (b) production delays on specific SKUs; (c) logistics constraints — truck capacity limits or weight restrictions; (d) import shipments split across multiple containers arriving on different dates. Partial shipments create several operational challenges: (a) goods receipt complexity — receiving team must match delivered quantities against PO line quantities, not just total PO value; (b) backorder management — undelivered lines must be tracked with expected delivery dates; (c) AP invoice matching — vendor invoices may cover partial shipments, complicating 3-way match (W7); (d) inventory planning — partially fulfilled POs affect replenishment calculations and store availability; (e) cost allocation — freight cost must be allocated across partial deliveries. This workflow establishes systematic partial shipment processing at the PO line level, complementing W2 (PO cycle), W3 (warehouse receiving), W7 (AP invoice processing), and W56 (backorder management).

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Partial Shipment Notification & Pre-Receipt Planning**: (a) vendor notifies Buyer of partial shipment via vendor portal (W866) or email: which lines are shipping now, which lines are backordered, expected ship date for backordered lines; (b) system updates PO: shipped lines marked "In Transit," backordered lines marked "Backorder" with expected date; (c) if backorder will cause stockout at store level: Buyer evaluates options — source from alternate vendor (W631), transfer from another DC (W218), or adjust store replenishment allocation (W105); (d) DC receiving team notified of incoming partial shipment for dock scheduling (W585) | Buyer / System | VP Merchandising | 10–15 min |
| 2 | **Partial Goods Receipt Processing**: (a) DC Receiving Team processes delivery against PO: scan each received line, confirm quantity received vs. quantity ordered; (b) for fully received lines: system marks line as "Received Complete"; (c) for partially received lines: system records quantity received and marks remaining as backordered; (d) for lines not received at all: system confirms backorder status; (e) receiving team flags any quality issues (damaged packaging, wrong items) per W681 (DC quality control); (f) system updates inventory for received quantities only; (g) goods receipt document generated showing received vs. backordered quantities | DC Receiving Team / System | DC Operations Manager | 10–20 min per PO |
| 3 | **Backorder Tracking & Vendor Follow-Up**: (a) system generates backorder aging report: PO number, line item, quantity backordered, original promised date, revised expected date, days overdue; (b) Buyer reviews backorder report daily and follows up with vendors: confirm expected delivery date, escalate if overdue > 7 days, negotiate partial delivery of backordered quantity if possible; (c) for critical items (A-class SKUs representing 80% of revenue): Buyer escalates to VP Merchandising for vendor intervention; (d) system tracks backorder fulfillment rate per vendor — feeds into vendor performance scorecard (W706) | Buyer | VP Merchandising | 30–60 min/day |
| 4 | **Partial Invoice Processing & 3-Way Match**: (a) vendor submits invoice for partial shipment via vendor portal (W867); (b) AP team performs 3-way match (W7): PO line quantity ordered vs. GR quantity received vs. invoice quantity; (c) for partial shipments: system matches invoice only against received quantities; (d) backordered quantities not invoiced until delivered; (e) if vendor invoices for backordered quantities before delivery: AP rejects invoice line and notifies Buyer; (f) freight cost allocated proportionally across received quantities (not backordered quantities) | AP Team / System | Finance Manager | 5–10 min per invoice |
| 5 | **Backorder Resolution & PO Closure**: (a) when vendor ships backordered lines: goods receipt processed per Step 2; (b) if backorder extends beyond useful timeframe (seasonal item past season, or customer demand has shifted): Buyer cancels backorder with vendor, PO line closed; (c) if vendor cannot fulfill backorder: Buyer sources alternate supply per W631 or W670; (d) PO closed when all lines are either received complete, cancelled, or closed by Buyer; (e) final PO fulfillment rate calculated: total quantity received ÷ total quantity ordered, tracked per vendor for sourcing decisions | Buyer / System | VP Merchandising | 10–15 min |

### System Touchpoints
- ERP PO module — line-level status tracking (ordered, shipped, received, backordered, cancelled)
- Vendor portal (W866, W867) — partial shipment notification and invoice submission
- DC receiving module — goods receipt with line-level matching
- AP module (W7) — partial 3-way match processing
- Backorder management module (W56) — aging and tracking
- Vendor performance scorecard (W706) — backorder rate tracking
- Inventory planning module — backorder impact on replenishment
- Analytics module — PO fulfillment rate, backorder aging trends

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Communication gaps**: Vendors may not proactively notify of partial shipments; receiving team discovers discrepancy at dock, creating confusion
- **Backorder accumulation**: Chronic partial shipments from a vendor can accumulate significant backorder value (PHP 5M–10M across all POs), impacting inventory availability and working capital
- **Invoice complexity**: Multiple invoices for a single PO (one per partial shipment) increase AP processing workload and 3-way match complexity
- **Store impact**: Backorders that cause store stockouts may not be immediately visible to store operations; proactive notification to affected stores needed


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-update of "Partial Shipment Notification & Pre-Receipt Planning" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-scan of "scan each received line, confirm quantity received vs." (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews backorder report daily and follows up with vendors" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-247 (ensure controlled execution — Purchase Order Cycle (PA-03.2).)
- operational: Buyer review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **Buyer**: Backorder management adds ~1–2 hours/day to existing workload; with ~10 buyers, each manages ~30–50 backordered lines — absorbed by existing team with improved tooling
- **DC Receiving Team**: Partial receipt processing adds minimal incremental time (already part of GR process)
- **AP Team**: Partial invoice matching adds ~20% more invoice processing effort for partial shipments — absorbed by existing team
- **No permanent incremental headcount**

### Time Estimate
- Notification & planning: 10–15 min per partial PO
- Goods receipt: 10–20 min per PO
- Backorder follow-up: 30–60 min/day (all backorders)
- Partial invoice processing: 5–10 min per invoice
- PO closure: 10–15 min per PO
- **Total per partial shipment**: 35–60 min (excluding ongoing backorder follow-up)

## W1411. Import PO Customs Documentation Package Preparation, Broker Coordination & Compliance Checklist

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Import PO confirmed (W2B) and vendor ships goods; documentation package must be prepared before goods arrive at Philippine port |
| **Frequency** | ~400–600 TEUs/month (profile §7.1); each import PO typically requires one customs documentation package; ~20–30 import POs/month (many POs are multi-container) |
| **Volume** | ~20–30 documentation packages/month (1 per import PO per the Frequency); peak during pre-season build-up (Feb–Mar, Sep–Oct) |
| **Owner** | Customs Broker Coordinator (Finance/Supply Chain) |
| **Participants** | Customs Broker Coordinator, Buyer, Customs Broker (licensed), Vendor (documentation), Freight Forwarder, Treasury (LC/draft payment per W232) |

### Background

BuildRight imports ~40% of COGS from international vendors (China, Taiwan, Indonesia, Malaysia, Japan, Europe), requiring ~400–600 TEUs/month of containerized shipments. Philippine customs clearance requires a comprehensive documentation package for each import entry, and any missing or incorrect document can result in container detention at the port, demurrage charges (PHP 5,000–15,000/container/day per W249), customs examination, or seizure. The Philippines Bureau of Customs (BOC) operates under the Customs Modernization and Tariff Act (RA 10863), implemented through BOC Customs Administrative Orders, and import processing is facilitated through the E2M Customs system. This workflow ensures all required documents are prepared, verified, and submitted before vessel arrival.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Upon PO confirmation (W2B), Customs Broker Coordinator creates an import entry checklist for the PO: (a) Commercial Invoice (CI) — from vendor, (b) Packing List (PL) — from vendor, (c) Bill of Lading (B/L) or Airway Bill — from shipping line or freight forwarder, (d) Certificate of Origin (CO) — from vendor/chamber of commerce (required for ASEAN-Korea FTA, ASEAN-Japan CEPA, or other preferential tariff rates), (e) Import Permit / Clearance — from DTI-BPS for regulated products (steel, cement, electrical products per ICC/SOC requirements W447), (f) Sales Contract or Purchase Order — from BuildRight, (g) Insurance Certificate — if CIF terms; or BuildRight's open marine cargo policy if FOB terms, (h) Fumigation Certificate — for wooden packaging materials (ISPM-15 compliance), (i) Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS) — for paint, chemicals, solvents, (j) FDA Certification — for household hazardous substances (W479), (k) Letter of Credit documents — if LC payment terms (W232) | Customs Broker Coordinator | Supply Planning Manager | 30 min/PO |
| 2 | Customs Broker Coordinator contacts vendor to confirm documentation readiness: vendor is contractually required to send documents within 48 hours of vessel sailing; Coordinator sends reminder at vessel sailing and at 48-hour mark | Customs Broker Coordinator | Buyer | 15 min/PO |
| 3 | Vendor submits documents via email or vendor portal: Commercial Invoice, Packing List, Bill of Lading, Certificate of Origin; Customs Broker Coordinator logs receipt date and verifies completeness against checklist (step 1) | Customs Broker Coordinator | — | 15 min/PO |
| 4 | Customs Broker Coordinator reviews each document for accuracy: (a) CI total matches PO value, (b) CI item descriptions match PO item descriptions and HS codes, (c) PL quantities match CI quantities, (d) B/L container numbers match booking confirmation, (e) CO origin country matches vendor location and claimed preferential tariff, (f) HS code classification is correct per Philippine Tariff and Customs Code (TCCP) — incorrect HS codes result in duty underpayment (penalties) or overpayment (cash flow loss) | Customs Broker Coordinator | Customs Broker | 30 min/PO |
| 5 | If discrepancies found: Customs Broker Coordinator contacts vendor for corrected documents; if vendor cannot correct before vessel arrival: Coordinator works with Customs Broker to determine if document can be corrected during customs filing or if amendment is needed post-clearance (higher risk) | Customs Broker Coordinator | Buyer | 15–60 min |
| 6 | Customs Broker Coordinator delivers complete documentation package to licensed Customs Broker: (a) all original documents or certified true copies, (b) import entry data pre-filled in BOC E2M system format, (c) HS code recommendations with tariff rate and duty estimate, (d) payment instructions for duties and taxes (Treasury provides fund per W232) | Customs Broker Coordinator | Customs Broker | 15 min |
| 7 | Customs Broker files import entry with BOC through E2M system: system validates HS codes, calculates duties (customs duty + 12% VAT on CIF + duty + landed cost components), and generates assessment notice; Broker pays duties and taxes via BOC-accredited bank or electronic payment (ADB - Authorized Depository Bank) | Customs Broker | Customs Broker Coordinator | 1–2 days (filing + payment + BOC processing) |
| 8 | BOC releases cargo (green lane: auto-release; yellow lane: document review; red lane: physical examination); Coordinator tracks release status daily; if red lane: Broker coordinates physical examination with BOC examiner; examination adds 3–7 days to clearance time | Customs Broker / Customs Broker Coordinator | Supply Planning Manager | 1–7 days |
| 9 | Upon BOC release: Coordinator triggers port drayage and DC delivery (W1169); provides delivery instructions to freight forwarder with container number, DC destination, and delivery appointment; all customs documents archived in ERP document management for 10-year BIR retention (W256) | Customs Broker Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 30 min |
| 10 | Monthly: Customs Broker Coordinator generates import compliance dashboard: (a) average clearance time (vessel arrival to cargo release) by port of entry, (b) document completeness rate at first submission (% of POs with zero discrepancies), (c) demurrage cost incurred (from W249), (d) red lane examination frequency, (e) duty and tax payments by entity; dashboard shared with Supply Planning Manager and Controller | Customs Broker Coordinator | Controller | 2 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Import PO customs checklist: auto-generated from PO details with required documents listed based on origin country, product category (HS code), and regulatory requirements (W1411.1)
- Document receipt logging: date stamp and completeness tracking per PO per document type (W1411.3)
- HS code validation engine: CI item descriptions and HS codes checked against Philippine TCCP database with tariff rate calculation (W1411.4)
- E2M customs filing integration: import entry data pre-populated from PO and CI/PL documents (W1411.7)
- Customs clearance status tracker: real-time BOC lane assignment and release status per container (W1411.8)
- Import compliance dashboard: clearance time, document quality, demurrage, examination rate, duty payments (W1411.10)
- Integration with W2B (import PO — trigger), W232 (LC management — LC documents), W239 (customs duty reconciliation — duty calculation verification), W249 (port demurrage — cost tracking), W447 (DTI-BPS certification — import permit), W1169 (port drayage — delivery trigger), W256 (document archiving — retention)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Late vendor documentation**: international vendors (especially Chinese manufacturers) frequently send incomplete or inaccurate commercial invoices (wrong HS codes, mismatched quantities, missing certificates of origin); each round of document correction adds 1–3 days to the clearance timeline
- **Port congestion in Manila**: ~60% of import containers arrive at Manila International Container Port (MICP) or Manila South Harbor; these ports regularly experience congestion, extending container dwell time and demurrage costs regardless of documentation quality
- **HS code misclassification risk**: BuildRight imports 35,000 SKUs across 13 categories; incorrect HS code classification can result in duty underpayment (BOC audit liability up to 5 years back + penalties) or overpayment (permanent cash flow loss); Customs Broker expertise is critical; mitigated by the Customs Broker Coordinator (Finance/Supply Chain) review and approval gate
- **ASEAN FTA utilization gaps**: BuildRight may fail to claim preferential tariff rates under ASEAN trade agreements for eligible imports from Indonesia, Malaysia, and Vietnam, overpaying duties by 0–5% per container; Certificate of Origin must be obtained from the vendor's local chamber of commerce


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-document of "creates an import entry checklist for the PO" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-send of "send documents within 48 hours of vessel sailing" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "logs receipt date and verifies completeness against checklist" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-247 (ensure controlled execution — Purchase Order Cycle (PA-03.2).)
- operational: Customs Broker Coordinator (Finance/Supply Chain) review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
- Per PO documentation package: ~2–3 hours (checklist creation + document collection + review + broker delivery)
- Monthly volume: ~30 packages × 2.5 hours = ~75 hours/month
- Customs clearance tracking: ~15 min/day × 22 days = ~5.5 hours/month
- Monthly compliance dashboard: ~2 hours/month
- Total: ~80 hours/month (~4 hours/business-day)

### Staffing Implication
- Customs Broker Coordinator (1 FTE): ~80 hours/month ≈ half-time (≈45%), shared with import coordination; this is a dedicated position within the Supply Chain or Finance team
- Licensed Customs Broker (outsourced): filing and representation at BOC; retainer basis ~PHP 50,000–80,000/month
- No incremental BuildRight headcount beyond existing Customs Broker Coordinator role

## W5567. Procurement-Impersonation & Fake-PO Goods-Diversion Response

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | A supplier reports receiving BuildRight purchase orders nobody placed — fake PO documents, spoofed buyer email or chat instructions, or release/pickup directions to a carrier BuildRight never booked — or order-to-receipt reconciliation (W1006/W2) shows goods delivered to an address or collected by a party BuildRight never designated |
| **Frequency** | Rare but a recognized B2B fraud class: ~1–2 confirmed attempts per year across the ~800–1,000-vendor base, spiking after any public data exposure of buyer names, PO formats or delivery-site details |
| **Volume** | 1 event × the affected supplier's shipments (typically 1–5 truckloads of diverted goods per attempt) × the supplier-notification and verification wave across the exposed category's vendor list |
| **Owner** | Procurement Specialist (event owner); VP Legal & Compliance (impersonation & no-liability posture) |
| **Participants** | Buyer (category owner), Supplier (victim of the impersonation), AP/Finance (no genuine payable exists — the W770/W556 rails stay silent), VS-100 (fraudulent-document & demand response), IT Security (spoofed-domain/mailbox forensics per W383/W5518), Loss Prevention (W839 case where an insider fed the fraud), VS-27.3 (look-alike domain takedown), VS-63 (store receiving alert on diverted POs) |

### Background

The fraud class is the mirror image of the two impersonation events the corpus already owns: W5559 owns diverted *payments* on genuine obligations, and W5563 owns fake *sellers* defrauding consumers — but nobody owned fraudsters impersonating BuildRight as the *buyer*: fake purchase orders that carry BuildRight's name, PO format and delivery-site conventions order goods to a fraudster's address, picked up by a carrier BuildRight never booked. No BuildRight payment ever moves, so there is no bank recall to run (the W5559 machinery does not apply); the loss sits with the defrauded supplier, and the exposure is legal (suppliers pressing BuildRight on the "orders"), operational (genuine POs colliding with diverted ones in receiving) and reputational (BuildRight's brand used as the fraud instrument). The event needs an owner for the supplier-notification wave, the void-order posture, the goods-recovery assistance, and the PO-integrity retrofit that makes fake POs detectable at the supplier's end.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Verify & void**: confirm the reported PO carries a number/format BuildRight never issued (W2's numbering, buyer codes, site conventions); issue the supplier a written void-order notice on authenticated channels — fraudulent orders are void ab initio and create no BuildRight obligation | Procurement Specialist / VP Legal | VP Legal & Compliance | Same day |
| 2 | **Preserve & trace**: secure the fake documents, headers and pickup instructions; IT Security pulls mail-log evidence of spoofing or a compromised buyer mailbox (W383/W5518); trace the pickup carrier and delivery address with the supplier for recovery and the police report | IT Security / Procurement | CIO | 1–3 days |
| 3 | **Supplier wave**: notify the exposed category's vendor list through the vendor portal (W62-class channels) — BuildRight POs are validated through the portal only; never act on email-, chat- or phone-originated order or release changes; single verification contact published | Procurement / VS-03 portal admin | Procurement Manager | 3–5 days |
| 4 | **Receiving alert**: stores and DCs flagged to hold any inbound shipment matching the diverted POs (number, SKU set, carrier); matched loads documented and the supplier's genuine re-delivery prioritized | VS-63 / Store & DC Receiving | VP Supply Chain | Same window |
| 5 | **Law-enforcement & infrastructure action**: evidence pack to the PNP Anti-Cybercrime Group; look-alike domain and spoofed-mail infrastructure takedown via VS-27.3; fraudulent-document response under VS-100 | VP Legal / VS-27.3 | VP Legal & Compliance | 1–2 weeks |
| 6 | **Insider check**: where the fraud used genuine PO formats or unannounced site details, LP reviews access and conduct per W839 before the event closes | Loss Prevention / Internal Audit | VP Legal & Compliance | Per case |
| 7 | **PO-integrity retrofit**: portal-side PO authentication (digital order codes suppliers can self-verify), buyer-contact change protocol with dual-channel callback, and the supplier-verification clause added to W62-class contracts | Procurement / IT | CPO | 2–6 weeks |

### System Touchpoints

- PO numbering/sequence records evidencing which order numbers BuildRight issued (links to W2)
- Vendor-portal broadcast messaging with acknowledgment tracking for the supplier wave
- Receiving hold flags on diverted PO numbers at store/DC inbound queues (links to W1006)
- Mail-security evidence export (W383/W5518 class) and look-alike-domain takedown case (VS-27.3)
- Legal case file for the void-order notices, demand response and ACG referral (links to VS-100)
- Contract-clause update queue on the W62 template register

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Payment-assumption risk**: teams reflexively running the W5559 recall play that does not exist here — no BuildRight money moved; mitigated by Step 1's void-order posture and honest ledger (no genuine payable to freeze)
- **Supplier-relationship risk**: a defrauded supplier pressing BuildRight on the "orders" or shorting genuine replenishment; mitigated by Step 1's same-day authenticated void notice and Step 4's re-delivery priority
- **Recurrence risk**: the same operators re-hitting the category once attention fades; mitigated by Step 3's portal-only verification rule and Step 7's self-verifiable order codes
- **Insider-collusion risk**: fraud precision suggests internal sourcing of formats and schedules; mitigated by Step 6's W839 review before closure
- **Receiving collision risk**: diverted and genuine loads crossing at a dock; mitigated by Step 4's hold flags keyed to the diverted PO numbers

### Automation Opportunity

- PO-authentication codes rendered on every issued order, self-verifiable on the supplier portal (replaces trust in letterhead)
- Portal broadcast with per-supplier acknowledgment tracking for the verification wave (replaces manual Step 3 calls)
- Receiving hold list auto-published to inbound queues from the void-order register (replaces manual Step 4 notices)
- Mail-log anomaly query pack for spoofed-buyer patterns feeding W383 triage

### Controls

- CTL-247 (ensure controlled execution — Purchase Order Cycle (PA-03.2).)
- operational: suppliers are notified that orders validate through the portal only, with reported fake POs voided in writing within 24 hours; diverted PO numbers are hold-flagged at receiving until the event closes; any suspected insider dimension is referred to LP/W839 before closure

### Time Estimate

Per confirmed attempt ≈ 8–16 staff-hours for verification, void notices and the supplier wave plus 4–8 hours of IT-Security forensics and legal action; the ~1–2-attempts-per-year cadence stays absorbed within the procurement team, IT Security and Legal — no incremental headcount.

### Staffing Implication

- Absorbed by the procurement team, IT Security and Legal; no incremental headcount.
