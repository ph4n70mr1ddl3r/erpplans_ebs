## W1771. Installment Sale Transaction Recording & Revenue Recognition

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Consumer installment transaction completed at POS or ecommerce |
| **Frequency** | ~90,000–120,000 installment transactions/month (~3–5% of POS, per the canon; consumer card/BNPL stream — settled by the financing partner T+1–T+3, never collected by BuildRight; the in-house trade stream collected by BuildRight is the separate ~200–300-collections/month basis of W1773) |
| **Volume** | ~3–5% of all sales transactions |
| **Owner** | Revenue Accountant |
| **Participants** | Revenue Accountant, Cashier (in-store), System (ecommerce) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | POS records installment sale transaction: (a) revenue recognized at full selling price at point of sale (BuildRight sells to customer; financing partner pays BuildRight), (b) VAT output recorded at 12% on selling price, (c) cost of goods sold recorded (standard margin), (d) MDR (merchant discount rate) recorded as selling expense, (e) for third-party financing: BuildRight receives full amount from partner (net of MDR) within T+1 to T+3, (f) for in-house trade account installments: AR recognized per W1772 | System | — | Automated |
| 2 | Revenue recognition per PFRS 15: (a) control transfers to customer at point of sale (or delivery for ecommerce), (b) revenue = gross transaction amount, (c) MDR = transaction cost (selling expense), (d) no financing component in revenue (third-party financing), (e) for in-house deferred payment: assess if significant financing component exists (if payment term > 12 months, may need to discount revenue to present value) | Revenue Accountant | Accounting Manager | Monthly review |
| 3 | Daily reconciliation: installment transactions in POS vs. settlement expected from financing partners; discrepancies investigated within 24 hours | Revenue Accountant | Accounting Manager | 30 min/day |


### System Touchpoints
- POS installment sale recording — revenue at full selling price at point of sale (step 1)
- PFRS 15 revenue recognition — control transfer at POS (delivery for ecommerce), gross revenue, MDR as expense (step 2)
- Daily POS vs. partner-settlement reconciliation (step 3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Recognition-timing error**: revenue deferred over the installment term instead of at sale; mitigated by PFRS 15 point-of-sale recognition rules enforced in posting logic (step 2)
- **Settlement-mismatch blindness**: POS transactions and partner settlements drift apart unnoticed; mitigated by daily reconciliation with discrepancy investigation (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "records installment sale transaction" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-count of "discount revenue to present value" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-flag for investigation of "investigated within 24 hours" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-356 (ensure controlled execution — Installment Sale Processing & Monitoring (PA-38.2).)
- operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
Daily POS-vs-expected-settlement reconciliation 30 min ≈ 183 hours/year at the ~90,000–120,000 installment transactions/month canon (per W1771); monthly PFRS 15 revenue-recognition review within close (transaction posting and revenue recognition automated).

## W1772. In-House Installment Receivable Management (Trade Accounts)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Trade or corporate account customer requests installment payment terms for project purchase |
| **Frequency** | ~200–300 in-house installment arrangements active at any time |
| **Volume** | ~50–100 new arrangements/month |
| **Owner** | Credit Manager |
| **Participants** | Credit Manager, Sales Team, Customer, Accounting Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trade/B2B customer requests installment terms for large project: (a) contractor building a residential project: PHP 200K materials, requests 3-month payment, (b) developer outfitting 50 units: PHP 5M, requests 6-month payment, (c) government project: PHP 10M, requests payment upon project milestone | Sales Team | Credit Manager | During sales |
| 2 | Credit Manager evaluates request: (a) customer credit score and payment history per W16.1, (b) project viability assessment, (c) determines terms: down payment % (typically 20–30%), installment period (3–12 months), interest rate (if applicable per credit policy), (d) for amounts > PHP 500K: CFO approval required | Credit Manager | CFO (large amounts) | 1–2 days |
| 3 | Installment agreement executed: (a) signed agreement with payment schedule, (b) post-dated checks (PDCs) collected from customer per Philippine practice, (c) AR invoice created in system with installment schedule, (d) system auto-generates AR entries as each installment due date arrives | Credit Manager / Customer | Accounting Manager | 1 day |
| 4 | In-house installment receivable tracked in AR aging with separate classification from standard trade AR; collection monitored per W1773–1774 | AR Accountant | Credit Manager | Continuous |


### System Touchpoints
- Installment request intake for trade/B2B customers (step 1)
- Credit evaluation — W16.1 score, payment history, project viability (step 2)
- Agreement execution with payment schedule and post-dated checks (step 3)
- Separate in-house installment AR classification (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Unsecured-exposure risk**: the customer's PDCs bounce and the receivable is effectively unsecured; mitigated by credit evaluation plus monitoring under W1773/W1774 (steps 2–3)
- **Misclassification risk**: in-house installments hide inside standard trade AR, masking risk; mitigated by the separate AR classification (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-request of "requests installment terms for large project" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-validate of "Installment agreement executed" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-track of "tracked in AR aging with separate classification from standard trade AR" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-356 (ensure controlled execution — Installment Sale Processing & Monitoring (PA-38.2).)
- operational: Credit Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
~2–4 hours of Credit Manager effort per arrangement (credit evaluation over a 1–2-day elapsed window + agreement execution over 1 day) × ~50–100 new in-house arrangements/month ≈ 1,200–4,800 hours/year; receivable tracking continuous within AR aging (~200–300 active arrangements).

## W1773. Installment Payment Collection & Monitoring

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Installment due date (monthly for in-house installments) |
| **Frequency** | ~200–300 collections/month |
| **Volume** | ~PHP 50–100M in monthly in-house installment collections (the ~200–300 active trade arrangements of W1772, avg. arrangement PHP 1M–3M amortizing over 3–12 months; consumer card/BNPL installments are partner-settled per W1771 and are not collected by BuildRight) |
| **Owner** | AR Collector |
| **Participants** | AR Collector, Customer, Treasury |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System generates installment due reminders: (a) 5 days before due date: SMS/email reminder to customer, (b) due date: payment expected (PDC deposited or customer pays online), (c) 1 day past due: follow-up call from AR Collector | System / AR Collector | Credit Manager | Automated + manual |
| 2 | Payment received: (a) PDC deposited on due date; cleared in bank → system auto-applies to AR, (b) online payment (bank transfer, GCash) → Treasury applies to AR, (c) walk-in payment at store → cashier processes → system applies to AR, (d) system updates AR balance and generates receipt | Treasury / Cashier / System | AR Collector | Per payment |
| 3 | Monthly: AR Collector reviews installment portfolio: (a) payment adherence rate (% of installments paid on time), (b) upcoming large payments (flag for proactive follow-up), (c) customers with history of late payments (tighten terms on future purchases) | AR Collector | Credit Manager | 1 day/month |


### System Touchpoints
- Due-date reminder engine — 5-day SMS/email reminders (step 1)
- PDC deposit and auto-application to AR on clearing; online payment capture (step 2)
- Monthly portfolio review — adherence rate, upcoming large payments (step 3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **PDC-bounce risk**: post-dated checks dishonor on deposit; mitigated by the W1381 bounced-check recovery discipline and immediate re-scheduling demand (step 2)
- **Late-payment drift**: small delays normalize into delinquency; mitigated by adherence-rate monitoring with early-tier action (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-generate of "generates installment due reminders" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-update of "updates AR balance and generates receipt" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews installment portfolio" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-356 (ensure controlled execution — Installment Sale Processing & Monitoring (PA-38.2).)
- operational: AR Collector review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
PDC deposits and auto-application largely automated across ~200–300 monthly collections (~PHP 50–100M/month); exception handling at realized volume (dishonored checks per W1381); monthly portfolio adherence review 1 day ≈ 12 days/year.

## W1774. Installment Delinquency Tracking & Collection Action

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Installment payment > 30 days past due |
| **Frequency** | ~20–50 delinquent accounts/month |
| **Volume** | Target: < 5% delinquency rate |
| **Owner** | Credit Manager |
| **Participants** | Credit Manager, AR Collector, Legal Counsel, Customer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Delinquency tiers and actions: (a) 1–30 days past due: reminder calls and SMS; friendly follow-up; understand reason for delay (common: customer waiting for own project payment), (b) 31–60 days: formal demand letter; hold on new orders; Credit Manager calls customer; negotiate restructuring if customer has genuine difficulty, (c) 61–90 days: legal demand letter; restrict account; engage collection agency or Legal Counsel for legal action, (d) > 90 days: write-off consideration per policy; legal action; report to credit bureaus (if BuildRight participates) | AR Collector / Credit Manager | CFO | Per account |
| 2 | Restructuring: for customers with good history but temporary difficulty: (a) extend payment period, (b) reduce monthly amount, (c) waive late fees (if prompt payment resumes), (d) document restructured terms | Credit Manager | CFO | Per account |
| 3 | Write-off: accounts > 180 days delinquent and all collection efforts exhausted: (a) Credit Manager recommends write-off, (b) CFO approves (< PHP 500K) or CEO approves (> PHP 500K), (c) bad debt expense recorded, (d) account sent to external collection agency on contingency basis | Credit Manager | CFO | Per account |


### System Touchpoints
- Tiered delinquency actions — 1–30 days reminders; 31–60 demand; 60–90 legal (step 1)
- Restructuring options for good-history customers — extension, reduced amounts (step 2)
- Write-off approval — CFO authorization after exhaustion of efforts (step 3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Escalation-delay risk**: accounts age while collection stays "friendly"; mitigated by enforced tier timelines (step 1)
- **Premature-write-off risk**: recoverable accounts written off for convenience; mitigated by CFO approval gated on documented collection exhaustion (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "Delinquency tiers and actions" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-document of "document restructured terms" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-record of "recorded" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-356 (ensure controlled execution — Installment Sale Processing & Monitoring (PA-38.2).)
- operational: Credit Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Tiered collection handling ~20–60 min per delinquent account (reminder calls/SMS at 1–30 days, restructuring for good-history customers, write-off processing beyond 180 days per W556) × ~20–50 delinquent accounts/month ≈ 80–500 hours/year against the < 5% delinquency target.

## W1775. Installment Sale Returns & Cancellation Processing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer returns merchandise purchased on installment; or cancels order before delivery |
| **Frequency** | ~2–3% of installment transactions (~200–350 returns/month) |
| **Volume** | ~2–3% of installment transactions |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Cashier, Credit Manager, Financing Partner, Customer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customer requests return of item purchased on installment: (a) standard return policy applies (7-day return for non-defective items; longer for defective), (b) Store Manager verifies return eligibility per W1622 | Store Manager | — | 10 min |
| 2 | For third-party financed purchases: (a) return processed in POS; system sends refund instruction to financing partner, (b) financing partner reverses installment plan (or adjusts remaining balance), (c) MDR charged on original transaction may not be refundable — absorbed as cost, (d) if customer has already made installment payments: financing partner handles refund to customer | Cashier / System | Store Manager | 10 min |
| 3 | For in-house installment (trade accounts): (a) Credit Manager reviews return; if legitimate: credit note issued to reduce AR balance, (b) if return is partial: AR reduced by returned amount; remaining installments continue, (c) if return is full and customer made payments: credit note for payments received; any balance refunded | Credit Manager / Store Manager | Accounting Manager | 15 min |
| 4 | Inventory returned to stock per W1626 (disposition); cost of goods sold reversed | Receiving Clerk / System | — | 5 min |


### System Touchpoints
- Return policy application — 7-day non-defective window on installment purchases (step 1)
- Third-party refund instruction to the financing partner (step 2)
- In-house credit note reducing AR balance (step 3)
- Inventory disposition per W1626 with COGS reversal (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Refund-loop-breakage risk**: BuildRight refunds but the customer still owes the financing partner; mitigated by partner refund-instruction SLAs (step 2)
- **Credit-error risk**: credit notes issued for the wrong amount or against the wrong agreement; mitigated by Credit Manager review before posting (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-request of "requests return of item purchased on installment" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-send of "sends refund instruction to financing partner" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "For in-house installment" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-356 (ensure controlled execution — Installment Sale Processing & Monitoring (PA-38.2).)
- operational: Store Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
40 min per occurrence (per-step effort 10 min + 10 min + 15 min + 5 min) — at ~100–200 installment returns/year ≈ 100–200 occurrences/year ≈ 66.7–133 hours/year.

## W1776. Promotional Installment Subsidy Accounting

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | 0% interest promotion active; BuildRight subsidizes interest cost to financing partner |
| **Frequency** | ~6–10 promotional periods/year |
| **Volume** | ~PHP 5–15M in annual installment subsidies |
| **Owner** | FP&A |
| **Participants** | FP&A, Accounting Manager, VP Merchandising, Category Managers |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | During promotional period: (a) system records each 0% installment transaction at full selling price, (b) financing partner charges BuildRight a subsidy fee (higher MDR or explicit subsidy amount) to compensate for interest they forego, (c) subsidy cost recorded as promotional selling expense (allocated to the promoting category) | System | Accounting Manager | Automated |
| 2 | Monthly: FP&A tracks promotional subsidy cost: (a) total subsidy amount by promotion and category, (b) incremental sales generated by promotion vs. non-promotional period, (c) subsidy cost as % of incremental gross margin, (d) ROI: incremental margin ÷ subsidy cost (target: > 3x) | FP&A | CFO | 1 day/month during promos |
| 3 | Post-promotion: Accounting Manager reconciles subsidy charges from financing partner against system records; discrepancies resolved; final promotional P&L prepared for category review | Accounting Manager | CFO | 1 week post-promo |


### System Touchpoints
- 0% transaction recording at full price with partner subsidy charge capture (step 1)
- Monthly subsidy tracking by promotion and category vs. incremental sales (step 2)
- Post-promotion reconciliation of partner subsidy charges to system records (step 3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Hidden-subsidy-overspend risk**: subsidy cost exceeds the margin on promotional lift; mitigated by monthly subsidy-vs-incremental-sales tracking (step 2)
- **Subsidy-leakage risk**: partner subsidy charges never reconciled to transactions; mitigated by the post-promotion reconciliation (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "records each 0% installment transaction at full selling price" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-generate of "generated by promotion vs." (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "review" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-356 (ensure controlled execution — Installment Sale Processing & Monitoring (PA-38.2).)
- operational: FP&A review and approval gate; periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
During each promotional period: monthly subsidy tracking 1 day ≈ 6–10 days/year across ~6–10 promo periods; post-promotion reconciliation of partner subsidy charges 1 week per cycle at partial occupancy; in-transaction 0%-installment recording automated.

## W1777. Installment Sale Tax Compliance (VAT, WHT)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly tax filing |
| **Frequency** | Monthly |
| **Volume** | Part of regular tax filing |
| **Owner** | Tax Accountant |
| **Participants** | Tax Accountant, Revenue Accountant, CFO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | VAT on installment sales: (a) output VAT recognized at point of sale (full selling price × 12%), not over the installment period — per Philippine VAT rules (gross receipt is taxable upon sale, not upon collection), (b) for third-party financed sales: BuildRight receives net amount (gross minus MDR) but remits VAT on gross selling price, (c) input VAT on cost of goods sold claimed normally | Tax Accountant | CFO | Monthly |
| 2 | MDR VAT treatment: (a) MDR paid to financing partner includes 12% VAT, (b) BuildRight can claim input VAT on MDR as the service was used in the course of business, (c) financing partner issues VAT invoice for MDR charged | Tax Accountant | CFO | Monthly |
| 3 | In-house installment interest income (if applicable): (a) interest earned on in-house installment plans forms part of the gross selling price and is subject to VAT (12%), (b) interest income reported in monthly VAT return (BIR Form 2550M) | Tax Accountant | CFO | Monthly |


### System Touchpoints
- VAT on installment sales — output VAT at point of sale on full selling price × 12% (step 1)
- MDR input VAT claim — 12% VAT component of MDR fees (step 2)
- In-house interest income VAT treatment (step 3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **VAT-timing error**: output VAT spread over the installment term instead of recognized at sale; mitigated by point-of-sale VAT posting rules (step 1)
- **Missed-input-VAT risk**: recoverable input VAT on MDR never claimed; mitigated by VAT-inclusive MDR treatment in the monthly filing (step 2)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-collect of "VAT on installment sales" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-issue of "issues VAT invoice for MDR charged" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-report of "reported in monthly VAT return" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-208 (ensure consumer-financing settlement & compliance.)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
VAT on installment sales, MDR input-VAT recovery, and in-house interest-income treatments ride the monthly tax provision and filing cycle per W590 — no incremental standalone effort (computations system-supported).

## W1778. Installment Portfolio Reporting & Aging Analysis

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly management reporting |
| **Frequency** | Monthly |
| **Volume** | 1 report covering all in-house installment receivables |
| **Owner** | Credit Manager |
| **Participants** | Credit Manager, FP&A, CFO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Monthly installment portfolio report: (a) total installment receivable outstanding, (b) aging: current, 1–30 days, 31–60 days, 61–90 days, > 90 days, (c) aging as % of total portfolio, (d) provision for bad debts: current provision rate × aging buckets (e.g., 1% current, 5% for 31–60, 25% for 61–90, 50% for > 90), (e) top 20 largest installment accounts with status, (f) write-offs for the month, (g) new installment arrangements for the month | Credit Manager / AR Collector | CFO | 1 day/month |
| 2 | CFO reviews portfolio health: (a) target: > 95% of portfolio current (not past due), (b) if delinquency rate increasing: tighten credit approval criteria, (c) if portfolio growing rapidly: assess concentration risk (single customer or project > 10% of portfolio) | CFO | CEO | 30 min/month |
| 3 | Quarterly: CFO presents installment portfolio summary to Board as part of financial review | CFO | Board | Quarterly |


### System Touchpoints
- Monthly installment portfolio report — outstanding, aging tiers, delinquency rate (step 1)
- CFO health review — > 95% current target with tightening triggers (step 2)
- Quarterly Board summary within the financial review (step 3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Deterioration-blindness risk**: delinquency worsens gradually between reviews; mitigated by monthly aging trend against the 95%-current target (steps 1–2)
- **Re-aging-concealment risk**: restructured accounts look cured; mitigated by tracking restructures separately from genuine cures (step 1)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "Monthly installment portfolio report" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "reviews portfolio health" (replaces manual Step 2).

### Controls
- CTL-356 (ensure controlled execution — Installment Sale Processing & Monitoring (PA-38.2).)
- operational: Credit Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
recurring effort 30 min/month ≈ 6 hours/year; cadence-carried steps as authored (1 day/month, Quarterly).
