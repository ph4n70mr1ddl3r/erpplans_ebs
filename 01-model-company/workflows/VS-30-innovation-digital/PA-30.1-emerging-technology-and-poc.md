## W202. Predictive Maintenance for Industrial Assets

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Sensor alert from critical equipment (e.g., DC Conveyor, Generator, Forklift) |
| **Frequency** | Real-time monitoring |
| **Volume** | Covers 200 stores + 4 DCs with critical equipment |
| **Owner** | Maintenance Manager |
| **Participants** | IT (IoT Team), Maintenance, Vendor |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **IoT Monitoring**: Sensors track vibration, temperature, and duty cycles of DC assets | System | — | Real-time |
| 2 | **Anomaly Detection**: ML model identifies patterns preceding failure (e.g., "Motor temp rising > 10% above norm") | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | **Predictive Work Order**: System auto-generates Work Order in W188; flags as "Predictive - High Urgency" before failure occurs | System | Maintenance Mgr | Automated |
| 4 | **Execution**: Technician performs targeted repair (e.g., bearing replacement) instead of waiting for scheduled PM | Technician | — | 1 hour |
| 5 | **Validation**: Technician confirms sensor data returned to normal; closes Work Order | Technician | — | 10 min |

### System Touchpoints

- IoT Gateway for sensor data ingestion (Step 1)
- ML anomaly detection engine hosted on cloud or edge compute (Step 2)

- Cloud computing costs for AI workloads scale with the number of monitored assets across 4 DCs and 200 stores.
- Skills gap for AI/ML talent in Philippines limits the team's ability to tune anomaly detection models without external vendor support.

## W690. Digital Transformation Initiative Portfolio Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual strategic planning; departmental digital initiative proposal; technology investment request |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly portfolio review; continuous intake |
| **Volume** | ~15–20 active digital initiatives at any time; ~5–8 new proposals/quarter |
| **Owner** | CIO (Digital Transformation Lead) |
| **Participants** | Department Heads (business sponsors), Data Science Lead, IT Project Manager, Finance (investment review), CEO (strategic alignment) |

### Background

BuildRight has committed to digital transformation across its operations (AI personalization W200, RPA W201, computer vision W203, predictive maintenance W202, retail analytics W208). However, digital initiatives often compete for the same limited resources (Data Science team, IT development capacity, cloud computing budget) and must be prioritized against each other based on strategic value, feasibility, and resource availability. This workflow manages the portfolio of digital initiatives from ideation through implementation using a stage-gate process.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Initiative intake**: Department Head submits digital initiative proposal via innovation portal: business problem, proposed technology solution, expected benefits (revenue increase, cost reduction, customer experience improvement), estimated investment (PHP), timeline, resource requirements, alignment to strategic priorities (ecommerce growth, operational efficiency, customer experience) | Department Head | CIO | 2-4 hours |
| 2 | **Feasibility assessment**: IT evaluates technical feasibility: (a) technology maturity assessment (proven, emerging, experimental); (b) data availability and quality check — does required data exist and is it clean enough; (c) integration complexity with existing ERP and systems; (d) security and privacy implications per RA 10173; (e) resource requirements: Data Science team capacity, IT development effort, cloud computing cost estimate | IT Business Analyst | Data Science Lead | 2-3 days |
| 3 | **Business case development**: Finance and Department Head develop business case: (a) total cost of ownership (development, cloud compute, ongoing maintenance, training); (b) projected benefits with assumptions and sensitivity analysis; (c) NPV and payback period calculation; (d) risk assessment (technology risk, adoption risk, regulatory risk) | Finance Analyst / Department Head | CFO | 2-3 days |
| 4 | **Stage-gate review**: CIO convenes Digital Transformation Steering Committee (CIO, CFO, COO, relevant VP): (a) present proposal, feasibility assessment, and business case; (b) score initiative on: strategic alignment (1-5), ROI potential (1-5), feasibility (1-5), risk level (1-5); (c) gate decision: Approve (proceed to development), Defer (re-evaluate next quarter), Reject (with rationale), Pivot (modify scope/approach); (d) approved initiatives added to portfolio with priority ranking | CIO | CEO | 2 hours per proposal |
| 5 | **Portfolio balancing**: quarterly, CIO reviews active portfolio for balance: (a) mix of quick wins (<3 month payback) vs. strategic bets (longer-term transformational value); (b) resource allocation: ensure Data Science team capacity is not over-committed; (c) budget utilization: track actual cloud computing and development spend vs. approved budget; (d) identify and cancel underperforming initiatives (those missing milestones or with degraded business case) | CIO | CEO | 4 hours/quarter |
| 6 | **Implementation oversight**: for approved initiatives, IT Project Manager manages execution: (a) agile sprint planning for development work; (b) UAT with business users; (c) change management and user training; (d) go-live and hypercare; (e) post-implementation benefits realization tracking (compare actual benefits to business case projections at 3, 6, 12 months) | IT Project Manager | CIO | Ongoing per initiative |
| 7 | **Portfolio reporting**: quarterly, CIO presents Digital Transformation Dashboard to Executive Committee: initiative status (on-track, at-risk, delayed), budget utilization, benefits realized vs. projected, portfolio health score, upcoming pipeline | CIO | CEO | 2 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints

- Innovation portal (proposal intake, tracking)
- Project portfolio management (PPM) tool
- Stage-gate workflow with scoring rubric
- Resource management (Data Science team capacity planning)
- Cloud cost management integration (W596 FinOps)
- BI dashboard for portfolio health reporting
- Benefits realization tracking module

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Resource contention**: Data Science team (estimated 3-5 FTE) cannot support all 15-20 initiatives simultaneously; portfolio management must ruthlessly prioritize
- **Scope creep**: digital initiatives tend to expand beyond original scope as new possibilities emerge during development; stage-gate discipline required
- **Benefits realization gap**: projected benefits in business cases often not achieved; requires rigorous post-implementation tracking and accountability
- **Technology hype cycle**: emerging technologies (generative AI, IoT, blockchain) may be proposed before they are mature enough for BuildRight's operational context; feasibility assessment must be objective


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "technology solution, expected benefits" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-validate of "check — does required data exist and is it clean enough" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "score initiative on: strategic alignment" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-223 (ensure responsible AI/ML & pilot governance.)
- operational: CIO (Digital Transformation Lead) review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication

- **CIO**: ~8–12 hours/quarter on portfolio reviews and steering committee. Absorbed within existing role.
- **IT Project Manager**: full-time on managing active initiatives.
- **Finance Analyst**: ~4–6 hours/quarter on business case reviews. Absorbed within existing role.
- **No incremental headcount** beyond existing IT Project Manager.

### Time Estimate

- Proposal development: 2-4 hours
- Feasibility + business case: 4-6 days
- Stage-gate review: 2 hours per proposal
- Quarterly portfolio review: 4 hours
- Benefits realization tracking: 2 hours/quarter per completed initiative

## W691. Emerging Technology Scouting & Proof-of-Concept Evaluation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual technology landscape review; industry conference or trade show insight; competitor technology adoption report; vendor technology briefing |
| **Frequency** | Continuous scouting; quarterly evaluation cycle; 2-3 proof-of-concept (POC) evaluations/year |
| **Volume** | ~10–15 technology trends monitored; 2-3 POCs conducted per year |
| **Owner** | CIO |
| **Participants** | CIO, Data Science Lead, Department Heads (business validators), External Technology Partners |

### Background

The retail hardware industry is being transformed by technologies such as autonomous mobile robots (AMRs) for warehouse picking, RFID for real-time inventory visibility, augmented reality (AR) for in-store product visualization, generative AI for customer service, and blockchain for supply chain traceability. BuildRight must systematically evaluate these technologies to determine which are ready for adoption, which require further maturation, and which are not applicable to the Philippine retail hardware context.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Technology landscape monitoring**: CIO continuously monitors emerging technologies relevant to retail hardware: (a) subscribe to industry analyst reports (Gartner, Forrester, McKinsey retail technology); (b) attend 2-3 retail technology conferences/year (NRF, Retail Asia, local tech summits); (c) monitor competitor technology adoption (via W624 competitor intelligence); (d) track Philippine tech ecosystem: DOST grants, startup innovation, university research | CIO | CIO | Ongoing (~4 hours/month) |
| 2 | **Technology radar update**: quarterly, CIO updates BuildRight Technology Radar: (a) **Adopt** (proven, ready for production — e.g., RPA, basic ML); (b) **Trial** (promising, needs POC — e.g., RFID, AMR); (c) **Assess** (worth monitoring — e.g., AR for retail, generative AI); (d) **Hold** (not recommended — e.g., blockchain for retail supply chain in PH context, VR for home improvement); present radar to CIO and Digital Transformation Steering Committee | CIO | CIO | 4 hours/quarter |
| 3 | **POC proposal**: for technologies in "Trial" quadrant, CIO develops POC proposal: (a) hypothesis to test (e.g., "RFID will reduce store cycle count time by 50% while improving accuracy to 99%"); (b) scope: 1-2 stores or 1 DC zone for 30-60 days; (c) success criteria: measurable KPIs with pass/fail thresholds; (d) estimated cost: hardware, software, vendor support, internal resource time; (e) risk mitigation: no disruption to live operations during POC | CIO | CIO | 1-2 days |
| 4 | **POC execution**: (a) configure technology in isolated test environment or limited production scope; (b) baseline measurement: record current KPIs before POC starts; (c) run POC for 30-60 days; (d) collect quantitative data (time savings, accuracy improvement, cost reduction) and qualitative feedback from users; (e) CIO monitors POC progress weekly | IT Business Analyst | CIO | 30-60 days per POC |
| 5 | **POC evaluation & decision**: after POC completion: (a) compare results against success criteria; (b) document findings: what worked, what didn't, unexpected results; (c) total cost of ownership analysis for full deployment; (d) decision: Scale (proceed to full deployment via W690), Extend POC (more testing needed), Shelve (technology not ready), or Partner (seek vendor partnership for custom solution); (e) present evaluation to Digital Transformation Steering Committee | CIO | CIO | 2-3 days per POC |
| 6 | **Knowledge management**: all POC results documented in technology knowledge base: technology description, POC scope and results, decision rationale, applicable business context; accessible to all Department Heads for future reference; prevents duplicate POCs and enables informed technology decisions | IT Business Analyst | CIO | 2 hours per POC |

### System Touchpoints

- Technology radar visualization tool
- POC management module (scope, timeline, KPI tracking)
- Knowledge base for technology evaluation results
- Integration with W690 portfolio management for POC-to-project transition
- Vendor management per W36 for technology vendor evaluation
- Budget management integration per W677

### Pain Points / Risks

- **POC cost escalation**: technology vendors may offer free/discounted POCs but charge premium pricing for full deployment; TCO analysis must account for full production costs
- **POC-to-production gap**: results from a controlled 2-store POC may not scale to 200 stores with varying conditions (connectivity, staff capability, store layout)
- **Philippine infrastructure constraints**: technologies that work in developed markets (e.g., IoT sensors requiring reliable broadband) may not work reliably across all BuildRight locations (provincial stores with intermittent connectivity)
- **Vendor lock-in risk**: POCs that deeply integrate with a specific vendor's technology may create switching costs that limit future flexibility; mitigated by CTL-331 (controlled execution — Emerging Technology & PoC (PA-30.1))


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "monitors emerging technologies relevant to retail hardware" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-monitor of "updates BuildRight Technology Radar" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-track of "develops POC proposal" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-331 (ensure controlled execution — Emerging Technology & PoC (PA-30.1).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication

- **CIO**: ~8–12 hours/month on technology scouting and POC oversight. Absorbed within existing role.
- **IT Business Analyst**: ~20–40 hours per POC (setup, monitoring, evaluation). Absorbed within existing role.
- **POC hardware/software costs**: budgeted per POC (PHP 500K-2M per POC depending on technology).

### Time Estimate

- POC execution: 30-60 days (IT oversight ~2–4 hours/week)
- POC evaluation: 2-3 days
- Knowledge documentation: 2 hours per POC

## W1457. RFID-Based Smart Shelf Inventory Monitoring & Automated Replenishment Trigger PoC

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual innovation portfolio review per W690; or Category Manager identifies high-value category with chronic stockout issues |
| **Frequency** | PoC planning: quarterly; PoC execution: 3–6 month pilot; post-PoC evaluation: at pilot completion |
| **Volume** | PoC scope: 1 flagship store, power tools category (~1,750 SKUs, unit value PHP 3,000–25,000) |
| **Owner** | IT Innovation Lead |
| **Participants** | IT Innovation Lead, Store Manager (pilot store), Category Manager (power tools), RFID Vendor, CIO, Finance (CAPEX) |

### Background

BuildRight's 200 stores face persistent inventory accuracy challenges in high-value categories: power tools (1,750 SKUs, PHP 3,000–25,000 unit value) and hardware fasteners (4,900 SKUs with high shrinkage per W23.3). Current cycle counting per W13 targets 97% accuracy but high-value categories often fall below this due to theft and miscounting. RFID technology enables real-time inventory visibility: embedded RFID antennas continuously monitor stock levels and automatically trigger replenishment alerts while detecting unusual removal patterns indicating theft. This workflow manages the end-to-end PoC from planning through evaluation.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **PoC planning & vendor selection**: Select pilot store (highest power tools shrinkage in Metro Manila); evaluate 3 RFID vendors on read accuracy in metal-heavy retail, Philippine experience, ERP integration, cost per tag (target ≤ PHP 15); define success criteria: inventory accuracy ≥ 99%, theft detection improvement ≥ 30% | IT Innovation Lead | CIO | 2–3 weeks |
| 2 | **Infrastructure installation**: RFID vendor installs smart shelf antennas on 8 shelving units, backroom portal reader, handheld reader, and middleware server; tag all existing power tools inventory (~1,500 units) | RFID Vendor / IT Innovation Lead | Store Manager | 1 week |
| 3 | **Baseline measurement**: Wall-to-wall physical count of power tools; record stockout rate, replenishment lead time, shrinkage rate, and cycle counting labor hours | IT Innovation Lead / Store Manager | — | 1 day |
| 4 | **PoC execution (3–6 months)**: Smart shelves continuously read tags; auto-generate replenishment alerts to Stock Associate handheld; detect potential theft (item leaves shelf without POS transaction); flag misplaced items; weekly accuracy review vs. baseline | System / IT Innovation Lead | CIO | Ongoing |
| 5 | **Post-PoC evaluation**: Compile results: accuracy improvement, stockout reduction, shrinkage reduction, labor savings, false positive rate on theft alerts; calculate ROI; recommend expand/extend/discontinue | IT Innovation Lead | CIO | 1–2 weeks |
| 6 | **Scale-up planning (if approved)**: Phased rollout to top 20 high-shrinkage stores, then all stores; negotiate volume pricing (tag target ≤ PHP 8); integrate with replenishment engine per W4; CAPEX per W21 | IT Innovation Lead / CIO | CFO | 2–3 months |

### System Touchpoints
- RFID middleware with shelf-level inventory tracking; Automated replenishment alert integration with handheld devices; Theft detection integration with LP system per W23.1; PoC metrics dashboard with ROI calculation; Integration with W13, W4, W23.1, W691, W690

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Metal interference**: power tools' metal content reduces RFID read accuracy to 85–90%; specialized metal-mount tags cost 3–5× more
- **Tag cost at scale**: PHP 10–15/tag × 2.8M units = PHP 28M–42M/year
- **False theft alerts**: customers browsing trigger movement alerts; may overwhelm Store Managers
- **Philippine vendor support**: limited local RFID infrastructure; equipment downtime waiting for overseas technicians


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-detect of "detection improvement ≥ 30%" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-record of "record stockout rate, replenishment lead time, shrinkage rate, and cycle…" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-review of "review vs." (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-331 (ensure controlled execution — Emerging Technology & PoC (PA-30.1).)
- operational: IT Innovation Lead review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- IT Innovation Lead: ~40 hours/month during pilot; Store Manager: ~3 hours/week during pilot. Absorbed.
- No incremental headcount.

### Time Estimate
- **Total PoC**: ~200–300 hours over 6 months

## W1458. Autonomous Mobile Robot (AMR) Goods-to-Person Picking PoC for DC Operations

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual innovation portfolio review per W690; or DC Manager identifies pick productivity bottleneck during peak season |
| **Frequency** | PoC planning: quarterly; PoC execution: 3–6 month pilot; post-PoC evaluation: at completion |
| **Volume** | PoC scope: DC3 (South Luzon Hub, 40,000 sqm); pilot zone: 1 aisle of tiles/plumbing SKUs (~800 SKUs) |
| **Owner** | IT Innovation Lead |
| **Participants** | IT Innovation Lead, DC Manager (DC3), AMR Vendor, CIO, VP Supply Chain, Finance (CAPEX) |

### Background

BuildRight's 4 DCs process ~5,000 store replenishment orders/month. Current picking is RF-directed (person-to-goods model) where Stock Associates walk 12–15 km/shift. AMR goods-to-person picking (robots bring shelves to stationary pick stations) can improve pick productivity by 50%+, reduce walking by 70%+, and accelerate fulfillment. This PoC at DC3 evaluates whether AMR technology delivers measurable improvements for BuildRight's DC operations, particularly for medium-velocity SKUs (tiles, plumbing) where pick density is lower and walking distances are longer.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **PoC planning**: Define pilot zone (~500 sqm, 800 SKUs); evaluate 3 AMR vendors (Geek+, HAI Robotics, Locus) on navigation, payload (tiles weigh 20–30 kg — AMR must handle 50 kg+), pick station ergonomics, WMS integration, Philippine support, unit cost (PHP 500K–1.5M each); success criteria: pick productivity ≥ +50%, walking reduction ≥ 70%, accuracy ≥ 99.5%, ROI payback ≤ 3 years | IT Innovation Lead | CIO | 2–3 weeks |
| 2 | **Pilot zone setup**: Deploy 3–5 AMR units in pilot aisle; install pick station; map existing layout into AMR fleet software (no infrastructure changes); integrate with WMS; train 3 DC Stock Associates as pick station operators | AMR Vendor / DC Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2 weeks |
| 3 | **Baseline measurement**: Measure current RF-directed picking: units/labor hour, pick cycle time, walking distance/shift, pick accuracy for pilot zone SKUs | IT Innovation Lead / DC Ops Supervisor | — | 3 days |
| 4 | **PoC execution (3–6 months)**: AMR transports shelf pods to pick station; operator scans/packs items per WMS instructions; AMR returns pod and retrieves next; system measures units/hour, utilization, queue time, accuracy, uptime; weekly metrics review | System / DC Manager | CIO | Ongoing |
| 5 | **Post-PoC evaluation**: Compile results: productivity vs. baseline, AMR uptime, operator satisfaction; calculate full DC ROI (20–30 units × PHP 500K–1.5M = PHP 15M–60M/DC); recommend expand/extend/discontinue | IT Innovation Lead | CIO | 1–2 weeks |
| 6 | **Scale-up (if approved)**: Phased rollout to DC3, DC1/DC2, DC4; labor redeployment plan for displaced pickers; CAPEX per W21 | IT Innovation Lead / VP Supply Chain | CFO | 2–3 months |

### System Touchpoints
- AMR fleet management with WMS integration; Pick station workstation with WMS scanning; AMR telemetry dashboard; Baseline vs. AMR comparison engine; Integration with W4, W15, W690, W691

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Heavy payload for tiles**: tiles weigh 20–30 kg; AMR pods may exceed 200 kg; cost increases with payload capacity
- **Philippine labor vs. automation ROI**: DC labor at PHP 15K–20K/month is low by global standards; ROI depends on productivity improvement more than labor savings
- **Vendor support in Philippines**: Chinese AMR vendors (Geek+, HAI Robotics) may have limited Philippine support

## W1569. Computer Vision-Based Shelf Gap Detection, Planogram Compliance Monitoring & Automated Replenishment Alerting PoC

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual innovation portfolio review per W690; or persistent stockout issue identified in store analytics per W1567; or vendor co-innovation proposal |
| **Frequency** | PoC planning: quarterly; PoC execution: 3-month pilot; post-PoC evaluation: at completion |
| **Volume** | PoC scope: 5 pilot stores (mix of Metro Manila and provincial); coverage: power tools and hardware fastener aisles (~500 SKUs per store) |
| **Owner** | IT Innovation Lead |
| **Participants** | IT Innovation Lead, Computer Vision Vendor, Store Manager (pilot stores), Department Supervisor, Category Manager (Tools/Hardware), COO |

### Background

Out-of-stock (OOS) items on store shelves represent lost sales and customer frustration. With 35,000 active SKUs across 200 stores, manual shelf auditing (Stock Associates visually checking shelves) is labor-intensive and infrequent — a shelf gap between the last audit and the next may persist for hours or days, during which customers encounter empty slots and either leave or buy a substitute. Computer vision technology, deployed via existing store CCTV cameras or dedicated shelf-facing cameras, can detect shelf gaps (empty slots where product should be), flag planogram non-compliance (product in wrong position), and automatically trigger replenishment alerts to Stock Associates' handheld devices. This PoC evaluates the accuracy and business value of computer vision-based shelf monitoring in BuildRight's warehouse-format store environment.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **PoC planning**: Define pilot scope and success criteria: (a) pilot stores: 5 stores (2 Metro Manila, 1 Cebu, 1 Davao, 1 North Luzon — mix of store sizes and lighting conditions); (b) pilot aisles: power tools section (high-value items, manageable SKU count ~150) and hardware fastener wall (high-SKU density, small items ~350 SKUs); (c) technology options: (i) retrofit existing CCTV cameras with computer vision software (lower cost, lower image quality for shelf-level detail), (ii) dedicated shelf-facing cameras per aisle (higher cost, higher accuracy); (d) success criteria: (i) shelf gap detection accuracy ≥ 90% (verified by manual audit), (ii) planogram compliance detection accuracy ≥ 85%, (iii) alert-to-replenishment time reduction ≥ 50% vs. current manual process, (iv) estimated ROI payback ≤ 2 years for chain-wide rollout | IT Innovation Lead | CIO | 2–3 weeks |

| 6 | **Scale-up (if approved)**: Phased rollout: Phase 1 — high-value aisles (power tools, appliances) across all 200 stores; Phase 2 — full store coverage; integrate with replenishment system per W4 for automated restock order generation | IT Innovation Lead / COO | CFO | 6–12 months |

## W1570. In-Store Customer Navigation & Wayfinding Mobile App PoC, Indoor Positioning & Product Location Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual innovation portfolio review per W690; or customer feedback indicating difficulty finding products in large-format stores; or ecommerce BOPIS pickup time optimization initiative |
| **Frequency** | PoC planning: quarterly; PoC execution: 3-month pilot; post-PoC evaluation: at completion |
| **Volume** | PoC scope: 5 pilot stores; target: customers using BuildRight mobile app for in-store navigation during pilot |
| **Owner** | IT Innovation Lead |
| **Participants** | IT Innovation Lead, Indoor Positioning Vendor, Ecommerce Manager, Store Manager (pilot stores), UX Designer, GM, Digital Commerce Inc. |

### Background

BuildRight's big-box warehouse-format stores range from 8,000–15,000 sqm of selling area with departments spanning lumber yards, tile galleries, plumbing sections, electrical aisles, and tool walls. First-time customers and infrequent visitors often struggle to find specific products — "Where are the PVC pipes?" or "I need 10mm bolts, which aisle?" Customer surveys indicate that 20–30% of walk-in customers ask staff for directions, consuming Sales Associate time and creating a suboptimal customer experience. An in-store navigation feature within BuildRight's existing mobile app (W615) would allow customers to: (a) search for a product and see its exact location on an interactive store map, (b) get turn-by-turn walking directions to the product (like Google Maps indoors), (c) find related products nearby ("Customers who bought PVC pipes also bought pipe cement — it's in the next aisle"), and (d) navigate to the BOPIS pickup counter for online orders. This PoC evaluates indoor positioning technology (Bluetooth beacons or Wi-Fi fingerprinting) and the customer value of in-store wayfinding.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **PoC planning**: Evaluate indoor positioning technologies: (a) Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) beacons: install beacons throughout store at ~5-meter intervals; accuracy: 1–3 meters; cost: PHP 500–1,000/beacon × ~200 beacons/store = PHP 100–200K/store; (b) Wi-Fi fingerprinting: use existing store Wi-Fi access points; accuracy: 3–5 meters; lower hardware cost but lower accuracy; (c) select technology: BLE beacons for higher accuracy (recommendation); (d) pilot stores: 5 stores with varying layouts; (e) success criteria: (i) positioning accuracy ≤ 3 meters for 90% of readings, (ii) customer adoption: > 10% of app users use navigation feature during pilot, (iii) customer satisfaction: > 80% find the feature useful, (iv) reduction in "where is..." staff inquiries ≥ 20% | IT Innovation Lead | CIO | 2–3 weeks |
