## W689. AI/ML Model Governance, Bias Audit & Ethical Review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New AI/ML model deployment request; quarterly model review cycle; incident involving AI decision (customer complaint, pricing error, bias allegation) |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly model review; ad-hoc for new deployments and incidents |
| **Volume** | ~8–12 production AI/ML models (personalization W200, demand forecasting W1206, CV planogram W1339, fraud detection, credit scoring, churn prediction W618, dynamic pricing, RPA bots W201); ~2–4 new model deployments/year |
| **Owner** | Data Science Lead |
| **Participants** | CIO, Legal Counsel (privacy), Department Head (business owner), Internal Audit, Ethics Committee (for high-impact models) |

### Background

BuildRight operates multiple AI/ML models that directly affect customers (personalization, pricing, credit scoring) and operations (demand forecasting, inventory optimization, fraud detection). The Philippine Data Privacy Act (RA 10173) and NPC circulars require accountability for automated decision-making that affects individuals. The National AI Strategy (Department of Science and Technology) promotes responsible AI adoption. This workflow establishes governance, periodic bias auditing, and ethical review to prevent harm from AI systems and maintain regulatory compliance.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Model inventory & classification**: Data Science Lead maintains AI/ML model registry: model name, business owner, input data sources, output decisions, affected stakeholders (customers, employees, vendors), risk classification (Low/Medium/High/Critical based on impact of wrong decision); update registry quarterly | Data Science Lead | CIO | 2 hours/quarter |
| 2 | **Pre-deployment ethics review**: for new models classified Medium or above — (a) Data Science Lead presents model to Ethics Committee: purpose, data inputs, decision logic, potential harms (discrimination, privacy violation, financial harm); (b) Legal reviews NPC compliance: automated decision-making per RA 10173, DPIA if processing personal data; (c) Ethics Committee approves, conditions, or rejects deployment; conditions may include: monitoring requirements, bias testing thresholds, human-override capability | Data Science Lead / Legal | Ethics Committee | 1-2 days per model |
| 3 | **Quarterly bias audit**: for all High/Critical models — (a) run fairness metrics across protected attributes (geography, socioeconomic indicators proxied by store location): demographic parity, equalized odds, disparate impact ratio; (b) for personalization model (W200): verify product recommendations are not systematically excluding products relevant to specific geographic areas (Mindanao vs. Metro Manila); (c) for credit scoring model: verify approval rates are not disproportionately lower for specific regions; (d) document findings in model audit report | Data Scientist | Data Science Lead | 4-8 hours/model/quarter |
| 4 | **Performance drift monitoring**: monthly, Data Scientist reviews model performance metrics against baseline: accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, RMSE (forecasting models); if any metric degrades >10% from baseline, trigger model retraining or investigation; log drift events in model registry | Data Scientist | Data Science Lead | 2-4 hours/month |
| 5 | **Data quality & privacy review**: quarterly, review data inputs for each model — (a) verify data sources are still authorized per RA 10173 and NPC registration; (b) check for data leakage between training and production datasets; (c) verify data retention policies are applied to model training data; (d) for models using customer data: confirm consent basis and purpose limitation | Data Scientist / Legal | CIO | 4 hours/quarter |
| 6 | **Incident response for AI failures**: if an AI model produces harmful or incorrect outputs at scale (e.g., pricing model sets negative prices, credit model rejects all applicants from a region): (a) Data Science Lead activates model kill-switch (revert to rule-based fallback); (b) post-incident review: root cause, affected population, remediation; (c) Ethics Committee review if customer harm occurred; (d) NPC notification if personal data breach involved | Data Science Lead | CIO | 1-3 days per incident |
| 7 | **Annual AI governance report**: Data Science Lead compiles annual report for Executive Committee: model portfolio summary, bias audit results, performance trends, incidents and remediation, compliance status (RA 10173, NPC), AI investment ROI, strategic recommendations; report shared with Internal Audit per W359 | Data Science Lead | CIO | 2 days/year |

### System Touchpoints

- AI/ML model registry with metadata, risk classification, and audit history
- Model monitoring dashboard (accuracy, drift, fairness metrics)
- Ethics Committee review portal (submission, approval, conditions tracking)
- NPC DPIA register integration (W647)
- Model kill-switch / fallback mechanism (W55 IT DR integration)
- Internal Audit integration per W359

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Skills gap for AI ethics in Philippines**: limited local expertise in AI fairness and bias auditing; may require external consultancy for initial framework setup
- **Data quality issues affecting bias audits**: incomplete or biased training data (e.g., historical sales data skewed toward Metro Manila stores) can produce misleading fairness metrics
- **Model complexity vs. explainability**: more sophisticated models (deep learning, ensemble methods) are harder to audit for bias and explain to regulators; trade-off between accuracy and transparency
- **NPC enforcement uncertainty**: Philippine NPC has not yet issued specific AI/automated decision-making regulations beyond general RA 10173 provisions; governance framework may need rapid adaptation when regulations are issued


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-update of "update registry quarterly" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "logic, potential harms" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "document findings in model audit report" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-223 (ensure responsible AI/ML & pilot governance.)
- operational: Data Science Lead review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication

- **Data Science Lead**: ~8–12 hours/quarter on model governance and bias audits. Absorbed within existing role.
- **Legal Counsel**: ~2–4 hours/quarter on AI privacy reviews. Absorbed within existing role.
- **External AI ethics consultant**: ~5–10 days/year for initial framework and periodic audits.
- **No incremental headcount**.

### Time Estimate

- New model ethics review: 1-2 days per model
- Quarterly bias audit: 4-8 hours per High/Critical model
- Monthly drift monitoring: 2-4 hours total
- Annual governance report: 2 days

## W1206. AI-Powered Demand Forecasting Model Training, Accuracy Monitoring & Retraining Cycle

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled monthly model performance review; or triggered by forecast accuracy degradation below threshold |
| **Frequency** | Monthly accuracy monitoring; quarterly model retraining; annual full model rebuild |
| **Volume** | Forecasting engine generates predictions for 35,000 active SKUs across ~204 locations (200 stores + 4 DCs) = ~7.14M SKU-location forecasts per planning cycle |
| **Owner** | Data Science Lead |
| **Participants** | Data Science Lead, Supply Planner, Category Manager, IT Data Engineer, VP Merchandising (model governance) |

### Background

BuildRight Depot's demand forecasting engine (referenced in W31) uses AI/ML models to predict demand at the SKU-location level, powering automated replenishment, seasonal buy planning, and S&OP. The accuracy of these models directly impacts inventory investment, stockout rates, and waste (especially for shelf-life-sensitive items like cement). This workflow manages the ongoing lifecycle of the AI forecasting models: monitoring accuracy, diagnosing degradation, retraining with fresh data, and governing model changes to ensure the forecasting engine remains reliable and continues to improve.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monthly accuracy monitoring**: System calculates forecast accuracy metrics for the past month: (a) MAPE (Mean Absolute Percentage Error) by SKU category, by store cluster, by DC, (b) WAPE (Weighted Absolute Percentage Error) for revenue-weighted accuracy, (c) bias (systematic over-forecast vs. under-forecast), (d) accuracy by lead time bucket (7-day, 14-day, 30-day forecast horizons), (e) exception report: SKUs where MAPE > 40% or bias > ±20%; Data Science Lead reviews dashboard and identifies accuracy degradation trends | System / Data Science Lead | VP Merchandising | 2 hours/month |
| 2 | **Root cause analysis for degradation**: When accuracy degrades below threshold (MAPE > 35% for category, or > 40% for individual SKU clusters): Data Science Lead investigates: (a) data quality issues (missing POS transactions from offline stores, late GR postings, incorrect item master data), (b) concept drift (demand pattern changed due to new competitor store opening, infrastructure project, or seasonal anomaly), (c) feature drift (input variables no longer predictive — e.g., weather data source changed), (d) model staleness (model not retrained with recent data); documents findings in model performance log | Data Science Lead | — | 4–8 hours/analysis |
| 3 | **Quarterly model retraining**: Every quarter: Data Science Lead executes model retraining pipeline: (a) extracts latest 24 months of cleaned demand data (POS transactions, replenishment orders, ecommerce orders), (b) updates feature engineering pipeline: (i) calendar features (holidays, paydays, school calendar), (ii) weather features (PAGASA rainfall, temperature data), (iii) promotional features (sale events, markdown calendar), (iv) external features (construction permits, LGU infrastructure projects), (c) retrains model ensemble (time-series models + gradient boosting + neural network blend) on updated data, (d) validates on holdout period (most recent 3 months), (e) compares new model accuracy vs. production model; clears the holdout gate (> 2% WAPE improvement) for champion-challenger validation (Step 4) | Data Science Lead / IT Data Engineer | VP Merchandising | 20–30 hours/quarter |
| 4 | **Champion-challenger validation**: Before promoting retrained model to production: (a) run champion (current production model) and challenger (newly trained model) in parallel for 2 weeks, (b) compare forecast accuracy and bias side-by-side on identical SKU-location forecasts, (c) if challenger wins: promote to production with automated rollback capability if accuracy drops in first week, (d) if champion wins: retain current model and investigate challenger weaknesses for next training cycle | Data Science Lead | VP Merchandising | 2 hours/quarter (after automated parallel run) |
| 5 | **Annual full model rebuild**: Once a year: Data Science Lead conducts comprehensive model review: (a) evaluates whether current model architecture is still optimal (e.g., evaluate new transformer-based models, evaluate external data sources), (b) benchmarks BuildRight's forecast accuracy against retail industry benchmarks (target: MAPE < 25% at category level, < 35% at SKU level), (c) evaluates new features and data sources (e.g., foot traffic data, social media sentiment, competitor pricing), (d) if warranted, rebuilds model with updated architecture and features, (e) presents model performance report and improvement roadmap to VP Merchandising and CIO | Data Science Lead | CIO | 40–60 hours/year |
| 6 | **Model governance and documentation**: Data Science Lead maintains model governance documentation: (a) model card describing purpose, inputs, outputs, accuracy, limitations, (b) data lineage documentation (what data feeds the model, where it comes from, how it's cleaned), (c) model change log (every retraining, every feature change, every architecture update), (d) bias and fairness assessment (does the model systematically over/under-forecast for specific store types or regions), (e) compliance with BuildRight's AI governance policy per W689; documentation reviewed by VP Merchandising semi-annually | Data Science Lead | VP Merchandising | 4 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Automated forecast accuracy calculation engine (MAPE, WAPE, bias) integrated with Demantra (W1206.1 — fit-gap C5)
- Data quality monitoring pipeline (missing data detection, outlier flagging) feeding accuracy diagnostics (W1206.2)
- ML model training pipeline with feature engineering, training, and validation automation (W1206.3)
- Champion-challenger parallel execution environment with automated comparison (W1206.4)
- Model registry with version control, model cards, and change history (W1206.6)
- Forecast accuracy dashboard accessible to Supply Planners and Category Managers (W1206.1)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Model accuracy plateau**: ML models may reach an accuracy ceiling where further training yields diminishing returns; mitigated by annual architecture review in Step 5 and evaluation of new external data sources
- **Data quality dependency**: model accuracy is only as good as input data; offline POS transactions (uploaded with delay), missing ecommerce events, and incorrect item master data degrade forecasts; mitigated by data quality pipeline in Step 2 and data governance per W1177
- **Overfitting to recent patterns**: retraining on recent data may cause models to overfit to short-term demand anomalies (e.g., post-disaster surge) that won't repeat; mitigated by 24-month training window and holdout validation in Step 3
- **Business user trust**: if Supply Planners don't trust the AI forecast, they override it manually (per W596), negating the model's value; mitigated by forecast accuracy transparency (Step 1 dashboard) and involving Supply Planners in accuracy review discussions


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "reviews dashboard and identifies accuracy degradation trends" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "documents findings in model performance log" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "validates on holdout period" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-223 (ensure responsible AI/ML & pilot governance.)
- operational: annual architecture review in Step 5 and evaluation of new external data sources; operational: data quality pipeline in Step 2 and data governance per W1177; operational: 24-month training window and holdout validation in Step 3

### Staffing Implication
- **Data Science Lead**: ~15–20 hours/month on monitoring, retraining, and governance. Absorbed within existing role.
- **IT Data Engineer**: ~8–10 hours/quarter supporting retraining pipeline. Absorbed within existing role.
- **Supply Planner**: ~2 hours/month reviewing accuracy dashboard and providing feedback. Absorbed within existing role.
- **VP Merchandising**: ~2 hours/quarter on model governance review. Absorbed within existing role.
- **No permanent incremental headcount**.

### Time Estimate
Monthly accuracy monitoring 2 hours ≈ 24 hours/year; accuracy-degradation root-cause analysis 4–8 hours per event at realized volume; quarterly retraining cycle 26–36 hours (retraining 20–30 hours + champion-challenger validation 2 hours + governance documentation 4 hours) ≈ 104–144 hours/year; annual full model rebuild 40–60 hours — total ≈ 168–228 hours/year (24 monthly + 104–144 quarterly + 40–60 annual) plus RCA events across the ~7.14M SKU-location forecasts per cycle.

## W1339. Computer Vision-Based Shelf Audit & Planogram Compliance Monitoring

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — weekly shelf audit cycle per store; or triggered by planogram compliance score drop below 80%; or new planogram deployment requiring compliance validation |
| **Frequency** | Weekly automated shelf audit for top-50 planogram categories; monthly full-store audit; quarterly model retraining |
| **Volume** | 200 stores × 12 major categories × weekly audit = ~2,400 category-level audits/week; top-50 high-priority planograms audited monthly (10,000/month, phased 2,500/week); remaining planograms audited monthly |
| **Owner** | Data Science Lead |
| **Participants** | BI Analyst, Category Manager, Store Manager, Visual Merchandising Coordinator, IT Data Engineer |

### Background

Planogram compliance — ensuring products are placed on the correct shelves in the correct quantities and facing — directly impacts sales performance. Manual planogram auditing per W86 requires physical store visits, is time-consuming (60–90 min per category per store), and is inherently sample-based (auditors cannot visit all 200 stores weekly). Computer vision technology, using images captured by store associates or fixed cameras, can automate planogram compliance checking: detecting out-of-stock shelf positions, misplaced products, incorrect facing, and missing price labels. This workflow deploys computer vision models trained on BuildRight's product image library (from W50 PIM) to automate shelf auditing, complementing manual audits per W1174 with continuous, scalable monitoring.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Image capture**: Store associates capture shelf images using mobile app during daily walkthrough (W5A): (a) dedicated shelf audit module in store operations app: select category → capture 3–5 images of shelf section covering the full planogram area, (b) images tagged with store ID, category, shelf section, date/time, and associate ID, (c) capture protocol: images must cover full shelf width and height, adequate lighting (use flash in dim areas), minimal blur (steady hand or elbow brace), (d) for pilot stores (10 stores): fixed overhead cameras capture daily images of high-priority categories (power tools, paint wall, tile gallery) | Store Associate | Store Manager | 5–10 min/category |
| 2 | **Computer vision inference**: AI model processes captured images: (a) product detection: identify individual products on shelf using object detection model trained on BuildRight's product image library (35,000 SKUs with images from W50), (b) shelf position mapping: map detected products to planogram positions (which product should be where per planogram definition in space planning module), (c) compliance scoring per shelf section: (i) correct product in correct position (% compliance), (ii) out-of-stock positions (empty shelf where product should be), (iii) facing count: correct number of product facings vs. planogram, (iv) misplaced products: products in wrong position, (v) missing price labels, (d) generate compliance report per category per store with visual overlay showing detected issues on shelf image | System | — | Automated (< 30 sec/image) |
| 3 | **Exception triage**: BI Analyst reviews compliance exceptions flagged by computer vision: (a) categories with compliance < 80% escalated to Store Manager for immediate correction, (b) recurring out-of-stock positions flagged for replenishment review per W4 (is replenishment failing or is demand exceeding forecast?), (c) persistent misplacement patterns flagged for Category Manager — is the planogram unrealistic for the physical shelf configuration?, (d) false positive review: BI Analyst samples 5% of AI-flagged issues and compares to actual shelf condition — is the model making accurate detections? | BI Analyst | Category Manager | 2 hours/week |
| 4 | **Store Manager compliance workflow**: Store Manager receives weekly compliance report via store operations app: (a) list of non-compliant shelf positions with AI-generated visual overlay showing what's wrong, (b) corrective action: Store Associate repositions products per planogram, (c) Store Associate captures follow-up image after correction, (d) system re-runs inference to confirm correction, (e) compliance improvement tracked week-over-week per store, (f) store compliance score feeds into store performance scorecard per W1214 | Store Manager / Store Associate | Regional Manager | 15–30 min/week |
| 5 | **Model performance monitoring & retraining**: Data Science Lead monitors computer vision model performance: (a) track accuracy: precision and recall of product detection, false positive rate for out-of-stock detection, facing count accuracy, (b) quarterly: retrain model with new product images from W50 (new SKUs added to assortment) and corrected annotations from BI Analyst false positive review, (c) evaluate new model version vs. production model — promote only if accuracy improvement > 2%, (d) monitor for seasonal challenges: Christmas decorations in store may confuse model, planogram resets introduce new configurations | Data Science Lead | CIO | 4 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints

- Mobile shelf audit app with image capture, tagging, and upload
- Computer vision inference engine with product detection model trained on PIM image library (W50)
- Planogram compliance dashboard with visual overlay, store ranking, and trend tracking
- Integration with W50 (PIM — product image library for model training), W86 (planogram — planogram definitions), W4 (replenishment — out-of-stock triggers), W1174 (LP audit — compliance scoring alignment), W1214 (store scorecard — compliance KPI)

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Image quality dependency**: blurry, poorly lit, or partially captured images reduce model accuracy; mitigated by capture protocol guidelines and real-time image quality check in app
- **SKU recognition difficulty**: some hardware products look similar (different grades of the same bolt, slight color variations in tiles); mitigated by training model on high-resolution product images and using shelf position context as a disambiguation signal
- **Store associate adoption**: adding image capture to daily walkthrough increases workload; mitigated by limiting capture to top-50 planograms and demonstrating compliance improvement benefits to Store Manager
- **Fixed camera infrastructure cost**: overhead cameras in pilot stores require CAPEX (~PHP 50,000–100,000 per category per store); cost justified for high-value categories (power tools, paint) where planogram compliance most impacts sales


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-capture of "capture" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-count of "Computer vision inference" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews compliance exceptions flagged by computer vision" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-332 (ensure controlled execution — AI/ML & Automation (PA-30.2).)
- operational: in-app capture-protocol and image-quality gating at capture time; operational: model retraining gated on annotated false-positive review with version-promotion threshold >2% accuracy improvement; operational: capture scope capped to the audited planogram set with Store Manager visibility of results

### Staffing Implication

- **Data Science Lead**: ~4 hours/quarter on model monitoring (per step 5). Absorbed.
- **BI Analyst**: ~2 hours/week on exception triage. Absorbed.
- **Store Associates**: ~5–10 min additional per category per week. Absorbed.
- **No incremental headcount.**

### Time Estimate

- Image capture: ~60–120 min/store/week (12 categories × 5–10 min) × 200 stores = ~200–400 hours/week chain-wide
- Exception triage: 2 hours/week
- Store correction: 15–30 min/store/week
- Model retraining: 4 hours/quarter
- **Annual estimate: ~13,000–26,000 hours (store associates: image capture 10,400–20,800 + correction 2,600–5,200) + ~100 hours (BI) + ~16 hours (data science)**

## W1340. Conversational AI Customer Service Chatbot Lifecycle Management & Escalation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer initiates chat on BuildRight website, mobile app, or Facebook Messenger; or escalation from chatbot to human agent |
| **Frequency** | ~15,000–20,000 chat interactions/month; ~60% resolved by chatbot without human intervention |
| **Volume** | ~180,000–240,000 chat interactions/year; ~108,000–144,000 automated resolutions/year |
| **Owner** | Digital Commerce Manager |
| **Participants** | Customer Service Agent (escalation), Data Science Lead (NLP model), Content Specialist (knowledge base), IT Operations (platform), Marketing (conversation design) |

### Background

BuildRight's ecommerce channel per W10 and customer service per W13 receive significant inquiry volume across multiple digital channels: website chat widget, mobile app in-app messaging, Facebook Messenger (a primary customer communication channel in the Philippines), and Viber. Common inquiries include: product availability by store, pricing, order status, return policy, installation service availability, and loyalty program questions. A conversational AI chatbot can resolve 60–70% of routine inquiries without human agent involvement, reducing customer service cost per contact, providing 24/7 availability (especially valuable for Overseas Filipino Worker (OFW) customers in different time zones buying for family projects), and improving response time. This workflow manages the chatbot lifecycle: conversation design, training, deployment, monitoring, escalation, and continuous improvement.

### Steps

### Background

BuildRight processes ~8,500–9,500 vendor invoices per month for AP processing per W7, ~1,200 customer return receipts per month (the ~40/day AR-credit-memo canon, ~0.04% of POS volume) for return processing per W12, and ~6,000 delivery receipts per month at 4 DCs for goods receipt per W3 (~6,500–6,600 incl. DSD; canon §15.1). Manual document processing — opening invoices, reading key fields (vendor name, invoice number, date, line items, amounts), manually keying data into ERP — is labor-intensive, error-prone, and slow. Intelligent Document Processing (IDP) uses OCR (Optical Character Recognition) + NLP (Natural Language Processing) + ML to automatically extract structured data from unstructured documents, validate against ERP data, and route for processing. For BuildRight, IDP can automate: (a) vendor invoice data extraction for three-way matching per W7 (PO → GR → Invoice), (b) return receipt scanning for return validation per W12, (c) delivery receipt processing for goods receipt automation per W3. Philippine vendor invoices are particularly challenging: diverse formats, mixed Filipino/English, handwritten adjustments, and variable quality scans/photos.

### Background

BuildRight Depot's 35,000 active SKUs across 200 stores require continuous shelf monitoring to maintain product availability. Currently, stock-outs are detected through: (a) customer complaints (too late — sale is lost), (b) Stock Associate visual inspection during shelf replenishment (intermittent), and (c) cycle counting per W276 (weekly but covers only ~700 SKUs per day). The average stock-out duration before detection is estimated at 4–8 hours for fast-moving items, representing significant lost sales. IoT-enabled shelf monitoring — using weight sensors, optical sensors, or RFID readers embedded in shelving — can detect stock-outs in near-real-time (< 5 minutes) and automatically trigger replenishment actions. This workflow deploys and operates BuildRight's IoT shelf monitoring system, starting with a 10-store pilot focused on high-velocity, high-margin SKUs (A-items representing 80% of revenue).

| Field | Detail |

| **Owner** | Senior Data Scientist |
| **Participants** | Senior Data Scientist, Category Manager, VP Merchandising, CFO, Procurement Manager |

### Background

BuildRight's COGS of ~PHP 42–45B/year is driven by procurement from ~800–1,000 vendors. The top 20 vendors account for ~45% of COGS (~PHP 19–20B), making even a 1% improvement in negotiated pricing worth PHP 190–200M annually. Traditionally, vendor negotiations rely on Category Managers' experience, historical price data, and vendor relationship knowledge. This workflow develops an AI-powered vendor negotiation optimization engine that provides Category Managers with data-driven negotiation recommendations.

## W1572. Intelligent Document Processing for Vendor Invoice Automation, Receipt OCR & GL Code Auto-Classification

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vendor invoice received (email, paper scan, or vendor portal upload per W7); or monthly AP volume review showing manual processing bottleneck |
| **Frequency** | Continuous — ~8,500–9,500 vendor invoices/month; each invoice triggers IDP processing |
| **Volume** | ~102,000–114,000 invoices/year; ~70–75% merchandise invoices (3-way match per W7), ~25–30% non-PO/recurring invoices (2-way match per W7C) |
| **Owner** | Senior Data Scientist |
| **Participants** | Senior Data Scientist, AP Manager, AP Clerk, IT Integration Specialist, CFO, IDP Vendor |

### Background

BuildRight processes ~8,500–9,500 vendor invoices per month, of which ~6,715 are merchandise invoices requiring 3-way matching (PO → GR → Invoice) and ~2,000–3,000 are non-PO invoices (utilities, rent, services) requiring 2-way matching. Manual invoice processing involves: (a) opening the invoice (email attachment, paper scan, or vendor portal), (b) reading and extracting key fields (vendor name, invoice number, date, line items, amounts, tax), (c) matching against PO and goods receipt, (d) classifying the GL account, cost center, and tax code, and (e) routing for approval per W7. Manual processing takes 10–20 minutes per invoice, with ~5–8% error rate (wrong GL coding, missed early payment discounts, duplicate payment). Intelligent Document Processing (IDP) uses OCR (Optical Character Recognition) + NLP (Natural Language Processing) + ML to automate extraction, matching, and classification, reducing processing time to 2–5 minutes per invoice with < 2% error rate. This workflow implements and manages IDP for BuildRight's AP function.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **IDP platform selection & setup**: (a) evaluate IDP vendors: (i) accuracy for Philippine invoices (BIR-registered format with VAT breakdown, withholding tax), (ii) support for multilingual invoices (English, Filipino, Chinese for import vendors), (iii) integration with ERP AP module per W7, (iv) handling of handwritten vs. printed invoices, (v) pricing model (per-page or per-invoice); (b) select vendor and configure: (i) train OCR model on BuildRight's top 50 vendor invoice layouts (each vendor has unique invoice format), (ii) define extraction fields: vendor name, TIN, invoice number, invoice date, PO number, line item description, quantity, unit price, total amount, VAT amount, EWT amount, payment terms; (c) IT Integration Specialist connects IDP platform to ERP via API | Senior Data Scientist / IT Integration Specialist / IDP Vendor | CFO | 6–8 weeks |
| 2 | **Invoice ingestion & extraction**: For each incoming invoice: (a) ingestion: (i) email invoices: auto-forward AP email to IDP platform, (ii) paper invoices: scan at AP office, upload to IDP, (iii) vendor portal invoices: API pull from vendor portal per W705; (b) OCR extraction: IDP processes invoice and extracts all defined fields with confidence scores; (c) for confidence > 95%: auto-accept extraction; for confidence 80–95%: flag for AP Clerk review; for confidence < 80%: route to manual processing | System / IDP Platform | — | Automated (2–3 min/invoice) |
| 3 | **3-way matching automation** (for PO-based invoices): (a) system matches extracted PO number and line items to purchase order in ERP: (i) quantity match: invoice quantity ≤ PO quantity (within tolerance ±5%), (ii) price match: invoice unit price ≤ PO unit price (within tolerance ±2%), (iii) GR match: goods receipt exists in ERP for the PO quantity; (b) if all three match: auto-approve for payment scheduling per W7; (c) if mismatch: generate exception report with discrepancy details for AP Clerk investigation | System | AP Manager | Automated |
| 4 | **GL auto-classification** (for non-PO invoices): ML model classifies non-PO invoices: (a) features: vendor name, invoice description text, amount, historical GL coding for same vendor; (b) predict: GL account, cost center, tax code (VAT, EWT category); (c) model trained on 2 years of historical non-PO invoice coding; (d) for confidence > 90%: auto-classify; for confidence < 90%: route to AP Clerk with model prediction as suggestion; (e) AP Clerk corrections feed back to model for continuous learning | System / ML Model / AP Clerk | AP Manager | Automated + 1 min review for low-confidence |
| 5 | **Continuous improvement**: Monthly: (a) Senior Data Scientist monitors IDP performance: (i) extraction accuracy by vendor (track accuracy degradation for new vendor formats), (ii) 3-way match auto-approval rate (target: > 70% auto-matched, < 30% exceptions), (iii) GL classification accuracy, (iv) AP Clerk correction rate (corrections indicate model errors); (b) retrain GL classification model quarterly with corrected data; (c) add new vendor layouts when new vendors are onboarded per W34; (d) quarterly report to CFO: (i) invoices processed by IDP vs. manual, (ii) processing time reduction, (iii) error rate reduction, (iv) estimated annual cost savings (AP Clerk time × hourly rate) | Senior Data Scientist / AP Manager | CFO | 4 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints

- IDP platform — OCR extraction, confidence scoring, and exception routing (W1572.2)
- ERP AP module integration — PO lookup, GR matching, GL posting (W1572.3–4)
- ML GL classification model — vendor and description-based GL/cost center/tax prediction (W1572.4)
- Performance monitoring dashboard — extraction accuracy, match rate, classification accuracy by vendor (W1572.5)
