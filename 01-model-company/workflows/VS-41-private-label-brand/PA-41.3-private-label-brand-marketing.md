## W1846. Private Label Brand Identity Development & Guideline Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Initial private label strategy approval (W1830); periodic brand refresh (every 3–5 years) |
| **Frequency** | One-time initial development; periodic refresh |
| **Volume** | 2 brand tiers: "BuildRight Essentials" (economy) and "BuildRight Pro" (professional grade) |
| **Owner** | CMO |
| **Participants** | CMO, VP Merchandising, Category Manager, Brand Designer (external agency or in-house) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CMO and VP Merchandising define brand architecture: (a) "BuildRight Essentials": positioned as quality-equivalent to national brands at 15–25% lower price; target customer: value-conscious DIYer and homeowner; visual identity: clean, practical, trustworthy — color palette: blue and white with orange accent, (b) "BuildRight Pro": positioned as professional-grade quality at competitive pricing; target customer: contractors, builders, electricians, plumbers; visual identity: robust, technical, premium — color palette: black and gold with blue accent, (c) brand positioning statements, brand personality traits, and tone of voice for packaging and marketing communications | CMO | CEO | 1–2 weeks |
| 2 | Brand Designer develops comprehensive brand guidelines document: (a) logo design for each brand tier: primary, secondary, monochrome versions, minimum size requirements, clear space rules, (b) color palette: primary, secondary, and accent colors with Pantone, CMYK, RGB, and HEX specifications, (c) typography: primary and secondary typefaces for packaging, signage, and digital, (d) packaging design templates: layout grids for each package type (blister card, box, bag, label, hang tag), (e) photography style: product photography guidelines (lighting, background, angle), (f) iconography: standard icons for product features (waterproof, heavy-duty, ergonomic, etc.), (g) regulatory label placement: standardized position for required Philippine labeling elements (SRP, manufacturing details, safety warnings) | Brand Designer | CMO | 4–6 weeks |
| 3 | CMO and VP Merchandising review and approve brand guidelines; Legal Counsel reviews for trademark registrability and conflict with existing brands in the Philippine market; upon approval: distribute to all stakeholders (Buyers, factories, packaging designers, Marketing team, store operations) | CMO / Legal | CEO | 1 week |
| 4 | Ongoing: CMO manages brand guideline updates: (a) annual review: are guidelines current and being followed? collect feedback from Buyers (factory compliance issues) and stores (customer perception), (b) periodic refresh (every 3–5 years): update visual identity if brand perception has shifted or competitive landscape requires differentiation update, (c) brand guideline version control: all stakeholders notified of updates; outdated templates removed from circulation | CMO | CEO | 1–2 days/year |

### System Touchpoints
- Brand guideline document management system with version control (W1846.2–4)
- Packaging template library accessible to Buyers, factories, and packaging designers (W1846.2d)
- Brand asset management: logos, icons, photography standards in digital asset master (W316)
- Integration with W316 (digital asset master), W314 (brand guidelines in product content)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Factory non-compliance with brand guidelines**: Factories may deviate from brand guidelines (incorrect logo placement, wrong colors, unauthorized modifications); brand guideline enforcement requires pre-production artwork proofing (W1847)
- **Brand dilution**: If BuildRight launches too many private label SKUs across too many categories without clear differentiation between "Essentials" and "Pro" tiers, customers may perceive the brands as generic rather than purposeful


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "logo design for each brand tier" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "review and approve brand guidelines" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-update of "updates" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-366 (ensure controlled execution — Private Label Brand, Packaging & Marketing (PA-41.3).)
- operational: CMO review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **Brand Designer**: External agency engagement recommended for initial brand development (~PHP 300K–800K one-time); ongoing maintenance absorbed by Marketing team (~1–2 days/year)
- No permanent incremental headcount

### Time Estimate
- Initial brand development: 6–9 weeks elapsed; ~80–120 hours active time
- Annual review: 1–2 days/year
- Periodic refresh: 4–6 weeks elapsed every 3–5 years

## W1847. Private Label Packaging Design, Artwork Approval & Production

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New SKU launch (W1832); packaging redesign; regulatory labeling change |
| **Frequency** | ~20–30 new SKU packaging designs/year; ~5–10 redesigns/year |
| **Volume** | 1 artwork per SKU per package type |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Category Manager, Packaging Designer, QA Manager, Legal Counsel, Buyer (factory coordination) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Packaging Designer creates artwork per brand guidelines (W1846): (a) select correct template per brand tier and package type, (b) input product-specific content: product name, size, quantity, material, usage instructions, safety warnings, (c) generate barcode (EAN-13 or UPC-A) per GS1 standards, (d) include regulatory marks: BPS/ICC mark (if required), FDA notification number (if required), "Made in [Country of Origin]", (e) bilingual labeling: English and Filipino per DTI requirement, (f) submit artwork proof to Category Manager | Packaging Designer | Category Manager | 2–4 hours/SKU |
| 2 | Approval workflow: (a) Category Manager reviews: product information accuracy, pricing display, shelf appeal, competitive differentiation, (b) QA Manager reviews: regulatory labeling compliance, safety warning completeness, barcode scannability, (c) Legal Counsel reviews: trademark usage, claim substantiation (e.g., "heavy duty" must be defensible), Consumer Act compliance, (d) if any reviewer requests changes: Packaging Designer revises and resubmits; maximum 2 revision rounds | Category Manager / QA Manager / Legal | VP Merchandising | 2–5 days |
| 3 | Buyer transmits approved artwork to factory: (a) send print-ready files in factory's required format (PDF/X, AI, or PSD), (b) specify print substrate (cardboard, plastic, label stock), print method (offset, flexo, digital), and finish (matte, gloss, UV), (c) factory produces packaging proof (digital or physical) for final BuildRight approval before mass production | Buyer | Category Manager | 1–2 weeks |
| 4 | Category Manager approves packaging proof: (a) verify color accuracy against brand guidelines, (b) verify text legibility at actual package size, (c) verify barcode scans correctly at actual print quality, (d) if approved: authorize factory to proceed with mass production, (e) if not approved: request corrections and repeat proof cycle | Category Manager | — | 1–2 hours |
| 5 | Packaging artwork archived in system: (a) final artwork file stored in digital asset master (W316) linked to SKU, (b) version history maintained for traceability, (c) artwork reusable for reorders unless product specification or regulatory requirements change | Packaging Designer | Category Manager | 15 min/SKU |

### System Touchpoints
- Packaging template library from brand guidelines (W1846.2d)
- Barcode generation module per GS1 standards (W1847.1c)
- Multi-level artwork approval workflow with Legal and QA review (W1847.2)
- Digital asset master for artwork storage and version control (W1847.5)
- Integration with W316 (digital asset master), W252 (item master — barcode linkage), W1846 (brand guidelines)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Color inconsistency between proof and production**: Factory printers may not match brand guideline Pantone colors exactly; color tolerance standards should be specified in factory contract
- **Approval bottleneck**: Multi-reviewer approval (Category Manager + QA + Legal) can delay artwork by 1–2 weeks per revision round; standardize templates and pre-approve common elements to reduce review scope
- **Filipino language translation quality**: Poorly translated safety warnings or product instructions can create both safety risks and regulatory non-compliance; engage professional translator for initial templates


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "Country of Origin]" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-scan of "scannability" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-generate of "print-ready files in factory's required format" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-366 (ensure controlled execution — Private Label Brand, Packaging & Marketing (PA-41.3).)
- operational: Category Manager review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **Packaging Designer**: ~20–30 designs/year × 2–4 hours = ~40–120 hours/year; if in-house: part-time role shared with Marketing; if outsourced: ~PHP 2,000–5,000 per artwork × 30 = ~PHP 60K–150K/year
- No permanent incremental headcount

### Time Estimate
- Design: 2–4 hours/SKU
- Approval: 2–5 days/SKU (elapsed)
- Factory proof cycle: 1–2 weeks
- Total per SKU: ~3–4 weeks elapsed; ~4–6 hours active time

## W1848. Private Label In-Store Display, Planogram & Merchandising Standards

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New private label category launch; annual planogram refresh; store reset |
| **Frequency** | Per category launch (~2–4/year); annual planogram refresh |
| **Volume** | New category launches require planogram updates across 200 stores |
| **Owner** | Store Operations Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Store Operations Coordinator, Category Manager, Marketing, Store Managers |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Category Manager defines planogram placement strategy for private label: (a) shelf placement: private label products positioned adjacent to national brand equivalents (not isolated in separate section) — encourages comparison and substitution, (b) eye-level placement for high-volume private label SKUs (fasteners, hand tools), (c) "Better Value" shelf talkers highlighting price differential vs. national brand, (d) cross-merchandising: private label adhesives displayed near lumber; private label paint rollers near paint station, (e) "BuildRight Recommends" endcap displays for new private label launches (2–4 week rotation) | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1–2 days |
| 2 | Store Operations Coordinator develops planogram specifications: (a) shelf allocation: linear footage per private label SKU per store cluster (based on store size and local demand), (b) display fixture requirements: peg hooks for blister-packed items, shelf dividers for boxed items, bin displays for bulk fasteners, (c) signage and POS materials: price tags, shelf talkers, category headers, endcap signage, (d) distribute planogram and merchandising guide to all 200 stores | Store Operations Coordinator | VP Store Operations | 1–2 weeks |
| 3 | Store Merchandising execution: (a) each store implements planogram per guide during next scheduled reset (W1407 — seasonal department reset), (b) Department Supervisors oversee execution, (c) Store Manager verifies compliance and submits photo evidence through store manager app | Store Manager / Department Supervisor | Store Operations Coordinator | 1 day/store |
| 4 | Store Operations Coordinator conducts planogram compliance audit: (a) monthly: review photo evidence from stores; flag non-compliant stores for corrective action, (b) quarterly: in-person planogram audit at 10–15 randomly selected stores, (c) compliance rate target: ≥ 90% of stores adhering to planogram specifications | Store Operations Coordinator | VP Store Operations | Ongoing |

### System Touchpoints
- Planogram management module with store-cluster-specific layouts (W1848.2)
- Store manager app for photo evidence submission and compliance tracking (W1848.3c)
- Planogram compliance dashboard (W1848.4)
- Integration with W1407 (seasonal department reset), W67 (store performance review)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Planogram non-compliance**: Store Managers may deviate from planogram due to local space constraints, inventory availability, or personal preference; without photo evidence and audit, compliance drops below 60%
- **Space allocation conflict**: Adding private label SKUs to planogram requires displacing national brand SKUs or expanding shelf space; national brand vendors may resist reduced shelf allocation


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-distribute of "distribute planogram and merchandising guide to all 200 stores" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-submit of "submits photo evidence through store manager app" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-review of "review photo evidence from stores" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-366 (ensure controlled execution — Private Label Brand, Packaging & Marketing (PA-41.3).)
- operational: Store Operations Coordinator review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by existing Store Operations Coordinator and Store Manager roles

### Time Estimate
- Planogram strategy: 1–2 days per category launch
- Planogram specification: 1–2 weeks per launch
- Store execution: 1 day per store per reset
- Compliance audit: ongoing (~4–8 hours/month)
- Total per launch: ~3–4 weeks across all stores; ~40–60 hours active time

## W1849. Private Label Launch Campaign Planning & Execution

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New private label category ready for launch (products received at stores) |
| **Frequency** | ~2–4 category launches/year |
| **Volume** | Each launch covers 8–15 SKUs across 200 stores |
| **Owner** | CMO |
| **Participants** | CMO, Category Manager, VP Merchandising, Marketing Team, Store Operations |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pre-launch planning (4–6 weeks before launch): (a) CMO and Category Manager define launch objectives: sales target, category penetration target, customer awareness target, (b) Marketing develops launch campaign: (i) in-store: product demonstration events, "New at BuildRight" signage, Sales Associate talking points, (ii) digital: social media posts (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok) showcasing product features, (iii) loyalty: double points on private label purchases for launch month, (iv) trade: direct communication to trade accounts with sample offer and introductory pricing, (c) budget allocation: marketing spend for launch (typically 2–3% of projected Year 1 category revenue) | CMO / Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1–2 weeks |
| 2 | Sales Associate training (2 weeks before launch): (a) Marketing creates product training module: product features, quality story, price advantage vs. national brands, customer talking points, common questions and answers, (b) HR distributes training through Oracle Learning Management (OLA) per W51 (training), (c) Department Supervisors conduct brief team huddles on launch products in relevant departments | Marketing / HR | Store Manager | 1 week |
| 3 | Launch week execution: (a) Marketing activates campaign: in-store signage deployed, social media posts published, loyalty email/SMS sent to relevant customer segments, (b) Category Manager monitors real-time sales data: daily sales by SKU by store, (c) Store Operations ensures planogram compliance per W1848, (d) Sales Associates actively promote new products using talking points | Marketing / Category Manager / Store Operations | CMO | 1 week |
| 4 | Post-launch review (4 weeks after launch): (a) Category Manager and FP&A analyze launch performance: actual sales vs. forecast, sell-through rate, margin performance, customer return rate, (b) CMO analyzes marketing effectiveness: campaign reach, conversion, cost per acquisition, (c) Category Manager decides: (i) if successful: continue standard merchandising; expand SKU range if demand supports, (ii) if below forecast: adjust marketing, pricing, or planogram placement; consider discontinuation if performance doesn't improve within 3 months, (d) lessons learned documented for next launch | Category Manager / CMO / FP&A | VP Merchandising | 2–3 days |

### System Touchpoints
- Campaign management module for launch campaign planning and execution (W1849.1)
- Oracle Learning Management (OLA) for Sales Associate training (W1849.2)
- Real-time sales analytics dashboard for launch monitoring (W1849.3b)
- Post-launch performance analytics: sales, margin, marketing ROI (W1849.4)
- Integration with W83 (marketing campaigns), W51 (training & development), W67 (store performance), W1848 (planogram)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Launch timing**: Launching during slow season (rainy season Jun–Aug) or competing with major national brand promotions can dampen results; align launches with peak home improvement periods (Jan–Feb post-holiday, Mar–Apr summer)
- **Sales Associate engagement**: Without effective training and motivation, Sales Associates default to recommending familiar national brands; incentive programs (SPIFs — Sales Performance Incentive Funds) for private label recommendations can drive associate engagement during launch


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "accounts with sample offer and introductory pricing" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-create of "creates product training module" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-monitor of "monitors real-time sales data" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-366 (ensure controlled execution — Private Label Brand, Packaging & Marketing (PA-41.3).)
- operational: CMO review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by existing Marketing, Category Management, and Store Operations teams
- **Launch marketing budget**: ~PHP 500K–2M per category launch (~2–3% of projected Year 1 revenue)

### Time Estimate
- Pre-launch planning: 1–2 weeks
- Sales Associate training: 1 week
- Launch week: intensive monitoring (~2–4 hours/day)
- Post-launch review: 2–3 days
- Total per launch: ~9.5–11.5 weeks elapsed (4–6 wks planning + launch week + review at 4 wks + 2–3 days wrap-up); ~60–100 hours active time

## W1850. Private Label Customer Perception Survey & Brand Health Tracking

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Semi-annual brand health survey; post-launch survey (3 months after category launch) |
| **Frequency** | Semi-annual comprehensive survey; post-launch pulse surveys |
| **Volume** | ~500–1,000 survey respondents per survey cycle (loyalty program members + trade account customers) |
| **Owner** | CMO |
| **Participants** | CMO, Market Research Analyst, Category Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Market Research Analyst designs survey instrument: (a) awareness: "Have you noticed BuildRight's own brand products in store?", (b) perception: "How would you rate the quality of BuildRight Essentials/Pro products compared to national brands?", (c) purchase intent: "Would you consider buying BuildRight brand for [category]?", (d) purchase behavior: "Have you purchased BuildRight brand products? If yes, would you repurchase?", (e) price perception: "Do you perceive BuildRight brand products as good value?", (f) improvement suggestions: "What would make you more likely to buy BuildRight brand products?", (g) segment analysis: separate responses for DIY customers, trade professionals, and corporate buyers | Market Research Analyst | CMO | 2–3 days |
| 2 | Survey distribution: (a) email survey to loyalty program members (target: 500–800 responses), (b) SMS survey link to trade account customers (target: 100–200 responses), (c) in-store intercept survey at 10 stores during peak weekend (target: 100–200 responses), (d) survey incentive: 50 loyalty points for completion | Market Research Analyst | CMO | 1–2 weeks |
| 3 | Market Research Analyst analyzes results: (a) overall brand health score (composite of awareness, perception, purchase intent, repurchase), (b) segment analysis: DIY vs. trade vs. corporate, (c) category-level analysis: which product categories have strongest private label acceptance?, (d) trend comparison: results vs. previous survey (improving/declining/stable), (e) verbatim analysis of open-ended responses for actionable insights | Market Research Analyst | CMO | 1 week |
| 4 | CMO presents brand health findings to VP Merchandising and Category Managers: (a) actionable recommendations: product quality improvements, pricing adjustments, marketing message refinements, (b) category-specific actions: invest in categories with high acceptance; reevaluate categories with low acceptance, (c) competitive positioning: how does BuildRight private label perception compare to competitor private labels? | CMO | VP Merchandising | 2 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- Survey platform integration with loyalty/CRM for targeted distribution (W1850.2)
- Survey response analytics with segment and trend analysis (W1850.3)
- Brand health dashboard with historical tracking (W1850.4)
- Integration with W13.3 (customer data & CRM), W17 (loyalty program)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Survey fatigue**: Loyalty members receive multiple survey requests; keep survey short (5–7 minutes) and offer meaningful incentives to maintain response rates
- **Response bias**: Customers who voluntarily respond to surveys may be disproportionately satisfied or dissatisfied; supplement with in-store intercept surveys for balanced sampling


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "account customers" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-analyze of "Market Research Analyst analyzes results" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-366 (ensure controlled execution — Private Label Brand, Packaging & Marketing (PA-41.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication
- **Market Research Analyst**: Survey design, distribution, and analysis adds ~1 week per survey cycle × 2 cycles/year = ~2 weeks/year — absorbed if dedicated analyst exists; otherwise outsourced to research agency (~PHP 100K–300K per survey)

### Time Estimate
- Survey design: 2–3 days
- Distribution and collection: 1–2 weeks
- Analysis: 1 week
- Presentation: 2 hours
- Total per survey cycle: ~3–4 weeks elapsed; ~40–60 hours active time
- Aggregate: ~80–120 hours/year

## W1851. Private Label Competitor Benchmarking & Market Positioning Review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual competitive review; triggered by significant competitor private label launch or market shift |
| **Frequency** | Annual comprehensive review; ad-hoc for competitive threats |
| **Volume** | ~3–5 major competitors benchmarked (Wilcon Depot, CitiHardware, ACE Hardware, Handyman, local independents) |
| **Owner** | CMO |
| **Participants** | CMO, Market Research Analyst, Category Manager, VP Merchandising |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Market Research Analyst compiles competitor private label intelligence: (a) identify which competitors have private label programs, (b) catalog competitor private label product ranges: categories, SKU counts, brand names, (c) price benchmarking: competitor private label SRP vs. national brand SRP vs. BuildRight private label SRP (mystery shopping at 3–5 competitor stores per region), (d) quality assessment: purchase competitor private label products for side-by-side comparison with BuildRight and national brands, (e) packaging and branding comparison: design quality, shelf impact, labeling compliance, (f) in-store merchandising: planogram placement, signage, promotional support | Market Research Analyst | CMO | 1–2 weeks |
| 2 | Category Manager evaluates competitive positioning: (a) where does BuildRight have a private label advantage? (price, quality, range, merchandising), (b) where do competitors have an advantage? (c) are there product categories where competitors have private label but BuildRight does not? Should BuildRight enter? (d) are there categories where BuildRight should exit private label due to competitor dominance? | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1–2 days |
| 3 | CMO and VP Merchandising review and decide on positioning adjustments: (a) pricing adjustments if competitor private labels are significantly cheaper, (b) quality investment if competitor products are perceived as higher quality, (c) range expansion in underserved categories, (d) marketing message refinement to differentiate BuildRight's private label story | CMO / VP Merchandising | CEO | 1 day |

### System Touchpoints
- Competitor intelligence database with product, pricing, and merchandising data (W1851.1)
- Price benchmarking analytics: competitor vs. BuildRight vs. national brand (W1851.1c)
- Integration with W14.3 (competitive intelligence — VS-14 marketing), W40 (price management)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Mystery shopping limitations**: Competitor store employees may recognize frequent visitors; rotate mystery shoppers and use multiple agencies
- **Competitor intelligence timeliness**: Annual benchmarking may miss mid-year competitor launches; supplement with ongoing social media monitoring and trade account feedback

- System auto-capture of "Category Manager evaluates competitive positioning" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "review and decide on positioning adjustments" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-366 (ensure controlled execution — Private Label Brand, Packaging & Marketing (PA-41.3).)
- operational: CMO review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by Market Research Analyst and Category Manager roles
- **Mystery shopping cost**: ~PHP 50K–100K/year for 3–5 competitor stores × 4 regions × 2 visits/year

### Time Estimate
- Intelligence compilation: 1–2 weeks
- Positioning evaluation: 1–2 days
- Decision meeting: 1 day
- Total per review: ~3–4 weeks elapsed; ~60–80 hours active time

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Trade professional skepticism**: Contractors are brand-loyal to products they trust for structural integrity and safety (e.g., specific nail and screw brands); overcoming skepticism requires hands-on product demonstration and free samples, not just price promotion
- **Liability concern**: Trade professionals using private label fasteners or adhesives in structural applications may hold BuildRight liable for construction failures; clear disclaimers, quality testing documentation, and product liability insurance per W59 are essential
