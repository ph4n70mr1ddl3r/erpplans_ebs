## W1830. Private Label Category Opportunity Assessment & Product Selection

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual category strategy review or margin gap identification by Category Manager |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly category opportunity reviews; ~2–4 new product development initiatives per year |
| **Volume** | Target portfolio of ~4,000–5,000 private label SKUs (~11–14% of active SKUs, per the W1265/W1513 canon) across 8–10 sub-categories |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Category Manager, VP Merchandising, FP&A, Market Research Analyst, Merchandise Planner |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Category Manager identifies candidate categories for private label entry based on: (a) current gross margin vs. target margin — categories where national brand margins are below 25% are priority candidates, (b) category maturity — high-volume staple categories (hardware fasteners, hand tools, paint accessories, adhesives, PPE, cleaning supplies, storage, garden tools) where product differentiation is minimal and brand loyalty is low, (c) competitive landscape — categories where competitors (CitiHardware, Wilcon Depot) lack strong private label presence, (d) customer willingness — trade professionals and DIYers are willing to buy private label for commodity items (nails, screws, tarps, drop cloths, sandpaper, paint rollers) when quality meets specifications, (e) supply base availability — at least 3 qualified manufacturers in China, Taiwan, or Philippines capable of producing to specification | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 2–4 hours/category |
| 2 | FP&A builds financial model for each candidate category: (a) current category sales volume and gross margin by SKU, (b) projected private label volume capture (% of category volume expected to shift from national brands to private label — typically 15–30% within 2 years), (c) private label cost estimate based on preliminary factory quotes and landed cost modeling (FOB cost + freight + duty + insurance + packaging + quality testing), (d) target retail price positioning (typically 15–25% below national brand SRP while maintaining 15–25% higher gross margin), (e) break-even analysis: minimum order quantity (MOQ) vs. expected sell-through timeline, (f) payback period on initial inventory investment, (g) projected annual margin improvement (PHP amount and % improvement) | FP&A | Category Manager | 4–8 hours/category |
| 3 | Market Research Analyst validates customer demand: (a) analyze loyalty program purchase data for target customer segments — identify which customers buy commodity items frequently and would be receptive to a quality-equivalent private label alternative, (b) review customer complaint data for existing national brands — recurring quality issues indicate opportunity for BuildRight to offer a better alternative, (c) benchmark competitor private label offerings in Philippine hardware/DIY retail, (d) conduct informal store-level feedback with Sales Associates on customer willingness to switch, (e) present findings and recommendation to Category Manager | Market Research Analyst | Category Manager | 1–2 days |
| 4 | Category Manager and VP Merchandising make go/no-go decision per category: (a) if approved: define product scope (number of SKUs, target specifications, quality tier — "BuildRight Essentials" for economy or "BuildRight Pro" for professional grade), (b) set target launch date (typically 6–9 months from approval for imported items; 3–4 months for locally sourced), (c) assign Buyer as project lead for sourcing, (d) approve budget for factory sourcing, sample development, and initial order, (e) if not approved: document rationale and revisit at next quarterly review | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1–2 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- Category sales and margin analytics by SKU and brand (W1830.2)
- Customer purchase history analysis from loyalty/CRM data (W1830.3)
- Competitor pricing intelligence module (W1830.3c)
- Product lifecycle status management for new SKU creation (W1830.4a)
- Budget approval workflow for product development investment (W1830.4d)
- Integration with W1 (assortment planning), W679 (assortment optimization), W85 (product costing & margin analysis)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Overestimating private label capture rate**: BuildRight may assume 25% category volume shift but only capture 10–15% if customers are more brand-loyal than expected; conservative volume projections with upside optionality (start small, expand if successful) are safer
- **Quality risk**: Private label products that fail to meet quality expectations damage the BuildRight brand across all categories, not just the offending product; quality must match or exceed national brand benchmarks before launch; mitigated by CTL-364 (controlled execution — Private Label Product Development & Sourcing (PA-41.1))
- **MOQ commitment**: Import factories require minimum orders of 5,000–20,000 units per SKU; if sell-through is slower than projected, excess inventory ties up working capital and may require markdowns that erode the margin advantage


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-capture of "FP&A builds financial model for each candidate category" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "review customer complaint data for existing national brands — recurring quality…" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-document of "document rationale and revisit at next quarterly review" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-364 (ensure controlled execution — Private Label Product Development & Sourcing (PA-41.1).)
- operational: Category Manager review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **Category Manager**: Private label strategy is an extension of existing category management responsibility; ~4–8 hours/quarter dedicated to opportunity assessment — absorbed by existing team
- **FP&A**: Financial modeling adds ~4–8 hours/category; absorbed within existing FP&A capacity
- **Market Research Analyst**: Demand validation adds ~1–2 days/category; absorbed if 1–2 analyst roles exist; otherwise, Category Manager absorbs with data team support
- No incremental headcount

### Time Estimate
- Category opportunity screening: 2–4 hours/category
- Financial modeling: 4–8 hours/category
- Market demand validation: 1–2 days/category
- Go/no-go decision: 1–2 hours
- Total per category assessment: ~3–4 days elapsed; ~20–30 hours active time
- Aggregate: ~4 assessments/quarter × 25 hours = ~100 hours/quarter across all participants

## W1831. Private Label Factory Sourcing, Audit & Qualification

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Category approved for private label development (W1830) |
| **Frequency** | ~4–6 new factory sourcing engagements/year; ~2–3 factory audits/year |
| **Volume** | Typically evaluate 3–5 factories per product category; qualify 1–2 per category |
| **Owner** | Buyer (Sourcing Lead) |
| **Participants** | Buyer, Quality Assurance Manager, Category Manager, Import Coordinator |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Buyer identifies candidate factories through: (a) existing vendor network — current BuildRight vendors who also manufacture for other brands, (b) trade show contacts (Canton Fair, Philippine International Trade Fair), (c) sourcing platforms (Alibaba, Global Sources, ThomasNet), (d) industry referrals from Category Manager network, (e) factories already producing similar private label products for competitors (reverse engineering approach) | Buyer | Category Manager | 1–2 weeks |
| 2 | Buyer conducts preliminary screening of 3–5 factories per category: (a) factory profile: years in business, annual revenue, production capacity, export markets, (b) product capability: can they manufacture to BuildRight specifications? existing product catalog overlap with target category? (c) quality certifications: ISO 9001, ISO 14001 (environmental), BSCI (social compliance), product-specific certifications (e.g., CE marking for electrical items, PSA certification for pressure equipment), (d) compliance: Philippine import requirements (DTI-BPS ICC certification per W447, FDA for applicable items), (e) financial stability: trade references, bank references, Dun & Bradstreet or equivalent credit report, (f) communication capability: English proficiency, responsiveness, willingness to work with Philippine import documentation | Buyer | Category Manager | 3–5 days |
| 3 | Buyer requests and evaluates product samples from 2–3 shortlisted factories: (a) evaluate against BuildRight specification requirements, (b) compare quality side-by-side with national brand benchmark products, (c) conduct internal testing (hand tool durability, fastener tensile strength, paint adhesion, PPE impact resistance — per product category), (d) request pricing: FOB, CIF Manila, MOQ per SKU, payment terms, lead time, (e) evaluate packaging capability: can factory apply BuildRight branding and packaging per specifications? | Buyer / Quality Assurance Manager | Category Manager | 2–3 weeks |
| 4 | Quality Assurance Manager conducts factory audit (on-site for new factories; desk audit for factories already supplying BuildRight): (a) production process walkthrough: raw material storage, manufacturing lines, quality control checkpoints, finished goods warehousing, (b) quality management system review: incoming material inspection, in-process QC, final inspection, defect tracking, corrective action process, (c) social compliance assessment: working conditions, child labor policy, fire safety, waste disposal, (d) capacity verification: can factory meet BuildRight's projected volume within required lead times without compromising quality?, (e) sample retention: QA Manager retains reference samples from factory audit for future quality comparison | Quality Assurance Manager | Category Manager | 2–3 days/factory (including travel for on-site audits) |
| 5 | Buyer presents sourcing recommendation to Category Manager: (a) comparative matrix: factory capability, quality score, pricing, MOQ, lead time, risk assessment, (b) recommended factory selection with rationale, (c) negotiation strategy for contract terms, (d) fallback factory if primary selection fails during contract negotiation | Buyer | Category Manager | 2–4 hours |
| 6 | Upon factory selection: Buyer creates factory as approved vendor in ERP per W36 (vendor onboarding) with classification "Private Label Manufacturer"; adds factory to approved supplier list with product category scope, quality audit score, and renewal date | Buyer | Category Manager | Per W36 |

### System Touchpoints
- Vendor master creation with "Private Label Manufacturer" classification (W1831.6)
- Vendor qualification and approval workflow (W36)
- Quality audit scoring module with audit history (W1831.4)
- Sample tracking: reference sample retention records linked to vendor and SKU
- Sourcing project tracker: factory evaluation status, comparison matrix, decision documentation
- Integration with W36 (vendor onboarding), W44 (vendor scorecard — private label factories included in performance reviews), W160 (private label factory audit — expanded scope), W631 (strategic sourcing)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Factory quality inconsistency**: Factories may produce excellent samples but inconsistent production runs; initial production order should include strict incoming quality inspection per W681 and statistically significant lot testing before store distribution
- **Intellectual property risk**: BuildRight product specifications and packaging designs shared with factories may be used to produce similar products for competitors; non-disclosure agreements and exclusivity clauses are essential but difficult to enforce in China; mitigated by CTL-173 (prevent non-compliant private-label products from reaching shelf)
- **Factory dependency**: Relying on a single factory per product category creates supply chain risk; if factory experiences production issues, financial difficulties, or political disruption (e.g., China-Philippines trade tensions), BuildRight has no immediate alternative source


### Automation Opportunity
- System workflow notification of "Buyer identifies candidate factories through" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "catalog overlap with target category?" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-request of "requests and evaluates product samples from 2–3 shortlisted factories" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-173 (prevent non-compliant private-label products from reaching shelf.)
- operational: Buyer (Sourcing Lead) review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **Buyer (Sourcing Lead)**: Factory sourcing adds ~20–30 hours/category; at 4–6 engagements/year = ~100–180 hours/year. One Buyer should be designated as Private Label Sourcing Lead.
- **Quality Assurance Manager**: Factory audits add ~2–3 days/factory × 2–3 factories/year = ~6–9 days/year. If BuildRight does not have a dedicated QA Manager, this function must be staffed or outsourced to a third-party inspection firm (SGS, Bureau Veritas, TÜV).
- **Import Coordinator**: Private label imports follow standard W2B process; absorbed by existing role.

### Time Estimate
- Factory identification and preliminary screening: 1–2 weeks per category
- Sample evaluation and quality testing: 2–3 weeks per category
- Factory audit (on-site): 2–3 days per factory (including travel)
- Sourcing recommendation and vendor setup: 2–4 hours per category
- Total per category: ~6–8 weeks elapsed; ~60–80 hours active time
- Aggregate: ~4–6 categories/year × 70 hours = ~280–420 hours/year

## W1832. Private Label Product Specification & Sample Development

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Factory qualified (W1831) and product scope defined (W1830) |
| **Frequency** | ~20–30 new private label SKU specifications/year |
| **Volume** | Average 8–15 SKUs per product development initiative |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Category Manager, Buyer, Quality Assurance Manager, Packaging Designer, Merchandise Planner |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Category Manager develops detailed product specification for each SKU: (a) product description and intended use, (b) material composition and grade (e.g., "carbon steel Grade 8.8" for bolts, "304 stainless steel" for screws, "polypropylene woven 120 GSM" for tarps), (c) dimensional specifications with tolerances (length, width, thickness, weight), (d) performance requirements (tensile strength, corrosion resistance, impact resistance, load capacity — per product type), (e) color and finish requirements, (f) packaging specifications: unit pack quantity, display box quantity, master carton quantity, barcoding (EAN-13 or UPC-A), (g) labeling requirements: product name, BuildRight logo, country of origin, material composition, size, quantity, usage instructions, safety warnings per Philippine labeling regulations (Consumer Act RA 7394), DTI-BPS requirements, (h) shelf life requirements (if applicable — adhesives, chemicals, paint accessories), (i) quality control checkpoints: what must be inspected at factory, at incoming receipt, and at store | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1–2 hours/SKU |
| 2 | Buyer transmits specification to qualified factory; factory produces first article samples (FAS): (a) factory produces 3–5 units per SKU per specification, (b) factory provides specification conformance report documenting materials, dimensions, and test results, (c) factory ships samples to BuildRight HQ for evaluation | Buyer | Category Manager | 2–4 weeks (factory production + shipping) |
| 3 | Quality Assurance Manager conducts first article inspection (FAI): (a) dimensional verification: measure all critical dimensions against specification tolerances using calibrated instruments, (b) material verification: confirm material composition matches specification (may require lab testing — tensile test, hardness test, salt spray test for corrosion resistance), (c) functional testing: test product in intended application (e.g., drive screws into wood, test paint roller nap absorption, test tarp waterproofing), (d) packaging and labeling verification: confirm packaging meets specification, barcodes scan correctly, labels comply with Philippine regulations, (e) document FAI results: pass/fail per specification parameter; if fail, document specific non-conformances and required corrective actions | Quality Assurance Manager | Category Manager | 1–2 hours/SKU |
| 4 | If FAI fails: Buyer communicates non-conformances to factory; factory produces revised samples; repeat step 2–3. Maximum 3 sample rounds before escalating to Category Manager for factory re-evaluation (W1831.5 fallback factory). Typical: 1–2 sample rounds for experienced factories; 2–3 rounds for new factories. | Buyer / Quality Assurance Manager | Category Manager | 2–4 weeks per sample round |
| 5 | Packaging Designer develops packaging artwork for approved products: (a) brand identity: "BuildRight Essentials" or "BuildRight Pro" logo placement per brand guidelines, (b) product photography or illustration, (c) bilingual labeling (English and Filipino per DTI requirement), (d) barcode placement and print quality specification (GS1 standards), (e) color-coded category identification (e.g., blue for hardware, green for garden, orange for PPE), (f) sustainability messaging if applicable (recycled materials, recyclable packaging) | Packaging Designer | Category Manager | 1–2 days/SKU batch |
| 6 | Merchandise Planner creates SKU in item master: (a) item type = "Standard Stock — Private Label", (b) brand field = "BuildRight Essentials" or "BuildRight Pro", (c) product specifications linked to SKU record, (d) reorder parameters set per W2A (ROP, safety stock, EOQ), (e) pricing populated per W1833 (cost modeling output), (f) product content populated for ecommerce listing per W316 (digital asset master) | Merchandise Planner | Category Manager | 15 min/SKU |

### System Touchpoints
- Product specification database linked to SKU master (W1832.1)
- First article sample tracking: sample round, FAI result, conformance report (W1832.3)
- Packaging artwork management and version control (W1832.5)
- Item master creation with private label classification (W1832.6)
- Oracle Quality inspection for FAI documentation (W1832.3 — fit-gap C12)
- Integration with W316 (digital asset master), W315 (product lifecycle), W252 (item master governance)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Specification ambiguity**: Vague specifications lead to samples that don't meet expectations; Category Managers must invest time in precise, measurable specifications rather than subjective descriptions like "good quality"
- **Sample-to-production variance**: First article samples are often produced with extra care by factory quality teams; production runs may have wider quality variation; initial production lot should be subject to heightened incoming inspection
- **Packaging design delays**: Artwork approval cycles (Category Manager → VP Merchandising → Legal for regulatory compliance review) can add 2–4 weeks; standardize brand templates to accelerate


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "logo, country of origin, material composition, size, quantity" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-document of "documenting materials, dimensions, and test results" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-scan of "scan correctly, labels comply with Philippine regulations" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-173 (prevent non-compliant private-label products from reaching shelf.)
- operational: Category Manager review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **Category Manager**: Specification development adds ~1–2 hours/SKU × 20–30 SKUs/year = ~30–60 hours/year — absorbed
- **Quality Assurance Manager**: FAI adds ~1–2 hours/SKU × 20–30 SKUs/year = ~30–60 hours/year — absorbed
- **Packaging Designer**: Either a dedicated role or outsourced to agency; ~1–2 days per SKU batch of 8–15 SKUs × 2–3 batches/year = ~2–6 days/year — if in-house, part-time role or shared with Marketing
- **No incremental headcount beyond QA Manager role identified in W1831**

### Time Estimate
- Specification development: 1–2 hours/SKU
- First article production (factory): 2–4 weeks
- FAI and approval: 1–2 hours/SKU
- Sample revision rounds (if needed): 2–4 weeks per round
- Packaging design: 1–2 days per batch
- SKU creation: 15 min/SKU
- Total per SKU: ~6–10 weeks elapsed (mostly factory production time); ~4–6 hours active BuildRight time
- Aggregate: ~20–30 SKUs/year × 5 hours = ~100–150 hours/year active time

## W1833. Private Label Cost Modeling, Pricing Strategy & Margin Target Setting

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Factory pricing received (W1831) and product specification finalized (W1832) |
| **Frequency** | Per product development initiative; annual cost review for existing private label portfolio |
| **Volume** | ~20–30 new SKU cost models/year; ~4,000–5,000 existing SKUs reviewed annually |
| **Owner** | FP&A |
| **Participants** | FP&A, Category Manager, Buyer, VP Merchandising |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | FP&A builds detailed landed cost model per SKU: (a) FOB cost per unit from factory quote, (b) ocean freight estimate per unit (container utilization factor applied), (c) customs duty per HS code (0–15% depending on product category under ASEAN trade agreements), (d) 12% VAT on CIF + duty, (e) marine insurance, (f) port charges and demurrage buffer (W249), (g) customs broker fee per entry, (h) inland freight from port to DC, (i) DC handling cost per unit (pick, pack, ship), (j) packaging cost (if not included in FOB), (k) quality testing cost allocation per unit, (l) total landed cost per unit | FP&A | Category Manager | 30 min/SKU |
| 2 | Category Manager sets pricing strategy: (a) target retail price: 15–25% below national brand SRP for equivalent product, (b) target gross margin: 40–55% (vs. 25–35% for national brands — private label should deliver 15–25% margin uplift), (c) quantity break pricing: tiered for trade professionals buying in bulk, (d) promotional pricing: define seasonal markdown parameters (max 20% discount still maintains 30%+ gross margin), (e) SRP positioning: ensure private label price point is visibly lower than national brand on shelf (psychological pricing: PHP 89 vs. national brand PHP 115) | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 30 min/SKU |
| 3 | FP&A validates margin waterfall: (a) retail price − landed cost = gross margin per unit and %, (b) gross margin − DC handling − store labor allocation − shrinkage allowance (1.5%) − markdown allowance (5%) = net margin per unit, (c) annual volume projection × net margin per unit = annual profit contribution, (d) compare to national brand profit contribution displaced: private label must deliver higher total profit contribution even at lower retail price, (e) if margin target not met: Buyer renegotiates FOB cost with factory or Category Manager adjusts retail price target | FP&A | Category Manager | 15 min/SKU |
| 4 | VP Merchandising approves pricing per tiered authority: (a) individual SKU SRP changes: Category Manager authority, (b) category pricing strategy (target margin band): VP Merchandising approval, (c) if pricing strategy deviates from standard private label margin target (below 35% gross margin): CFO approval required | VP Merchandising / CFO | VP Merchandising | 30 min/batch |
| 5 | Annual cost review for existing private label portfolio: (a) FP&A compares actual landed cost to model — identify cost drift from exchange rate changes, freight rate changes, factory price increases, (b) Category Manager reviews actual sell-through margin vs. target — identify SKUs below target margin, (c) Buyer renegotiates factory pricing or sources alternative factory for underperforming SKUs, (d) Category Manager recommends price adjustments, discontinuation, or SKU rationalization per W1835 | FP&A / Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1 week/year |

### System Touchpoints
- Landed cost calculation engine (W2B.12) adapted for private label cost modeling
- Pricing management module with private label SRP, trade price, and promotional price bands
- Margin analysis dashboard: private label vs. national brand margin comparison by category
- Annual cost review analytics: actual vs. model cost variance tracking
- Integration with W2B (import landed cost), W85 (product costing & margin analysis), W40 (price change execution), W1835 (lifecycle performance review)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **FX volatility impact**: Private label products sourced from China are denominated in USD; a 5% PHP depreciation against USD can wipe out the margin advantage; consider building 3–5% FX buffer into cost model or using forward contracts per W80
- **Hidden costs**: Landed cost models often underestimate port charges, customs examination delays, and quality rejection rates; include 5–10% contingency in initial models and calibrate with actuals after first 3 orders
- **Cannibalization of higher-margin national brands**: If private label captures 30% of category volume but 40% of that volume comes from BuildRight's own higher-margin national brand sales (not competitor sales), the net margin gain is lower than projected


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-test of "testing cost allocation per unit" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "Category Manager sets pricing strategy" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "validates margin waterfall" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-364 (ensure controlled execution — Private Label Product Development & Sourcing (PA-41.1).)
- operational: FP&A review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **FP&A**: Cost modeling adds ~30 min/SKU × 20–30 new SKUs/year + 1 week annual review = ~50–55 hours/year — absorbed
- **No incremental headcount**

### Time Estimate
- Landed cost model: 30 min/SKU
- Pricing strategy: 30 min/SKU
- Margin validation: 15 min/SKU
- Approval: 30 min/batch
- Annual cost review: 1 week
- Total per new SKU: ~1.5 hours active time
- Aggregate: ~20–30 new SKUs × 1.5 hours + 40 hours annual review = ~70–85 hours/year

## W1834. Private Label Purchase Order & Import Logistics Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Product specification approved (W1832), pricing finalized (W1833), and launch date confirmed |
| **Frequency** | ~15–25 private label import orders/year; replenishment orders per W2A |
| **Volume** | Initial order: MOQ per SKU (5,000–20,000 units); replenishment per ROP/W2A |
| **Owner** | Buyer |
| **Participants** | Buyer, Import Coordinator, Quality Assurance Manager, DC Receiving, Finance |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Buyer creates import PO per W2B with private label flag: (a) factory as vendor, (b) SKU lines with quantities per MOQ or demand forecast, (c) specification reference document attached, (d) packaging and labeling requirements documented, (e) quality inspection requirements: pre-shipment inspection (PSI) at factory before shipment — mandatory for first 3 orders per factory; optional thereafter if quality score ≥ 95%, (f) LC or TT payment terms per factory agreement | Buyer | Category Manager | Per W2B |
| 2 | Quality Assurance Manager arranges pre-shipment inspection (PSI) through third-party inspection firm (SGS, Bureau Veritas) or travels to factory: (a) inspect random sample per AQL (Acceptable Quality Level) 2.5 standard, (b) verify product conformance to approved specification (W1832), (c) verify packaging and labeling conformance, (d) verify quantity and packing, (e) if PSI fails: issue corrective action request to factory; Buyer negotiates rework or shipment delay; if critical failure: Buyer cancels shipment and sources alternative | Quality Assurance Manager / Inspection Firm | Category Manager | 2–3 days |
| 3 | Import Coordinator manages logistics per W2B (import PO lifecycle): (a) freight booking, (b) customs documentation with correct HS codes for private label products, (c) DTI-BPS ICC clearance if required for product category (steel, electrical items), (d) coordinate delivery to DC | Import Coordinator | Buyer | Per W2B |
| 4 | DC Receiving conducts heightened incoming quality inspection for private label (especially for first 3 orders from factory): (a) inspect per sampling plan: 10% of master cartons opened and inspected for first order; 5% for second and third orders; standard receiving inspection thereafter per W681, (b) verify product matches approved specification and reference sample retained from W1831.4, (c) verify packaging and labeling: barcodes scan correctly, labels comply with Philippine regulations, BuildRight branding applied per specification, (d) if quality issues found: quarantine stock, notify Quality Assurance Manager and Buyer; do not distribute to stores until resolved | DC Receiving / Quality Assurance Manager | DC Operations Manager | 1–2 hours/container |
| 5 | Finance processes landed cost per W2B.12 and AP per W7; quality inspection costs allocated to product landed cost | Finance / AP | Controller | Per W2B/W7 |

### System Touchpoints
- Import PO module with private label flag and specification linkage (W1834.1)
- Pre-shipment inspection result tracking linked to PO (W1834.2)
- Oracle Quality inspection for incoming inspection with heightened first-order protocols (fit-gap C12) (W1834.4)
- Reference sample management for comparison at receipt (W1831.4 → W1834.4)
- Landed cost engine with quality inspection cost allocation (W1834.5)
- Integration with W2B (import PO), W3 (DC receiving), W681 (incoming quality), W7 (AP processing)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Pre-shipment inspection timing**: PSI must be completed before vessel loading; if factory delays production, PSI timing compresses and inspection firm may not be available on short notice; result: shipment without PSI, receiving defective goods
- **First-order quality variance**: Even after approved FAI (W1832), production quantity runs can have material variance; heightened receiving inspection for first 3 orders is critical to catch factory quality drift early
- **Container utilization**: Private label MOQs may not fill a full container; Buyer should consolidate multiple private label SKUs or combine with other import orders to optimize container utilization and reduce per-unit freight cost


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-document of "creates import PO per W2B with private label" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-validate of "verify product conformance to approved specification" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "logistics per W2B" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-364 (ensure controlled execution — Private Label Product Development & Sourcing (PA-41.1).)
- operational: Buyer review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **Buyer**: Private label PO management adds ~2–3 hours/order × 15–25 orders/year = ~30–75 hours/year — absorbed
- **Quality Assurance Manager**: PSI coordination adds ~1 day/order × 6–10 PSI/year = ~6–10 days/year; incoming inspection adds ~1–2 hours/container — absorbed
- **Import Coordinator**: Follows standard W2B process; absorbed

### Time Estimate
- PO creation: per W2B (~1 hour)
- PSI coordination and review: 2–3 days elapsed per inspection
- Logistics management: per W2B (~8–12 hours over 45–90 day cycle)
- Heightened receiving inspection: 1–2 hours/container
- Total per order: ~10–15 hours active time over 45–90 day cycle
- Aggregate: ~15–25 orders × 12 hours = ~180–300 hours/year

## W1835. Private Label Product Lifecycle Performance Review & Rationalization

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly performance review cycle; annual portfolio rationalization |
| **Frequency** | Monthly dashboard; quarterly deep-dive by category; annual portfolio review |

## W1837. Private Label Supplier Contract Renewal, Renegotiation & Transition Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Factory contract expiration (typically 1–2 year terms); performance decline triggering supplier change |
| **Frequency** | ~8–12 contract renewals/year; ~1–2 supplier transitions/year |
| **Volume** | ~15–25 active private label factory contracts at any time |
| **Owner** | Buyer |
| **Participants** | Buyer, Category Manager, Quality Assurance Manager, FP&A, VP Merchandising |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System alerts Buyer 90 days before contract expiry with performance summary: (a) on-time delivery rate, (b) quality rejection rate, (c) price competitiveness vs. market benchmarks, (d) responsiveness and communication score, (e) total spend volume | System | Buyer | Automated |
| 2 | Buyer evaluates renewal options: (a) **Renew at existing terms**: factory performing well (scorecard ≥ 85%), pricing competitive, no supply risk — recommend renewal, (b) **Renegotiate**: factory performing adequately (scorecard 70–85%) but pricing has drifted above market or quality needs improvement — negotiate improved terms (price reduction, quality improvement plan, better payment terms), (c) **Transition to new factory**: factory underperforming (scorecard < 70%), quality issues unresolved, or pricing uncompetitive — initiate W1831 sourcing for replacement factory, (d) **Dual-source**: add second factory to reduce supply risk for critical categories | Buyer | Category Manager | 2–4 hours/contract |
| 3 | For renegotiation: Buyer negotiates with factory using performance data and market benchmarks: (a) price adjustment request based on landed cost model update (W1833), (b) quality improvement commitment with measurable targets, (c) delivery performance improvement plan, (d) updated MOQ and payment terms, (e) if factory agrees: update contract in system with revised terms, (f) if factory refuses reasonable terms: proceed to transition (step 4) | Buyer | Category Manager | 1–2 weeks |
| 4 | For transition to new factory: (a) initiate W1831 sourcing for replacement factory — allow 3–4 months for sourcing, sample development, and qualification, (b) place final order with current factory to cover inventory during transition period (typically 3–6 months of supply), (c) once new factory qualified: place initial order per W1834, (d) upon first receipt from new factory: conduct heightened quality inspection per W1834.4, (e) if new factory quality acceptable: discontinue orders from old factory; let existing inventory sell through, (f) if new factory quality fails: continue with old factory under revised terms while sourcing second alternative | Buyer / Quality Assurance Manager | Category Manager | 3–6 months |
| 5 | FP&A tracks transition cost: (a) dual-sourcing period inventory carrying cost, (b) new factory qualification cost (samples, testing, travel), (c) any transition pricing differential, (d) report transition ROI to Category Manager and VP Merchandising | FP&A | Category Manager | Monthly during transition |

### System Touchpoints
- Contract expiry alerting with 90-day advance notice (W1837.1)
- Factory performance scorecard integration (W44, W706)
- Contract management with renewal/renegotiation/transition status tracking
- Transition inventory planning: dual-source coverage modeling
- Cost tracking for supplier transitions
- Integration with W1831 (factory sourcing), W1834 (import order management), W44 (vendor scorecard)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Transition supply gap**: If current factory production stops before new factory is qualified, stockouts occur on private label SKUs; the 3–6 month overlap period with final order from old factory is critical
- **Quality regression during transition**: New factory's first production run may have different quality characteristics than samples; heightened inspection and willingness to reject first production lot if non-conforming is essential
- **Stranded tooling**: If BuildRight paid for custom molds, dies, or tooling at the old factory, transitioning requires negotiation for tooling transfer or new tooling investment at the replacement factory


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-alert of "alerts Buyer 90 days before contract expiry with performance summary" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-issue of "issues unresolved, or pricing uncompetitive — initiate W1831 sourcing for…" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-update of "update" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-364 (ensure controlled execution — Private Label Product Development & Sourcing (PA-41.1).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by existing Buyer, QA Manager, and FP&A roles

### Time Estimate
- Contract evaluation: 2–4 hours/contract
- Renegotiation: 1–2 weeks elapsed per contract
- Factory transition: 3–6 months elapsed; ~40–60 hours active time
- Aggregate: ~8–12 renewals/year × 4 hours + ~1–2 transitions × 50 hours = ~80–140 hours/year
