No separate BOPIS transaction type exists (W11 steps 9–11).

## W206. Mobile POS (mPOS) & Queue-Busting Operations

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | High foot traffic / long queues at main checkout; or customer needing checkout in Yard/Materials area |
| **Frequency** | Peak hours (weekends, holidays); and for bulky outdoor sales |
| **Volume** | ~5–10% of total store transactions |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (mPOS Operator), Cashier (for cash handling if needed), Customer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Deployment**: Store Manager monitors queues; dispatches Sales Associates with tablets/mPOS devices to the floor | Store Manager | — | 2 min |
| 2 | **Scanning**: Associate scans items in customer's cart or in the Yard area; system applies real-time pricing and promos | Associate | — | 3–5 min |
| 3 | **Customer Lookup**: Associate scans Loyalty Card or looks up customer via phone/email (W17) | Associate | — | 1 min |
| 4 | **Tendering**: (a) If Card/E-wallet: Associate processes payment on the mPOS device; (b) If Cash: Associate generates "Pre-order Barcode" and directs customer to "Express Cash" lane | Associate | — | 2 min |
| 5 | **Receipt**: System generates Digital Receipt (Email/SMS) or prints via Bluetooth belt printer | System | — | 30 sec |
| 6 | **Fulfillment**: If items are in Yard: Associate marks items as "Released" in system; guard verifies digital receipt at gate | Associate | — | 2 min |
| 7 | **Sync**: Transactions sync instantly with central ERP inventory and Finance; no manual EOD re-entry required | System | — | Automated |

### System Touchpoints
- mPOS tablet application with full SKU/Price/Loyalty integration
- Bluetooth printer / Digital receipt integration
- Yard-gate verification dashboard
- Real-time inventory deduction (same as fixed POS)

### Pain Points / Risks
- WiFi coverage dead zones in lumber yard and outdoor materials areas — mPOS tablets lose connectivity, forcing associates to walk the customer back to a fixed POS terminal, defeating the queue-busting purpose
- Cash handling vulnerability — mPOS "Express Cash" lane introduces a second cash handling point without the same dual-control safeguards as fixed terminals, increasing cash variance risk
- Battery life limitations during peak hours — tablet batteries drain under continuous use; if charging stations are not readily available, mPOS devices become unavailable during the busiest periods
- Yard gate verification bottleneck — associates marking items as "Released" in the yard require guard verification at exit; if the guard is occupied, customers wait with large items, creating a new bottleneck outside the store


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-dispatch of "dispatches Sales Associates with tablets/mPOS devices to the floor" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-scan of "scanning" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-generate of "generates" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-263 (ensure controlled execution — Transaction Processing (PA-08.1).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
~10–12 min per mPOS transaction (3–5 min scanning + 1 min customer lookup + 2 min tendering + 30 sec receipt + 2 min yard release if applicable). Deployment decision: ~2 min Store Manager assessment. At ~5–10% of total store transactions (~23–47 mPOS transactions/store/day), mPOS handles ~4,600–9,400 transactions/day chain-wide.

### Staffing Implication
- Sales Associates serve as mPOS operators during peak hours. No incremental headcount — redeployed from floor selling during queue-busting periods.
- Each store needs 2–3 mPOS tablets and trained operators. Training is brief (~30 min per associate) and absorbed into onboarding.
- Peak-hour deployment consumes ~2–3 Sales Associate hours/store/day during weekends and holidays. Offset by reduced queue abandonment.

## W272. Cashier Over/Short Dispute & Deduction Resolution

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | End-of-day cash count reveals a variance (shortage or overage) beyond the allowable threshold. |
| **Frequency** | Daily |
| **Volume** | Varies (1-5 per store per week) |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Cashier, Store Cashier Supervisor, HR, Finance |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System flags variance during W5F (Store Closing): Z-report shows cash count vs. system total differ by > PHP 200 | System | Cashier Supervisor | 5 min |
| 2 | Cashier and Supervisor recount the till together; both sign a variance incident report with count details | Cashier / Supervisor | Store Manager | 15 min |
| 3 | Store Manager reviews CCTV footage, POS transaction log, and void/refund history (W37) for the cashier's shift | Store Manager | Regional Manager | 30 min |
| 4 | If shortage is confirmed due to cashier error (not system or customer cause), Store Manager routes incident to HR with supporting evidence | Store Manager | HR Manager | 10 min |
| 5 | HR generates salary deduction authorization per PH Labor Code Art. 113 (written authorization required; deduction limited to the actual loss and must not bring wages below the minimum wage); cashier signs | HR Manager | Store Manager | 30 min |
| 6 | Payroll deducts approved amount from next semi-monthly run; Finance reconciles cash clearing account (W10) | Payroll Officer | Finance Manager | Automated |

### System Touchpoints
- POS EOD Variance Reporting (W5F Z-report)
- CCTV / LP Exception Dashboard (W37)
- HR Incident and Deduction Module (W72)
- Payroll Deduction Processing (W10)
- Cash Clearing Account in GL (W9A)
- Regional Manager Variance Alert Dashboard

### Pain Points / Risks
- DOLE disputes if salary deductions lack written authorization (Art. 113, Labor Code: max 1 week's pay, must not bring below minimum wage)
- Cashier morale and turnover risk from frequent investigations, especially in high-volume stores
- "Covering up" variances — cashiers personally covering shortages to avoid reports, masking systemic issues
- Union grievances if deduction policy is applied inconsistently across stores
- CCTV footage retention limitations (typically 30 days) delaying investigations for variances discovered late


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "System flags variance during W5F" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-report of "report with count details" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "log, and void/refund history" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-184 (ensure POS settlement & tender reconciliation completeness.)
- operational: Store Manager review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Staffing Implication
1-5 variances per store per week × 200 stores = 200-1,000 incidents/week chain-wide. Each investigation ~1–2 hours. Absorbed by Store Managers (200) with escalation to Regional Managers and HR for confirmed cases. No incremental headcount; workload is a known store management responsibility.

### Time Estimate
1-2 hours per incident (30 min initial investigation + 30-60 min for confirmed cases with HR processing).
Breakdown: 5 min Z-report variance flag + 15 min dual recount + 30 min CCTV/POS log review + 10 min HR routing if confirmed + 30 min HR deduction processing.
At 200-1,000 incidents/week chain-wide, total Store Manager investigation time is ~200–1,000 hours/week.

## W281. Self-Checkout (SCO) Exception & Intervention Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer triggers a system exception at a self-checkout kiosk (e.g., weight mismatch, age-restricted item, voided item). |
| **Frequency** | Very High (Multiple times per hour) |
| **Volume** | Dozens per store per day |
| **Owner** | SCO Attendant |
| **Participants** | Customer, SCO Attendant, Loss Prevention |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customer scans an item at the SCO kiosk; system detects an exception (weight mismatch in bagging area, age-restricted item, barcode duplication, or unexpected item removal) | Customer | — | — |
| 2 | SCO terminal locks the transaction, flashes an amber light above the kiosk, and displays a "Please Wait for Assistance" message | System | SCO Attendant | Real-time |
| 3 | SCO Attendant receives a mobile alert on their handheld device detailing the exception type, SKU, and kiosk number | System | SCO Attendant | < 10 sec |
| 4 | Attendant physically approaches the kiosk, verifies the customer and item (checks ID for age-restricted items per RA 9211 §9 tobacco / PD 1619 volatile-solvent bans, verifies product match for weight mismatches), and scans their employee badge to unlock the terminal | SCO Attendant | SCO Supervisor | 30-60 sec |
| 5 | Attendant clears the exception (approve, void item, or override price) and the transaction resumes; if fraud suspected, Attendant discreetly alerts LP via mobile app | SCO Attendant | LP Officer | 15-30 sec |
| 6 | System logs the override with: Attendant ID, exception type, SKU, timestamp, action taken, and kiosk number; feeds data to LP analytics dashboard (W37) | System | LP Officer | Automated |

### System Touchpoints
- POS SCO Module (weight-scale integration, age-restriction logic, barcode validation)
- SCO kiosk hardware (amber light indicator, customer-facing display)
- Mobile Staff App (real-time exception alerts to attendant handheld)
- LP Exception Dashboard (W37 — override frequency analytics, pattern detection)
- Age-Restricted Product Master (POS-018 integration)
- Employee Badge Scanner at SCO terminal

### Pain Points / Risks
- High intervention rates (>15% of transactions) lead to customer frustration and SCO abandonment, negating labor savings
- "Sweethearting" — attendants routinely clearing exceptions without proper verification, increasing shrinkage
- Weight-scale calibration drift causing false exceptions and unnecessary interventions
- Age-restricted items (solvents, cutting tools per POS-018) requiring manual ID verification slows the self-checkout experience
- LP risk: customers "skip-scanning" items during attendant distraction at another kiosk
- Age-restricted-sales law compliance — penalties for selling restricted products to minors without ID verification (RA 9211 §9 tobacco-sale ban; PD 1619 volatile-solvent ban)
- Equipment reliability — kiosk hardware failures (scanner, scale, card reader) requiring frequent IT dispatch (W265)


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-scan of "scans an item at the SCO kiosk" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-alert of "alert on their handheld device detailing the exception type, SKU, and kiosk…" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-validate of "Attendant physically approaches the kiosk, verifies the customer and" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-263 (ensure controlled execution — Transaction Processing (PA-08.1).)
- operational: SCO Attendant review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
Dozens of interventions per store per day × 200 stores = thousands of daily interventions chain-wide. Each intervention ~1–2 min. 1-2 SCO Attendants per store (200-400 FTE) dedicated to monitoring 4-6 SCO kiosks. SCO Supervisor oversees 2-3 Attendants. Significant staffing commitment but offset by reduced traditional cashier FTE.

### Time Estimate
30-90 seconds per intervention; SCO Attendant role is continuous monitoring during store hours.
Breakdown: 10 sec mobile alert receipt + 30-60 sec physical approach, verify, and badge scan + 15-30 sec exception clearance and transaction resumption.
Dozens of interventions per store per day × 1-2 min each = continuous Attendant workload during peak hours. 1-2 SCO Attendants per store (200-400 FTE chain-wide) offset by reduced traditional cashier FTE.

## W516. Self-Checkout (SCO) Daily Operations

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Store opening (SCO station activation) or customer approaches SCO station |
| **Frequency** | Daily during store operating hours |
| **Volume** | ~2 SCO stations per store × ~46 transactions/station/day = ~92 SCO transactions/store/day (~20% of the ~467 daily store transactions at stores with SCO); rolling out to 100 stores initially |
| **Owner** | SCO Attendant (dedicated role or Lead Cashier doubling) |
| **Participants** | SCO Attendant, Customer, Store Manager (escalation), Loss Prevention (analytics) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | At store opening (W5A), SCO Attendant powers on SCO stations; system runs self-diagnostic (scanner, scale, payment terminal, receipt printer, coin dispenser); stations report "Ready" or flag hardware issues to IT Helpdesk (W48) | SCO Attendant | Store Manager | 10 min |
| 2 | System loads daily price file, promotional rules, age-restricted SKU list, and product master to SCO stations (same data set as staffed POS terminals per W5A.5) | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | Customer initiates SCO session: scans first item; customer-facing screen displays item name, price, and running total; system validates scanned barcode against product master | Customer / System | — | Per item |
| 4 | **Weight verification**: after customer places scanned item in bagging area, system compares item weight against master weight tolerance (±5%); if mismatch: system pauses and alerts SCO Attendant with visual/audible signal | System / SCO Attendant | SCO Attendant | 15 sec if alert |
| 5 | **Skip-scan detection**: system monitors bagging area weight against cumulative expected weight of scanned items; if weight exceeds expected by tolerance threshold (indicating unscanned item in bagging area): system pauses transaction and alerts SCO Attendant | System / SCO Attendant | SCO Attendant | 15 sec if alert |
| 6 | If customer scans age-restricted SKU (per POS-029 age-restricted list): system pauses transaction; SCO Attendant approaches customer, requests government-issued photo ID, verifies age on SCO Attendant terminal (separate from SCO customer screen); SCO Attendant enters approval or denial on Attendant terminal — customer screen shows "Age verification in progress"; if denied: system removes item from transaction and displays denial message per W520 | SCO Attendant | SCO Attendant | 1 min |
| 7 | Customer completes scanning all items; system displays total with promotional pricing auto-applied (same promo engine as W5B.6); customer selects payment method on screen | Customer / System | — | 30 sec |
| 8 | **Payment processing**: cash — system accepts bills/coins via cash recycler, calculates and dispenses change; card — customer inserts/taps on PIN pad, payment processed per standard card integration (W5B.7); e-wallet — customer scans QR code on SCO screen or taps phone on NFC reader; split tender — customer selects "Split Payment" and chooses tender combination | Customer / System | — | 30–90 sec |
| 9 | System prints receipt; offers e-receipt option per W528; customer collects receipt and bags | System / Customer | — | 15 sec |
| 10 | **Exception / intervention escalation**: if SCO station encounters any unresolvable exception (coupon validation failure, loyalty card read error, catch-weight item, trade account pricing, void request, price dispute, suspected fraudulent scanning): SCO Attendant converts SCO transaction to assisted checkout at the SCO station or redirects customer to nearest staffed POS terminal; SCO Attendant can recall SCO transaction on their terminal and complete as assisted checkout per W5B | SCO Attendant | Store Manager | 2–5 min per event |
| 11 | At store closing (W5F), SCO Attendant closes SCO stations: system generates SCO-specific Z-report (transaction count, revenue by tender, void count, exception count, weight-alert count); SCO Attendant reconciles cash recycler contents against Z-report; secures cash per W5F.5–5b standard procedures | SCO Attendant | Store Manager | 15 min |
| 12 | Weekly: Store Manager reviews SCO analytics dashboard — SCO transaction volume as % of total store throughput, SCO exception rate, SCO void rate, weight-alert frequency, skip-scan alert frequency, average SCO transaction time vs. staffed POS, SCO customer satisfaction (from receipt QR survey W65); identifies SCO stations or stores with high exception rates for LP review or retraining | Store Manager | Regional Manager | 30 min/week |

### System Touchpoints
- SCO station self-diagnostic at startup: scanner, scale, payment terminal, receipt printer, coin dispenser health check (W516.1)
- Weight verification system: master item weight with ±5% tolerance; bagging area weight sensor comparison per scan (W516.4)
- Skip-scan detection: cumulative weight monitoring against expected weight from scanned items (W516.5)
- Age-restricted product prompting at SCO with Attendant terminal approval workflow (W516.6, POS-029, W520)
- Multi-tender payment at SCO: cash recycler, card PIN pad, e-wallet QR/NFC (W516.8, POS-004)
- SCO-to-assisted checkout conversion: transaction recall on Attendant terminal (W516.10)
- SCO-specific Z-report and cash recycler reconciliation (W516.11, POS-009)
- SCO analytics dashboard: throughput, exception rate, void rate, weight-alert frequency, skip-scan frequency, avg transaction time (W516.12)
- Integration with W5A (store opening — SCO startup), W5B (in-store selling — SCO as parallel channel), W5F (store closing — SCO Z-report), W206 (mPOS — complementary queue-busting), W281 (SCO exception management — detailed exception types), W37 (loss prevention — SCO analytics feed), W48 (helpdesk — SCO hardware issues), W265 (POS hardware maintenance — SCO peripheral management)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Shrinkage risk**: SCO has inherently higher shrinkage than staffed POS (industry average 2–4× higher); skip-scan detection mitigates but does not eliminate; LP must monitor SCO shrinkage rates via inventory variance correlation (W248)
- **Customer frustration with weight alerts** — false-positive weight alerts (item packaging variation, customer rearranging bags, leaning on bagging area) interrupt workflow and degrade SCO experience; requiring frequent Attendant intervention defeats self-service purpose
- **Cash recycler reliability** — cash recyclers in SCO stations jam more frequently than staffed POS cash drawers (coins, crumpled bills); a jammed recycler takes the entire SCO station offline until IT or maintenance resolves
- **SCO adoption rates** — in Philippine hardware retail (contractors, tradespeople buying in bulk), SCO may see lower adoption than grocery or general merchandise; high-bulk, catch-weight, and trade account transactions cannot be processed at SCO, limiting applicability
- **Exception flooding during peak hours** — multiple SCO stations triggering exceptions simultaneously overwhelms a single SCO Attendant, creating customer wait times and negative experience


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-scan of "scanner, scale, payment terminal, receipt printer, coin dispenser" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-file of "file, promotional rules, age-restricted SKU list, and product master to SCO…" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "validates scanned barcode against product master" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-263 (ensure controlled execution — Transaction Processing (PA-08.1).)
- operational: SCO Attendant (dedicated role or Lead Cashier doubling) review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
SCO startup: ~10 min/store/day. SCO closing: ~15 min/store/day. SCO Attendant monitoring: continuous during operating hours (1 Attendant per 2–4 SCO stations). Weekly analytics review: ~30 min/week. Total incremental labor per SCO-equipped store: ~8–10 hours/day for Attendant coverage + 10 min opening + 15 min closing + 30 min/week analytics = ~55–60 staff-hours/week per store.

### Staffing Implication
- **1 SCO Attendant per store** (dedicated role) for stores with 2+ SCO stations; absorbed by Lead Cashier at stores with only 1 SCO station. Initial rollout phase: 100 SCO-equipped stores × 1 FTE = 100 incremental FTEs; at the W281 target-state deployment (4–6 kiosks per store, 1–2 attendants) staffing scales to 200–400 FTE chain-wide.
- Alternative: SCO Attendant role rotates from existing cashier pool (no incremental FTE) but reduces staffed POS coverage by 1 cashier per shift.
- Training time: SCO Attendant requires standard cashier certification (W518) plus 4-hour SCO-specific training module.

## W518. Cashier Onboarding, POS Training & Competency Certification

| Field | Detail |

| **Frequency** | As needed — estimated 200–300 new cashiers/year across 200 stores (turnover replacement + new store hires + seasonal temp conversion) |

## W533. POS Real-Time Event Streaming & Continuous Sync

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Every completed POS transaction (sale, void, return, refund, layaway payment, gift card issuance/redemption) |
| **Frequency** | Continuous — every transaction generates an event; ~93,333 events/day across 600 terminals |
| **Volume** | Peak: ~2,000 events/minute during weekend sale events; average: ~65 events/minute |
| **Owner** | IT Operations (event bus infrastructure); System (automated processing) |
| **Participants** | POS Terminal, ERP Message Bus, ERP Modules (Inventory, GL, Loyalty, BI), IT Operations |

### Background

The POS system operates on an event-driven architecture where every transaction is a discrete, immutable event published to the ERP message bus in near-real-time. This replaces the legacy nightly-batch approach and ensures that inventory, financial, loyalty, and ecommerce systems are always current. The event bus decouples POS terminals from downstream processing — terminals publish events and continue selling regardless of whether downstream systems process immediately or with slight delay.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Latency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cashier completes transaction at POS terminal (sale, void, return, refund per W5B); terminal generates complete event payload: transaction ID, timestamp, terminal ID, store ID, cashier ID, line items (SKU, quantity, price, discounts, catch-weight details), tender breakdown, customer ID (if loyalty/trade), authorization details (if void/override), event sequence number | System | — | < 1 sec |
| 2 | Terminal publishes event to ERP message bus via HTTPS/REST; event is encrypted in transit; message bus acknowledges receipt with sequence-confirmed response; if acknowledgment not received within 30 seconds: event queued locally for retry (offline mode per W535) | System | — | < 30 sec |
| 3 | Message bus persists event to durable storage; assigns internal event ID; event is now immutable and tamper-evident (hash chain) | System | — | < 1 sec |
| 4 | **Inventory consumer**: ERP inventory module consumes event; deducts quantities from store location on-hand; updates available-to-promise (ATP) for all channels (ecommerce, BOPIS, marketplace); publishes inventory update event for downstream consumers | System | — | < 60 sec from sale |
| 5 | **GL consumer**: ERP financial module consumes event; posts journal entries (Dr. Cash/AR / Cr. Revenue, Dr. COGS / Cr. Inventory, VAT entries); entries posted per transaction (not batched) | System | — | < 60 sec from sale |
| 6 | **Loyalty consumer**: Loyalty engine (in-house stack — fit-gap D9) consumes event; posts points earned to customer account; processes points redeemed (if applicable); updates customer tier progress | System | — | < 60 sec from sale |
| 7 | **Ecommerce ATP consumer**: ecommerce module consumes inventory update event; updates per-store and per-DC availability on website/app within 5 minutes; prevents overselling | System | — | < 5 min from sale |
| 8 | **BI consumer**: analytics module consumes event; updates real-time sales dashboards, store performance, category sales velocity | System | — | < 5 min from sale |
| 9 | **Continuous price push (inbound)**: throughout the day, ERP publishes price change events, new item events, discontinued item events, and promotional rule events to the message bus; POS terminals subscribe and apply updates to local data store within 60 seconds of publication (per POS-047); enables same-day promo launches and emergency price freezes (POS-024) without waiting for nightly batch | System | — | < 60 sec per change |
| 10 | **Offline event replay**: when POS terminal reconnects after offline period (per W5G), terminal replays all queued offline events from last-acknowledged sequence number; ERP deduplicates and processes in original sequence order; zero data loss guaranteed | System | — | 2–5 min per offline shift |

### System Touchpoints
- Event-driven POS architecture: each transaction is a discrete, immutable event published to message bus (W533.1–2)
- Durable message bus with at-least-once delivery and idempotent consumer deduplication (W533.3)
- Multi-consumer event processing: inventory, GL, loyalty, ecommerce ATP, BI (W533.4–8)
- Continuous price/item/promo push from ERP to POS terminals (W533.9, POS-047)
- Offline event replay with sequence tracking and zero data loss (W533.10, W5G)
- Event hash chain for tamper-evident audit trail (W533.3)
- Integration with W525 (nightly reconciliation — validates continuous sync completeness), W5G (offline recovery — event replay), W535 (offline capability — local event queue), W11/W19 (ecommerce — ATP consumer), W17 (loyalty — loyalty consumer), W35 (BI — analytics consumer), W40 (price changes — price push source), W13 (promotions — promo push source), W252 (item master — new item push source)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Message bus availability** — if the message bus is unavailable, POS terminals queue events locally and continue selling; but downstream systems (ecommerce ATP, BI dashboards) show stale data until bus is restored; message bus must have high-availability clustering with automatic failover
- **Event ordering across terminals** — events from Terminal 1 and Terminal 2 at the same store are processed independently; if both terminals sell the same last item simultaneously, the second event creates a negative stock situation; resolved by store-level inventory allocation (each terminal decrements from a shared store-level ATP) or terminal-to-terminal LAN sync (W535)
- **Consumer processing lag during peak** — during sale events (~2,000 events/minute), if the GL or loyalty consumer falls behind, there is a temporary lag between the sale and the GL posting; acceptable as long as lag stays under 5 minutes; horizontal scaling of consumers mitigates
- **Event payload size for catch-weight transactions** — catch-weight items (lumber, wire) include additional metadata (length, weight, custom pricing) that increases event payload; not significant at individual event level but contributes to aggregate bus throughput during peak


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "discounts, catch-weight details" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-assign of "assigns internal event ID" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-update of "updates available-to-promise" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-263 (ensure controlled execution — Transaction Processing (PA-08.1).)
- operational: failover

### Time Estimate
- Event publication latency: < 30 seconds from transaction completion to message bus receipt
- Downstream consumer processing: < 60 seconds for inventory, GL, loyalty; < 5 minutes for ecommerce ATP and BI
- Continuous price push latency: < 60 seconds from ERP change to terminal update
- Offline event replay: 2–5 minutes per offline shift of transactions
- No human time required — fully automated continuous processing

### Staffing Implication
- **IT Operations**: event bus infrastructure monitoring is part of existing IT Operations duties (~15 min/day reviewing event bus health dashboard); no incremental headcount
- Message bus infrastructure is a standard ERP component (not a custom-built system); vendor provides HA, monitoring, and alerting

## W535. POS Offline Capability Scope & Local Operations

### Background

POS terminals are designed to operate fully offline for ≥ 8 hours per NFR-011. This is not a degraded "emergency mode" but a fully designed operational state. Each terminal maintains a local embedded data store with the complete active SKU catalog, customer records, and pricing — enabling accurate selling without any central system connection. Terminal-to-terminal LAN sync ensures that all terminals in the same store maintain a consistent view of store-level inventory during offline periods.

## W1276. POS Multi-Tender Split Payment Processing & Reconciliation

| Field | Detail |

### Background

BuildRight Depot's POS functional requirements explicitly specify multi-tender support — the ability to split payment across cash, card, e-wallet, loyalty points, and gift card within a single transaction (POS-004). ## W1538. POS Multi-Tender Partial Refund Processing, Change Allocation & Tender Reversal Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|

| **Volume** | ~20–30 per store per month |

| **Participants** | Customer Service Rep, Cashier, Store Manager (for approvals > PHP 10,000) |

### Background

BuildRight's POS supports multi-tender transactions (cash, credit/debit card, e-wallet, loyalty points, gift card) per POS-004. When a customer returns only some items from a multi-tender original transaction, the refund must be allocated back to the original tender methods in the correct proportions. Philippine regulations (BIR) require that refunds follow the original payment trail, and card network rules (Visa, Mastercard, JCB) mandate refunds to the original card. ## W5547. Payment-Network & Acquirer Outage Response (Card Scheme / Acquirer Downtime)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Chain-wide or regional inability to authorize or settle card transactions during trading hours — a card-scheme outage (Visa/Mastercard/JCB), an acquiring-bank processor platform failure, or loss of connectivity between the POS estate and the acquirer exceeding 15 minutes at peak or 60 minutes off-peak |
| **Frequency** | ~2–4 events/yr chain-wide (industry reality; most resolve within 1 hour; the rare multi-hour or multi-day processor failure is the tail case this protocol covers) |
| **Volume** | 1 event × 200 stores × the ~36% card tender share of 2.8M monthly POS transactions (≈ 33,000 card transactions/day chain-wide) × peak-hour lost-basket exposure per the PHP 840K/day per-store revenue canon |
| **Owner** | Head of Customer Service (chain-wide protocol); Store Manager (site execution) |
| **Participants** | Store Managers & Front-End Supervisors, IT Service Desk (connectivity triage vs acquirer-side), acquirer relationship manager, Loss Prevention (outage-window fraud watch), Finance (settlement catch-up per W537/W3701), Marketing/Communications (customer messaging), Ecommerce lead (if online checkout affected) |

### Background

W535 owns store-side POS offline capability (local operations when the *store's* link is down) and W537 owns the daily card-terminal settlement cycle — but nobody owns the *network-side* outage: the scheme or acquirer platform is down everywhere, online authorization is impossible regardless of store connectivity, and the response is a chain-wide tender-policy event, not a store recovery procedure. The failure modes are specific: queues abandoning baskets at peak, declined-looking transactions that actually authorized (double-charge risk on retry), offline-authorization fraud abuse in the window, and settlement batches that need reconciliation after recovery. This workflow is that event canon per the E-24 custody ruling: outage response and tender policy live here; settlement discrepancies always land in W537/W3701 reconciliation; a suspected cyber cause routes to the VS-27 incident canon.

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Outage detection & classification**: acquirer heartbeat/terminal dashboard or store reports trigger the event; classify: scheme-side (all issuers affected) vs acquirer-processor side vs BuildRight connectivity (the last routes to W535/W1531 mechanics instead); confirm scope (chain/regional/single store) and expected duration from the acquirer's status line | IT Service Desk / Head of Customer Service | Head of Customer Service | < 15 min |
| 2 | **Chain-wide tender-degradation notice**: push the standard protocol to all affected stores — POS messaging "card payments temporarily unavailable — cash, GCash/Maya and bank transfer accepted"; queue management (open lanes, floor staff to queues); high-basket customers offered item-hold or invoice-now-pay-later only per existing store-credit policy (never improvised) | Head of Customer Service / Store Managers | VP Store Operations | < 30 min |
| 3 | **Alternative-tender surge**: e-wallet QR signage deployed at lanes; cash handling surge per W541-class cash-office rhythm; manual imprint/fallback authorization only where and as the acquirer instructs (contract-defined); save-the-sale desk for large baskets with delivery (delivery-on-payment options per W5D) | Store Managers / Front-End | Head of Customer Service | During outage |
| 4 | **Offline authorization discipline**: where the acquirer permits offline floor limits during outages, enforce the configured caps and the manual-referral phone line; LP watches the window for stolen-card abuse of offline authorization; every offline transaction logged for post-recovery settlement | Front-End / LP | Store Manager | During outage |
| 5 | **Recovery & settlement catch-up**: on acquirer all-clear, terminals resettle pending batches; Finance runs the W537 settlement reconciliation with a duplicate-charge sweep (authorizations during the outage that were retried); customer-facing duplicate concerns handled by Customer Service with receipts | IT / Finance | Finance Manager | 1–2 days |
| 6 | **Customer follow-through**: for customers who saw declines that later authorized — proactive receipt/charge clarifications; chargeback-prevention evidence (receipts, CCTV per W471 rules) retained for the dispute window; goodwill per policy where a wrong decline caused hardship | Customer Service / LP | Head of Customer Service | 1–2 weeks |
| 7 | **Post-mortem & acquirer accountability**: RCA with the acquirer/processor; SLA-credit claims per contract; the event and response gaps feed the tender-resilience roadmap (secondary acquirer evaluation, terminal multi-acquirer failover) | Head of Customer Service / Finance | CFO | 1–2 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- POS terminal status dashboard & acquirer heartbeat monitor (chain-wide event detection)
- Tender-availability flags pushed to POS lanes (card-disabled + alternative-tender messaging)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Lost-basket risk**: peak-hour card unavailability abandons queues; mitigated by the < 30-minute protocol push, alternative-tender surge and item-hold options
- **Double-charge risk**: customers/cardholders charged for retried transactions that had authorized; mitigated by the Step-5 duplicate-charge sweep before statements hit
- **Fraud-window risk**: offline floor-limit abuse with stolen cards; mitigated by configured caps, LP monitoring and full offline-transaction logging
- **Improvisation risk**: stores inventing invoice-now-pay-later or imprint fallback beyond policy; mitigated by the standard protocol message and the only-where-acquirer-instructs rule

### Automation Opportunity

## W5564. Mobile-Wallet Platform Outage Response (E-Wallet Tender Downtime)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | A mobile-wallet provider platform failure (GCash/Maya class) disabling e-wallet tender — QR scan-to-pay and NFC wallet authorization — chain-wide during trading hours while card and cash rails keep working; distinct from W5547's card-scheme/acquirer class (different rail, counterparty and settlement) and from W535's store-connectivity class (the store's link is up; the wallet provider is down) |
| **Frequency** | ~2–4 degradation/outage events per year across the wallet providers (app-wide incidents are recurring industry reality); most resolve within 1–2 hours, the rare multi-hour event is the tail case this protocol covers |
| **Volume** | 1 event × 200 stores × the e-wallet tender share (~15% of 2.8M monthly POS transactions ≈ ~14,000 wallet transactions/day chain-wide) × the alternative-tender surge at peak |
| **Owner** | Head of Customer Service (chain-wide tender protocol); Store Manager (site execution) |
| **Participants** | Cashier, Front-End Supervisor, IT Service Desk (rail diagnosis), wallet-provider relationship manager, Loss Prevention (outage-window fraud watch), Finance (wallet-settlement catch-up), Ecommerce lead (checkout wallet options), Marketing/Communications (customer messaging) |

### Background

W5547 owns the card-scheme/acquirer outage and W535 the store-side offline mode — but neither owns the wallet-platform event: the counterparty is the wallet provider (not the acquirer), the authorization rail is QR/NFC wallet APIs (not card schemes), the fallback is not offline floor limits (cards and cash still work — wallet tender must be suspended cleanly), the settlement catch-up runs on the provider's T+1 payout statement (not acquirer batches), and the fraud window is phished-OTP social engineering and double-scan attempts rather than offline-auth abuse. One tender-outage canon per rail: cards ride W5547, wallets ride W5564.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rail diagnosis**: distinguish provider outage (multiple stores + provider status channels) vs store connectivity (W535) vs acquirer path (W5547); engage the provider relationship manager for confirmation and ETA | IT Service Desk | IT Operations | 10–20 min |
| 2 | **Tender-policy directive**: disable wallet tender via the POS tender-type flag (a directive, never ad-hoc cashier rejections); script to all crews; alternative-tender surge per the W5547 law | Head of Customer Service | Head of Customer Service | Same window |
| 3 | **Customer handling**: signage and scripts at entrances and lanes; queued wallet-payers offered card/cash; no manual receipts or IOUs for wallet payments (the wallet may retry and double-charge on recovery) | Store Manager | Store Manager | Same window |
| 4 | **Fraud watch**: outage windows breed "wallet error" refund scams, phished-OTP social engineering and double-scan attempts; LP watch per W605/W3699 signals | Loss Prevention | LP Manager | Same window |
| 5 | **Ecommerce fallback**: wallet options disabled at checkout; card/COD fallback messaging per W659 | Ecommerce lead | Digital Commerce GM | Same window |
| 6 | **Recovery & settlement catch-up**: re-enable tender after provider confirmation; sweep stranded wallet authorizations against the provider's T+1 settlement statement so no authorized payment is lost or double-charged | Finance / IT | Controller | 1–2 days post-recovery |
| 7 | **After-action**: provider SLA/penalty posture per contract; outage duration and tender-shift capture feeding the tender-mix model | Finance / Head of Customer Service | CFO | Post-event |

### System Touchpoints
- POS tender-type availability flags with restore confirmation and audit trail
- Wallet-gateway transaction-status dashboard (authorized vs stranded)
- Wallet settlement-statement ingest to the Finance reconciliation queue (W537-adjacent)
- Store digital-signage/comms push for customer messaging
- Ecommerce checkout payment-option flags (links to W659)
- Provider incident correspondence file for the SLA/penalty record

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Misdiagnosis risk**: store-level connectivity cures applied to a provider outage waste the response window; mitigated by Step 1's rail-diagnosis protocol
- **Double-charge risk**: wallet retries authorize stranded payments on recovery; mitigated by Step 3's no-manual-receipt rule and Step 6's stranded-authorization sweep
- **Queue-abandonment risk**: peak wallet queues convert to walkaways; mitigated by Step 2's fast tender-flag and Step 3's signage
- **Fraud-window risk**: scam refund and social-engineering waves ride the outage; mitigated by Step 4's LP watch
- **Settlement leakage**: stranded wallet authorizations never reconciled post-recovery; mitigated by Step 6's statement-match queue

### Automation Opportunity
- Provider status feed auto-triaging the rail diagnosis (replaces manual channel-checking)
- Tender-type flags auto-disable/restore on the incident state with POS audit trail
- Stranded-authorization exception queue fed from the gateway status dashboard
- Settlement-statement auto-match for the recovery sweep

### Controls
- CTL-263 (ensure controlled execution — Transaction Processing (PA-08.1).)
- operational: wallet tender is disabled and restored only by directive flag with audit trail; stranded authorizations reconciled against the provider statement before the event is closed; outage-window refund exceptions routed to LP review

### Time Estimate
Per event ≈ 10–20 staff-minutes per store for the tender-flag and customer handling (≈ 35–65 hours chain-wide for a 200-store event) plus 4–8 hours of service-desk and Finance catch-up; at the 2–4-events-per-year cadence this stays absorbed within store crews and the payment-operations desk.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed by store crews, the IT service desk and Finance payment operations; no incremental headcount.
