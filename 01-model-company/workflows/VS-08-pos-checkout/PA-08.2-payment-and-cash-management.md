## W517. POS Cashier Shift Handover & Drawer Accountability

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Shift change (typically mid-day, ~2:00 PM at stores with 2 shifts) |
| **Frequency** | Daily per POS terminal with shift-based staffing |
| **Volume** | ~1 double-shifted terminal × 200 stores = ~200 mid-shift handovers/day; the remaining 2 terminals per store operate single-shift and only require EOD per W5F |
| **Owner** | Outgoing Cashier / Incoming Cashier |
| **Participants** | Outgoing Cashier, Incoming Cashier, Store Manager (variance escalation) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Outgoing Cashier completes or suspends any in-progress transaction per W521; system enforces no pending transactions before handover can proceed | Outgoing Cashier | — | 1 min |
| 2 | System generates X-report (mid-day sales summary by tender type for this terminal since opening or last handover) — distinct from Z-report which is end-of-day | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | Outgoing Cashier counts physical cash in drawer; compares to X-report expected cash | Outgoing Cashier | — | 5 min |
| 4 | If cash variance > PHP 200: Outgoing Cashier documents variance reason in system; system alerts Store Manager; Store Manager investigates per W272 (cashier over/short dispute resolution) | Outgoing Cashier / Store Manager | Store Manager | 10 min if variance |
| 5 | Outgoing Cashier records drawer count in system: cash by denomination, total cash, credit/debit card slips count, e-wallet transaction count, total tender amount per X-report, variance (if any) | Outgoing Cashier | — | 5 min |
| 6 | Outgoing Cashier removes cash drawer (till); places in tamper-evident bag with handwritten or printed count slip; secures in backroom safe | Outgoing Cashier | Store Manager | 5 min |
| 7 | Store Manager or Lead Cashier issues sealed replacement drawer with standard float (PHP 5,000 per W5A.4) from safe to Incoming Cashier | Store Manager / Lead Cashier | Store Manager | 2 min |
| 8 | Incoming Cashier logs into POS terminal; system displays handover screen showing X-report totals from Outgoing Cashier; Incoming Cashier confirms receipt of sealed drawer and acknowledges handover in system | Incoming Cashier | — | 3 min |
| 9 | System records handover event: outgoing cashier ID, incoming cashier ID, terminal ID, X-report totals, drawer count, variance (if any), handover timestamp; links to W272 if variance escalated | System | — | Automated |
| 10 | If no replacement cashier available (sick call, no-show): Store Manager assigns float cashier from available staff; if no staff available, Store Manager operates terminal directly or closes terminal for shift (reducing store checkout capacity) | Store Manager | — | 5 min |

### System Touchpoints
- X-report generation per terminal per shift (W517.2) — distinct from EOD Z-report (W5F.2)
- System block on handover if pending transactions exist (W517.1, W521)
- Drawer count entry with denomination breakdown and variance flagging (W517.3–5)
- Handover event logging with outgoing/incoming cashier IDs, terminal, X-report, variance (W517.9)
- Automatic W272 escalation for variances > PHP 200 at handover (W517.4)
- Integration with W5A (opening — initial float), W5F (closing — EOD Z-report), W272 (cashier over/short), W278 (mid-day cash skimming), W212 (Smart Safe — sealed drawer storage)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Handover queue delays** — customers waiting in line during handover (peak 2 PM shift change coincides with afternoon rush); mitigated by staggering handover across terminals (not all at once)
- **Cash count disputes** — outgoing and incoming cashiers disagreeing on drawer count, especially when tamper-evident bag is not used and drawer is transferred open; system-enforced count-before-swap mitigates
- **Float availability** — insufficient pre-prepared sealed drawers in safe (Store Manager needs 3 sealed drawers ready for shift change); if float preparation is skipped, handover takes longer while drawer is counted and re-floated
- **Variance pattern undetected** — recurring small variances (< PHP 200) at handover that don't trigger alerts but accumulate over weeks; Store Manager should review weekly variance trends per cashier
- **Cashier login sharing** — incoming cashier using outgoing cashier's login if handover screen is skipped; system should enforce logout/login cycle at shift change


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-generate of "System generates X-report" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-count of "counts physical cash in drawer" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-document of "documents variance reason in system" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-264 (ensure controlled execution — Payment & Cash Management (PA-08.2).)
- operational: staggering handover across terminals (not all at once); operational: should enforce logout/login cycle at shift change

### Time Estimate
~20–25 min per handover (1 min finalize + automated X-report + 5 min count + 5 min record + 5 min drawer swap + 3 min login/confirm). Variance investigation adds 10 min. Across ~200 mid-shift handovers/day chain-wide, total labor is ~65–85 hours/day (plus EOD closes per W5F).

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — handover is a standard cashier and Store Manager responsibility absorbed within shift structure.
- Store Manager must prepare 3 sealed float drawers before shift change (part of mid-day routine, ~15 min).
- Across ~200 mid-shift handovers/day, total chain-wide handover labor is ~65–85 hours/day (plus EOD closes per W5F), fully absorbed by existing cashier and Store Manager FTEs.

## W537. POS Card Terminal & Acquirer Settlement Operations

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | End of cashier shift (batch close) or end-of-day store close (W5F); also triggered by card reader error or settlement exception |
| **Frequency** | Daily — per shift per terminal (3 terminals × 200 stores EOD + ~1 double-shifted terminal × 200 stores = ~800 batch closes/day) |
| **Volume** | ~1.0M card transactions/month (36% of 2.8M POS transactions); ~5,000 card transactions/store/month (~167/day/store) |
| **Owner** | Store Manager (settlement review); IT Operations (terminal troubleshooting) |
| **Participants** | Cashier (batch close), Store Manager (exception review), IT Helpdesk (terminal issues), Bank Acquirer (settlement processing) |

### Background

Approximately 36% of BuildRight's POS transactions are paid via credit or debit card (EMV chip, contactless/NFC, or manual key-entry). Each POS terminal has an integrated PIN pad that communicates with the bank acquirer for authorization. At end-of-shift and end-of-day, card transactions must be batch-closed and settled with the acquirer. This workflow covers the daily card terminal operations lifecycle: authorization processing, batch close, settlement reconciliation, exception handling, and PIN pad troubleshooting.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Card authorization at POS**: during checkout (W5B.7), customer presents card; cashier or customer taps/inserts/swipes on PIN pad; POS system sends authorization request to acquirer via integrated payment gateway; system receives approval/decline with authorization code; transaction completed with card tender line on receipt | Cashier / System | — | 5–15 sec per transaction |
| 2 | **Contactless / QR payment**: customer taps contactless card or scans e-wallet QR on PIN pad for amounts below contactless limit (PHP 2,000); system processes without PIN; amounts above limit require PIN entry; e-wallet payments (GCash, Maya) processed via same PIN pad terminal using QR display | Cashier / System | — | 3–10 sec |
| 3 | **Shift-end card batch close**: at shift handover (W517), cashier or Store Manager initiates card batch close on each terminal; system transmits all authorized card transactions since last batch close to acquirer for settlement; acquirer confirms batch total (count + amount); batch close receipt printed and retained | Cashier / Store Manager | Store Manager | 2–5 min per terminal |
| 4 | **Batch total verification**: Store Manager compares card batch total on close receipt to POS card tender total from X-report (mid-shift) or Z-report (end-of-day); verifies transaction count and total amount match; flags variance > PHP 100 for investigation | Store Manager | — | 5 min |
| 5 | **Settlement exception handling**: if batch close fails (communication error, acquirer timeout, total mismatch), system queues batch for retry; Store Manager contacts acquirer support if retry fails after 3 attempts; unsettled transactions held in terminal memory and retransmitted on next successful connection; exception logged per W48 (helpdesk ticket if IT assistance needed) | Store Manager / IT Helpdesk | Store Manager | 10–30 min per exception |
| 6 | **PIN pad error troubleshooting**: if PIN pad malfunctions during transaction (offline, printer error, communication failure), cashier follows troubleshooting protocol: (a) restart PIN pad, (b) test with service card, (c) if persistent, log IT ticket per W265; cashier redirects card customers to adjacent terminal; if all terminals down, cash-only mode per W5E | Cashier / IT Helpdesk | Store Manager | 5–15 min |
| 7 | **Daily settlement reconciliation**: next-business-day, Finance receives settlement file from acquirer per W99 (payment settlement reconciliation); settlement file matched to POS batch totals; discrepancies (MDR fees, chargebacks, rejected transactions) flagged for investigation per FIN-037 | Finance / System | Finance Manager | Per W99 |
| 8 | **Quarterly terminal inventory & firmware audit** (per the Duration and staffing basis): IT Operations verifies all 600 PIN pads are online, running current firmware, and PCI-DSS compliant; firmware updates pushed during off-hours; non-compliant terminals flagged for replacement per W265 | IT Operations | IT Manager | Quarterly |

### System Touchpoints
- Integrated card payment gateway on each POS PIN pad (W537.1–2)
- Card batch close integration with acquirer settlement platform (W537.3–4)
- Settlement file matching with ERP payment reconciliation (W537.7, W99)
- IT asset management for PIN pad firmware and compliance tracking (W537.8, W265)
- Integration with W5B (card tender during checkout), W5F (EOD close), W517 (shift-end batch close), W99 (settlement reconciliation), W265 (hardware maintenance), W48 (helpdesk), W5E (system-down fallback)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Batch close failure during shift handover** — delays cashier checkout, creates queue; mitigated by automatic retry and terminal memory buffer
- **Settlement discrepancy** — acquirer MDR fee timing differences cause reconciliation variances; mitigated by next-day settlement file matching per W99
- **PCI-DSS compliance across 600 terminals** — firmware updates and compliance certification must be centrally managed; mitigated by quarterly audit and automated firmware push
- **Card decline customer friction** — ~2–3% decline rate in Philippines causes queue delays; mitigated by express redirect to next terminal
- **EMV fallback to manual entry** — increased fraud risk and higher MDR; restricted to manager authorization only; mitigated by CTL-264 (controlled execution — Payment & Cash Management (PA-08.2))


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "Card authorization at POS" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-scan of "scans e-wallet QR on PIN pad for amounts below contactless limit" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-generate of "printed and retained" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-264 (ensure controlled execution — Payment & Cash Management (PA-08.2).)
- operational: automatic retry and terminal memory buffer; operational: next-day settlement file matching per W99

### Time Estimate
- Per-transaction card processing: 5–15 sec (no incremental time vs. cash; absorbed in checkout)
- Shift-end batch close: 2–5 min/terminal × 3 terminals = 6–15 min per shift per store
- Daily batch verification: 5 min per store
- Settlement exception: 10–30 min per occurrence (estimated 2–3 per store per month)
- **Total per store per day**: ~20–30 min absorbed in existing shift handover and EOD procedures

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — card terminal operations absorbed by existing cashiers and Store Manager
- IT Operations: ~2 hours/quarter for PIN pad firmware audit (absorbed in existing W265 maintenance schedule)
- Finance: settlement reconciliation absorbed in existing W99 daily reconciliation process

## W541. POS Cash Office Operations & Bank Deposit Preparation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | End of cashier shift (W517) or end-of-day store close (W5F) when cash drawers are submitted to cash office |
| **Frequency** | Daily — 2 shifts × 200 stores = 400 cash office processing cycles/day |
| **Volume** | ~PHP 2.1B in monthly cash sales (42% of PHP 5.04B); ~PHP 350,000/day/average store in cash |
| **Owner** | Store Manager (cash office operations); Finance (bank deposit verification) |
| **Participants** | Cash Office Clerk / designated Store Manager on-duty, Cashier (drawer handover), CIT Guard (cash pickup per W212), Bank (deposit processing) |

### Background

While W5F covers end-of-day store close procedures and W517 covers cashier shift handover, neither details the cash office back-office operations that occur after cashiers submit their drawers. The cash office is the secure back-room area where drawer counts are verified, variances documented, bank deposits prepared, and cash secured. For BuildRight with ~PHP 350,000/day in cash per store, the cash office process is critical for cash security, accurate financial reporting, and loss prevention. Stores with Smart Safe (W212) have partially automated this process, but all stores require a structured cash office workflow.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Drawer receipt from cashier**: at shift end (W517.7) or EOD (W5F.2), cashier brings sealed cash drawer or tamper-evident cash bag to cash office; Cash Office Clerk verifies tamper-evident seal is intact; records receipt time and cashier name | Cash Office Clerk | Store Manager | 2–3 min per drawer |
| 2 | **Drawer count and verification**: Cash Office Clerk opens drawer/bag in cash office (secure room with CCTV); counts bills by denomination; counts coins by denomination; records actual cash amount per cashier; system compares actual count to POS system expected amount (opening float + cash sales − cash refunds − cash payouts − voids) | Cash Office Clerk | — | 5–8 min per drawer |
| 3 | **Variance documentation**: if variance between actual and expected > PHP 50 (configurable threshold): (a) system flags variance for investigation per W272 (cashier over/short), (b) Cash Office Clerk documents variance with cashier name, amount, date, shift, (c) Store Manager notified, (d) cashier notified and signs variance acknowledgment; variance < PHP 50 recorded but not escalated | Cash Office Clerk / Store Manager | Store Manager | 3–5 min per variance |
| 4 | **Non-cash tender verification**: for stores without Smart Safe, Cash Office Clerk also verifies non-cash tender totals: (a) card batch totals vs. POS card tender report (per W537), (b) e-wallet settlement totals vs. POS e-wallet tender report, (c) trade account charges vs. POS trade account report; discrepancies flagged per W537 or W261 | Cash Office Clerk | — | 5 min |
| 5 | **Bank deposit preparation**: Cash Office Clerk prepares bank deposit: (a) selects bills and coins for deposit (keeping working float for next day), (b) fills bank deposit slip with cash breakdown by denomination, (c) includes any check payments received, (d) seals deposit in tamper-evident bank bag with deposit slip, (e) records deposit amount in cash office log; for Smart Safe stores (W212): Smart Safe counts and verifies cash automatically; Cash Office Clerk confirms Smart Safe count and initiates deposit report | Cash Office Clerk | Store Manager | 10–15 min |
| 6 | **Cash securing**: remaining working float (next day's opening float per W5A) placed in store safe; bank deposit bag secured in safe pending CIT pickup (W212) or manager bank run; cash office log updated with safe contents (working float + deposit bag); safe locked and time-lock set | Cash Office Clerk | Store Manager | 5 min |
| 7 | **Cash office close report**: system generates cash office close report: (a) per-cashier actual vs. expected with variances, (b) total cash received, (c) bank deposit amount, (d) working float retained, (e) non-cash tender summary, (f) net cash position; Store Manager reviews and digitally signs; report transmitted to Finance via event stream (W533) | System / Cash Office Clerk | Store Manager | 5 min |
| 8 | **CIT pickup or bank deposit execution**: for stores with CIT service (W212, FIN-025): CIT guard arrives per schedule; two-person custody transfer (Cash Office Clerk + CIT guard); both sign custody transfer log; CIT guard transports deposit to bank; for stores without CIT: Store Manager or designated supervisor makes bank run before close; bank deposit receipt obtained and filed | Cash Office Clerk / CIT Guard | Store Manager | 10–15 min |

### System Touchpoints
- POS system expected drawer amounts per cashier per shift (W541.2, from W5B transaction data)
- Cash office variance flagging and escalation to W272 (cashier over/short) (W541.3)
- Card and e-wallet tender verification per W537 and W261 (W541.4)
- Smart Safe integration for automated cash counting (W212) (W541.5)
- Cash office close report generation and transmission via W533 (W541.7)
- CIT custody transfer documentation integration with FIN-025 (W541.8)
- Integration with W5A (opening float — next-day float source), W5F (EOD close — triggers cash office), W517 (shift handover — drawer submission), W212 (Smart Safe — automated counting), W272 (over/short — variance investigation), W278 (mid-day skim — mid-day deposit), FIN-025 (CIT — armored car), W89 (bank reconciliation — deposit matching), W48 (helpdesk — safe/office issues)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Cash counting error** — manual counting of ~PHP 350,000/store/day is error-prone; mitigated by denomination-level count recording and dual-verification for variances > PHP 200
- **Robbery risk during bank run** — stores without CIT service are vulnerable during cash transport; mitigated by CIT service for high-volume stores, varying bank run times for others
- **Cash office bottleneck during peak** — multiple cashiers submitting drawers simultaneously at shift change; mitigated by staggered shift handover schedule and Smart Safe automation (W212)
- **Counterfeit detection** — manual counterfeit detection unreliable for high-volume cash; mitigated by UV counterfeit detection pens in cash office and Smart Safe counterfeit detection sensors
- **Cash float planning** — insufficient float for next day disrupts store opening; mitigated by minimum float threshold alerts and automatic CIT cash delivery scheduling


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "records receipt time and cashier name" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-count of "count and verification" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "documentation" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-264 (ensure controlled execution — Payment & Cash Management (PA-08.2).)
- operational: denomination-level count recording and dual-verification for variances > PHP 200; operational: CIT service for high-volume stores, varying bank run times for others; operational: staggered shift handover schedule and Smart Safe automation (W212)

### Time Estimate
- Drawer receipt and count: 7–11 min per cashier (× up to 3 cashiers per shift per store = 21–33 min/shift)
- Variance documentation: 3–5 min per variance (~20% of drawers = 1–2 variances/shift)
- Bank deposit preparation: 10–15 min
- Cash securing: 5 min
- Report review and sign: 5 min
- CIT pickup: 10–15 min
- **Total per store per day**: ~90–120 min (split between 2 shifts = 45–60 min/shift)
- **Total chain-wide per day**: ~300–400 hours

### Staffing Implication
- **Cash Office Clerk**: 1 per store (part-time, 4–6 hours/day covering both shifts); currently implied by existing roles but not explicitly staffed; role may be combined with back-office/admin responsibilities
- For Smart Safe stores (W212): Cash Office Clerk time reduced by ~30–40% (automated counting)
- CIT guard cost: per FIN-025 contract; varies by location

## W543. POS Consignment Sell-Through Transaction Processing & Vendor Settlement Trigger

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer purchases a consignment item at POS; consignment sell-through period settlement |
| **Frequency** | ~42,000 consignment POS transactions/month (~1.5% of POS transactions) |
| **Volume** | ~PHP 378M/month in consignment sales (~7.5% of in-store revenue) |
| **Owner** | Merchandising Manager (consignment terms); Store Manager (POS processing compliance) |
| **Participants** | Cashier (scanning), System (ownership transfer detection), Finance (vendor settlement), Vendor (consignment partner) |

### Background

BuildRight carries consignment inventory from select vendors (large appliance brands, seasonal garden items, specialty tools). Per INV-009/INV-017, consignment items are vendor-owned until sold, at which point ownership transfers to BuildRight and immediately to the customer (sell-through). At POS, the system must distinguish consignment items from owned inventory, apply appropriate pricing, trigger ownership transfer, and accumulate sell-through data for vendor settlement (FIN-018). No existing workflow covers the POS terminal-level processing of consignment transactions.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Consignment item scan at checkout**: cashier scans consignment item barcode; POS system identifies item as consignment from item master flag (MDM — consignment indicator); system displays consignment indicator on cashier screen (discrete — not shown to customer); standard pricing and promotions applied per normal POS rules (W5B) | Cashier / System | — | No incremental time vs. standard scan |
| 2 | **Consignment ownership transfer**: at transaction completion (payment tender accepted), system executes ownership transfer for each consignment line item: (a) vendor-owned consignment inventory reduced by quantity sold, (b) standard owned inventory not affected (no owned stock existed), (c) ownership transfer event published via W533 with consignment flag, (d) COGS entry posted using consignment cost (not standard cost), (e) revenue posted per normal sales transaction | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | **Consignment sell-through data accumulation**: system accumulates daily consignment sell-through data per vendor: (a) item-level sales quantities and values, (b) date and store of sale, (c) transaction reference numbers; data published to vendor settlement accumulation table for FIN-018 processing | System | — | Automated |
| 4 | **Consignment return processing**: when a consignment item is returned (W12), system reverses ownership transfer: (a) inventory returned to vendor-owned consignment stock (not BuildRight owned stock), (b) COGS reversed, (c) revenue reversed per standard return processing, (d) sell-through accumulation adjusted; if item is defective, system flags for vendor RTV per consignment terms rather than standard RTV | System / Cashier | Store Manager | Per W12 |
| 5 | **Consignment pricing exception**: if vendor requests a temporary price change on consignment items (promotional pricing, markdown), pricing update flows through standard price push mechanism (POS-047) with consignment-specific GL posting (vendor-funded discount); Store Manager does not need to distinguish at POS — system handles automatically | System | — | Automated |
| 6 | **Monthly vendor settlement trigger**: at month-end, Finance runs consignment settlement per FIN-018; POS sell-through data accumulated in W543.3 provides the settlement basis; settlement report shared with vendor for reconciliation; AP voucher created for vendor payment (consignment cost × quantity sold − vendor-funded discounts − defectives) | Finance / System | Finance Manager | Per FIN-018 |

### System Touchpoints
- Item master consignment flag (MDM) detection at POS scan (W543.1)
- Ownership transfer event with consignment-specific GL posting (W543.2, W533)
- Consignment sell-through accumulation table (W543.3)
- Consignment return reversal to vendor-owned stock (W543.4, W12)
- Price push integration with consignment GL override (W543.5, POS-047)
- Monthly settlement integration with FIN-018 (W543.6)
- Integration with W5B (checkout — consignment scan), W12 (returns — consignment return), W533 (event streaming — ownership transfer event), INV-009/INV-017 (consignment inventory), FIN-018 (consignment settlement), W252 (item master — consignment flag), W289 (pricing master — consignment pricing), W525 (nightly reconciliation — consignment inventory reconciliation)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Consignment inventory inaccuracy** — if consignment flag is incorrect in item master, ownership transfer posts to wrong GL; mitigated by item master governance (W252) with consignment flag validation at SKU creation
- **Consignment stockout not visible to cashier** — consignment ATP shown same as owned stock; if vendor recalls consignment stock, POS may sell unavailable items; mitigated by real-time consignment inventory deduction and vendor recall processing per W29
- **Settlement dispute** — vendor may dispute sell-through quantities; mitigated by transaction-level sell-through data (W543.3) as evidence
- **Consignment vs. owned confusion at return** — cashier may not distinguish consignment returns; mitigated by system-enforced return routing based on item master flag


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-scan of "scan at checkout" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-post of "posted using consignment cost" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-flag of "flags for vendor RTV per consignment terms rather than standard RTV" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-264 (ensure controlled execution — Payment & Cash Management (PA-08.2).)
- operational: item master governance (W252) with consignment flag validation at SKU creation; operational: real-time consignment inventory deduction and vendor recall processing per W29; operational: transaction-level sell-through data (W543.3) as evidence

### Time Estimate
- Consignment scan at POS: no incremental time vs. standard scan
- Ownership transfer: automated (no cashier action)
- Consignment return: no incremental time vs. standard return (system auto-detects)
- Monthly settlement data preparation: automated per FIN-018
- **Total incremental POS time**: zero — consignment processing is entirely system-driven at POS level

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount at store level — consignment processing is transparent to cashier
- Finance: monthly consignment settlement processing absorbed into existing FIN-018 workflow
- Merchandising: consignment terms management absorbed into existing vendor management

## W1268. E-Wallet (GCash/Maya) Daily Settlement & Reconciliation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | End-of-day store closing; daily e-wallet settlement window |
| **Frequency** | Daily per store (200 stores) + consolidated HQ review |
| **Volume** | ~420,000 e-wallet transactions/month (~15% of 2.8M total POS transactions); ~PHP 756M/month in e-wallet tender (~15% of PHP 5.04B in-store revenue) |
| **Owner** | Cash Office Clerk (store-level); Treasury Analyst (HQ reconciliation) |
| **Participants** | Cash Office Clerk, Store Manager (exception escalation), Treasury Analyst, Finance Analyst |

### Background

E-wallet payments (GCash, Maya) represent ~15% of BuildRight Depot's POS transactions — approximately 14,000 transactions per day across 200 stores (420,000/month), with an estimated PHP 756M in monthly e-wallet tender. Unlike card payments that settle through traditional bank acquirers (covered by W537), e-wallet payments settle through Philippine digital payment providers with different mechanics: QR code-based or NFC-initiated transactions, next-day (T+1) settlement to designated bank accounts, per-transaction merchant discount rates (MDR) of 1.5–2.0%, and separate reconciliation portals. GCash and Maya each provide merchant dashboards and daily settlement reports. With 600 POS terminals processing e-wallet payments, a dedicated settlement and reconciliation workflow is essential to ensure all e-wallet tenders are captured, settled, and reconciled daily. W537 covers card terminal settlement; this workflow covers the parallel e-wallet settlement process.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **End-of-day e-wallet report generation**: at store closing per W574, system generates e-wallet tender summary per terminal and per store: (a) total GCash transactions — count and amount, (b) total Maya transactions — count and amount, (c) per-terminal breakdown, (d) any failed/declined e-wallet transactions during the day, (e) total e-wallet refunds processed | System | — | Automated |

E-wallet transactions flow as follows: (a) customer initiates payment via QR code scan or NFC tap at POS terminal; (b) e-wallet provider authorizes and captures payment; (c) POS records payment as e-wallet tender type; (d) e-wallet provider settles to BuildRight's bank account on T+1 (next business day) for GCash and T+1 to T+2 for Maya; (e) settlement is a batch lump sum covering all transactions from a given day — not per-transaction settlement. Without systematic daily reconciliation, e-wallet discrepancies accumulate into material unreconciled differences that are difficult to resolve after 30 days.

## W1468. Multi-Entity Cash Concentration, Zero-Balance Sweeping & Intercompany Settlement Automation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Daily — end-of-business cash positioning; or triggered by zero-balance sweep schedule; or monthly intercompany settlement cycle |
| **Frequency** | Daily sweeping for 5 entities across 4 banking partners; monthly intercompany netting and settlement |
| **Volume** | ~200 store bank accounts + 4 DC accounts + 5 entity main accounts = ~209 accounts; ~PHP 5B/month in total cash flows |
| **Owner** | Treasury Manager |
| **Participants** | Treasury Analyst, Controller, Bank Relationship Manager, IT (system integration), Entity Finance Leads |

### Background

BuildRight operates 5 legal entities across 4 banking partners (BDO, BPI, Metrobank, Chinabank), each maintaining separate bank accounts for operations. The 200 stores and 4 DCs each deposit daily cash receipts into local bank accounts. Without cash concentration, funds are fragmented across ~209 accounts, earning low interest in checking accounts while operating entities may need to borrow. The Treasury function must: (a) sweep excess cash from operating accounts to a central concentration account daily, (b) fund operating accounts as needed to cover disbursements, (c) settle intercompany balances between the 5 entities (e.g., BuildRight Depot Inc. owes BuildRight Logistics Inc. for warehousing services), and (d) ensure transfer pricing compliance for intercompany transactions per W87. This workflow manages the daily cash concentration, sweeping, and intercompany settlement process across the BuildRight group.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Daily cash position consolidation** (morning): Treasury Analyst retrieves previous day's bank balances across all ~209 accounts via bank API/EBBS integration: (a) store deposit accounts — cash receipts from W1354 deposits; (b) DC operating accounts; (c) entity main accounts; (d) USD accounts for import payments; (e) investment accounts per W318; Treasury Analyst produces consolidated cash position report by entity and by bank | Treasury Analyst | Treasury Manager | 45 min |
| 2 | **Zero-balance sweep execution**: For each operating account (stores, DCs), system executes automated end-of-day sweep: (a) accounts with balance > target operating balance: excess swept to entity concentration account at same bank; (b) accounts with balance < target: funded from entity concentration account to maintain minimum operating balance; (c) sweep parameters: store accounts maintain PHP 50,000 minimum; DC accounts maintain PHP 200,000 minimum; entity concentration accounts maintain PHP 5M minimum; (d) Treasury Analyst reviews sweep results next morning and investigates any exceptions (failed sweeps, insufficient concentration balance) | System / Treasury Analyst | Treasury Manager | Automated + 15 min review |
| 3 | **Entity-level cash concentration**: (a) Treasury Manager reviews entity-level cash positions weekly: which entities have surplus cash, which have deficits; (b) surplus entities lend to deficit entities via intercompany current accounts per W87 transfer pricing; (c) system records intercompany loan: principal, interest rate (arm's-length per BSP guidelines), term, and settlement date; (d) monthly: intercompany interest calculated and recorded per W87 consolidation | Treasury Manager | CFO | 2 hours/week |
| 4 | **Monthly intercompany netting & settlement**: (a) Controller compiles monthly intercompany balances: (i) Depot Inc. → Logistics Inc. (warehousing fees), (ii) Depot Inc. → Digital Commerce Inc. (ecommerce fulfillment fees), (iii) Depot Inc. → Property Management Inc. (lease payments), (iv) Holdings Inc. → all entities (management fees); (b) Treasury Manager runs multi-lateral netting: offset payables and receivables between entities to minimize actual cash movements; (c) net settlements executed via bank transfer between entity accounts; (d) system posts intercompany settlement entries with supporting documentation per W87 | Controller / Treasury Manager | CFO | 1 day/month |
| 5 | **Bank account reconciliation**: (a) Treasury Analyst reconciles all bank statements against ERP cash ledger weekly: (i) deposits per W1354 matched to bank credits, (ii) sweeps matched to bank debit/credit entries, (iii) intercompany settlements matched to bank transfers, (iv) bank fees and charges identified and recorded; (b) unreconciled items investigated and resolved within 5 business days | Treasury Analyst | Controller | 4 hours/week |
| 6 | **Quarterly bank relationship review**: Treasury Manager meets with each banking partner quarterly: (a) review account structures, sweep arrangements, and service fees; (b) negotiate improved sweep terms, higher deposit rates for concentration accounts; (c) evaluate banking technology (API reliability, EBBS features); (d) assess credit facilities per W319 | Treasury Manager | CFO | 2 hours/bank/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Bank API/EBBS integration — real-time balance retrieval and automated sweep initiation (W1468.1–2)
- Cash positioning module — consolidated view across all entities, banks, and accounts (W1468.1)
- Zero-balance sweep engine — configurable sweep rules per account with target balance and concentration hierarchy (W1468.2)
- Intercompany accounting module per W87 — loan recording, interest calculation, netting settlement (W1468.3–4)
- Cash Management (CE, fit-gap A4) — automated matching of bank statements to ERP entries (W1468.5)
- Integration with W87 (intercompany), W1354 (store deposits), W318 (investments), W319 (debt facilities), W541 (cash office)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Bank API reliability** — Philippine banking APIs may experience downtime, delaying sweep execution and cash positioning; manual fallback required
- **Multi-bank complexity** — 4 banking partners each with different API standards, fee structures, and sweep capabilities; standardization is difficult
- **Intercompany timing differences** — when one entity records an intercompany transaction in a different period than the counterparty, consolidation requires manual elimination entries per W87
- **Transfer pricing scrutiny** — BSP and BIR may scrutinize intercompany lending rates; rates must be demonstrably arm's-length (benchmark against market rates)
- **Store account minimum balance adequacy** — PHP 50,000 minimum may be insufficient during high-volume periods (payroll week, vendor payment week); insufficient balances cause payment failures


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "accounts via bank API/EBBS integration" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "reviews sweep results next morning and investigates any exceptions" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-record of "records intercompany loan" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-264 (ensure controlled execution — Payment & Cash Management (PA-08.2).)
- operational: Treasury Manager review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

- **Treasury Analyst**: daily cash positioning and reconciliation — ~5 hours/day; absorbed
- **Treasury Manager**: weekly entity concentration and quarterly bank reviews — ~6 hours/week; absorbed
- **Controller**: monthly intercompany netting — 1 day/month; absorbed
- **No incremental headcount**

## W5566. Payment-Terminal Tampering & Card-Skimmer Response (PIN-Pad Compromise Event)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Discovery of a card-skimming overlay/shimmer/pinhole device on a PIN pad or payment terminal, a device with broken or absent tamper-evident seals or swapped hardware, a terminal missing from its assigned lane, or an acquirer/card-brand/PCI forensic alert naming compromised devices at BuildRight sites |
| **Frequency** | Rare but a standing industry threat: ~1–3 device-compromise discoveries chain-wide per year across the ~3,200-unit PIN-pad estate; organized skimming crews rotate through retail lanes, so the discovery cadence is unpredictable |
| **Volume** | 1 discovery × the terminal-fleet inspection sweep (200 stores × ~16 payment devices = ~3,200 units) × the card-present transactions at the affected lane(s) feeding the compromised-card assessment |
| **Owner** | Store Manager (site response); IT Operations (payment-device estate, forensics & fleet sweep) |
| **Participants** | Cashier / Front-End Supervisor (first observers), Loss Prevention (CCTV preservation & suspect-window analysis), Payment Operations Manager (acquirer & card-brand liaison, W1205 PCI forensic engagement), Security Guard (lane closure & device custody), VS-125 (compromised-card fraud watch), VS-91 (personal-data assessment where cardholder data was captured), Store Communications (VS-63) |

### Background

The corpus owns the terminal's steady state and its availability: W537 runs the daily card-terminal operations lifecycle and settlement, W5547/W5564 own the outage rails, W1205 runs the PCI-DSS compliance program and annual QSA audit, and VS-125.2 watches card-present fraud at the transaction level — but nobody owned the device-compromise event itself: the physical-evidence rule (the device and its overlay are forensic evidence under chain of custody, never pried off or re-seated by store staff), the lane-quarantine and terminal-fleet sweep, the acquirer/card-brand notification duty that protects interchange rights, the compromised-card population assessment that decides customer and issuer messaging, or the tamper-evident-seal program retrofit that turns one discovery into a standing detection control. Skimming is the classic layered attack — the overlay captures the PAN while the pinhole captures the PIN — and the PCI DSS physical-inspection expectations (W1205's Requirement 9 class) only work at event time if someone owns the response.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Discovery & freeze**: suspected device (bulky overlay, loose fascia, damaged seal, unknown pigtail) → do not remove or power-cycle beyond isolating the lane; photograph in place; close the lane; the device stays under security custody until IT Security/forensics release it | Cashier / Front-End Supervisor | Store Manager | Immediate |
| 2 | **Preserve & escalate**: CCTV hold on the lane and approach paths per W838 covering at least 72 hours before discovery; note the last known-clean inspection from the W537 daily checks; escalate to IT Operations and Payment Operations Manager same hour | Store Manager / LP | IT Operations | Same hour |
| 3 | **Notify acquirer & card brands**: device-compromise notification through the acquirer per the scheme's PFI-trigger rules; engage the PCI forensic investigator through the W1205 program where card data capture is confirmed | Payment Operations Manager | CFO | 24 hours |
| 4 | **Fleet sweep**: chain-wide physical inspection directive — every terminal checked against seal/serial baseline, photographed, logged; stores confirm within the directive window; anomalies quarantine per Step 1 | IT Operations / Store Managers | VP Store Operations | 48–72 hours |
| 5 | **Compromised-card assessment**: affected lane(s), dates and transaction populations compiled from W537 settlement logs and W1425 reconciliation; feeds issuer compromise alerts via the acquirer and the VS-125 fraud watch for presentment abuse | Payment Operations Manager / VS-125 | CFO | 3–7 days |
| 6 | **Privacy & customer messaging**: where captured cardholder data is confirmed, route the VS-91 assessment (PAN + PIN is personal data under RA 10173; the W53 clock applies from confirmation); scripts for customer inquiries ready at the affected store | VS-91 / DPO | Data Protection Officer | Per W53 |
| 7 | **Retrofit & after-action**: tamper-evident seals and serial-baseline registry standing on the fleet; W537 daily checklist gains the seal/skimmer visual; LP briefing on the suspect pattern; inspection-sweep findings feed the W1205 evidence pack | IT Operations / LP | CFO | 2–4 weeks |

### System Touchpoints

- Terminal asset register with seal state, serial baseline and photo log per inspection sweep
- POS lane-status flags (quarantined lane, device out of service) mirrored to the W537 daily check
- CCTV preservation-hold workflow (W838) scoped to the lane and approach coverage
- Acquirer/card-brand case file for the compromise notification and PFI engagement (links to W1205)
- Settlement-log query set (W537/W1425) bounding the affected card-present transaction population
- VS-91 breach-assessment intake where cardholder data capture is confirmed (links to W53)

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Evidence-destruction risk**: staff prying off an overlay or re-seating the terminal destroys forensics and can void interchange protections; mitigated by Step 1's freeze-in-place rule and security custody
- **Under-scope risk**: treating a discovery as one bad device when the crew hit several lanes or stores; mitigated by Step 4's chain-wide sweep and Step 5's transaction-population analysis
- **Notification-gap risk**: late acquirer/card-brand notification shifts fraud liability to BuildRight; mitigated by Step 3's 24-hour clock riding the W1205 program
- **PIN-panel complacency risk**: daily checks that look only at functionality miss physical tamper; mitigated by Step 7's seal/skimmer visual in the W537 checklist
- **Panic-messaging risk**: speculative public statements before the data assessment; mitigated by Step 6's confirmed-data gate and scripted responses

### Automation Opportunity

- Terminal seal/serial baseline as a mobile check-in app with photo capture, auto-flagging anomalies into the sweep queue (replaces manual Step 4 tally)
- Lane-quarantine flag propagating automatically to POS lane assignment and the W537 daily-check state
- Settlement-log query templates auto-bounding the affected transaction population by lane and date window
- Acquirer case portal integration tracking the compromise notification and PFI status against the 24-hour clock

### Controls

- CTL-264 (ensure controlled execution — Payment & Cash Management (PA-08.2).)
- operational: payment devices are inspected against the seal/serial baseline daily (W537) and quarantined-in-place on suspicion; device-compromise notifications to the acquirer within 24 hours of discovery; fleet-sweep completion is evidenced per store before the event closes

### Time Estimate

Per single-device discovery ≈ 4–8 staff-hours for the site response (freeze, custody, documentation) plus ~15–30 minutes per store for the fleet-sweep inspection (≈ 50–100 staff-hours chain-wide for the sweep) plus 8–16 hours of payment-operations, forensic and assessment work; at the 1–3-discoveries-per-year cadence this stays absorbed within store crews, IT Operations and the payment-operations desk.

### Staffing Implication

- Absorbed by store crews, IT Operations, Loss Prevention and Payment Operations; no incremental headcount.
