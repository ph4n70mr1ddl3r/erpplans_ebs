## W4. Store Replenishment (DC → Store)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | System generates replenishment suggestion based on min/max or demand forecast |
| **Frequency** | 2–3 physical deliveries per store per week; ~5,000 replenishment orders/month total (each delivery truck carries 2–3 orders consolidated for that store) |
| **Volume** | ~167 orders/day across all DCs (~42 per DC/day); avg ~50 lines per order |
| **Owner** | Supply Planner |
| **Participants** | Supply Planner, DC Pick/Pack/Ship team, Truck Driver, Store Receiving Clerk, Store Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System calculates replenishment needs per store: current stock vs. min/max or forecast | System | — | Automated (nightly) |
| 2 | Supply Planner reviews suggested orders each morning; adjusts for promotions, seasonality, store-specific factors | Supply Planner | Supply Planning Manager | 2–3 hours/day |
| 3 | Planner confirms orders; system groups into waves by DC and delivery route | Supply Planner | Supply Planning Manager | 1 hour/day |
| 4 | DC receives pick wave; WMS assigns pick tasks to staff by zone | WMS / DC Supervisor | DC Supervisor | 5 min (assignment) |
| 5 | Picker pick items from bins per RF gun direction; scan-confirm each item | Picker | DC Supervisor | 2–4 hours/wave |
| 6 | Packers pack into store-labeled totes/cases; scan-confirm shipment | Packer | DC Supervisor | 1–2 hours/wave |
| 7 | Load truck per route sequence (multi-drop) | Loading Crew | DC Supervisor | 30–60 min |

| 13 | Replenishment moved to sales floor or backroom stock | Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | 30–60 min |

**Total cycle time**: 1–3 days from order creation to store shelf

### System Touchpoints
- Dual-sourced item management: for SKUs that are replenished through both DC delivery (W4) and DSD (W18) — typically cement, lumber, and bulky building materials — system maintains a primary/secondary supply channel indicator per SKU per store; ATP calculation aggregates on-hand + incoming DC replenishment + incoming DSD orders; when auto-replenishment (W4.1) generates a transfer order for a dual-sourced SKU, system checks if a DSD delivery is already scheduled within the replenishment lead time and adjusts the transfer order quantity accordingly to avoid overstocking; Store Manager can override the default supply channel per SKU via handheld; monthly: Supply Planner reviews dual-sourced SKU inventory levels to identify overstocking or conflicting deliveries (W4, W18)
- Replenishment calculation engine (min/max, forecast-based) (W4.1)
- Constrained allocation rules: when available supply is insufficient for all stores, system applies configurable allocation logic (e.g., equal distribution, rank by store revenue, prioritize A-stores) — Planner reviews and adjusts before confirming (W4.2)
- Store order creation and wave planning (W4.3)
- WMS pick/pack/ship with RF scanning (W4.4–6)
- In-transit inventory visibility (W4.8)
- Store receiving with discrepancy handling (W4.10–12)
- Real-time inventory update at both DC and store (W4.8, W4.12)
- FEFO (First Expired First Out) directed picking: for items with shelf-life tracking (paint, adhesives, chemicals, cement), WMS directs pickers to pick the earliest-expiring batch first; system sequences pick tasks by expiry date within the same SKU; ensures fresher stock remains in DC for later dispatch (W4.5)
- Inventory ownership clarification: goods moving from DC to store in W4 are Depot Inc. inventory at both locations; DC facilities are operated by Logistics Inc. (which charges monthly warehousing/distribution fees per W14), but Depot Inc. owns the merchandise throughout; W4 transfer orders are intra-entity inventory movements (no per-TO IC invoice); IC invoicing applies only to inter-entity goods transfers (W22) or service fees (W14)
- New store demand ramp-up (first 90 days): for newly opened stores (W16), auto-replenishment parameters (ROP, safety stock, min/max) derived from comparable store averages may not reflect actual local demand patterns during the ramp-up period; during the first 90 days post-opening, Supply Planner overrides auto-replenishment with manual review — (a) system flags all replenishment orders for new stores with "Ramp-Up" status requiring Planner confirmation (no auto-release), (b) Planner reviews suggested orders daily against early sell-through data and adjusts quantities based on actual demand velocity observed in the first weeks, (c) Store Manager provides daily feedback on fast-moving and slow-moving items via the W4B store-initiated replenishment request channel, (d) after 90 days, Demand Planner analyzes accumulated sales data to calculate store-specific ROP/safety stock parameters per W31.8; system transitions the store from "Ramp-Up" to standard auto-replenishment; parameters reviewed again at 180 days; this manual override period prevents both overstocking (tying up working capital in a new store) and stockouts (damaging customer first impressions)
- Constrained allocation rule governance: when available supply is insufficient for all stores, system applies configurable allocation logic; Supply Planning Manager defines allocation method per SKU or category (equal distribution, rank by store revenue, prioritize A-stores, proportional to historical demand); allocation rules reviewed and approved by VP Supply Chain; rule changes logged with old method, new method, reason, approver, and effective date; monthly: Supply Planning Manager reviews allocation fairness dashboard showing per-store fill rate and allocation share; fairness disputes escalated by Store Managers through W4B channel feed into allocation rule review; quarterly: allocation methodology reviewed as part of W31.8 parameter governance cycle (W4.2)

### Pain Points / Risks
- Constrained allocation disputes: when DC stock is insufficient, Supply Planners face pressure from Store Managers and Regional Managers to prioritize their stores, creating internal friction that the allocation governance framework (W105) attempts to mediate but cannot fully eliminate.
- New store ramp-up overstocking risk: during the 90-day ramp-up period, manually overriding auto-replenishment parameters is labor-intensive and prone to error — early overestimation ties up working capital in slow-moving stock at new locations.
- Dual-sourced SKU replenishment conflicts: when both DC delivery (W4) and DSD (W18) supply channels are active for the same SKU, timing mismatches can result in double-ordering, especially if the DSD schedule changes after the DC transfer order is already confirmed.
- FEFO picking compliance is inconsistent — WMS directs pickers to earliest-expiring batch, but in high-volume waves pickers sometimes bypass FEFO sequence to meet productivity targets, leading to aged stock accumulation in DC.
- In-transit inventory visibility gaps for inter-island deliveries (W66) mean Supply Planners cannot provide accurate ETAs to Store Managers during typhoon-related delays.


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-calculate of "calculates replenishment needs per store" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "reviews suggested orders each morning" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-route of "Planner confirms orders" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-25 (safeguard inventory in transit.)
- operational: Supply Planner review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Daily replenishment review (steps 2–3): 2–4 hours/day for 2–3 Supply Planners. DC pick/pack/ship (steps 4–7): 4–8 hours per wave across shifts. Store receiving (steps 10–12): 30–60 min per delivery. Total end-to-end cycle: 1–3 days from order creation to store shelf.

### Staffing Implication
- **2–3 Supply Planners** (in HQ): ~167 orders/day to review. At 2–3 hours/day for review + 1 hour for wave management = 3–4 hours/day. 2–3 planners share this plus demand forecasting. Reasonable within the 40-person Supply Chain & Logistics team (per profile §3.3).
- **Per DC**: 15–20 Pickers, 8–10 Packers, 4–6 Loading Crew (working in shifts to handle ~42 orders/day with ~50 lines each). Fits within ~150 DC headcount.
- **Store Receiving Clerks**: 30–60 min per delivery × 2–3 deliveries/week = ~1–3 hours/week. Absorbed by existing store receiving staff.
- **No incremental headcount beyond planned staffing.**

### System Touchpoints
- Store-initiated replenishment request form in POS/terminal/handheld with SKU selection, quantity, urgency, and justification fields (W4B.1)
- Supply Planner dashboard showing request alongside current supply position (on-hand, ATP, open POs, pending replenishment) (W4B.2)
- Request status lifecycle: Submitted → Under Review → Approved (Scheduled / Pending Supply) / Denied → Fulfilled / Closed (W4B.3–7)
- Weekly request analytics: approval rate, requesting stores, frequently requested items feeding into ROP parameter review (W31.8) (W4B.8)
- Integration with W4 (replenishment order creation), W2A (expedited PO), W22 (inter-DC transfer), W31 (forecast and ROP adjustment)

### Pain Points / Risks
- Store Managers in remote or provincial locations (Visayas, Mindanao) submit a disproportionate share of ad-hoc requests because demand patterns are less predictable than Metro Manila stores, stretching Supply Planner review capacity.
- Denied requests create friction with Store Managers who have local market knowledge that the central planning team may lack; escalation to Regional Manager adds a political dimension that can slow the standard W4 replenishment cycle.
- High-frequency ad-hoc requests for the same SKUs signal that ROP/safety stock parameters are misconfigured (W31.8), but the feedback loop from W4B analytics to parameter adjustment can take 30–60 days, during which stores experience repeated stockouts.
- Emergency expedited requests that require a separate shipment (not riding the next wave) incur incremental 3PL freight cost of PHP 5,000–15,000 per trip, which is rarely tracked back to the requesting store's P&L.

- **Supply Planner**: ~100–200 requests/month ÷ 20 working days = ~5–10/day. At ~10 min each for review = ~1 hour/day incremental. Absorbed within existing 2–3 planner team.
- **Store Managers**: ~0.5–1 requests/month × 10 min = negligible.

### Staffing Implication (W4 overall)

## W6. Cycle Counting & Inventory Accuracy

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Daily schedule (rolling through all sections) |
| **Frequency** | Daily per store; each SKU counted at least once per quarter |
| **Volume** | ~700 SKUs/day per store; 200 stores × 700 = 140,000 SKU counts/day chain-wide |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Stock Associate (counter), Department Supervisor (reviewer), Store Manager (approver for adjustments) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System generates daily count assignment by section/aisle | System | — | Automated (nightly) |
| 2 | Stock Associate retrieves count sheet on handheld/RF device | Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | — |
| 3 | Physically count each SKU in assigned section; enter quantity into device (4 Stock Associates each count ~175 SKUs/day, taking ~30 min each at ~10 sec/SKU) | Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | ~40 min per associate |
| 4 | System compares physical count to system count; flags variances | System | — | Automated |
| 5 | For flagged items: Stock Associate recounts (blind recount) | Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | 15–30 min |
| 6 | If variance confirmed: Department Supervisor reviews and approves adjustment | Dept. Supervisor | Store Manager | 15 min |
| 7 | If adjustment > PHP 10,000 or > 5% of SKU value: Store Manager approval required | Store Manager | Store Manager | 5 min each |
| 8 | System posts inventory adjustment; audit trail recorded | System | — | Automated |
| 8a | **In-store damage discovered during operations**: Stock Associate identifies damaged item on sales floor (customer drop, water damage, forklift damage, yard weather damage); creates damage report in system with photo and cause code; Department Supervisor reviews and approves disposition: (a) markdown and sell at reduced price, (b) scrap with supervisor authorization, (c) Return to Vendor per W3.6a process; system posts inventory adjustment and loss to damage/scrapping account | Stock Associate / Dept. Supervisor | Store Manager | 10 min |
| 9 | Root cause analysis for recurring variances (theft, damage, receiving errors) | Dept. Supervisor | Store Manager | Weekly review |

**Cycle**: 35,000 SKUs ÷ 700/day = 50 working days per full cycle (~10 weeks ≈ quarterly)

### System Touchpoints
- Automated count assignment by zone/section (W6.1)
- RF device / handheld count entry (W6.3)
- Variance detection and blind recount workflow (W6.4–5)
- Inventory adjustment with tiered approval (W6.6–7)
- Immutable audit trail for all adjustments (W6.8)
- In-store damage discovery reporting with photo, cause code, and disposition workflow (W6.8a)
- Near-expiry alerting: during cycle counts, system flags items approaching expiry (configurable threshold per category, e.g., 90 days for paint, 60 days for cement); Department Supervisor reviews flagged items and initiates disposition per W13.9a (markdown), W3.6a (RTV), or W13.9b (scrap/liquidation) (W6)
- Negative inventory resolution: system generates daily negative inventory alert listing all SKU-locations where on-hand < 0; at store level, Stock Associate investigates root cause (timing lag from offline POS transactions per W5G, receiving error, mispick, or cycle count needed); at DC level, Inventory Control clerk investigates (pending GR posting, allocation error, picking error); resolution action depends on cause — recount and adjust (W6), wait for pending transaction posting, or force adjustment with supervisor approval; system blocks negative-inventory locations from ecommerce ATP availability until resolved; monthly report of negative inventory frequency by location feeds into inventory accuracy improvement initiatives

### Pain Points / Risks
- With only 4 Stock Associates per store (per profile §12.1) and no slack for absenteeism, cycle counting is the first task deferred during peak trading hours, causing accuracy degradation that compounds over weeks.
- Negative inventory resolution requires immediate investigation that competes with customer-facing duties; unresolved negative inventory blocks ecommerce ATP availability (W11), directly impacting online sales.
- Near-expiry alerting during cycle counts generates disposition decisions (markdown, RTV, scrap) that require Department Supervisor approval — if the Supervisor is unavailable, expired stock accumulates on shelves in categories like paint and adhesives.
- Blind recount process adds 15–30 min per flagged section; during high-variance periods (post-promo, post-typhoon), the recount workload can exceed Stock Associate capacity, causing counting backlogs.
- In-store damage discovery (W6.8a) during cycle counts creates a dual workflow burden — the associate must simultaneously complete the count and document damage with photos and cause codes, slowing both processes.


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "count assignment by section/aisle" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-capture of "enter quantity into device" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-flag of "compares physical count to system count" (Step 4, already system-executed).

### Controls
- CTL-11 (separate inventory counting from adjustment approval.); CTL-21 (ensure inventory accuracy.)
- operational: Department Supervisor review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Daily count per Stock Associate: ~40 min for ~175 SKUs (700 ÷ 4 associates). Blind recount: 15–30 min additional for flagged items. Adjustment processing: 5–15 min per adjustment (including approvals). Total per store per day: ~2–3 hours across 4 Stock Associates (counting + recounts + adjustments).

### Staffing Implication
- **4 Stock Associates per store (per profile §12.1)**: Each counts ~175 SKUs/day (~40 min; 700 ÷ 4 associates), with remainder of time on replenishment, receiving, damage reporting, and BOPIS picking. The count of 4 (per §12.1) covers the counting plan with modest slack, though peak absenteeism still defers counting (per the pain point).

## W42. Annual Physical Inventory Execution

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Year-end close calendar (typically December 31 or last business day of fiscal year) |
| **Frequency** | Annual; each store and DC counted once per year |
| **Volume** | 35,000 SKUs × ~205 locations (200 stores + 4 DCs); executed in coordinated 3–5 day window per region |
| **Owner** | Cost Accountant (overall); Store Manager (per store); DC Manager (per DC) |
| **Participants** | Cost Accountant, Store Managers, DC Managers, all store staff, inventory count teams, IT, Internal Audit |

### Pre-Count Planning (2–3 weeks before count)

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cost Accountant issues physical inventory instructions: count dates, methodology, team assignments, system freeze schedule | Cost Accountant | Controller | 4 hours |
| 2 | IT configures count sheets in system: generate zone-based count sheets per location with expected system quantities (hidden for blind count) | IT Team | Cost Accountant | 1 day |
| 3 | Store Managers organize count teams: assign zones (8 zones per store), designate team leaders, schedule shifts for 2-day count | Store Manager | VP Store Operations | 2 hours/store |
| 4 | Store Managers ensure all receiving and shipments are posted before count; process pending transactions; stage goods in correct locations | Store Manager | VP Store Operations | 4 hours/store |
| 5 | Internal Audit prepares to observe counts at selected locations (sample: 20–30 stores + all DCs) | Internal Audit | CFO | 1 week |

### Count Execution (Day 1 — full count; Days 2–3 — recounts and adjustments; Days 4–5 — residual clean-up if needed)

> **Feasibility note**: Counting 35,000 SKUs per store with the ~29 store staff in 2 days is infeasible for a full wall-to-wall count. BuildRight addresses this with a **tiered counting strategy** (counting tiers are value-based cuts and deliberately tighter than the profile §6.6 ABC classes, whose A+B band is 50% of SKUs / 17,500): (a) **count-tier-1 items** — all A items plus the highest-value B items (~10,500 SKUs, ~95% of inventory value) — receive full physical count during the annual window; (b) **count-tier-2 items** — the remaining lower-value B/C items (~24,500 SKUs, referred to below as the C-tier) — are validated by extrapolating from rolling cycle counts (W6) conducted throughout the year — the annual process confirms that cycle count accuracy for C-tier items meets the ≥ 97% threshold, rather than recounting every C-tier item. This reduces the per-store counting burden to ~10,500 SKUs ÷ ~29 staff ÷ 2 days ≈ ~180 SKUs/person/day, achievable at ~3 min/SKU including travel between locations. DCs, with higher item density and forklift-access racking, allocate 3–5 days for a full count of all SKUs.
>
> **C-tier extrapolation methodology**: C-tier inventory accuracy is validated using a statistical sampling approach — (a) throughout the year, W6 cycle counts cover all C-tier SKUs at least once per quarter (per the quarterly cycle in W6); (b) each C-tier SKU's cycle count accuracy (physical vs. system quantity) is recorded per count event; (c) at year-end, Cost Accountant computes the aggregate C-tier accuracy rate = (total C-tier SKU-locations where physical matched system within tolerance) ÷ (total C-tier SKU-locations counted during the year); (d) if aggregate accuracy ≥ 97%, C-tier items are considered validated and no additional count is required at year-end; (e) if aggregate accuracy < 97%, Cost Accountant identifies the worst-performing C-tier categories (by variance rate) and adds them to the annual count scope; (f) sample size for interim confidence: to achieve 95% confidence that the true accuracy rate is within ±1% of the observed rate, a minimum of ~500 C-tier SKU-locations must be cycle-counted per quarter per store (approximately 2% of C-tier SKU-locations, achievable within W6 daily count volume of ~700 SKUs/day); (g) this methodology is reviewed and approved by Internal Audit annually as part of the W42 physical inventory observation; (h) **below-threshold contingency**: if aggregate C-tier accuracy falls below 97%, Cost Accountant quantifies the gap and identifies worst-performing C-tier categories by variance rate; additional items added to the annual count scope in priority order (highest-variance categories counted first); if additional scope threatens the 2-day count window, Cost Accountant escalates to Controller with a proposed extended count schedule (up to 5 days with staggered zone freezing per W42 system freeze mitigation); Controller approves or denies extension; if extension denied, Cost Accountant counts the highest-risk C-tier items within the standard window and accepts a qualified inventory opinion for the remaining C-tier items — Controller discloses the qualification in the year-end close package (W9B); Internal Audit documents the below-threshold outcome and includes it in the W42 observation report.

## W1413. DC-Level Cycle Count Discrepancy Root Cause Analysis, Corrective Action & Recount Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Cycle count variance exceeds threshold (> 1% by value or > 3 units per SKU); or recurring variance on same SKU/location across consecutive counts |
| **Frequency** | Triggered — approximately 5–10% of daily cycle counts produce actionable variances at 4 DCs; ~80–100 discrepancy investigations/month (per the Time Estimate ≈25/DC/month) |
| **Volume** | ~1,000–1,200 DC cycle count discrepancy investigations/year (≈100/month per the Time Estimate); recurring discrepancy patterns affect ~50–100 SKU-locations |
| **Owner** | DC Operations Manager |
| **Participants** | DC Operations Manager, Inventory Control Clerk, Warehouse Supervisor, LP Officer, Supply Planner |

### Background

While W6 covers the cycle counting process itself, W1413 addresses the structured investigation process that must follow when a cycle count reveals a discrepancy beyond acceptable thresholds. DCs are the central inventory control point for BuildRight — inaccurate DC inventory cascades into incorrect store replenishment, missed BOPIS fulfillment, and distorted demand signals. Without rigorous root cause analysis, the same discrepancies recur, masking systemic issues (putaway errors, pick errors, receiving count errors, theft). This workflow ensures every significant DC inventory variance is investigated, root-caused, and corrected at the source.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cycle count completes (W6); system compares physical count to system on-hand; if variance exceeds threshold: (a) value variance > 1% of SKU value at that location, or (b) quantity variance > 3 units, or (c) SKU has been flagged as "recurring variance" in previous 3 counts: system automatically flags count for investigation and holds the adjustment from posting | System | — | Automated |
| 2 | Inventory Control Clerk receives investigation alert with details: SKU, location (bin/slot), system on-hand, physical count, variance (units and value), SKU velocity (A/B/C), and count history (number of variances on this SKU/location in last 12 months) | Inventory Control Clerk | — | 5 min |
| 3 | **Immediate recount**: Inventory Control Clerk performs blind recount of the flagged SKU at the flagged location (does not see expected count); if recount matches original physical count: variance confirmed; if recount matches system on-hand: original count was in error, variance cleared, no further investigation needed | Inventory Control Clerk | Warehouse Supervisor | 15 min/SKU |
| 4 | If variance confirmed after recount: Inventory Control Clerk expands search to adjacent locations (same SKU may be in multiple bins): (a) check reserve storage locations for the SKU, (b) check staging areas for pending outbound shipments, (c) check dock area for recently received but not-yet-putaway inventory; if missing quantity is found in an alternate location: location error identified, bin-to-bin transfer posted, root cause = "Putaway Location Error" | Inventory Control Clerk | — | 20 min/SKU |
| 5 | If missing quantity not found in alternate locations: Inventory Control Clerk checks transaction history for the SKU at that location for the past 7 days: (a) all inbound receipts (W3), (b) all outbound picks (W4, W22), (c) any bin-to-bin transfers, (d) any returns processing (W651), (e) any adjustments posted; looks for unconfirmed picks (picked but not shipped), uncompleted putaways (received but not confirmed to bin), or quantity entry errors | Inventory Control Clerk | DC Operations Manager | 30 min/SKU |
| 6 | Inventory Control Clerk classifies root cause from standardized dropdown: (a) **Receiving Error** — GR quantity incorrect vs. actual received; (b) **Putaway Error** — items put to wrong bin/location; (c) **Pick Error** — wrong quantity picked for outbound order; (d) **Shipping Error** — quantity shipped differs from pick quantity; (e) **Damaged/Destroyed** — items damaged and removed but not recorded; (f) **Theft/Shrinkage** — no operational explanation found; escalate to LP; (g) **System Error** — software processing error (rare); (h) **Documentation Error** — transaction entered incorrectly | Inventory Control Clerk | DC Operations Manager | 5 min |
| 7 | **Corrective action by root cause**: (a) Receiving Error — retrain Receiving Clerk on count verification, add dual-count protocol for high-value items; (b) Putaway Error — verify WMS directed putaway accuracy, retrain putaway staff; (c) Pick Error — check pick path accuracy, verify RF scanner calibration, retrain picker; (d) Shipping Error — add pack verification scan before dispatch; (e) Damaged — update damage reporting process, add damage chute signage; (f) Theft — escalate to LP Officer for investigation per W37, review CCTV, check employee access logs; (g) System Error — report to IT Helpdesk (W48) for investigation; (h) Documentation Error — retrain on transaction entry | DC Operations Manager / Warehouse Supervisor | DC Operations Manager | 30 min |
| 8 | DC Operations Manager approves inventory adjustment: system posts adjustment to correct on-hand; adjustment journal entry created with root cause code, investigation notes, and corrective action reference; GL impact: Dr/Cr Inventory with offset to Shrinkage/Damage/Loss account per chart of accounts | DC Operations Manager | Controller (if value > PHP 50,000) | 10 min |
| 9 | **Recurring variance escalation**: if same SKU/location has 3+ variances in 12 months: system flags as chronic; DC Operations Manager initiates deep investigation: (a) 30-day intensive monitoring with daily micro-counts of the SKU/location, (b) process observation — DC Operations Manager physically observes receiving, putaway, and picking activities for the SKU, (c) bin location physical audit — verify bin dimensions and capacity match WMS configuration, (d) if theft suspected: LP Officer installs targeted CCTV or conducts covert surveillance per W23 | DC Operations Manager / LP Officer | VP Supply Chain | 4 hours/SKU |
| 10 | Monthly: DC Operations Manager generates cycle count discrepancy analysis report: (a) total variances by root cause category, (b) total inventory value adjustment, (c) top 20 SKU-locations by recurring variance frequency, (d) corrective action closure rate, (e) DC inventory accuracy trend (target ≥ 99% for A-items, ≥ 97% overall); report shared with VP Supply Chain and Controller | DC Operations Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Automated variance flagging: threshold-based triggers (% value, quantity, recurring SKU flag) with investigation hold on adjustment posting (W1413.1)
- Investigation workspace: SKU details, count history, transaction history (receipts, picks, transfers, adjustments) for past 7 days (W1413.2, W1413.5)
- Root cause classification: standardized dropdown with 8 categories mapping to corrective action templates (W1413.6–7)
- Inventory adjustment posting: root cause-coded journal entry with GL impact; approval tiered by value threshold (W1413.8)
- Recurring variance tracker: 12-month rolling count history per SKU-location with auto-flag at 3+ variances (W1413.9)
- Monthly discrepancy analysis dashboard: root cause distribution, value impact, top recurring SKU-locations, accuracy trend (W1413.10)
- Integration with W6 (cycle counting — count data source), W3 (DC receiving — receiving error investigation), W4 (store replenishment — pick error investigation), W22 (inter-DC transfer — transfer error investigation), W37/W23 (loss prevention — theft escalation), W92 (inventory adjustment — adjustment posting), W109 (store receiving — parallel store process), W48 (IT helpdesk — system error reporting)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Investigation backlog**: at 5–10% of daily cycle counts producing actionable variances, 4 DCs generating ~1,000 counts/day produce ~3–5 investigation alerts/day chain-wide (≈80–100/month per the Frequency); Inventory Control Clerks process ~15–20 investigations/day across the 4 DCs, creating a persistent backlog during peak periods
- **"Catch-all" root cause classification**: investigators may default to "Documentation Error" as the root cause rather than conducting thorough investigation; this masks systemic issues and prevents meaningful corrective action
- **LP escalation threshold too high**: the 3-variance-in-12-months threshold for theft escalation means a slow, sustained pilferage of 1–2 units per month per SKU is not flagged for LP investigation for a full year; the cumulative loss may be significant for high-value items (power tools, appliances)
- **Adjacent location search time**: expanding the search to alternate locations (step 4) is time-consuming in a 35,000 sqm DC with thousands of bin locations; without WMS bin-search tools, the investigator may abandon the search prematurely


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "Cycle count completes" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-alert of "alert with details" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-match of "matches original physical count" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-253 (ensure controlled execution — Inventory Accuracy & Counting (PA-05.1).)
- operational: DC Operations Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
- Per investigation: ~75 min average (5 min alert + 15 min recount + 20 min location search + 30 min transaction analysis + 5 min classification)
- Monthly volume: ~25 investigations/DC × 4 DCs = ~100 investigations/month
- Corrective action implementation: ~30 min per investigation
- Monthly report: ~2 hours/month
- Total: ~175 hours/month (~8 hours/business-day across 4 DCs)

### Staffing Implication
- Inventory Control Clerks: 1 per DC (4 total); ~30 hours/month each on discrepancy investigation; primary responsibility within existing role
- DC Operations Manager: ~2 hours/week per DC on review and approval; absorbed
- LP Officers: ~10 hours/month across 4 DCs on theft escalation investigations; absorbed
- No incremental headcount

## W1486. Lumber Yard Inventory Measurement, Board Foot Calculation & Dimensional Grading Verification

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Weekly cycle count schedule for lumber yard; also triggered upon receipt of lumber shipments and when discrepancy between system quantity and physical count exceeds 3% |
| **Frequency** | Weekly cycle count; per-shipment verification on receipt; monthly full yard reconciliation |
| **Volume** | ~50–80 SKUs per store in lumber/builder's wood; ~15–30 lumber receipts/store/month; weekly spot-check of 10–15 SKUs |
| **Owner** | Lumber Yard Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Lumber Yard Associate, Stock Associate, Department Supervisor |

### Background

BuildRight stores maintain a lumber yard carrying 50–80 SKUs of dimensional lumber (tanguile, apitong, yakal), plywood, cement boards, and engineered wood products. Lumber is sold by the piece but priced by board foot (bf) for many species, creating a measurement challenge: actual dimensions of Philippine-sawn lumber often vary from nominal dimensions by ±5–10%, and moisture content affects weight and warping. Unlike boxed merchandise, lumber cannot be barcoded individually; it is managed by bundle count and species/size. Inventory accuracy in the lumber yard is historically the lowest of any department (typically 75–85% vs. 90–95% target for dry goods), driven by: (a) physical difficulty of counting bundles in outdoor yards, (b) moisture-induced swelling/shrinkage altering dimensions, (c) pilferage of individual pieces from bundles, (d) customer selectivity — customers pull pieces from bundles, rejecting warped or split boards, which breaks bundle integrity.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lumber receipt dimensional verification**: When lumber shipment arrives: (a) Lumber Yard Associate counts bundles per delivery receipt; (b) for each species/size, associate measures a sample of 5 pieces from different bundles using tape measure; (c) actual dimensions recorded and board foot calculated per piece; (d) average bf per piece × piece count × bundles = received bf; (e) variance > 5% from supplier invoice triggers dispute per PA-15.1; (f) moisture meter reading taken from 3 random pieces per species; (g) if moisture > 20% for kiln-dried lumber: shipment may be rejected or accepted at discount | Lumber Yard Associate | Lumber Yard Supervisor | 30–45 min per shipment |
| 2 | **Weekly lumber yard cycle count**: Weekly: (a) system selects 10–15 lumber SKUs for spot count based on ABC classification and time since last count; (b) Lumber Yard Associate physically counts pieces per SKU in the yard; (c) for high-value species (yakal, apitong), associate verifies dimensions of 3 random pieces per bundle to confirm grading; (d) system compares physical count to book quantity; (e) variance > 3% triggers recount and root cause investigation; (f) adjustment posted per PA-05.1 cycle count adjustment workflow with supervisor approval required for adjustments > PHP 10,000 | Lumber Yard Associate | Lumber Yard Supervisor | 60–90 min/week |
| 3 | **Bundle integrity monitoring**: Daily: (a) Lumber Yard Associate walks the yard checking bundle bands/strapping; (b) opened or partially depleted bundles re-tallied; (c) loose pieces identified and returned to correct bundles or written off as damaged; (d) customer-rejected pieces (warped, split, knotty beyond grade) set aside for markdown per PA-05.3 disposition rules; (e) findings logged in system for shrinkage tracking | Lumber Yard Associate | Department Supervisor | 20 min/day |
| 4 | **Monthly board foot reconciliation**: Monthly: (a) system generates lumber inventory valuation report showing book bf vs. theoretical bf based on receipts minus sales; (b) Lumber Yard Supervisor reconciles with physical yard inventory; (c) dimensional shrinkage factor applied (Philippine lumber typically shrinks 2–5% from green to air-dry state); (d) unexplained loss > 2% of monthly lumber COGS escalated to LP team per PA-23.3; (e) findings feed into vendor performance scorecard for lumber suppliers | Lumber Yard Supervisor | Department Supervisor | 2–3 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Lumber SKU master with nominal dimensions, species, grade, and board foot formula (W1486.1)
- Mobile cycle count terminal with piece-count entry and dimension override (W1486.2)
- Bundle tracking with receipt date, supplier, and piece count per bundle (W1486.1–3)
- Moisture meter reading capture field on receiving transaction (W1486.1)
- Board foot inventory valuation with dimensional shrinkage adjustment (W1486.4)
- Lumber supplier scorecard integration for quality/dimension disputes (W1486.1, .4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Nominal vs. actual dimension variance**: Philippine "2×4" lumber is typically 1.5×3.5 inches actual; pricing by board foot with actual dimensions creates confusion; system must clearly distinguish nominal (for ordering) from actual (for pricing and inventory)
- **Seasonal moisture variation**: lumber stored in outdoor yards swells during rainy season (June–November) and shrinks during dry season (March–May); weight-based inventory is unreliable
- **Customer cherry-picking**: high-grade lumber pieces get picked first, leaving lower-grade pieces that are harder to sell; eventually the bundle's average grade drops below what was paid for
- **Physical counting difficulty**: counting lumber pieces in outdoor yard during rain or extreme heat is unpleasant; associates may rush counts leading to inaccuracy


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "recorded and board foot calculated per piece" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-count of "Weekly lumber yard cycle count" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "logged in system for shrinkage tracking" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-253 (ensure controlled execution — Inventory Accuracy & Counting (PA-05.1).)
- operational: must clearly distinguish nominal (for ordering) from actual (for pricing and inventory)

### Staffing Implication
- Lumber Yard Associate: ~90 min/week on cycle count + 20 min/day bundle monitoring + ~45 min/receipt verification × ~20 receipts/month = ~25–30 hours/month. Absorbed within existing lumber yard staffing.
- Lumber Yard Supervisor: ~3 hours/month on reconciliation + ~1 hour/week oversight. Absorbed.
- No incremental headcount.

### Time Estimate
- Receipt verification: 30–45 min × ~20 receipts/month = 10–15 hours/store/month
- Weekly cycle count: 60–90 min × 4 = 4–6 hours/store/month
- Bundle monitoring: 20 min/day × 25 days = ~8 hours/store/month
- Monthly reconciliation: 2–3 hours
- **Total: ~25–33 hours/store/month**
