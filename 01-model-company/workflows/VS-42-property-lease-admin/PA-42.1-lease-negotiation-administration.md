## W1854. New Site Lease Negotiation & Execution

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New store or DC location approved by real estate team (W1652 site selection) |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 new leases/year (aligned with 10–15 new stores/year growth target) |
| **Volume** | Each lease negotiation covers 1 property; lease term typically 5–15 years |
| **Owner** | COO / Legal Counsel |
| **Participants** | COO, Legal Counsel, CFO, VP Store Operations, Landlord |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | COO identifies and shortlists candidate properties per W1652 (site selection): (a) location criteria: population density, trade area demographics, accessibility (highway frontage, public transport), proximity to competitors, (b) property specifications: selling area 8,000–15,000 sqm, adequate parking (100–200 slots), loading dock access for delivery trucks, zoning compliance (commercial), (c) ownership verification: clean title, no encumbrances, tax declarations current | COO | COO | Per W1652 |
| 2 | COO conducts preliminary lease negotiation with landlord: (a) rental rate per sqm (benchmark: PHP 300–800/sqm/month depending on location; Metro Manila premium), (b) lease term and renewal options (target: 10–15 years with 5-year renewal options), (c) annual escalation rate (typical: 3–5% per annum or CPI-linked), (d) tenant improvement allowance (landlord contribution to store fit-out), (e) common area maintenance (CAM) charges, (f) exclusivity clause (no competing hardware/home improvement tenant in same development), (g) early termination provisions, (h) assignment and subleasing rights | COO | CFO | 2–4 weeks |
| 3 | Legal Counsel drafts/reviews lease agreement: (a) Philippine legal compliance: Civil Code provisions on lease contracts, (b) IFRS 16 / PFRS 16 implications: classification as operating lease vs. finance lease, right-of-use (ROU) asset and lease liability accounting, (c) force majeure clause: typhoon, earthquake, pandemic provisions, (d) dispute resolution: arbitration vs. litigation, jurisdiction, (e) building permits and occupancy permits: landlord responsibility for securing, (f) insurance requirements: fire, earthquake, liability coverage minimums, (g) data privacy: landlord access to tenant premises and customer data considerations | Legal Counsel | VP Legal | 1–2 weeks |
| 4 | CFO reviews financial terms: (a) total lease obligation: monthly rent × 12 months × lease term + escalation, (b) NPV of lease payments for PFRS 16 lease liability calculation, (c) impact on BuildRight Property Management, Inc. cash flow and intercompany settlement with BuildRight Depot, Inc., (d) comparison to benchmark rents in same area, (e) approval: lease commitments > PHP 50M require CEO approval; > PHP 200M require Board approval | CFO | CEO / Board | 1 week |
| 5 | Lease execution: (a) COO and Legal Counsel coordinate lease signing with landlord, (b) notarization per Philippine legal requirements, (c) lease agreement registered in Oracle Property Manager (fit-gap F3), (d) Finance creates lease liability and ROU asset per PFRS 16 in BuildRight Property Management, Inc. books, (e) COO coordinates tenant fit-out with landlord and store construction team per W20 (real estate & construction) | COO / Legal / Finance | CFO | 1–2 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- Oracle Property Manager (fit-gap F3): lease register with key dates, terms, financial obligations (W1854.5c)
- PFRS 16 lease accounting: ROU asset and lease liability creation (W1854.5d)
- Property master data: location, landlord, property specifications (W1854.5c)
- Approval workflow for lease commitments (W1854.4e)
- Integration with W1652 (site selection), W20 (construction), W275 (IFRS 16 lease accounting), W40 (capex — tenant improvements)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Landlord leverage in high-demand locations**: Prime commercial lots in Metro Manila and Cebu command premium rents and landlords may resist exclusivity clauses, escalation caps, or early termination provisions
- **PFRS 16 balance sheet impact**: Long-term leases create significant lease liabilities on the consolidated balance sheet; each new store lease adds PHP 20–50M in lease liabilities, affecting debt-to-equity ratios and banking covenants
- **Title verification risk**: Incomplete or fraudulent property titles in provincial areas can lead to legal disputes after lease execution and store construction investment; thorough title verification through the Registry of Deeds is essential; mitigated by CTL-367 (controlled execution — Lease Negotiation & Administration (PA-42.1))


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-assign of "assignment and subleasing rights" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews lease agreement" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-create of "creates lease liability and ROU asset per PFRS 16 in BuildRight Property…" (replaces manual Step 5).

### Controls
- CTL-367 (ensure controlled execution — Lease Negotiation & Administration (PA-42.1).)
- operational: COO / Legal Counsel review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **COO**: Lease negotiation is a core responsibility; ~10–15 negotiations/year × 4–8 weeks each = near-full-time during peak expansion periods
- **Legal Counsel**: Lease review adds ~1–2 weeks per negotiation; absorbed within Legal team (may require external real estate counsel for complex negotiations at ~PHP 100K–300K per lease)
- No incremental headcount beyond existing roles

### Time Estimate
- Property identification: per W1652 (~2–4 weeks)
- Preliminary negotiation: 2–4 weeks
- Legal review: 1–2 weeks
- Financial review and approval: 1 week
- Execution: 1–2 weeks
- Total per lease: ~8–15 weeks elapsed; ~40–80 hours active time

## W1855. Lease Agreement Registration, Document Management & Key Date Tracking

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Lease agreement executed (W1854) |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 new leases/year; ongoing management of ~205 active leases |
| **Volume** | ~205 active lease agreements |
| **Owner** | Lease Administrator (Finance / Real Estate) |
| **Participants** | Lease Administrator, Legal Counsel, COO, Finance |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lease Administrator registers new lease in Oracle Property Manager (fit-gap F3): (a) lease number, landlord, property address, entity (BuildRight Property Management, Inc.), (b) key dates: commencement, expiry, renewal option deadline, rent escalation dates, break clause dates, (c) financial terms: base rent, CAM charges, escalation schedule, security deposit, (d) special conditions: exclusivity, TI allowance, maintenance responsibilities, (e) document attachment: scanned signed lease agreement, notarized copy | Lease Administrator | COO | 1–2 hours/lease |
| 2 | System generates automated alerts for key lease dates: (a) renewal option deadline: 12 months before lease expiry (option exercise deadline typically 6–9 months before expiry), (b) rent escalation: 90 days before escalation effective date, (c) lease expiry: 18 months before expiry for renewal or relocation planning, (d) break clause window: 6 months before break option deadline, (e) insurance renewal: 60 days before property insurance expiry, (f) landlord permit/license expiry: 60 days before expiry | System | Lease Administrator | Automated |
| 3 | Lease Administrator conducts annual lease file audit: (a) verify all key dates are current and accurate, (b) verify all amendments and modifications are recorded, (c) verify rent schedule matches current lease terms (including all escalations applied), (d) verify landlord contact information is current, (e) verify insurance certificates on file and current, (f) flag any leases with upcoming critical dates for action | Lease Administrator | COO | 1 week/year |

### System Touchpoints
- Lease register with full key date calendar and automated alerting (W1855.1–2)
- Document management for lease agreements and amendments (W1855.1e)
- Rent schedule management with escalation tracking (W1855.2b)
- Integration with W275 (PFRS 16 lease accounting), W317 (bank account/signatory management — security deposits)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Missed renewal deadlines**: If the lease administrator misses a renewal option deadline, the lease may auto-expire or the landlord may demand significantly higher rent for renewal; automated alerting with 12-month advance notice is critical
- **Document loss**: Physical lease agreements may be lost in provincial offices; digitized document management with backup is essential for a 200-store chain


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-scan of "scanned signed lease agreement, notarized copy" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-generate of "generates automated alerts for key lease dates" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-record of "Lease Administrator conducts annual lease file audit" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-220 (ensure lease-administration & PFRS 16 integrity.)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication
- **Lease Administrator**: Dedicated role managing ~205 active leases; annual registration, alert management, and audit add ~10–15 hours/month. Can be a dedicated position within Finance or Real Estate team, or absorbed by Finance Manager with ~20% allocation.

### Time Estimate
- New lease registration: 1–2 hours/lease
- Alert monitoring: ~2 hours/week
- Annual audit: 1 week
- Total: ~300–400 hours/year

## W1856. Lease Renewal Negotiation & Extension Processing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | System alert: renewal option deadline approaching (12 months before lease expiry) |
| **Frequency** | ~15–25 lease renewals/year (based on ~205 active leases with 10–15 year terms; some shorter) |
| **Volume** | 1 property per renewal; renewal typically 5–10 years |
| **Owner** | COO |
| **Participants** | COO, Legal Counsel, CFO, Store Operations, Landlord |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | COO evaluates renewal options 12 months before lease expiry: (a) store performance: sales per sqm, profitability, foot traffic trends — is this store worth renewing? (b) market rent benchmarking: has the area appreciated? What are current market rents for comparable properties? (c) landlord relationship: any recurring issues (maintenance, disputes, access problems)? (d) alternative locations: are there better properties available in the same trade area? (e) decision: (i) renew at current location, (ii) renegotiate terms, (iii) relocate to alternative, (iv) close store (if underperforming per W703) | COO | COO | 1–2 weeks |
| 2 | If renewing: COO negotiates renewal terms with landlord: (a) target: maintain or reduce current rent; leverage store improvement to area (increased foot traffic, commercial activity) as negotiation tool, (b) renewal rent escalation: negotiate cap on escalation increase for renewal term, (c) TI refresh: request landlord contribution to store renovation/refresh for renewal commitment, (d) extended term: negotiate longer renewal term (10+ years) for rent stability | COO | CFO | 2–4 weeks |
| 3 | Legal Counsel reviews and executes renewal agreement; Finance updates lease terms in system: (a) update lease end date and financial schedule, (b) recalculate PFRS 16 ROU asset and lease liability for renewed term, (c) update key date alerts in Oracle Property Manager (fit-gap F3) | Legal / Finance | CFO | 1–2 weeks |
| 4 | If relocating: COO initiates W1854 (new site lease negotiation) for alternative location and coordinates W1857 (lease termination) for current location; Store Operations coordinates store transition per W703 (store closure) and W702 (new store opening) | COO | COO | Per W1854 + W1857 |

### System Touchpoints
- Lease renewal alert and tracking (W1856.1 trigger)
- Store performance analytics for renewal decision (W1856.1a)
- PFRS 16 lease liability recalculation for renewed terms (W1856.3b)
- Integration with W67 (store performance), W702 (new store opening), W703 (store closure), W275 (IFRS 16 accounting)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Renewal rent shock**: Landlords in high-growth areas (e.g., Bonifacio Global City, Cebu IT Park) may demand 30–50% rent increases at renewal; without competitive alternatives, BuildRight faces margin erosion
- **Relocation cost**: Moving a store costs PHP 10–30M (new fit-out, inventory relocation, temporary closure, customer communication); renewing at a higher rent may be cheaper than relocating


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-issue of "COO evaluates renewal options 12 months before" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-request of "request landlord contribution to store renovation/refresh for renewal…" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews and executes renewal agreement" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-367 (ensure controlled execution — Lease Negotiation & Administration (PA-42.1).)
- operational: COO review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by COO, Legal, and Finance

### Time Estimate
- Renewal evaluation: 1–2 weeks
- Negotiation: 2–4 weeks
- Legal and financial processing: 1–2 weeks
- Total per renewal: ~6–10 weeks elapsed; ~30–50 hours active time

## W1857. Lease Termination & Move-Out Coordination

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Store closure decision (W703); lease expiry without renewal; early termination per lease clause |
| **Frequency** | ~2–5 lease terminations/year |
| **Volume** | 1 property per termination |
| **Owner** | COO |
| **Participants** | COO, Legal Counsel, Store Operations, Finance, VP Store Operations |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | COO reviews termination provisions in lease agreement: (a) notice period required (typically 6–12 months for early termination; 3–6 months for non-renewal at expiry), (b) early termination penalty (if applicable): typically 3–6 months' rent or unamortized TI allowance, (c) restoration obligations: condition in which property must be returned to landlord (original condition minus fair wear and tear), (d) security deposit return conditions | COO | Legal Counsel | 1–2 days |
| 2 | COO issues formal termination notice to landlord per lease provisions; Legal Counsel reviews notice for compliance with lease terms and Civil Code requirements | COO / Legal | COO | 1 week |
| 3 | Store Operations coordinates move-out: (a) store closure per W703 (store closure, consolidation & asset recovery), (b) inventory redistribution to other stores or DC per W45 (closure redistribution), (c) fixture and equipment removal: reusable fixtures relocated; permanent modifications restored per landlord requirements, (d) property handover walkthrough with landlord representative: document condition, agree on restoration scope, (e) coordinate security deposit return | Store Operations | VP Store Operations | 4–8 weeks |
| 4 | Finance processes termination accounting: (a) derecognize PFRS 16 ROU asset and lease liability, (b) recognize termination gain/loss, (c) process early termination penalty payment if applicable, (d) process security deposit return, (e) update lease register: close lease record | Finance | CFO | Per accounting close |
| 5 | Post-termination: (a) Legal Counsel confirms all obligations fulfilled; landlord provides written release, (b) COO archives terminated lease file, (c) if relocation: coordinate with W702 (new store opening) for replacement location | Legal / COO | COO | 1–2 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- Lease termination workflow with notice tracking (W1857.2)
- PFRS 16 lease derecognition processing (W1857.4a)
- Asset recovery and redistribution integration with W45, W703
- Security deposit tracking and return processing (W1857.3e)
- Integration with W703 (store closure), W45 (inventory redistribution), W275 (PFRS 16 accounting), W39 (asset disposal)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Restoration cost**: Returning a warehouse-format retail space to "original condition" can cost PHP 2–5M (removing installed shelving, electrical, paint, signage); negotiate restoration scope early during lease signing (W1854) to limit obligations
- **Security deposit disputes**: Landlords may claim damages exceeding fair wear and tear to withhold security deposits; photographic condition documentation at move-in and move-out is essential


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "reviews termination provisions in lease agreement" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-issue of "issues formal termination notice to landlord per lease provisions" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "document condition, agree on restoration scope" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-367 (ensure controlled execution — Lease Negotiation & Administration (PA-42.1).)
- operational: COO review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by existing COO, Legal, Store Operations, and Finance roles

### Time Estimate
- Termination review and notice: 1–2 weeks
- Move-out coordination: 4–8 weeks
- Financial processing: per accounting cycle
- Post-termination: 1–2 weeks
- Total: ~8–14 weeks elapsed; ~40–60 hours active time

## W1859. Lease Modification & Amendment Processing

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | COO identifies modification need: (a) space reduction: store underperforming and excess space can be surrendered, (b) space expansion: adjacent space available for store expansion, (c) rent restructuring: temporary rent relief during economic downturn or pandemic, (d) use change: add warehouse space or click-and-collect area, (e) subleasing: surplus space to be subleased to complementary tenant | COO | COO | 1–2 weeks |

| 3 | Legal Counsel drafts lease amendment: (a) specify modification effective date, (b) updated rent schedule and lease term (if changed), (c) updated space measurements (if space changed), (d) any new conditions or waived provisions, (e) both parties execute amendment; notarized per Philippine legal requirements | Legal Counsel | VP Legal | 1–2 weeks |

- Rent schedule update in AP module (W1859.4d)

### Pain Points / Risks

- **PFRS 16 remeasurement complexity**: Lease modifications trigger remeasurement of the ROU asset and lease liability under PFRS 16; complex modifications (partial termination + expansion) require careful accounting treatment

- System auto-document of "document all agreed changes in lease amendment" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-schedule of "schedule and lease term" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by COO, Legal, and Finance

### Time Estimate
- Modification identification: 1–2 weeks
- Negotiation: 2–4 weeks
- Legal processing: 1–2 weeks
- Financial update: per accounting cycle
- Total: ~6–10 weeks elapsed; ~20–40 hours active time

| 5 | Ongoing sublease management: (a) Lease Administrator collects sublease rent monthly, (b) monitors subtenant compliance with sublease terms, (c) if subtenant defaults: Legal Counsel initiates sublease termination and eviction per Philippine law | Lease Administrator | COO | Monthly |

### System Touchpoints
- Sublease register linked to master lease (W1860.4)

- GL/FA ROU sublease schedules (the A12 EXT — W1860.4b)
- Integration with W275 (PFRS 16 accounting), W235 (transfer pricing), W14 (IC transactions)

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Subtenant default**: If subtenant stops paying rent, BuildRight must still pay full rent to landlord while pursuing subtenant through legal channels; sublease security deposit mitigates but does not eliminate this risk

### Controls
- CTL-367 (ensure controlled execution — Lease Negotiation & Administration (PA-42.1).)
