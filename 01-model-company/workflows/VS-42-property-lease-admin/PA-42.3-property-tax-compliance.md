## W1870. Real Property Tax (RPT) Assessment Review & Payment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual RPT assessment notice from LGU; quarterly RPT payment due dates |
| **Frequency** | Annual assessment review; quarterly payments (Jan 31, Apr 30, Jul 31, Oct 31) |
| **Volume** | ~205 properties across ~50+ LGUs (municipalities/cities) |
| **Owner** | Tax Accountant |
| **Participants** | Tax Accountant, Lease Administrator, COO, Finance |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | LGU issues RPT assessment notice: (a) assessed value of land and improvement, (b) tax rate applied (typically 1–2% of assessed value for commercial properties per LGU schedule), (c) annual RPT amount due, (d) payment schedule (quarterly installments; 10% discount for advance annual payment in some LGUs) | LGU | — | External |
| 2 | Tax Accountant reviews assessment: (a) compare assessed value to previous year — flag significant increases (> 10%) for protest evaluation, (b) verify property classification (commercial vs. industrial) — incorrect classification results in higher tax rate, (c) verify property area measurements match lease agreement, (d) if assessment appears excessive: (i) file protest with LGU Assessor's Office within 60 days per Local Government Code, (ii) engage appraiser for independent valuation if contested amount is material (> PHP 100K annual difference), (e) determine if RPT is BuildRight or landlord responsibility per lease terms (most leases: landlord responsible for land RPT; BuildRight responsible for improvement RPT if tenant-built improvements) | Tax Accountant | Finance Manager | 1–2 hours/property |
| 3 | Tax Accountant processes quarterly RPT payments: (a) generate payment schedule for all properties with LGU, tax declaration number, amount, and due date, (b) process payment per LGU acceptance method: over-the-counter at LGU treasury, online payment (if available), or authorized agent bank, (c) obtain official receipt and update tax payment records, (d) file proof of payment with LGU to maintain good standing | Tax Accountant | Finance Manager | 2–4 days/quarter |
| 4 | Annual: Tax Accountant reconciles total RPT payments vs. budget and flags significant variances to COO and CFO | Tax Accountant | CFO | 1 day/year |

### System Touchpoints
- RPT tracking register per property with LGU, tax declaration number, assessment history, payment schedule (W1870.2–3)
- Assessment change alerting (W1870.2a)
- Payment processing and official receipt tracking (W1870.3)
- Integration with W90 (monthly tax filing), W1861 (lease portfolio dashboard)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Assessment inflation**: LGUs periodically conduct general revisions of property assessments, sometimes doubling or tripling assessed values; without protest within 60 days, inflated assessment becomes final
- **LGU payment inefficiency**: Many provincial LGUs do not offer online payment; physical payment at LGU treasury requires coordination across 50+ municipalities; missed payments incur 2% per month surcharge (24% per annum)
- **RPT responsibility ambiguity**: When BuildRight constructs improvements on leased land, RPT on the improvement may fall to BuildRight even though the land is landlord-owned; clear delineation in lease agreement is essential


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "discount for advance annual payment in some LGUs" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "Tax Accountant reviews assessment" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-record of "Tax Accountant processes quarterly RPT payments" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-369 (ensure controlled execution — Property Tax, LGU Compliance & Lease Accounting (PA-42.3).)
- operational: Tax Accountant review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Staffing Implication
- **Tax Accountant**: RPT management adds ~8–12 days/quarter (review + payment) = ~32–48 days/year — significant portion of Tax Accountant's role; may require part-time support during payment periods
- No incremental headcount beyond existing Tax Accountant

### Time Estimate
- Assessment review: ~1–2 hours × 205 = ~200–400 hours (spread over 2–3 months after assessment issuance)
- Quarterly payment processing: 2–4 days/quarter × 4 = 8–16 days
- Annual reconciliation: 1 day
- Total: ~300–500 hours/year

## W1871. Local Business Tax (LBT) & LGU Permit Renewal Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual business permit renewal deadline (typically January 20 per LGU) |
| **Frequency** | Annual renewal per LGU; quarterly LBT payments |
| **Volume** | ~205 locations across ~50+ LGUs |
| **Owner** | Regulatory Officer / Tax Accountant |
| **Participants** | Regulatory Officer, Tax Accountant, Legal Counsel, Store Managers |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | October: Regulatory Officer initiates annual renewal preparation: (a) compile list of all business permits expiring December 31, (b) verify required documents per LGU: (i) DTI/SEC registration, (ii) BIR registration certificate, (iii) SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG employer registration, (iv) barangay clearance, (v) fire safety inspection certificate (BFP), (vi) sanitation permit (city health office), (vii) building occupancy permit, (viii) previous year's LBT payment receipts, (ix) lease contract or proof of business address, (c) coordinate with Store Managers to obtain barangay clearances and local inspections | Regulatory Officer | VP Legal | 2–4 weeks |
| 2 | January: Regulatory Officer submits renewal applications to LGUs: (a) file business permit renewal per LGU schedule (most require submission by January 5–20), (b) pay LBT for the year (based on gross receipts from previous year per LGU tax ordinance), (c) obtain renewed business permit and mayor's permit, (d) distribute renewed permits to stores for display per Consumer Act RA 7394 requirement | Regulatory Officer | VP Legal | 2–3 weeks |
| 3 | Ongoing: Regulatory Officer monitors LGU compliance throughout the year: (a) new store opening: obtain business permit before store opening per W702, (b) store closure: notify LGU and cancel business permit per W703, (c) LGU inspection responses: coordinate with Store Manager for any LGU inspections (sanitary, fire safety, building, trade and industry) per W22.2, (d) track new LGU ordinances affecting business operations | Regulatory Officer | VP Legal | Ongoing |

### System Touchpoints
- Business permit tracking register per location with LGU, permit number, expiry date, renewal status (W1871.1a)
- Document checklist management per LGU requirements (W1871.1b)
- LBT payment tracking and budget allocation (W1871.2b)
- New store and closure permit workflow integration (W1871.3a–b)
- Integration with W702 (new store opening), W703 (store closure), W22 (compliance & regulatory), W90 (tax filing)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **LGU deadline variation**: Different LGUs have different renewal deadlines and documentation requirements; without centralized tracking, stores in provincial LGUs may miss deadlines and incur penalties or face temporary closure orders
- **Barangay clearance delays**: Some barangay captains delay clearance issuance for political or operational reasons; Store Manager relationship with barangay officials is critical
- **LBT calculation complexity**: LBT rates and tax bases vary by LGU (percentage of gross receipts, flat rate, or tiered schedule); incorrect calculation results in underpayment (penalties) or overpayment (cash flow loss)


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-document of "documents per LGU" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-file of "file business permit renewal per LGU schedule" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-inspect of "inspection responses" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-369 (ensure controlled execution — Property Tax, LGU Compliance & Lease Accounting (PA-42.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication
- **Regulatory Officer**: LGU permit management across 205 locations × 50+ LGUs is a dedicated function; ~2–4 weeks/year for renewal season + ongoing monitoring = near-full-time during Q1, part-time remainder of year
- **Store Managers**: Assist with local barangay clearance and inspection coordination (~2–4 hours/store during renewal season) — absorbed by existing role

### Time Estimate
- Renewal preparation: 2–4 weeks
- Filing and payment: 2–3 weeks
- Ongoing monitoring: ~4–8 hours/month
- Total: ~200–300 hours/year

## W1872. PFRS 16 Lease Accounting — Month-End Processing & Disclosure

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Month-end close cycle (W9A) |
| **Frequency** | Monthly |
| **Volume** | ~205 active leases across 5 legal entities |
| **Owner** | Finance (Lease Accounting Specialist) |
| **Participants** | Lease Accounting Specialist, Finance Manager, Controller, External Auditor |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Month-end: Lease Accounting Specialist processes PFRS 16 journal entries: (a) lease depreciation: Dr. Depreciation Expense / Cr. Accumulated Depreciation — ROU Assets (straight-line over lease term), (b) lease interest expense: Dr. Interest Expense / Cr. Lease Liability (effective interest method on lease liability balance), (c) variable lease payments (CAM, percentage rent): expensed as incurred — Dr. Rent Expense / Cr. Cash, (d) short-term and low-value leases (≤ 12 months with no purchase option, or low-value < USD 5,000 ≈ PHP 280K, per the VS-148 policy): exempted from PFRS 16 recognition; expensed straight-line — Dr. Rent Expense / Cr. Cash | Lease Accounting Specialist | Controller | 1–2 days/month |
| 2 | Month-end: process lease modifications, terminations, and new leases: (a) new leases (W1854): recognize ROU asset and lease liability at present value of future lease payments, (b) lease modifications (W1859): remeasure liability using revised discount rate; adjust ROU asset, (c) lease terminations (W1857): derecognize ROU asset and liability; recognize termination gain/loss, (d) rent escalations (W1863): remeasure liability using revised cash flows | Lease Accounting Specialist | Controller | 1–2 days/month |
| 3 | Quarter-end: prepare PFRS 16 disclosure schedules: (a) reconciliation of opening and closing lease liabilities, (b) ROU asset movements: additions, depreciation, disposals, impairments, (c) maturity analysis of undiscounted lease payments: ≤ 1 year, 1–5 years, > 5 years, (d) total lease expense: depreciation, interest, variable payments, short-term lease expense, (e) weighted-average lease term and discount rate, (f) extension and termination options exercise data, (g) provide to Controller for financial statement note preparation per W9A | Lease Accounting Specialist | Controller | 1 day/quarter |
| 4 | Year-end: coordinate with External Auditor on PFRS 16 testing: (a) provide lease population and sampling support, (b) support auditor testing of discount rate assumptions, lease term assessments, and modification accounting, (c) resolve auditor queries on complex leases (e.g., options to extend/terminate, variable payment structures) | Lease Accounting Specialist / Controller | CFO | 1–2 weeks/year |

### System Touchpoints
- GL/FA ROU schedules (the A12 EXT) with automated depreciation and interest calculations (W1872.1)
- Lease modification, termination, and new lease processing integration (W1872.2)
- Disclosure schedule generation per PFRS 16 requirements (W1872.3)
- Discount rate management: incremental borrowing rate per entity per lease term (W1872.4b)
- Integration with W9A (month-end close), W9B (year-end close), W275 (PFRS 16 — broader lease accounting reference)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Discount rate estimation**: PFRS 16 requires use of the rate implicit in the lease or the lessee's incremental borrowing rate; for Philippine entities, estimating IBR is complex and subjective — External Auditor may challenge the rate used, affecting lease liability measurement
- **Lease term assessment**: Options to extend or terminate must be assessed for reasonable certainty of exercise; incorrect assessment changes the lease liability and ROU asset amounts
- **System capacity**: Processing 205 leases monthly with modifications, escalations, and new/terminated leases requires robust lease accounting software; spreadsheet-based PFRS 16 accounting is error-prone at this volume


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "Accounting Specialist processes PFRS 16 journal entries" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-measure of "remeasure liability using revised discount rate" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-generate of "prepare PFRS 16 disclosure schedules" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-220 (ensure lease-administration & PFRS 16 integrity.)
- operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Staffing Implication
- **Lease Accounting Specialist**: Dedicated role within Finance team; PFRS 16 month-end processing adds ~5–8 days/month; quarterly disclosures add ~1 day/quarter; year-end audit adds 1–2 weeks — total ~80–120 days/year. This is a near-full-time role given the 205-lease portfolio across 5 entities.

### Time Estimate
- Monthly journal processing: 1–2 days/month
- Modification/termination processing: 1–2 days/month
- Quarterly disclosures: 1 day/quarter
- Year-end audit support: 1–2 weeks
- Total: ~35–65 days/year (per the component items; peak-season staffing ~5–8 days/month)

## W1874. Property Insurance Portfolio Management & Annual Renewal

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual insurance renewal cycle; new property addition; claim event |
| **Frequency** | Annual renewal; claims per event |
| **Volume** | ~205 properties insured; annual premium ~PHP 50–100M |
| **Owner** | Finance (Insurance Administrator) |
| **Participants** | Finance, COO, Insurance Broker, Insurance Companies, Legal Counsel |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Annual renewal preparation (90 days before policy expiry): (a) Finance compiles property schedule: all 205 locations with building specifications, replacement values, contents values, location details (flood zone, earthquake zone, typhoon exposure), (b) Insurance Broker obtains quotes from 3–5 insurance companies, (c) Finance evaluates: coverage adequacy, premium competitiveness, deductible levels, policy terms and exclusions, insurer financial strength rating, (d) negotiate policy terms: (i) blanket coverage vs. scheduled per-location coverage, (ii) business interruption coverage (rent loss during building repair), (iii) natural catastrophe coverage (typhoon, earthquake, flood — critical in Philippines), (iv) deductible optimization: higher deductible = lower premium but higher retained risk | Finance / Insurance Broker | CFO | 4–6 weeks |
| 2 | CFO approves insurance program: (a) total annual premium vs. budget, (b) coverage adequacy: are all properties adequately insured to replacement value? (c) deductible levels appropriate for BuildRight's risk appetite?, (d) execute policies; Finance pays annual premium | CFO | CEO | 1 week |
| 3 | Ongoing property schedule management: (a) new stores: add to insurance schedule within 30 days of lease commencement or property acquisition, (b) store closures: remove from schedule upon lease termination, (c) renovations: update replacement value after significant improvements, (d) Finance provides updated property schedule to insurer quarterly | Finance | CFO | Monthly |
| 4 | Claims management: (a) if property damage event (typhoon, fire, flood): (i) Finance notifies insurer within 24 hours, (ii) COO coordinates damage assessment and emergency repairs per W26 (crisis response), (iii) Finance compiles claim documentation: damage photos, repair estimates, inventory loss, business interruption calculation, (iv) negotiate claim settlement with insurer adjuster, (v) Finance processes claim payment and repair reimbursement, (b) track claim history by location for underwriting analysis | Finance / COO | CFO | Per event |

### System Touchpoints
- Property insurance schedule management with replacement value tracking (W1874.3)
- Insurance policy registry with key dates, coverage details, premium history (W1874.1)
- Claims management module with documentation tracking (W1874.4)
- Integration with W59 (insurance policy lifecycle), W26 (business continuity & crisis), W47 (facility maintenance), W1861 (lease portfolio)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Underinsurance**: If property replacement values are not updated annually for construction cost inflation, properties may be underinsured; coinsurance clauses in property policies reduce claim payouts proportionally for underinsurance
- **Catastrophe coverage gaps**: Standard property insurance may exclude flood or earthquake; separate catastrophe policies are required for Philippine typhoon belt and earthquake zone exposure
- **Claims documentation**: Inadequate documentation (no pre-loss photographs, incomplete inventory records) delays claim settlement and reduces recovery amount


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-compile of "compiles property schedule" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System rule-based auto-approval of "approves insurance program" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-schedule of "Ongoing property schedule management" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-369 (ensure controlled execution — Property Tax, LGU Compliance & Lease Accounting (PA-42.3).)
- operational: annual insurance-coverage adequacy review vs reconstruction cost; policy schedule reconciled to the ~205-lease portfolio quarterly

### Staffing Implication
- **Finance (Insurance Administrator)**: Insurance portfolio management adds ~20–30 days/year for renewal + ongoing schedule management — absorbed by Finance team
- **Insurance Broker**: External partner for market placement and claims advocacy; annual commission ~10–15% of premium

### Time Estimate
- Annual renewal: 4–6 weeks elapsed; ~40–60 hours active time
- Property schedule updates: ~4–8 hours/month
- Claims management: ~20–40 hours per event
- Total: ~150–250 hours/year

| **Volume** | 1 comprehensive review per year; ad-hoc assessments for regulatory changes |
| **Owner** | Controller |
| **Participants** | Controller, CFO, External Auditor, Lease Accounting Specialist |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Annual: Controller reviews lease accounting policy: (a) discount rate methodology: is the incremental borrowing rate (IBR) calculation methodology still appropriate? (stores with significant sales decline where lease liability exceeds recoverable amount from store operations), (d) low-value and short-term lease thresholds: are thresholds still appropriate given inflation?, (e) sublease accounting policy: ensure classification (operating vs. finance) is correct for any active subleases per W1860 | Controller | CFO | 1–2 days |
| 2 | Ad-hoc: Controller assesses impact of IFRS amendments on lease accounting: (a) monitor IFRS amendments and interpretations from IASB, (b) assess applicability to BuildRight's lease portfolio, (c) quantify financial statement impact of any required changes, (d) implement changes with External Auditor concurrence, (e) update accounting policies and train Lease Accounting Specialist | Controller | CFO | 1–2 days per assessment |

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by Controller and CFO

### Time Estimate
- Annual policy review: 1–2 days
- Ad-hoc assessments: ~1–2 days per assessment (typically 0–2 per year)

### System Touchpoints
- Utility account registry per location with provider, account number, meter number (W1877.1)
- Consumption trend analytics with anomaly detection (W1877.2a)
- AP integration for utility payment processing (W1877.3)
- Integration with W111 (energy & utility management), W7 (AP processing), W1868 (IC billing), W702 (new store)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Estimated billing inaccuracy**: Utility companies in provincial areas may estimate readings for months; large discrepancies between estimated and actual consumption create billing shocks when actual reading is finally taken
- **Disconnection risk**: Utility disconnection for non-payment shuts down store operations (no lights, no POS, no air conditioning); utility bills must be prioritized in payment processing; mitigated by standard operating procedures and system-enforced validation rules
- **Utility deposit requirements**: Electric cooperatives may require deposits of PHP 50K–200K per store; total utility deposits across ~205 locations = PHP 10M–40M in locked capital

## W5529. Utility, Telecommunications & Site Deposits Paid — Register, Interest Reconciliation, Surety Replacement & Recovery-on-Closure

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | A new utility or telecommunications service connection requiring a deposit (store opening per W16/W702, DC/HQ changes) or a provider deposit-billing notice; the annual deposit-interest credit cycle or a provider tariff change; a store/DC closure or service disconnection (W703/W807/W1857); or the annual deposit-portfolio review |
| **Frequency** | Per-connection/per-provider event; annual interest-credit reconciliation; annual portfolio review; per-closure recovery |
| **Volume** | ≈ 450–700 active deposits (electricity, water, and telecom/internet accounts across 200 stores, 4 DCs and HQ, plus entity-level deposits); electric-cooperative deposits of PHP 50K–200K per store and Meralco-class commercial deposits of ~1–3 months' average bill put total locked capital at **PHP 10M–40M** (the W1877 pain-point canon); ~15–25 deposit opening/closure events/year; ~205 annual interest-credit verifications |
| **Owner** | Treasury Manager |
| **Participants** | Lease Administrator, Facilities Coordinator, Energy Manager, AP Clerk, Controller, Legal Counsel (guarantee documentation), CFO (surety-replacement decisions), External bank/surety (guarantee issuance via W232/W325), Utility/telecom providers |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Deposit register & policy custody**: maintain the deposits-paid register per provider account — provider, site, service type, amount, terms, interest entitlement, renewal/expiry dates, and GL account mapping; deposit approvals above PHP 1M require CFO sign-off; reconcile the register to the GL deposits-receivable accounts quarterly within the W636 balance-sheet reconciliation cycle | Treasury Manager | Controller | 20 min/month register upkeep + quarterly reconciliation |
| 2 | **Onboarding & adequacy**: process connection deposits for new sites and deposit top-ups where consumption growth or tariff escalation drops a deposit below the provider requirement; cross-check adequacy against the consumption data captured per W1877/W1687/W1563 before approving top-up or renegotiation | Treasury Manager / Facilities Coordinator | Controller | ~2 hours per opening/top-up event |
| 3 | **Interest-credit reconciliation**: verify each distribution-utility and telecom provider's annual deposit-interest credit against the register entitlement (EPIRA IRR customer-deposit interest rules for DUs); chase missing or short credits with the provider; post verified credits to GL | Treasury Manager / AP Clerk | Controller | 15 min per deposit per year |
| 4 | **Surety-replacement review**: annually evaluate replacing cash deposits with bank/surety guarantees where the provider accepts them (issuance routed through the W232 bank-guarantee / W325 corporate-guarantee machinery); CFO decides cash release vs guarantee fee per account | Treasury Manager | CFO | ~1 hour per major account per year |
| 5 | **Recovery-on-closure**: on disconnection or site closure, obtain final meter billing, offset deposits against final bills, and pursue refunds within the provider SLA; escalate overdue refunds to the provider relationship owner; attach the deposit-recovery checklist to the W703/W807 store-closure and W1857 lease-termination workflows | Treasury Manager / AP Clerk | Treasury Manager | ~4 hours per closure event |
| 6 | **Portfolio reporting**: annual deposits-portfolio report to the CFO — locked capital, interest credited vs entitled, guarantee fees avoided/incurred, recovery aging and open disputes; feeds the W326 treasury month-end close and the annual W636 reconciliation evidence pack | Treasury Manager | CFO | 8 hours/year |

### System Touchpoints
- Deposits-paid register (provider, site, amount, terms, interest entitlement) mapped to GL deposits-receivable accounts per entity
- W336 balance-sheet reconciliation workspace (W636 quarterly tie-out evidence)
- Utility consumption and account data per W1877/W1687/W1563 (adequacy inputs); closure checklists per W703/W807/W1857
- W232/W325 bank & corporate guarantee machinery (surety-replacement issuance); W7 payment processing for deposit billing

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Locked-capital drag risk**: deposits that outlive their adequacy need (consumption decline, provider deregulation, store downsizing) tie up PHP 10M–40M of cash earning deposit-rate interest well below the W318 investment yield; mitigated by the annual surety-replacement review (Step 4) and portfolio report (Step 6)
- **Interest-leakage risk**: distribution-utility and telecom interest credits are often issued as bill deductions and silently missed or mis-posted; mitigated by the annual per-deposit reconciliation (Step 3) against the register entitlement
- **Recovery-forfeiture risk**: closure refunds lapsed past provider claim windows are forfeited outright; mitigated by the closure checklist gate (Step 5) wired into W703/W807/W1857 and recovery-aging escalation in the Step-6 report

### Automation Opportunity
- Provider billing feeds auto-match interest credits to the register entitlement and raise gap tickets for missing credits (replaces manual Step 3 matching).
- Closure workflows auto-generate the deposit-recovery checklist with final-billing offsets and refund SLA timers (replaces manual Step 5 initiation).

### Controls
- CTL-369 (ensure controlled execution — Property Tax, LGU Compliance & Lease Accounting (PA-42.3).)
- operational: deposits above PHP 1M require CFO approval before payment
- operational: quarterly register-to-GL reconciliation with Controller sign-off
- operational: no closure checklist completes with an unresolved deposit-recovery item

### Time Estimate
Register upkeep 20 min × 12 = 240 min = 4 hours/year; onboarding/top-ups 2 hours × ~20 events = 40 hours/year; interest reconciliation 15 min × 205 deposits = 3,075 min ≈ 51 hours/year; surety reviews 1 hour × ~20 accounts = 20 hours/year; closure recovery 4 hours × ~15 closures = 60 hours/year; portfolio report ≈ 8 hours/year. Total **≈ 183 hours/year** (≈0.09 FTE), absorbed within the existing treasury team.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed within the Treasury & Banking sub-team (the register rides the W317 account-administration desk); Facilities and AP contributions are per-event and absorbed; no incremental headcount.
