## W1714. Quarterly Board Meeting Scheduling & Agenda Preparation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly board calendar (Q1–Q4 meetings) + ad-hoc meetings for urgent matters |
| **Frequency** | 4 regular meetings/year + 2–4 ad-hoc meetings/year |
| **Volume** | 6–10 board meetings/year |
| **Owner** | Corporate Secretary |
| **Participants** | Corporate Secretary, CEO, CFO, Board Chair |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Corporate Secretary maintains annual board meeting calendar: (a) Q1 meeting (February): previous year financial results, external audit report, dividend declaration, (b) Q2 meeting (May): Q1 results, annual strategy review, (c) Q3 meeting (August): H1 results, mid-year budget review, capex progress, (d) Q4 meeting (November): next year budget approval, strategic priorities, (e) ad-hoc: material acquisitions, officer changes, emergency matters | Corporate Secretary | Board Chair | Annual calendar |
| 2 | 30 days before meeting: Corporate Secretary solicits agenda items from Board Chair, CEO, CFO, and committee chairs; compiles draft agenda | Corporate Secretary | Board Chair | 3 days |
| 3 | CEO and CFO review draft agenda; add operational updates, financial reports, and action items from previous meeting; propose presenters for each agenda item | CEO / CFO | Board Chair | 2 days |
| 4 | Board Chair approves final agenda; Corporate Secretary distributes to all board members with meeting notice (minimum 5 business days before meeting per corporate by-laws) | Board Chair / Corporate Secretary | — | 1 day |
| 5 | For ad-hoc meetings: Corporate Secretary convenes upon request of Board Chair, CEO, or 2+ directors; notice period may be shortened for urgent matters; minutes must document urgency justification | Corporate Secretary | Board Chair | 1–2 days |

### System Touchpoints
- Board meeting calendar with automated reminders and notice generation (W1714.1)
- Agenda management module with item submission and approval workflow (W1714.2–4)
- Meeting notice distribution with read receipt tracking (W1714.4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Director availability**: Family corporation directors may have conflicting schedules; advance calendar coordination critical
- **Information overload**: Ensuring board receives complete information without being overwhelmed requires disciplined agenda management
- **Ad-hoc meeting urgency**: Material transactions (e.g., new store site commitments > PHP 50M) may require rapid board approval; corporate secretary must balance urgency with proper notice requirements

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "Corporate Secretary maintains annual board meeting calendar" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-compile of "compiles draft agenda" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-capture of "presenters for each agenda item" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-349 (ensure controlled execution — Board Meeting Management & Corporate Records (PA-36.1).)
- operational: Corporate Secretary review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Per board meeting ~4–6 days of Corporate Secretary effort window (agenda solicitation 3 days + CEO/CFO review 2 days + Chair approval and distribution 1 day) at 6–10 meetings/year ≈ 24–60 days/year of part-time effort; ad-hoc meetings 1–2 days each.

## W1716. Board Meeting Minutes Recording & Approval

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Board meeting convened |
| **Frequency** | 6–10 times/year |
| **Volume** | 1 set of minutes per meeting |
| **Owner** | Corporate Secretary |
| **Participants** | Corporate Secretary, Board Chair, all Directors |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Corporate Secretary records minutes during meeting: (a) attendance (directors present, absent, invited guests, management presenters), (b) quorum confirmed per by-laws, (c) key discussion points (summarized, not verbatim), (d) motions made and seconded, (e) vote results (unanimous, majority, dissenting opinions noted if director requests), (f) resolutions passed with exact wording, (g) action items assigned with owners and due dates, (h) time of adjournment | Corporate Secretary | — | During meeting |
| 2 | Within 5 business days of meeting: Corporate Secretary prepares draft minutes; circulates to Board Chair and CEO for review and accuracy confirmation | Corporate Secretary | Board Chair | 3–5 days |
| 3 | Board Chair approves draft minutes for circulation to all directors; directors review and may suggest corrections (factual only, not substantive changes to resolutions) | Board Chair | — | 5 days |
| 4 | Minutes presented for formal approval at next board meeting; approved minutes signed by Board Chair and Corporate Secretary; become official corporate record | Board Chair / Corporate Secretary | — | At next meeting |
| 5 | Signed minutes filed in corporate records per W1719; resolutions extracted and distributed for execution per W1717 | Corporate Secretary | — | 1 day |

### System Touchpoints
- Minutes template with required fields (attendance, quorum, motions, votes, actions) (W1716.1)
- Draft minutes review and approval workflow (W1716.2–3)
- Electronic signature for approved minutes (W1716.4)
- Resolution extraction and distribution (W1716.5)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Legal significance**: Board minutes are legal documents; inaccurate or incomplete minutes create legal risk (especially for director liability in Philippine corporate law)
- **Dissenting votes**: Directors who disagree with a resolution must have their dissent recorded to avoid liability for the decision; corporate secretary must proactively ask
- **Delay in approval**: If minutes are not approved until the next meeting (3 months later), corrections become difficult; consider email approval between meetings

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-capture of "Corporate Secretary records minutes during meeting" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "review and accuracy confirmation" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System rule-based auto-approval of "approves draft minutes for circulation to all directors" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-231 (ensure corporate-governance compliance.)
- operational: Corporate Secretary review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Per meeting-minutes cycle: in-meeting recording + draft preparation and circulation within 5 business days + Chair approval + next-meeting formal approval + filing ≈ 1–2 days of effort × 6–10 meetings/year ≈ 6–20 days/year (approval cycles largely elapsed).

## W1717. Corporate Resolution Generation & Authentication

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Board or shareholder decision requiring formal resolution; bank/counterparty requiring corporate authorization |
| **Frequency** | ~30–50 resolutions/year |
| **Volume** | Board resolutions: ~20–30/year; shareholder resolutions: ~5–10/year; management resolutions: ~5–10/year |
| **Owner** | Corporate Secretary |
| **Participants** | Corporate Secretary, Board Chair, CEO, requesting party |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trigger for resolution: (a) board decision at meeting (extracted from minutes per W1716), (b) written consent action (all directors sign without meeting — permitted per Revised Corporation Code for certain matters), (c) bank/counterparty request for authority certification (e.g., signing authority for bank accounts, authority to enter contracts > threshold), (d) regulatory filing requirement (SEC, BIR, LGU) | Corporate Secretary / Requesting Party | CEO | 1 day |
| 2 | Corporate Secretary drafts resolution: (a) standard format: company name, date, "WHEREAS" clauses (background/reasons), "RESOLVED" clauses (decision/authorization), (b) resolution types: (i) authority to sign documents, (ii) approval of contracts/transactions, (iii) officer appointments, (iv) capital expenditure authorization, (v) corporate structure changes, (vi) dividend declaration, (vii) bank account management, (viii) annual report approval | Corporate Secretary | — | 30 min–2 hours |
| 3 | Resolution authenticated: (a) board resolution: signed by Board Chair and Corporate Secretary, (b) shareholder resolution: signed by stockholders holding required voting power, (c) management resolution: signed by CEO (for delegated authority matters) | Board Chair / Corporate Secretary / CEO | — | 1–3 days |
| 4 | Certified copies issued to requesting parties: (a) bank: certified resolution for account opening/signing authority, (b) vendor: resolution authorizing contract execution, (c) LGU: resolution for business permit application, (d) SEC: resolution for annual filing | Corporate Secretary | — | 1 day |
| 5 | Original resolution filed in corporate records per W1719; resolution register updated with number, date, subject, and parties | Corporate Secretary | — | 15 min |


### System Touchpoints
- Resolution drafting workspace — standard WHEREAS/RESOLVED format from board decisions or written consent (steps 1–2)
- Authentication workflow — Board Chair + Corporate Secretary signatures; stockholder thresholds for shareholder resolutions (step 3)
- Certified-copy issuance register — banks (account/signing authority), vendors, regulators (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Unauthorized-resolution risk**: resolutions issued without proper authority bind the company improperly; mitigated by dual-signature authentication traceable to minutes or written consent (steps 1, 3)
- **Stale-resolution risk**: banks and counterparties act on superseded resolutions; mitigated by certified-copy control with reissue on any authority change (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-capture of "enter contracts > threshold" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-document of "Corporate Secretary drafts resolution" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-count of "account opening/signing authority" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-231 (ensure corporate-governance compliance.)
- operational: Corporate Secretary review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
45 min–2.25 hours per occurrence (per-step effort 30 min–2 hours + 15 min); elapsed timeline 1 day + 1–3 days + 1 day — at ~30–50 resolutions/year ≈ 30–50 occurrences/year ≈ 22.5–112 hours/year.

## W1719. Corporate Record Retention & Document Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Creation of any corporate document (resolutions, minutes, filings, contracts, legal documents) |
| **Frequency** | Continuous |
| **Volume** | ~500–1,000 corporate documents/year |
| **Owner** | Corporate Secretary |
| **Participants** | Corporate Secretary, Legal Counsel, all departments |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Corporate Secretary maintains corporate record retention schedule per Philippine law and best practice: (a) articles of incorporation and by-laws — permanent, (b) board and shareholder minutes — permanent, (c) corporate resolutions — permanent, (d) SEC filings — permanent, (e) contracts (material) — 10 years after expiry, (f) legal correspondence — 7 years, (g) corporate governance policies — permanent (with version history), (h) stock and transfer ledger — permanent, (i) officer and director records — permanent | Corporate Secretary | Legal Counsel | Pre-configured |
| 2 | All corporate documents filed in corporate record management system: (a) document number, date, type, subject, entity, (b) electronic copy (scanned original + working copy), (c) physical original stored in fireproof corporate safe at HQ (for signed documents), (d) indexed for search and retrieval, (e) access restricted by document sensitivity | Corporate Secretary | — | 15 min/document |
| 3 | Quarterly: Corporate Secretary audits record completeness: (a) all board meetings have minutes on file, (b) all resolutions are numbered and indexed, (c) SEC filings are current, (d) stock ledger is up to date, (e) missing documents identified and remediated | Corporate Secretary | Legal Counsel | 1 day/quarter |
| 4 | Document destruction: only per retention schedule; requires Corporate Secretary and Legal Counsel approval; destruction log maintained permanently | Corporate Secretary | Legal Counsel | As needed |


### System Touchpoints
- Retention schedule per Philippine law — articles/by-laws permanent; statutory filings and minutes per mandated periods (step 1)
- Corporate record management system — document numbering, type, entity, electronic copies (step 2)
- Quarterly completeness audit — minutes on file, resolutions numbered and indexed (step 3)
- Controlled destruction — dual approval (Corporate Secretary + Legal) with a permanent destruction log (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Missing-record risk**: gaps in minutes or resolutions surface during SEC or audit scrutiny; mitigated by the quarterly completeness audit (step 3)
- **Premature-destruction risk**: records destroyed before the legal window closes or during a litigation hold; mitigated by dual approval and the permanent destruction log (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "record retention schedule per Philippine law and best practice" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-scan of "scanned original + working copy" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "documents identified and remediated" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-349 (ensure controlled execution — Board Meeting Management & Corporate Records (PA-36.1).)
- operational: Corporate Secretary review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Document filing 15 min/document × ~500–1,000 corporate documents/year ≈ 125–250 hours/year; quarterly completeness audit 1 day ≈ 8 hours/year (retention schedule pre-configured; destruction dual-authorized as needed).

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | Corporate Secretary reviews related-party transaction register: (a) identifies transactions requiring board approval per Revised Corporation Code (certain related-party transactions require 2/3 board approval and disclosure to SEC), (b) verifies arm's-length pricing documentation for intercompany transactions (transfer pricing study by external advisor), (c) ensures related-party transactions are properly recorded in board minutes | Corporate Secretary | Legal Counsel | 1 day/quarter |
| 3 | CFO ensures proper accounting: (a) intercompany transactions recorded in each entity's books, (b) elimination entries during consolidation per PA-17.2, (c) related-party disclosures in financial statement notes per PFRS 124 (nature of relationship, amounts, outstanding balances, terms and conditions) | CFO | External Auditor | Part of financial close |
| 4 | External auditor reviews related-party disclosures during annual audit; confirms completeness and PFRS 124 compliance | External Auditor | — | During annual audit |

### System Touchpoints
- Related-party transaction identification across the 5 BuildRight entities — warehousing, ecommerce, lease, service fees (step 1)

- **Family corporation complexity**: Family-owned corporations have inherent related-party risks (family members in multiple business entities); comprehensive identification is challenging

- **Disclosure completeness**: Failure to disclose all related-party transactions is a common external audit finding and SEC violation risk

- System auto-record of "recorded in board minutes" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-count of "CFO ensures proper accounting" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-review of "reviews related-party disclosures during annual audit" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-231 (ensure corporate-governance compliance.)
- operational: Corporate Secretary review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Related-party identification continuous (system-flagged); quarterly register review 1 day/quarter ≈ 8 hours/year across ~50–100 related-party transactions/quarter; accounting rides the financial close; external-audit review annually.

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |

| 2 | Committee chair sets agenda; Corporate Secretary prepares materials specific to committee mandate: (a) Audit Committee: internal audit reports (VS-21), external audit findings, management letter responses, financial statement review, risk dashboard, (b) Executive Committee: urgent matters requiring board action between meetings, (c) Compensation & Governance: compensation benchmarking, policy updates, governance assessment results | Committee Chair / Corporate Secretary | Board Chair | 3–5 days before meeting |

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Rubber-stamp risk**: committees approve management proposals without genuine scrutiny; mitigated by mandate-specific materials prepared independent of the proposing executive (step 2)

### Automation Opportunity

- System auto-generate of "prepares materials specific to committee mandate" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-record of "Committee meetings conducted" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-349 (ensure controlled execution — Board Meeting Management & Corporate Records (PA-36.1).)
- operational: Corporate Secretary review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Committee meetings 1–3 hours each × 9–12 meetings/year ≈ 9–36 hours of meeting time plus ~1 day of agenda/material preparation per meeting ≈ 72–96 hours/year across the 3 standing committees; committee recommendations take 15–30 min of each board meeting.

## W5499. Director Induction, Onboarding & Continuing Board Education Program

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | (a) Election/appointment of a new director or officer-director (per W1727); (b) annual board calendar; (c) material regulatory/governance change |
| **Frequency** | New-director onboarding: 1–3 events/year (board refresh cycle); continuing education: annual board retreat + quarterly 30–45-min regulatory briefings; per-committee deep-dives 1–2/year |
| **Volume** | All directors and committee members of the 5-entity group (board and committee rosters per VS-36.1 governance records; ~10–15 participants per session); ~6–10 board meetings/year whose agendas this program feeds (W1714) |
| **Owner** | Corporate Secretary |
| **Participants** | Board Chair, CEO, CFO, CHRO, Head of Internal Audit (VS-21), external counsel/advisers, external director-education providers, site hosts (store/DC leadership) |

### Background

The SEC Revised Code of Corporate Governance expects directors to be properly inducted and to keep abreast of their fiduciary duties, the group's business, and the regulatory environment — and W1730's annual governance self-assessment asks whether the board has the knowledge to govern. Yet the catalog processed director *appointments* (W1727), *meetings* (W1714–W1716), and *self-assessment* (W1730) with no workflow owning the education that sits between them ('director induction', 'board onboarding', and 'board education' appear in zero PA files). Distinct from: VS-19.4/W719 (employee L&D and DEI training), W1484 (store-manager onboarding), VS-33 (strategy content the board receives), VS-104 (the external-affairs intelligence that feeds the regulatory briefings).

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | New-director onboarding pack & program: group/entity structure (5 legal entities incl. the property company VS-97 and captive VS-153), governance charter and committee charters, code of ethics & conflict-of-interest policy (W1734), delegation-of-authority matrix (W1732), D&O insurance briefing (VS-26), recent board minutes and standing resolutions, and the current strategy pack (VS-33); scheduled within 30 days of election/appointment per W1727 | Corporate Secretary | Board Chair | 3–5 days preparation; 1–2 days delivery |
| 2 | Executive & functional immersion: structured meetings with CEO, CFO, CHRO, Head of Internal Audit, and VP Legal; one-day site immersion — one operating store, one DC (VS-04), and the store-support functions (VS-177 field leadership as host) — to ground oversight in frontline reality | Corporate Secretary / Executives | CEO | 1–2 days/new director |
| 3 | Fiduciary & regulatory education: duties of care/diligence/loyalty, confidentiality and insider-information handling, related-party transaction duties (W1720), SEC reportorial obligations (W1718), and exposure briefings on competition law (VS-129), anti-graft constraints around B2G sales (VS-46/VS-86), data privacy (W1735), and election-period conduct (VS-132) | Corporate Secretary / External Counsel | Board Chair | 0.5–1 day/new director + refreshers |
| 4 | Risk & controls immersion: ERM top risks and appetite (W122), internal-audit plan and open findings (VS-21), fraud/ethics/speak-up program (VS-119), BCP and catastrophe exposure (VS-26/VS-69), and cyber posture (VS-27.3) — the same risk picture the Audit Committee receives (W1721) | Head of Internal Audit / Head of Internal Audit & Risk-function | Audit Committee Chair | 0.5 day |
| 5 | Financial & commercial deep-dive: P&L drivers and store-level economics (W1405 pattern), capex program and post-investment reviews (VS-40), treasury & financing structure (VS-18), tax posture (VS-79), and the merchandise/supply-chain operating model (VS-01/VS-02) at board-literacy depth | CFO / FP&A | CFO | 0.5–1 day |

## W5544. Emergency Executive Succession & Decision-Rights Continuity Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Death, sudden medical incapacitation, immediate-effect resignation, or board removal of the CEO or any C-level executive; or the simultaneous unavailability of a quorum-critical set of officers (e.g. CEO + CFO) in a single event |
| **Frequency** | Rare; realistically < 1 C-level event per decade; the succession register and tabletop are exercised annually |
| **Volume** | 1 event × the decision-rights surface across 5 legal entities (signing authorities, bank mandates, BIR-accredited and eFPS-authorized signatories, SEC/GIS officer records, board quorum, ERP approval chains) × ~6,911 employees whose payroll, approvals and reporting lines route through the affected office |
| **Owner** | Board Chair (CEO events); CEO (other C-level events) — with the Corporate Secretary as the continuity mechanic |
| **Participants** | Board Chair, Corporate Secretary, Nomination/Governance function per W1721, CFO, CHRO, VP Legal, affected-function deputies, Treasury (bank mandates per W317), Tax Manager (BIR signatory continuity), IT (access/approval-workflow reassignment), IR (disclosure), Communications (W855/W134 discipline) |

### Background

W178 owns planned succession and PA-36.2's officer-change workflow updates SEC/GIS records when an officer change happens — including "(d) death or incapacity" as a trigger — but nobody owned the emergency itself: the first-24-hour board mechanics, the interim appointment, the delegation-of-authority bridge so payments and approvals do not stall, the bank/BIR signatory continuity that keeps W556 payment runs and statutory filings valid, and the disclosure clock for a listed company. A C-suite vacancy is not merely a personnel event: every ERP approval chain, bank mandate and statutory signatory keyed to the officer becomes a control gap the moment the seat empties, and the anti-pattern is rushed, undocumented reassignment that breaks segregation of duties. This workflow is the emergency bridge from vacancy to interim stability; the permanent succession then rides W178.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Activation & immediate board action**: Corporate Secretary convenes an emergency board meeting (telephonic/written-consent per the by-laws) within 24 hours; board appoints the pre-identified interim officer from the succession register (each C-role carries a named interim + two alternates); if the event removes the CEO, the Board Chair holds decision rights until the interim is seated | Corporate Secretary / Board Chair | Board Chair | < 24 h |
| 2 | **Decision-rights & signing bridge**: activate the sealed delegation-of-authority matrix — interim officer assumes the role's approval limits; Treasury re-points bank signatory mandates per W317 (dual-signatory rule preserved); Tax Manager updates BIR-accredited and eFPS-authorized signatory lists so statutory filings and payment authorizations stay valid (links to W5546's filing-continuity concern); corporate resolutions evidencing the interim appointment generated per W1717 | Corporate Secretary / Treasury / Tax Manager | CFO (financial authorities) / CEO (operational authorities) | 24–72 h |
| 3 | **ERP access & approval continuity**: IT re-assigns the officer's approval-workflow roles to the interim under change control, with segregation-of-duties re-checked per W1408 (no accumulation of conflicting approvals in the rush); the vacant office's mailbox and records preserved per W1719 retention and legal-hold rules if the event may be contested | IT / Corporate Secretary | CIO | 24–72 h |
| 4 | **Communication rings**: (a) internal: executive team and direct reports first, then all-staff announcement with the interim's authority stated; (b) external: IR runs the materiality assessment per W5017 — a CEO's death/incapacitation or unexpected departure of a listed company is a disclosable event per W5019; lenders notified where facility agreements require officer notices; key vendors/customers contacted by the interim's office; all external messaging rides the W855/W134 single-spokesperson discipline | CHRO (internal) / IR (external) / Corporate Secretary | CEO / Board Chair | 24–96 h |
| 5 | **Benefits, estate & D&O interface** (death/incapacitation): death-in-service benefits administered per W1325; D&O and key-man insurance claims notified per the VS-26 policy machinery (W857–W864 class); executive's family receives a single named liaison; personal-effects and estate matters handled by HR/Legal under privacy discipline | HR Benefits / VP Legal | CHRO | Per event |
| 6 | **Permanent succession**: the Nomination/Governance function runs the permanent search/promotion per W178 and the board's committee process (W1721); interim-to-permanent handover documented with a decision-rights diff so no delegated authority silently persists | Nomination/Governance / Corporate Secretary | Board Chair | 1–6 months |
| 7 | **Post-event review & register upkeep**: within 30 days — what stalled, which delegations were exercised, signatory updates that lagged; the succession register (each C-role: named interim, alternates, sealed delegations, signatory inventory) refreshed; annual tabletop exercise scheduled with the BCP drill calendar | Corporate Secretary | Board Chair | 1 week |

### System Touchpoints
- Officer/director master records + SEC GIS officer fields (links to W1717/W1718, expedited filing path)
- Bank mandate/signatory register (links to W317) with dual-authorization change control
- BIR-accredited & eFPS-authorized signatory lists (links to W5546 filing continuity)
- ERP approval-workflow & role reassignment under SoD re-check (links to W1408)
- Delegation-of-authority matrix register (sealed per-role delegations; activation logging)
- D&O / key-man insurance policy records (links to VS-26)
- IR disclosure desk & materiality log (links to W5017/W5019)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Authority-vacuum risk**: payment runs, capex approvals and statutory filings stall while the seat is empty; mitigated by the sealed delegation-of-authority matrix activated in Step 2 within 72 hours
- **Signatory-lapse risk**: BIR/eFPS authorizations keyed to the departed officer lapse silently, converting a governance event into a statutory-filing failure; mitigated by the signatory inventory in the succession register and the Step-2 Tax Manager update
- **SoD-erosion risk**: rushed reassignment accumulates conflicting approvals; mitigated by the mandatory W1408 re-check inside Step 3
- **Disclosure-timing risk**: delayed or selective communication of a CEO event to the market; mitigated by the W5017 materiality assessment wired into Step 4
- **Lingering-delegation risk**: interim authorities persist after the permanent appointment; mitigated by the decision-rights diff in Step 6

### Automation Opportunity
- Succession-register dashboard flags any C-role without a live interim + alternates and auto-attaches the signatory inventory to the activation checklist (replaces manual Step 2 assembly)
- Delegation activations auto-generate the resolution pack (W1717), the bank/BIR change requests and the W1408 SoD re-check ticket (replaces manual Steps 2–3 coordination)

### Controls
- CTL-349 (ensure controlled execution — Board Meeting Management & Corporate Records (PA-36.1).); CTL-231 (ensure corporate-governance compliance.)
- operational: no interim appointment proceeds without board resolution evidence (W1717) and dual-authorization on every bank/BIR signatory change
- operational: every delegation activated in an emergency is inventoried in the decision-rights diff at permanent handover

### Time Estimate
Per event: Steps 1–4 ≈ 40–80 staff-hours across Corporate Secretary, Treasury, Tax, IT, HR and IR in the first 96 hours; Steps 5–7 ≈ 30–60 staff-hours spread over weeks; permanent succession effort rides W178. Steady state: succession-register upkeep + annual tabletop ≈ 8–12 hours/year, absorbed by the Corporate Secretary's office — no incremental headcount.

### Staffing Implication
- Absorbed by the Corporate Secretary's office, Treasury, Tax, IT and HR benches; the annual tabletop rides the existing BCP drill calendar. No incremental headcount.
